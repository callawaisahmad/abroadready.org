Working in University Holidays on a Student Visa

🗓️ Sep 14, 2026⏱️ 17 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Every international student lands abroad with the same question in the first month: can I work to pay my rent? The answer depends on two separate rules — the number of hours you are allowed during term time, and the very different, much more generous rules for working in university holidays on a student visa. Get those two rules confused and the consequences range from wasted opportunities to a broken visa file, so it is worth understanding them separately before you sign your first contract. This guide explains exactly what working in university holidays on a student visa allows you to earn, the hour limits that still apply, and how to stay within your conditions.

Can I actually work to pay my rent as an international student?

Yes, with limits. Almost every study visa in the world allows some paid work, and the amount depends on your course and your country. What matters is that the limit changes during the year, and that it changes because of the university calendar rather than because of your own schedule. Students who treat the year as one single rule either give up well paid summer work they were allowed to accept, or push too hard in term time and put their visa at risk.

Working in university holidays on a student visa is legal, expected, and in most countries effectively unlimited, and thousands of Pakistani students use the summer, winter and Easter breaks to top up their funds. The confusion arises because students assume the strict term time limit applies all year round, so they quietly turn down full time jobs in summer, or worse, they work full time in term time and lose their visa.

Which countries does this guide cover?

This guide sorts out the holiday rules for the five most popular destinations for Pakistani students: the UK, Australia, Ireland, Canada, and New Zealand. The numbers below are accurate as of 2026, but immigration rules change, so always verify on the official government portal for your country before relying on a single figure.

What is the basic difference between term time and holiday work?

The core concept is simple. During term time — the academic period when your programme is running — you are limited to a set number of weekly or fortnightly hours. That limit exists so study stays your main activity and your full time student status stays enforceable.

But during scheduled holidays, sometimes called vacations or breaks, most countries deliberately remove the limit and let you work full time. Immigration authorities accept this because the university calendar itself confirms that you are not in taught classes during those weeks. In plain words: your visa says what kind of student you are, and your university calendar says when you are on holiday. The two together decide how many hours you can legally work on any given day of the year.

The holiday exemption: working in university holidays on a student visa legally

Think of the holiday exemption as the release valve on the student work rules. The daily reality for an international student is that a twenty hour week rarely covers rent in any of these countries, and a full time week usually does. That gap is the entire reason the exemption exists.

What does working in university holidays on a student visa actually mean?

It means that your work rights are read twice a year: once against the term time ceiling, and once against the official holiday dates. In the first window you are a part time worker with a hard cap. In the second window you are, in most of these countries, an unrestricted worker for as long as the calendar says the course is out of session. Nothing about your visa changes in between; only the date on the calendar does.

Why is the holiday exemption so important for rent and bills?

The holiday exemption is how the system lets you catch up: a summer of full time work can build a genuinely useful cushion, and it is fully legal as long as you only do it during the right weeks. If your term time income covers food and transport, the holiday window can cover a semester of rent in advance, a flight home, or the laptop replacement you have been putting off for a year.

What exactly does "scheduled vacation" mean?

The phrase to remember is "scheduled vacation", "scheduled holiday", or "scheduled closure", because it is the university calendar, not you, that decides when the window opens, and the rights attach to the calendar, not to your mood or your bank balance. Every immigration authority uses slightly different wording, but the legal test is the same in all five countries covered here.

Does a self-declared study break count as a holiday?

No, and this is the key distinction most students miss. A self-declared study break is not a holiday in the eyes of the visa rules. If your university runs terms from September to May, that is your academic year, and any weeks in the middle when you happen to have no lectures are still term time for the purposes of your work limit.

What about distance learning students and students repeating a module?

Distance learning students and students repeating a module discover that their "free" weeks are not official holidays at all, and students bridging two programmes often lose the summer entirely because the next enrolment has already started. The rules refer to the official academic calendar of your institution, meaning the dates published before the year starts, not the gaps created by your personal timetable.

Who do I ask if I am not sure whether a break is official?

If you are unsure, ask your university's international student office or read the official guidance for your country, and keep a printed copy of the calendar as evidence of every holiday you work in. International offices answer these questions every single week, so do not be embarrassed — bring the dates you are unsure about and ask for them in writing, so the answer is on a dated document you can keep.

Where exactly do the holiday dates start and end?

There is a timing trap at the edges of a holiday. In many countries the right to full time work switches on and off at midnight of the official holiday dates. Working full time one day before the holiday starts, or one day after it ends, is a breach even if it is a single shift.

What happens if I work one day too early or one day too late?

Employers who are careless about this can record your hours against your record, and if immigration later reviews your payment history, a single off days shift can become a serious problem when you try to extend your visa, renew your enrolment, or apply for a post study work permit. So treat the calendar dates as hard boundaries, like visa expiry dates, not as approximations that rhyme with your schedule. A shift that ends at 11pm on the last night of term is still a term time shift.

UK rules: term time limits versus full time in holidays

The UK is the country where working in university holidays matters most to Pakistani students, because UK tuition is high, London rent is relentless, and the exchange rate works against the rupee. It is also the country where students most often assume the term time rule runs all year.

Is working in university holidays on a student visa unlimited in the UK?

For the duration of the official holiday itself, yes — there is no hourly cap at all in the UK once the vacation starts. The cap returns the moment term time resumes, and a separate set of conditions applies after your course end date. So the accurate version of the sentence is: unlimited during official university vacations, twenty hours a week otherwise, and something else entirely once the course is finished.

How many hours can I work in the UK during term time?

Under the Student visa rules you may generally work up to twenty hours per week during term time, and you may work full time during official university holidays. The twenty hour figure is a ceiling, not a target. Most Pakistani students in the UK settle into somewhere between twelve and eighteen hours a week during term, which is enough to cover food and phone bills without touching rent.

Which UK students are not allowed to work at all?

Students on degree level courses qualify for work, while students on below degree level courses, such as some standalone English language programmes, are usually prohibited from working altogether, so check your specific course level first. This catches people out constantly, because an English language student and a master's student can be sitting in the same city on similar visas with completely different work rights.

How does the UK right to work check work?

Your right to work is stated on your visa and on your BRP or your electronic record, and your employer performs a right to work check using the Home Office online service, where you generate a share code from your own account. A compliant boss will ask for your share code before your first shift, and if they employ someone without the right to work they can be penalised heavily and even lose their licence.

Is the UK twenty hour limit per week or averaged over a month?

It is measured per week, not averaged over a month, and it counts every paid hour including the paid breaks inside a shift. Employers in the UK are legally responsible for checking your right to work, which is why a careful employer will not let you log a thirty hour week and simply average it down later. Unpaid breaks only help if they are genuinely unpaid in writing.

What do the official UK sources say about holiday work?

The full mechanics of the Student visa itself are explained in the UK student visa guide for Pakistani students, and the official employment pages on gov.uk set out the working conditions you should print and keep. If an employer or a friend tells you something different from what those pages say, the pages win.

Can you show me a worked UK example?

Suppose you are a postgraduate student at a UK university with term dates from late September to mid December, mid January to late March, and late April to early June. Your official summer holiday runs from June to September. In term time, your employer will not let you log more than twenty hours in any single week, because the payroll system and the compliance software check that.

In July, that same employer can put you on a forty hour week with no issue, because the calendar confirms you are in a scheduled vacation. If, instead, you work forty hours in a week in late November because you found a second job on a gig platform, your record will show a breach, and a later visa extension could be refused.

What is the safest habit for tracking UK hours?

Keep your own running hour log in parallel with your payslips, month by month, because when a renewal officer asks the question, a clean spreadsheet answers it better than a memory. Reconcile the log against your bank statements once a month, because a deposit from an employer that does not appear in your log is a question you will want to answer early rather than late.

Can I work full time after my UK course ends?

One more UK nuance: the period after your official course end is not unlimited holiday. Once your course period finishes, the Student route holiday logic no longer applies in the same way, and full time work may only continue within the conditions of your visa. If you are planning to work a full summer after your final exams and then leave, or switch into a Graduate visa, confirm the exact dates first, because the Graduate visa, not the Student visa, is what formally unlocks unrestricted work after your course ends.

Australia: 48 hours per fortnight and what holidays change

Australia is famous for the most generous work rights of the five countries, and the holiday rule is generous too. On a Student visa you may generally work up to 48 hours per fortnight during study periods, and you may work unlimited hours when your course is not in session.

How is the 48 hour fortnight actually calculated?

The fortnightly calculation is its own trap. The limit is 48 hours per fortnight, meaning over any consecutive 14 day period, not per calendar month and not averaged across the whole term. A student working 30 hours one week and 18 the next is fine, because the total is 48.

Is 40 hours one week and 14 the next legal in Australia?

No — that example fails. A student working 40 hours one week and 14 the next totals 54 over that fortnight window, which is over 48, so that fortnight is a breach even though neither week looks alarming on its own. Keep it simple: never touch 49 in any rolling 14 days. The rolling window means you also cannot hide a bad fortnight behind a quiet one, because any 14 day stretch that crosses the peak is what counts.

Why does the crossover fortnight cause problems?

In practice, shifts that fall inside a scheduled break but inside the same fortnight as the final week of term must be added together. If your term ends on a Friday and the holiday starts on Saturday, the two halves of that fortnight still share the same allowance in borderline cases, so check how your institution defines the session boundary and keep that crossover fortnight clean by logging every single hour. This is the single most common Australian breach, and it is entirely avoidable with a spreadsheet.

How do Australian authorities decide if you are genuinely on holiday?

As with the UK, the qualification is the official calendar: work beyond the limit is only allowed when your course is officially out of session, which typically means the scheduled breaks in your university's calendar, not the weeks when you have no lectures. Australian authorities describe this ability as reflecting the actual study schedule of the applicant, and they check enrolment records rather than taking your word for it.

Where can I verify the rules for working in university holidays on a student visa in Australia?

The official rules live on the Australian Department of Home Affairs pages, and the Student visa 500 page is the source you should check for the current fortnight limit. The Australia student visa guide from Pakistan walks through the whole application end to end, including how enrolment is confirmed after you arrive.

Does unpaid work count toward my Australian fortnight total?

There is an important reconciliation rule in Australia: paid work done within the fortnight is counted, and so is unpaid work for some registered providers in certain programs, such as mandatory professional experience placements under some circumstances. The safest habit is to total your hours across each 14 day block in a running spreadsheet, because a single mistake in January can surface months later when you renew your visa, and Australian compliance reviews have been tightening since 2024.

Ireland, Canada and New Zealand: the 40 hour club and the no cap country

Ireland holds a special place in this conversation because the term time limit is low, which makes the holiday window even more valuable. Canada and New Zealand behave differently again, and the three together cover most of what Pakistani students need to know about the rest of the English speaking world.

What are the Irish rules for working in university holidays on a student visa?

Students on a Stamp 2 visa, the standard study permission, can work up to twenty hours per week during term time and up to forty hours per week during the official holidays of the academic year, including the Christmas and summer breaks. Forty hours a week is not unlimited, so an Irish student still has to clock out at the end of the holiday window even if the money is good.

Does a gap between semesters count as a holiday in Ireland?

You cannot take a full time job outside those defined holidays, and the definition is the published academic calendar of your college, so a two week gap between semesters is not automatically a holiday. A reading week, a revision week, or a week when your lecturer was on leave all still count as term time in the eyes of the rules.

Where do I check the Irish working rules?

The Irish immigration service website publishes the working rules and its own holiday guidance, and your college international office can confirm the exact calendar dates it reports to the authorities. Ask for the dates the college has actually submitted, because what matters is the calendar the institution reports, not the one on the student portal.

Is undeclared cash work risky in Ireland?

Yes. The practical warning for Ireland is undeclared earnings. Working the permitted hours is legal, but earning cash in hand and failing to declare it, or stacking multiple part time jobs toward effectively full time hours outside a holiday, is not. Irish authorities enforce this more strictly than students expect, and the tax declaration trail is exactly what you will need later when you apply for a succeeding permissions upgrade after graduation. Pakistani students in Ireland should always ensure their employer registers them and issues payslips, because payslip history is the cleanest proof that every hour you worked was a legal hour.

What are the rules for international students in Canada?

Canada is the standout because its rule set is the simplest of the five. International students holding a study permit and studying full time at a designated learning institution can work off campus without any specified cap during scheduled academic breaks, such as the summer break, and up to 24 hours per week during regular academic sessions as of late 2024, a limit that remains in place through 2026. During a scheduled break there is simply no off campus limit at all, so a student enrolled over the winter can legally work a full time schedule all summer without a separate work permit, as long as they resume full time study in the fall.

What counts as a scheduled break in Canada?

The break must be scheduled in the institution's calendar; a voluntary gap in your enrolment does not qualify, and co op and internship work still falls under their own rules. If you take a leave of absence or reduce your course load to finish early, the calendar changes, and so does your holiday entitlement. Announce any change to your enrolment in writing and get the new dates confirmed before you accept a full time summer contract.

What are the New Zealand rules for international students?

New Zealand is the final member of the group, and its rule is clean: international students holding a study visa may work up to twenty hours per week during term time and up to forty hours per week during scheduled holidays. As in Ireland, the definition comes from the institution's academic calendar, so the summer holiday between semesters is the safe window for full time work.

Are New Zealand English language students allowed to work?

Students on English language courses of less than twenty six weeks are generally not allowed to work at all, and some postgraduate research students carry their own specific conditions. The New Zealand student visa guide covers the details for that route, including the conditions attached to research degrees and the exact definition of a scheduled holiday on that country's calendar.

What actually counts as a holiday at your university

This is the section that saves the most students from trouble, because the calendar is not what most people assume. The rules below apply across all five countries, and they are the ones that decide whether a week of full time work was legal.

Which document officially defines a holiday?

The official academic calendar published before your course starts is the reference document. Easter, winter, and summer breaks are almost always official holidays, and the university publishes their start and end dates every year. Save the version you were given at enrolment, because calendars get revised and you want the document that matches the dates you actually worked.

Is a reading week or study break a holiday?

A reading week, a study break, or a module gap is usually not a holiday for work purposes, because the university still considers the session open, even if no lectures run that week. This is the assumption that causes the most breaches, because from your side the week looks identical to a holiday. From the regulator's side, the session is open and the term time limit applies in full.

What happens once my course formally ends?

Once your course formally ends, you move into a post course period that has its own rules, so check your visa conditions before staying on for a full summer of work after your final exams. In the UK this is the point where the Graduate route matters, in Australia it is where a completion visa takes over, and in Canada and New Zealand your study permission may expire entirely if you do not have a bridging arrangement in place.

What is the trap for distance learning and part time students?

Distance learning and part time students have an extra trap. Since the work rules are tied to the course being out of session, a distance student completing modules year round often has no official holiday window at all. Some universities disclose the gaps that distance students can use, but if your enrolment shows the course running across the summer, the holiday exemption does not apply to you, regardless of how lightly you are studying. Before you accept a summer contract, ask the international office to confirm in writing whether your specific programme has any out of session period.

Is holiday work allowed if I am not a student in good standing?

No. Employment during an official holiday is only legal if you are still a student in good standing. If you suspend, defer, or withdraw from the course, your study permission changes, and any holiday clause in your visa stops protecting you. Withdrawing in May and then working a full summer in June on the strength of the old calendar is a classic visa breach, and it is the kind of error that ends study plans completely.

What evidence should I save about your holidays?

Finally, save the evidence. Screenshot your university calendar page with the official dates, keep the email the registrar sent about the term structure, and write the holiday dates into any contract with a holiday employer. If immigration ever checks your work record against the calendar, you want to point at a dated document, not at a memory of a conversation with a friend in the registrar's office. The part time jobs guide for international students lists the safest first jobs across all five countries, and the proof of funds guide explains how a legal work record actually supports your money evidence later.

How employers check your right to work, and the risks of getting it wrong

Employers in all five countries do a legal check before you start, and the mechanics differ. Knowing the check in advance means you can have the right document ready on day one instead of losing a shift while somebody works out how to verify you.

How do UK employers check my right to work?

In the UK, the employer uses the Home Office online right to work service and you supply a share code that you generate from your own gov.uk account, so the employer never copies documents at all. Generate the code on the morning of your first shift if the employer asks, because codes expire and a stale code is a wasted shift for both of you.

How do Australian employers check my right to work?

In Australia, the employer checks that you actually hold a valid Student visa with the right work condition, and many use the online verification service to confirm your record. They will also ask about your course dates, because the work condition and the out of session periods both depend on enrolment, not on the visa document alone.

How do Irish, Canadian and New Zealand employers check?

In Ireland, the employer checks your passport and your Stamp 2 permission, and reads the specific condition that states your permitted hours. In Canada, the employer usually asks for your study permit and validates the conditions. In New Zealand, the employer verifies your visa through the official online validation service. In every case, be ready to say the same thing about your course level and your holiday dates that your university would say.

Why are employers so cautious about checking?

In every case, the employer is not asking out of bureaucracy; they are legally liable if they employ a person without the right to work, so their caution is your protection. A cautious employer who checks your record on day one is far better for you than a careless one who never checks and then reports your hours to the immigration authority without ever having looked at your visa.

What are the consequences of getting the holiday rule wrong?

Getting the holiday rule wrong has a predictable chain of consequences. A first offence usually produces a warning and a smaller penalty, but a repeated breach, working full time across several terms, can trigger a compliance visit and a review of your enrolment. The pattern is consistent across countries: the problem is rarely the single breach, and it is the pattern of repeated breaches that turns a warning into a cancellation.

What happens to UK students who breach the limit?

For UK students, breaches can lead to curtailment, where your Student visa is cut short and you are given a deadline to leave. Curtailment is not automatic after one mistake, but it becomes far more likely once a breach is on record and a second one appears while the first is still being reviewed.

What happens to Australian students who exceed 48 hours?

For Australian students, exceeding the 48 hour fortnight limit can lead to cancellation after a recorded warning, and even a single cancelled visa then poisons future applications from Pakistan, because the refusal and cancellation history becomes visible to every later decision maker. A cancellation is therefore not just about this year; it changes the questions you will be asked on every future application, in Australia and elsewhere.

What is the most damaging breach for a visa renewal?

The quietest and most painful damage is at extension time: visa officers reviewing your renewal often pull your work records, and a student who appears to be working more than the rules allow looks like someone whose study visa is mostly a work permit. That perception, fair or not, decides the extension. This is why a tidy paper trail sometimes matters more than a large summer salary.

What is the honest bottom line for a Pakistani student?

Use the holiday allowance confidently, because it is generous and completely legal, but treat the calendar dates like visa expiry dates. Keep a running hour log, get payslips for everything, keep your enrolment active and full time, and when a job offer looks too good because it lets you work thirty hours in term time, it is not a bargain; it is a risk priced into the contract. A visa is worth more than any single summer of extra hours, because it is what gives you the right to be in the country at all.

How should I budget across the year?

When you budget your year, plan the holiday window as your real earning period and treat term time as pocket money. The study abroad cost and budget guide explains how to turn a realistic part time income into a working budget, so you do not have to lean on illegal hours at any point. The students who budget this way sleep better, because they are not quietly hoping a term time shift will stretch further than it can.

What actually counts as work, and what earns you less than you think

Even inside the holiday window, two questions cause most of the trouble: what legally counts as work, and how much of the money actually reaches you. Both have answers that surprise people, usually in the same direction.

What counts as "work" in the UK?

The holiday rules govern paid employment, but the definitions of "work" differ subtly between countries, and a wrong guess can cost a whole term of legality. In the United Kingdom, work means any employment, self employment, or work under a contract that produces pay or other remuneration, with a strong emphasis on cash in hand jobs still counting as work.

Do volunteer roles and gig app shifts count as work?

The nuance matters because some churches, temples and community bodies run volunteer arrangements that do not count as work, while a paid gig economy shift from a delivery app absolutely does count, even if the pay lands in an app wallet rather than a bank account. If money changes hands in any form, assume it counts and check the specific guidance rather than assuming the opposite.

How do the other countries define work?

Australian rules count paid work under any arrangement, and mandatory workplace training under some registered qualifications can also count toward the fortnight total, so a placement that feels like learning can still hit your hours. Irish and New Zealand rules are similar, and in Canada the off campus work cap applies to paid work, not to voluntary work for a non profit organisation. The practical rule is the same everywhere: if there is a payment, or a mandatory requirement attached to it, log it and count it.

How much will I actually take home in the UK?

The second surprise is how little net money some holiday jobs produce once tax and costs appear. A part time rate in the UK for a student role commonly sits in the region of 11 to 13 pounds an hour, from which tax and national insurance may be deducted if you earn above the threshold, and your commute eats both money and hours. A forty hour week in July sounds like a large number until you subtract an hour of travel each way, a lunch you buy, and the tax that leaves your payslip before it reaches your account.

Why is a gross rate comparison between countries misleading?

A flat comparison of gross rates across countries misleads, because Ireland and Australia have higher headline rates but higher costs of living and more aggressive tax brackets, while Canada shows strong hourly rates in cities with expensive rents. Gross pay tells you what your employer promised; net pay plus housing cost tells you what you can actually live on.

How do I calculate my real holiday income?

The honest way to plan is the net calculation: take the holiday hours you are allowed, the net hourly rate after tax, and the number of weeks in the holiday window, and multiply them into a range rather than a single romantic figure. Build the low, middle and high version, then budget against the low one. Add your expected term time earnings separately, and never assume a holiday window will be longer than the calendar says it is.

What is the trap with self employment and small side businesses?

There is also a structural trap with self employment and small side businesses. In the UK, a student who starts an online business, sells services to clients, or runs a tutoring operation is technically self employed, and self employment has its own tax, registration and student condition implications that casual employment does not. In Ireland, self employment interacts with the Stamp 2 conditions in ways that casual contract work avoids. Running a tutoring operation during term time is exactly the kind of thing that looks harmless and turns into an unauthorised work finding later.

What is the simplest route to provable holiday income?

If your only plan is a paid holiday job, the by far simplest route is normal employee status with a registered employer issuing payslips, and you should treat the phrase "we will pay you in cash, no paperwork" as an immediate warning that the hours may never be provable for your renewal, whatever the employer promises. In cash you keep more today and you have nothing at all to show at renewal, which is a bad trade for a student whose next decision depends on paperwork.

Holiday work versus post study work: protecting your future file

The strangest fact about holiday work is that its real value is not the salary; it is the work record you build, because that record becomes the backbone of your post study work application. If you treat the summer purely as an income opportunity, you get the money and lose the file. If you treat it as the start of a documented employment history, you get both.

How does working in university holidays on a student visa protect your post study file?

In the UK, the Graduate route does not require a specific work record, but employers hiring you after graduation will scrutinise every payslip you can show, and a clean history of legal hours with a regular employer does your CV favours that a cash job can never match. In Australia, the path to a temporary graduate visa involves showing you met your enrolment obligations, and your work history is one of the records that gets reviewed.

What happens in Ireland and New Zealand?

In Ireland and New Zealand, post study permission reviews look at your compliance history, and your record as a worker is part of it. In every one of these systems, the thousands of pounds you earn in a summer matter far less than the clean, dated, verified record of having earned them legally. The officer reviewing your graduate application is not trying to make your life difficult; they are trying to establish a pattern, and a clean record is a pattern that answers the question immediately.

Which employers create a record an officer can read?

This changes how you should treat every shift. A gig platform that pays you directly and reports you by your national insurance number creates a trail an officer can read; a cafe that fiddles your hours to stay under thresholds creates a trail that quietly contradicts the calendar. When you take holiday work, take it from employers who register you, put you on a rota, and pay through the tax system, and keep every schedule email in a folder. A rota is worth keeping even when the pay is the same, because it is the only document that shows you were working during the weeks you say you were on holiday.

Why do cash jobs create a record that contradicts your calendar?

Because an undeclared job leaves no official trace of when you worked, and when an officer asks a question your file cannot answer, the gap is read against you. When the time comes to extend your study permission or move to a work permission, you want to be the applicant whose file answers every question with a payslip, not the applicant whose file answers with a memory. Memory fails, and it fails at exactly the moment you most need it to be reliable.

What is a personal hour ledger?

One more protection worth building while you earn: a personal hour ledger. Many successful students keep a simple spreadsheet with a row per week, the date, the employer, the shift hours, and a note of whether the week falls inside term time or an official holiday. It takes thirty seconds a shift and it converts a messy paper trail into a story you can print in five minutes. The row for each week should also say which calendar page you checked, so the ledger and the evidence reference each other.

How does the ledger stop me from overworking?

It also stops the slow drift of overwork, because the act of writing down forty one hours in a term week makes the error obvious while it is still fixable, instead of hidden in a summary that only surfaces during a renewal audit. Build the ledger the day you start the first job, and keep it running until the day your studies end. The habit is the point: the ledger is not the evidence, it is what makes the evidence easy to find.

What to do if you realise you have already breached the rules

Mistakes happen, and pretending they did not is the most expensive response available. If you discover that you worked over your limit, or that a cash job went unrecorded, or that you worked a shift one day before the holiday officially began, the sequence below is the realistic path.

What is the first thing I should do after a breach?

First, stop immediately: do not add a second strike while you figure out the first one. Resist the temptation to keep the hours that have already been paid, because every additional week makes the eventual disclosure more complicated and the eventual record worse. Once the additional work has stopped, everything else in this list becomes manageable.

Why should I document the mistake honestly?

Second, document what happened honestly with dates, hours, and payslips, because a documented mistake presented voluntarily reads very differently from the same mistake discovered by an audit. Put the facts in writing while your memory is fresh, and keep the payslips that show exactly which weeks the hours fell in. A short, accurate timeline is more useful than a long defensive explanation.

What should I check about my visa and enrolment?

Third, check your visa conditions and whether your enrolment is still fully active, because an enrolment problem compounds a work problem. If you have also missed classes, let go of a module, or failed something, deal with that first or at least alongside the work question, because immigration reviews both together and a student with an enrolment issue and an hours issue looks like a much larger problem than either one alone.

Who should I ask for advice after a breach?

Fourth, seek professional advice from a registered immigration adviser or the free advice service your university operates; do not paste your situation into a foreign forum and follow strangers. A registered adviser can tell you whether the breach needs a disclosure at all, and a university adviser can tell you whether the calendar you assumed was the wrong one. Free university advice is underused, and it is exactly the resource that exists for this situation.

Why should I never build a renewal on a repaired lie?

Fifth, and most important, do not build your renewal on a repaired lie. If you are asked on a form whether you have ever breached your conditions, the cost of disclosure in writing is usually lower than the cost of being caught later, because immigration records keep work histories for years and the discoverable truth tends to surface at the moment you need the permission most. A renewal built on a false answer is a second breach waiting to be found, and this time it will be attached to the first one permanently.

What happens in the UK and in Australia after a breach?

In the United Kingdom, curtailment after a work breach starts with a notice and a chance to respond, and a prompt, honest response with evidence can change the outcome. In Australia, the Department of Home Affairs records warnings that affect later applications, so the damage is real but it is smaller and more predictable when you face it directly. Canada and New Zealand operate on similar principles: a documented, addressed, and stopped problem is treated very differently from an ongoing one.

What is the best outcome I can aim for after a breach?

Build the folder, tell your adviser the truth, and let the system treat you as what you are: a student who made one mistake and stopped. The difference between a warning and a cancellation is frequently nothing more than what happened in the four weeks after the mistake was discovered. Do those four weeks well, and the rest of your study period and your post study application are still intact.

A worked example: a full year of legal holiday work

To show how the rules combine across a real year, follow a Pakistani master's student in the UK whose course runs from late September to mid June, with the summer holiday running June to September. Then watch the same calendar logic applied in Australia and Canada, because the pattern is identical even though the numbers are not.

How much can a UK master's student earn in a legal year?

Term time: she works about sixteen hours a week in a campus cafe, which sits safely under twenty, and earns roughly 5,400 pounds over the academic year. Sixteen hours rather than nineteen leaves a buffer for the weeks when exams, deadlines or a heavy assignment make a full twenty hour week unrealistic, which is a deliberate margin rather than lost income.

What does the summer holiday add?

Summer: the cafe promotes her to a coordinator role at forty hours a week, and over twelve weeks she earns roughly 8,000 pounds. Total across the year is roughly 13,400 pounds of legal income, which materially changes her budget. She can now cover a full year of rent plus flights home out of the summer alone, and she does it without a single hour that an officer could question.

What would happen if she ignored the calendar?

If she had ignored the calendar and worked thirty hours during the spring term instead of using the summer, she would be in exactly the same financial position but in a compliance position that could sink her post study work application. The earnings are identical; the risk profile is completely different. That is the entire argument for using the holiday exemption properly rather than reaching for term time hours.

How does the same year look in Australia?

Now move the same student to Australia. Across term she averages 44 hours per fortnight at a supermarket, and in the summer break she moves to full time hours per week with no cap. Forty four rather than forty eight is again a deliberate margin, and it is what keeps her safe in a fortnight where a sick day or an extra shift could otherwise have tipped her over.

What does the crossover fortnight look like in practice?

The same discipline applies at the crossover: the fortnight that contains the last week of term and the first week of the holiday still has to be totalled, and she keeps that fortnight under 48 by logging every shift. This is the fortnight that turns a well behaved student into a cancelled visa, purely because two small numbers were added together at the wrong moment. She treats that fortnight as the most dangerous fortnight of the year, which it is.

How does Canada compare?

In Canada the equivalent shift is even simpler: her study permit has no off campus cap during the scheduled summer break, so she works a full contract without any hourly arithmetic, then returns to 24 hours a week when fall lectures start. The absence of arithmetic does not mean the absence of rules; it means the calendar, not the hour count, is the only thing she has to get right.

What is the pattern across every country?

The pattern across every country is identical: the calendar decides, the spreadsheet records, and the payslips prove it. The hours change, the visa names change, the tax system changes, and the reporting requirements change, but the three step discipline is the same in Dublin as it is in Dunedin. That is what makes this manageable for a student who has never worked abroad before.

Where should I start with working in university holidays on a student visa?

If you are just starting out, begin with a part time job in your first term to learn the employer expectations and the payslip rhythm, then use the holidays to ramp up. A first term job teaches you how the right to work check runs, how payroll records your hours, and what a payslip actually contains, so that the holiday job you really want is a step up rather than a leap into the unknown.

What is the one habit that makes all of this work?

Nobody fails for working too little in their first term; people fail for working too much when the calendar said they should not. Build the hour log in week one, celebrate every legal full time holiday shift you land, and always verify the current limit on the official immigration website of your country, because the figures in this article are accurate as of 2026 but can move by a term without notice. Working in university holidays on a student visa is one of the most generous rights you hold as an international student, and the students who get the most out of it are the ones who respect the calendar dates to the day.

Frequently asked questions

Can I work full time during university holidays on a student visa?+

Yes, in most countries, but only during scheduled university holidays defined in the official academic calendar. The UK, Australia, Ireland and New Zealand permit full time holiday work within set limits, and Canada has no off campus cap in scheduled breaks. Gaps in your own timetable do not count as holidays.

How many hours can I work in the UK during holidays?+

UK degree level students can work full time during official university vacations and up to twenty hours per week in term time. The switch happens at midnight on the official calendar dates, so working full time one day before a holiday starts is still a breach. Always verify the dates on the gov.uk site.

Does the 48 hour fortnight limit apply during Australian holidays?+

No. During scheduled breaks when your course is officially out of session, you can work unlimited hours in Australia. During study periods the 48 hours per fortnight limit applies to every rolling 14 day block. Count all paid shifts in that total, and keep the crossover fortnight between term and holiday clean.

What happens if I work more than the holiday rules allow?+

A breach can lead to a warning, the curtailment of your visa in the UK, or cancellation in Australia and New Zealand. On renewal, visa officers review your work records, and repeated overwork makes your study visa look like a work permit. Keep an hour log and payslips for every month.
