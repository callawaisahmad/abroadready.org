Post-Study Work Visa Comparison 2026: UK vs Canada vs Australia vs Germany

🗓️ Aug 17, 2026⏱️ 14 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Choosing where to study abroad is not just an academic decision. It is a career decision. The country where you spend two or three years earning a degree is also the country where you might launch your professional life, build a network, and eventually settle. The single biggest factor that determines whether that career launch happens is post-study work visa policy.

Without the right to stay and work after graduation, even the best degree forces you home immediately. With it, you gain a crucial window to find employment, gain experience, and in many cases transition to permanent residence. In 2026, the four most popular destinations for international students — the UK, Canada, Australia, and Germany — each offer distinct post-study work pathways with different durations, costs, eligibility rules, and permanent residency implications.

This comparison breaks down exactly what each country offers, where each system excels, and how to choose the right destination based on your career and immigration goals.

Key takeaways

  • UK Graduate Route: 2 years (3 years for PhD), must switch to Skilled Worker visa before expiry
  • Canada PGWP: up to 3 years, open work permit, clearest and fastest PR pathway
  • Australia Temporary Graduate 485: 2–4 years depending on qualification level and study location, with regional extensions
  • Germany Job Seeker Visa: 18 months, PR possible after approximately 4 years with qualifying employment
  • All four countries allow changing employers freely on the post-study work visa
  • Application costs: UK GBP 285, Canada CAD 255, Australia AUD 1,895, Germany EUR 75

UK Graduate Route

The UK Graduate Route allows international students who have completed a degree at a UK institution to stay and work for two years, or three years if they studied at PhD level. It is an open work permit, meaning you can work for any employer, change jobs at any time, be self-employed, or even work in a role unrelated to your degree.

Eligibility: You must have completed a degree at a UK higher education provider on a Student visa for a minimum period specified by that institution. The institution must be a licensed Student visa sponsor. You must apply from within the UK before your Student visa expires.

Duration: 2 years for bachelor's and master's graduates, 3 years for PhD graduates. The visa cannot be extended. You must switch to another visa category before it expires, typically the Skilled Worker visa.

Cost: GBP 285 application fee, plus the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) of GBP 1,035 per year. You also need to pay for biometrics and any associated costs. The total upfront cost including the IHS is approximately GBP 2,355 for a two-year Graduate Route.

PR pathway: The Graduate Route is not a direct route to permanent residence. You must switch to a Skilled Worker visa (which requires a qualifying job with a licensed sponsor and meeting a salary threshold) and then apply for Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) after five years of continuous residence on that visa. This makes the UK pathway longer and more employer-dependent than Canada or Australia.

Key limitation: The Graduate Route is a one-time opportunity. Once you have used it, you cannot apply again. If you do not secure a Skilled Worker sponsor before the two or three years expire, you may need to leave the UK.

Canada Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)

Canada's Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) is widely considered the most straightforward and generous post-study work pathway among major English-speaking destinations. It allows graduates of eligible Canadian programmes to work in Canada for a period that corresponds to the length of their study programme.

Eligibility: You must have completed a programme of at least 8 months at a designated learning institution (DLI) in Canada, studying full-time. The programme must lead to a degree, diploma, or certificate. You must apply within 180 days of receiving your final marks and must hold a valid study permit at the time of application or have held one within the past 180 days.

Duration: Programs of 8 months to less than 2 years receive a PGWP valid for the length of the programme. Programmes of 2 years or more receive a 3-year PGWP. This is a significant advantage for students in longer programmes.

Cost: CAD 255, which is among the lowest fees for any post-study work visa globally. There is no health surcharge component equivalent to the UK's IHS, though health coverage varies by province and you may need to arrange private insurance depending on your province of residence.

PR pathway: This is where Canada excels. PGWP holders working in Canada can qualify for permanent residence through Express Entry's Canadian Experience Class (CEC) programme, which gives significant CRS points for Canadian work experience. Provincial Nominee Programmes (PNPs) offer additional pathways. Many graduates receive PR invitations within one to two years of starting work. Canada's system is deliberately designed to convert international students into permanent residents.

Key limitation: The PGWP can only be issued once in a lifetime. If you complete a second programme in Canada, you are not eligible for another PGWP unless the second programme is at a higher level and you meet specific conditions. Choose your programme carefully.

Australia Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485)

Australia's Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485) is a flexible post-study work visa with duration that varies based on your qualification level and where you studied. It offers some of the longest post-study work rights available, particularly for graduates who studied in regional areas.

Eligibility: You must have completed a qualification at a CRICOS-registered institution in Australia. You must have held a student visa within the past six months and apply while in Australia (or in some cases offshore). You need adequate health insurance and must meet character and health requirements.

Duration: This is where the 485 visa varies significantly:

  • Graduate Work stream: 18 months for qualifications related to an occupation on the skilled occupation list
  • Post-Study Work stream: 2 years for bachelor's degree, 3 years for master's by coursework, 4 years for master's by research or doctoral degree
  • Regional extension: An additional 1–2 years for graduates who studied in designated regional areas of Australia, meaning some graduates can stay for up to 6 years total

Cost: AUD 1,895, which is the most expensive post-study work visa among these four countries. You also need Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC), which typically costs AUD 400–700 per year.

PR pathway: The 485 visa is a stepping stone to permanent residence. While on the 485 visa, you gain Australian work experience, which earns significant points under the General Skilled Migration points test. Graduates can then apply for the Skilled Independent visa (189), Skilled Nominated visa (190), or employer-sponsored visas (482 to 186). Regional graduates receive bonus points and priority processing for regional PR pathways.

Key advantage: The regional bonus is a powerful strategy. Studying in cities like Adelaide, Hobart, or regional universities in Queensland and Western Australia can extend your post-study work rights to 5–6 years and give you additional PR points that often make the difference between invitation and rejection.

Germany Job Seeker Visa

Germany offers a different model: an 18-month Job Seeker Visa that allows graduates of German universities to remain in the country while looking for employment. This is not a work permit, but it gives you substantial flexibility during your job search.

Eligibility: You must have completed a degree from a recognised German university or an equivalent foreign qualification that has been recognised through the anabin database. You must demonstrate that you can support yourself financially during the job search period, typically by showing a blocked account with approximately EUR 12,000.

Duration: 18 months. During this period, you can work up to 20 hours per week in any job to support yourself while searching for a position that qualifies for a work permit or EU Blue Card. You cannot be employed in a permanent, full-time role during this period — only temporary or part-time work is permitted.

Cost: EUR 75 for the visa itself, which is remarkably affordable. However, you must also account for the blocked account requirement of approximately EUR 12,000, health insurance (mandatory from day one, costing EUR 100–200 per month), and living expenses. Germany has no tuition fees at most public universities, which partially offsets these costs.

PR pathway: Once you find qualifying employment, you switch to an employment-based residence permit or an EU Blue Card. After four years of legal residence with a qualifying employment permit, you can apply for a permanent residence permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis). If you have B1-level German language skills, this timeline can be reduced to as little as 21 months under certain conditions. Germany's PR pathway is solid but slower than Canada's, and German language proficiency significantly accelerates the process.

Key advantage: Germany's combination of zero or very low tuition fees at public universities plus an affordable post-study work visa makes it financially attractive. The trade-off is that German language skills are essential for career success in most fields, and the 18-month job search window, while generous, requires more initiative than Canada's employer-integrated approach.

PR pathway ranking: from fastest to slowest

If permanent residence is your primary goal, the countries rank in this order based on ease, speed, and certainty of the PR process:

1. Canada — fastest and clearest. The PGWP-to-Express Entry pipeline is specifically designed for international graduates. Canadian work experience earns maximum CRS points, and Provincial Nominee Programmes provide additional options. Many graduates receive PR within one to two years of starting full-time work. No other country makes this transition as smooth.

2. Australia — clear but points-based. Australia's system is well-structured but competitive. The points test means your invitation depends on age, English scores, work experience, education, and other factors. Regional graduates have a significant advantage with bonus points and dedicated pathways. With the right profile, PR is achievable within three to five years.

3. Germany — solid with language dependency. Germany offers a reliable PR pathway, but it is heavily influenced by German language ability. With B1 or B2 German, the timeline shortens considerably. Without it, finding qualifying employment in many fields becomes significantly harder. PR after four years of employment is realistic for graduates who commit to language learning.

4. UK — requires employer sponsorship. The UK pathway is the most employer-dependent. You cannot apply for ILR on the Graduate Route itself; you must first secure a Skilled Worker visa with a qualifying employer, then wait five years. This means your PR timeline is at least seven years from graduation (2 years Graduate Route + 5 years on Skilled Worker). The process is well-defined but slow.

Decision framework: which country is right for you?

Choose Canada if: your priority is permanent residence and you want the fastest, most certain pathway. Canada's system is designed to retain international graduates, and the low cost of the PGWP combined with excellent PR prospects makes it the top choice for career-oriented immigrants.

Choose Australia if: you want strong post-study work rights and are willing to consider regional study. The 485 visa's extended duration for regional graduates, combined with bonus PR points, makes regional Australia one of the smartest strategic choices for long-term immigration. Even without regional study, the 2–4 year work permit and clear points system make Australia competitive.

Choose the UK if: you want access to a world-class job market, London-based opportunities, and prestigious employers. The Graduate Route is excellent for gaining two to three years of UK work experience, but you must plan ahead for the transition to a Skilled Worker visa. If you are targeting high-paying roles with large sponsors, the UK remains attractive despite the longer PR timeline.

Choose Germany if: you are willing to learn German, prefer low or zero tuition fees, and want to build a career in Europe's largest economy. Germany rewards patience and language commitment with long-term stability and a solid PR pathway, but it requires more personal initiative than the other three options.

Factors that affect your decision beyond the visa

Post-study work rights are important, but they are not the only factor. Tuition costs, quality of education, cost of living, language requirements, and personal preference all play a role. Canada and Australia offer the best balance of work rights and PR access. The UK offers unmatched prestige in certain fields. Germany offers the lowest cost of study but requires language commitment.

Consider also the industry you want to enter. Tech graduates may find Canada and Germany most welcoming. Finance and consulting graduates may prefer the UK. Healthcare and engineering graduates may find Australia's skilled occupation list most aligned with their qualifications. There is no single best answer — only the best answer for your specific situation.

Planning your next steps

Regardless of which country you choose, the planning should start before you apply to university. Your choice of programme, institution, and study location directly affects your post-study work options. A master's at a regional Australian university gives you six years of post-study work rights. A PhD at a UK Russell Group university gives you three. A two-year programme in Canada qualifies for a three-year PGWP. A free degree in Germany gives you 18 months to find work with no tuition debt.

Map your goals to the right destination, understand the specific visa rules, and build your strategy around both the study experience and what comes after. Frequently asked questions

How long can I stay and work after studying abroad?+

UK Graduate Route: 2 years (3 years for PhD). Canada PGWP: up to 3 years depending on programme length. Australia 485 visa: 2 to 4 years depending on qualification and location. Germany Job Seeker Visa: 18 months. New Zealand Post-Study Work Visa: 1 to 3 years.

Which post-study work visa leads to permanent residence?+

Canada offers the clearest PR pathway: PGWP holders can apply via Express Entry or Provincial Nominee Programmes. Australia's 485 visa is a stepping stone to skilled migration visas. The UK Graduate Route can lead to Skilled Worker sponsorship and eventually ILR. Germany's Job Seeker Visa can lead to an EU Blue Card or work permit with PR after 4 years.

Can I switch employers on a post-study work visa?+

Yes, on all four countries' post-study work visas. The UK Graduate Route, Canada PGWP, Australia 485, and Germany Job Seeker Visa are all open-work visas with no employer restriction. You can work for any employer, change jobs freely, or be self-employed.

What is the cost of a post-study work visa?+

UK Graduate Route: GBP 285. Canada PGWP: CAD 255. Australia 485: AUD 1,895. Germany Job Seeker Visa: EUR 75. These are government application fees and do not include biometrics, health checks or insurance.

Do I need to find a job within the post-study work visa period?+

For the UK and Australia, you generally need to transition to a work visa (Skilled Worker or employer-sponsored) before the post-study visa expires. In Canada, the PGWP itself is valid for its full duration regardless of employment. In Germany, you have 18 months to find qualifying employment and switch to a work permit.
