Study in France: The Complete 2026 Guide

France hosts 443,500 international students — the third-most in the world — because it combines world-class universities (PSL, Sorbonne, École Polytechnique) with remarkably low public tuition and genuinely generous work rights (964 hours/year). The catch is the Campus France procedure and, since 2026, non-EU fees that have finally started to bite.

Why Study in France?

France is the world's top destination for students on a budget who still want elite credentials. Public universities charge non-EU students a flat €2,895/year (Licence) or €3,941/year (Master) — a fraction of UK/US costs — while Grandes Écoles like École Polytechnique and business schools like HEC deliver world-ranked prestige. PhD study costs just €397/year for everyone, and most PhDs come with a salary or Eiffel-style scholarship.

Heads-up: from 1 August 2026 the proof-of-funds requirement rises from €615 to €877.50/month (≈€8,775 for 10 months). And a 2026 decree sharply narrowed fee waivers — most non-EU students now pay the differentiated fees listed above.

The Student Visa (VLS-TS "étudiant") & Campus France

For programmes over 6 months you get a VLS-TS (long-stay visa that acts as your residence permit once validated). For ~73 countries, the Campus France / Études en France (EEF) procedure is mandatory before you can even apply for the visa.

France's own ranking logic differs from the UK/US: elite research lives in the Grandes Écoles and university clusters, while a huge number of excellent but unranked public universities teach at nearly free prices.

Affordable & Easy-Entry Universities

France's public universities are the cheapest way into Europe: non-EU "differentiated" fees are just €2,902/year (bachelor) or €3,950 (master), and most plain licences are open access — apply via Parcoursup (EU/French-bac holders) or the Campus France "Études en France" procedure and you're in without an entrance exam. Aix-Marseille even auto-exempts 75% of the fee for ~70 nationalities (≈€724/yr).

"Open access" licences admit all qualified applicants; selectivity lives in tagged tracks (BUT, CPGE) and some English programs. Non-EU students with a foreign diploma apply via Campus France, not Parcoursup. Fee exemptions are capped (~30% of a uni's non-EU intake), so confirm yours early.

Degrees, Language & Living Costs

🎓 Licence — 3 years

180 ECTS. Most bachelor's are French-taught — you'll need DELF B2/TCF. France teaches in French unless the program is English-taught (1,000+ master's programs are).

📘 Master — 2 years

120 ECTS (M1+M2), plus some 1-year M2-only programs. English-taught masters typically need IELTS 6.5+ / TOEFL 90+.

🔬 Doctorat — 3–4 years

€397/year for everyone, usually salaried or via Eiffel/Excellence-Major scholarships. Strong research culture.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Campus France mandatory?+

For students from ~73 countries, yes — you must complete the Études en France procedure (account, documents, interview) and get Campus France's academic opinion before the consulate will consider your visa. Budget 3–6 weeks for it, and start ~5 months before your course begins (March–April for September entry).

How much money do I need to show for a French student visa?+

From 1 August 2026 the official requirement is €877.50/month (≈47% of SMIC, indexed annually) — about €8,775 for a 10-month academic year. This is up from €615/month. You show bank statements or a sponsorship letter; scholarship holders show their award letter.

Can I work while studying in France?+

Yes — up to 964 hours/year (~60% of full-time, roughly 20 hrs/week) with no separate work permit. Part-time work is meant to be secondary income, and together with CAF housing aid it genuinely reduces the cost burden in a way few countries match.

Do I need to speak French to study in France?+

Not for the 1,000+ English-taught programs (mostly master's and Grandes Écoles) — IELTS 6.5/TOEFL 90 is typical. For French-taught programs you need DELF B2/TCF minimum (C1 for some humanities/law). French is not required for the visa if your program is English-taught, but A2 helps at first multi-year renewal and B2 is now required for citizenship.

Can I stay in France after graduation?+

Yes — graduates can switch to the Talent route (qualified employees earning ≥1.5× SMIC ≈ €39,582/yr get a 4-year card), the EU Blue Card (€59,373/yr 2026 threshold), or set up as a micro-entrepreneur. Time as a student counts toward the 5-year residence requirement at reduced weight. See Immigrate to France.
