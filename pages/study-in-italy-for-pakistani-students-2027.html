Study in Italy for Pakistani Students: 2027 Guide

🗓️ Oct 10, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Study in Italy for Pakistani students in 2027 is one of the best value plays in Europe: income-based public tuition that can fall near zero, two separate funding streams, and degrees in engineering, medicine and design that carry global weight. Italy has climbed into the top ranks of student destination searches, and the reason is cost as much as reputation.

The direct answer is that Italy is not automatically free, but it can be very cheap. You apply for the MAECI government scholarship or the regional DSU grant, and either can remove tuition. Both have different rules and you cannot hold them together, so the first decision is which one fits your situation.

Study in Italy for Pakistani Students: Two Funding Routes

Italy funds international students through a national scheme and through regional student-welfare agencies. They are not interchangeable, and mixing them up is the most common mistake applicants make each cycle.

Because the two systems are run by different bodies and cannot be combined, the right first move is to read the eligibility rules for both, then commit to the one you can win. Applying to both and hoping is the fastest way to end up with neither.

MAECI: The Italian Government Scholarship

The MAECI scholarship is the headline award. It pays EUR 10,800 across nine months, roughly EUR 900 a month, and adds exemption from university tuition, leaving only a small regional tax of about EUR 140 and a stamp duty of a few euros. It is open to foreign students who are not resident in Italy and who come from the eligible country list, and the age limit is 18 to 30 at the application deadline.

The MAECI call for the coming academic year typically closes in late March, with applications submitted through the Study in Italy portal. The eligible-country list and the exact dates change each cycle, so read the current call document rather than an older summary.

Two conditions catch people out. First, the award cannot be combined with a regional DSU grant, so choosing MAECI means giving up any DSU entitlement. Second, you must be admitted to an eligible programme, so the scholarship and the university application run in parallel, not one after the other.

DSU, Income-Based Tuition and Living Costs

The DSU grant is administered region by region and is aimed at the right to study. It commonly bundles a tuition waiver, a place in student accommodation, meals in university canteens and a cash allowance, with the value set by family income through the ISEE system. The application window usually closes around 30 September, so it rewards students who arrive prepared rather than scrambling late.

Outside scholarships, public tuition is income-based. At many universities the maximum all-inclusive fee is around EUR 2,900, and lower family income cuts that sharply, sometimes to near zero. Private universities charge higher fees but often have their own merit scholarships that bring the net cost down.

Living costs sit in the middle of the European range, far below London or Zurich and roughly in line with smaller German cities. Northern cities such as Milan and Turin are pricier, while southern and central university towns are cheaper. Pre-Enrolment, Visas and Deadlines

Italy has a step most countries do not: pre-enrolment. You create a Universitaly profile, pick your programme, and pre-enrol, and that record is what allows you to apply for a type D study visa. Missing or delaying pre-enrolment is the single most common reason Pakistani applicants lose a whole year.

Plan the sequence backwards from your intake. Confirm the programme and any admission tests, complete pre-enrolment, assemble and legalise your documents, then apply for the visa with proof of funds and health cover. Our proof of funds guide covers the bank evidence consulates expect, and student visa refusal reasons shows what to avoid.

Legalisation is a common delay. Pakistani documents often need attestation and, in some cases, translation into Italian, and each step adds days. Start the paperwork months before the deadline rather than weeks.

What Italy Does Best

Italy is strongest in engineering, architecture, medicine, design and the arts, with a long list of historic universities and polytechnics. Many master’s programmes are taught entirely in English, particularly in engineering and economics, so you do not need fluent Italian to study, though learning some helps with daily life and internships.

Cost planning deserves its own line because Italy rewards students who understand the income test. The ISEE calculation uses your family’s income and assets, converted into euros, and a low figure unlocks the tuition waiver and the DSU package. Gather the income certificates and asset declarations early, translated and legalised, because the regional agencies will not accept an incomplete file.

Doctoral study is a strong option in Italy too. Many PhD programmes are funded with a monthly scholarship and no tuition, and they are advertised in spring for an autumn start. For a research-minded Pakistani applicant, a funded Italian PhD can be cheaper and more secure than a self-funded master’s.

When you shortlist universities, look beyond the famous names. Regional universities often have lower living costs, smaller classes and better access to DSU accommodation, and their degrees carry the same national value. The prestige gap matters far less than the funding and the fit.

Budget for the first months before any scholarship pays out. Rent deposits, the residence permit, health insurance and initial living costs all land before the first instalment arrives, so carry a buffer rather than relying on the award from day one. Keep your cost and budget plan updated as the exchange rate moves.

Timing and visas are linked. A late MAECI result can collide with the visa window, so keep both calendars visible. If funding is delayed, you may still need to show proof of funds for the visa, so keep that evidence ready in parallel rather than waiting for a result.

Safety, food and travel are part of the appeal, but the practical upside is mobility. A type D study visa and residence permit let you travel across the Schengen area, and Italy’s central position makes weekends across Europe affordable on a student budget.

Part-time work rules allow a limited number of hours, which helps with living costs but should not form the core of your funding plan. Italian language skills widen the jobs available, so even basic proficiency pays off over a two-year degree.

Recognition matters if you plan to return to Pakistan or move on. Check that your chosen degree is recognised by the relevant professional body in your field before you enrol, particularly for medicine and engineering.

For a budget-conscious Pakistani student who wants an EU degree with real funding options, Italy belongs on the shortlist next to Germany and Estonia. Always confirm the current MAECI deadline, eligible-country list and tuition rules on the official Study in Italy portal and on your chosen university page, such as the University of Pisa. Deadlines and figures change each cycle.

Frequently asked questions

Is Italy free for Pakistani students?+

Public university tuition is income-based and can be very low, and MAECI or DSU awards can waive it entirely. It is not automatically free, but it is among the cheapest EU destinations once funding is applied.

What does the MAECI scholarship cover?+

The Italian government MAECI award pays EUR 10,800 over nine months, about EUR 900 a month, and includes exemption from tuition, leaving only a small regional tax and stamp duty. It cannot be combined with a regional DSU grant.

How do I apply to an Italian university from Pakistan?+

Create a profile on Universitaly, choose your programme, then pre-enrol. Pre-enrolment is what lets you apply for the study visa, so start it early in the year before your intake.
