PTE Academic Complete Guide 2026: Format, Scoring & Preparation

🗓️ Aug 17, 2026⏱️ 28 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

If you are planning to study, work, or migrate abroad in 2026, the PTE Academic exam is one of the most important English language tests you can take. It is accepted by thousands of universities and governments around the world. It is fast, fair, and fully computer-based.

This guide covers everything you need to know about PTE Academic in 2026. You will learn the test format, all 20 question types, the scoring system, fee details by country, visa acceptance rules, and how to prepare effectively. Whether you are a first-time test taker or retaking the exam, this guide gives you a clear roadmap.

Let us start from the beginning.

1. What Is PTE Academic?

PTE Academic stands for Pearson Test of English Academic. It is an internationally recognised English language proficiency test designed for non-native English speakers who want to study or migrate to English-speaking countries.

The test is developed and managed by Pearson PLC, one of the world's largest education companies. Pearson also owns well-known qualifications like the PTE General and the PTE Academic UKVI version. However, PTE Academic is the most widely taken version worldwide.

Here are the key things to know about PTE Academic:

  • Computer-based test: Everything — speaking, writing, reading, and listening — is done on a computer. There are no human examiners.
  • AI scoring: Your test is marked by artificial intelligence, not humans. This means the scoring is objective, consistent, and free from bias.
  • Accepted worldwide: Over 3,500 institutions and government bodies accept PTE Academic. This includes universities in the UK, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, Germany, and the USA.
  • Fast results: You receive your scores within 2 to 5 working days. This is much faster than IELTS, which can take up to 13 days.
  • Available in 120+ countries: There are PTE test centres in over 120 countries worldwide. You can book a test at a centre near you.
  • Used for study and visa: PTE Academic is accepted for university admissions, student visas, skilled worker visas, and permanent residency applications in many countries.

PTE Academic is not just a test of English. It is a test of your ability to communicate in real academic and professional settings. The questions are based on real-life situations — university lectures, academic reading passages, and professional conversations.

The test is designed to be fair to all test takers. Because it uses AI scoring, every test is marked the same way. There is no room for examiner bias or mood-based grading. This is one of the biggest reasons why students prefer PTE over other English tests.

Another major advantage is the speed of results. In many cases, you can get your scores within 24 hours. This is extremely helpful if you have a tight deadline for university applications or visa submissions.

PTE Academic is also accepted by the UK Home Office for UK visa applications when taken at a UKVI-approved test centre. This means you can use PTE for your UK student visa, work visa, or settlement application. For more details on UK visa requirements, check our visa score requirements guide.

In summary, PTE Academic is a modern, fast, and reliable English test that is accepted globally. If you want a test that is scored fairly, gives quick results, and is accepted for both study and migration, PTE Academic is an excellent choice in 2026.

2. PTE Test Format — Complete Breakdown

Understanding the PTE test format is the first step to scoring well. The test is divided into three main sections. The total test duration is approximately 2 hours. There are 20 different question types across the three sections.

Here is the overall structure:

  • Part 1: Speaking and Writing — 54 to 67 minutes
  • Part 2: Reading — 29 to 30 minutes
  • Part 3: Listening — 30 to 43 minutes

There is no separate day for the Speaking test. Everything is completed in one sitting. This is different from IELTS, where the Speaking test may be scheduled on a different day.

Let us look at each section in detail.

Part 1: Speaking and Writing (54–67 minutes)

This is the longest section of the test. It combines both Speaking and Writing tasks. You will speak into a microphone for Speaking tasks and type your answers on the computer for Writing tasks.

Here are the question types in this section:

Read Aloud (6–7 items): You will see a text on the screen. You must read it aloud into the microphone. The text is usually a paragraph from an academic source. You have 30–40 seconds to prepare, then 30–40 seconds to read. This question type contributes to both your Reading and Speaking scores. It is one of the highest-scoring question types in the entire test. Practice this daily for best results.

Repeat Sentence (10–12 items): You will hear a sentence played through your headphones. After the audio ends, you must repeat the sentence exactly as you heard it. The sentences are 3–9 seconds long. This question type contributes to both Speaking and Listening scores. Your memory and concentration are tested here.

Describe Image (3–4 items): You will see an image on the screen — a graph, chart, table, map, or process diagram. You have 25 seconds to study it, then you must speak for 40 seconds describing what you see. You should describe the key features, trends, and any important data. This is a Speaking-only question type and contributes significantly to your Speaking score.

Re-tell Lecture (1–2 items): You will watch or listen to a short lecture clip (60–90 seconds). After it ends, you must retell the main points in your own words. You have 10 seconds to prepare and must speak for 40 seconds. This contributes to both Speaking and Listening scores.

Answer Short Question (5–6 items): You will hear a short question. You must answer in one word or a short phrase (3–5 words). These are general knowledge questions. Examples include: "What colour is the sky?" or "How many months are in a year?" This contributes to Speaking and Listening scores.

Summarise Written Text (1–2 items): You will read a passage of 150–200 words. You must write a one-sentence summary of the passage. The summary must be between 5 and 75 words. You have 10 minutes to complete this task. This contributes to both Writing and Reading scores.

Essay (1–2 items): You will be given a topic and must write an essay of 200–300 words. You have 20 minutes to write it. The topic is usually academic or opinion-based. This contributes to your Writing score. Use a clear structure: introduction, body paragraphs, and conclusion.

Part 2: Reading (29–30 minutes)

The Reading section tests your ability to understand academic texts. You will read passages and answer different types of questions. Time management is critical here because all reading questions share a single timer.

Here are the question types in this section:

Fill in the Blanks — Reading and Writing (5–6 items): You will see a passage with several blanks. A list of words appears below. You must drag and drop the correct word into each blank. This tests both your vocabulary and your understanding of context. This question type contributes to both Reading and Writing scores.

Multiple Choice, Multiple Answer (2–3 items): You will read a passage and select multiple correct answers from a list. There may be 2, 3, or 4 correct options. Be careful — there is a negative marking for wrong answers. This contributes to your Reading score.

Re-order Paragraphs (2–3 items): You will see several paragraphs that are jumbled up. You must drag them into the correct logical order. Look for topic sentences, linking words, and pronoun references to figure out the order. This contributes to your Reading score.

Fill in the Blanks — Reading (3–4 items): You will see a passage with gaps. You must select the correct word from a dropdown menu for each blank. This is similar to the Reading and Writing version but with a dropdown instead of drag and drop. This contributes to your Reading score.

Multiple Choice, Single Answer (2–3 items): You will read a passage and select one correct answer from the options. This is the most straightforward reading question type. This contributes to your Reading score.

Part 3: Listening (30–43 minutes)

The Listening section tests your ability to understand spoken English in academic contexts. You will listen to audio clips through headphones and answer questions. You can adjust the volume during this section.

Here are the question types in this section:

Summarise Spoken Text (1–2 items): You will listen to an audio clip (60–90 seconds). You must write a 50–70 word summary of what you heard. You have 10 minutes for each item. This contributes to both Listening and Writing scores.

Fill in the Blanks (3–4 items): You will listen to an audio clip while reading a transcript with missing words. You must type the missing words into the blanks. This tests both listening and spelling. This contributes to both Listening and Writing scores.

Multiple Choice, Multiple Answer (2–3 items): You will listen to an audio clip and select multiple correct answers from a list. There is negative marking for wrong answers. This contributes to your Listening score.

Select Missing Word (1–2 items): You will listen to an audio clip. At the end, the final word or phrase is replaced by a beep. You must choose the correct missing word from the options. This contributes to your Listening score.

Highlight Correct Summary (1–2 items): You will listen to an audio clip and read several summary options. You must select the summary that best matches what you heard. This contributes to both Listening and Reading scores.

Highlight Incorrect Words (2–3 items): You will listen to an audio clip while reading a transcript on the screen. Some words in the transcript are incorrect. You must click on each incorrect word to highlight it. This contributes to both Listening and Reading scores.

Write from Dictation (3–4 items): You will hear a short sentence. You must type the entire sentence exactly as you heard it. Each correct word earns you points. This contributes to both Writing and Listening scores. This is one of the highest-scoring question types in the test.

In total, there are 20 question types across the three sections. The entire test takes about 2 hours. There is no break between sections. You must manage your time carefully, especially in the Reading section where all questions share one timer.

3. PTE Scoring System — How Your Score Is Calculated

PTE Academic uses a 10–90 score scale. This is different from IELTS, which uses a 0–9 band scale. Your PTE score is calculated by AI-powered scoring technology developed by Pearson.

There are two types of scores in PTE:

  • Overall Score: This is your total score, ranging from 10 to 90. It is the average of your four communicative skills.
  • Communicative Skills Scores: These are your individual scores for each of the four language skills.

Communicative Skills

Your four communicative skills are scored separately:

  • Listening (10–90)
  • Reading (10–90)
  • Speaking (10–90)
  • Writing (10–90)

Each skill score reflects your ability in that specific area. Many universities and visa authorities require minimum scores in individual skills, not just the overall score. For example, a university might require PTE 60 overall with no less than 55 in each skill.

Enabling Skills

In addition to communicative skills, PTE also reports six enabling skills. These are sub-skills that contribute to your overall language ability:

  • Oral Fluency: How smoothly and naturally you speak
  • Pronunciation: How clearly your speech is understood
  • Spelling: How correctly you spell words
  • Grammar: How accurately you use grammar structures
  • Vocabulary: How well you use words and phrases
  • Written Discourse: How well you organise and structure your writing

These enabling skills are not scored on the 10–90 scale in the same way. They influence your communicative skill scores. For example, your Speaking score is influenced by Oral Fluency and Pronunciation.

How AI Scoring Works

PTE uses a technology called Pearson's automated scoring engine. This engine analyses your responses based on three main factors:

  • Content: What you said or wrote. Did you address the topic? Did you include relevant information?
  • Oral Fluency: How smoothly you spoke. Did you pause frequently? Did you speak at a natural pace?
  • Pronunciation: How clearly you spoke. Were your words easy to understand? Did you use correct stress and intonation?

For writing tasks, the AI analyses grammar, vocabulary, spelling, and written discourse. For reading and listening, the scoring is more straightforward because there are right and wrong answers.

The AI scoring is trained on millions of responses from test takers around the world. It is regularly validated against human scoring to ensure accuracy. Pearson claims that the AI scoring is as accurate as human scoring — and in some cases, more consistent.

4. PTE Academic vs PTE Academic UKVI — What Is the Difference?

This is one of the most common questions students ask. Many people confuse PTE Academic with PTE Academic UKVI. They are similar but have important differences.

Here is the key difference:

  • PTE Academic: The standard version of the test. Accepted by universities and governments worldwide for study, work, and migration purposes. This is the version most test takers take.
  • PTE Academic UKVI: A version of PTE specifically approved by the UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) authority. It is required for UK visa applications including Student visa, Skilled Worker visa, and settlement.

The test content is identical. Both versions have the same question types, same difficulty level, and same scoring system. The only difference is the test centre.

PTE Academic UKVI is only available at UKVI-approved test centres. These centres have additional security protocols required by the UK government. When you book your test, you must select the UKVI version if you are applying for a UK visa.

Here is when you need each version:

  • Choose PTE Academic if you are applying to universities in Australia, New Zealand, Canada, Germany, or the USA. This is the standard version for non-UK applications.
  • Choose PTE Academic UKVI if you are applying for a UK Student visa, Skilled Worker visa, or any other UK visa that requires an English language test. You must take this version at a UKVI-approved centre.

You can find UKVI-approved PTE centres on the Pearson website. Not all PTE centres offer the UKVI version. Check before you book.

Important: If you take the standard PTE Academic for a UK visa application, it will not be accepted. You must take the UKVI version. This is a strict requirement.

The cost of PTE Academic UKVI is slightly higher than the standard version in some countries. The extra cost covers the additional security and compliance requirements.

6. PTE Accepted for Visa by Country (2026)

One of the most important things to know is whether your target country accepts PTE Academic for visa purposes. The rules vary by country. Here is a detailed breakdown of the major study destinations.

United Kingdom

The UK accepts PTE Academic UKVI for all visa categories that require an English language test. This includes:

  • Student visa (Tier 4): You need PTE Academic UKVI with a minimum score depending on your course level.
  • Skilled Worker visa: You need PTE Academic UKVI at CEFR level B1 or above.
  • Settlement (Indefinite Leave to Remain): You need PTE Academic UKVI at CEFR level B1 or above.

UK visa score requirements:

  • CEFR B2 (degree-level courses): PTE 59–75 overall, with minimum 59 in each skill
  • CEFR B1 (below degree level): PTE 43–58 overall, with minimum 43 in each skill
  • Pre-sessional English courses: PTE 36–42 overall

You must take the test at a UKVI-approved PTE centre. Standard PTE Academic is not accepted for UK visas. Always verify the latest requirements on the UK government website before booking.

Australia

Australia accepts PTE Academic (standard version) for visa applications. This is one of the most popular destinations for PTE test takers.

Australia visa score requirements:

  • Student visa (subclass 500): Minimum PTE 42 overall, with no band below 36. Some universities require higher scores.
  • Graduate visa (subclass 485): Minimum PTE 59 overall, with minimum 50 in each band.
  • Skilled migration (General Skilled Migration): Minimum PTE 50 overall for most occupations. Some occupations require PTE 65 or 79.
  • Permanent Residency: Competent English = PTE 50, Proficient English = PTE 65, Superior English = PTE 79.

Australia is one of the easiest countries to use PTE for. The standard PTE Academic is accepted — no UKVI version needed.

Canada

Canada accepts PTE Academic for university admission and for some immigration programmes. There is also PTE Core, which is specifically designed for Canadian immigration.

Canada requirements:

  • Student Direct Stream (SDS): PTE Academic with a minimum score of 60 in each skill. This is an accelerated study permit processing stream.
  • Regular study permit: PTE Academic accepted by most Designated Learning Institutions (DLIs). Score requirements vary by institution.
  • Express Entry (Federal Skilled Worker): PTE scores are mapped to CLB (Canadian Language Benchmark) levels. CLB 7 = PTE 60 in each skill, CLB 9 = PTE 79 in each skill.
  • PTE Core: Designed for Canadian immigration. Accepted for Express Entry, Provincial Nominee Programme, and other immigration streams.

For SDS, you need PTE 60 in each of the four skills. This is the fastest pathway to a Canadian study permit. Check our guide to studying abroad without IELTS for more options.

New Zealand

New Zealand accepts PTE Academic for student visa and skilled worker visa applications.

  • Student visa: Minimum PTE 50–58 depending on the institution and course.
  • Skilled Migrant Category: PTE 65 or above for principal applicants.
  • Essential Skills Work Visa: PTE 42–50 depending on the skill level of the job.

New Zealand has clear and straightforward PTE requirements. The standard PTE Academic is accepted — no special version needed.

Germany

Germany accepts PTE Academic for university admission. However, PTE is generally not required for the student visa itself. The visa process in Germany focuses more on financial proof and university admission.

  • University admission: Most German universities accept PTE 55–65 for English-taught programmes.
  • Student visa: PTE is not officially required for the visa, but having a strong English score strengthens your application.

German universities increasingly accept PTE alongside IELTS and TOEFL. Check with your specific university for their PTE requirements.

United States

The USA accepts PTE Academic for university admission. However, PTE is not required for the F-1 student visa. The visa interview is in English, but there is no formal English test requirement for the visa itself.

  • University admission: Most US universities accept PTE 55–70. Top universities may require PTE 65+.
  • F-1 visa: No formal English test required, but a strong PTE score helps demonstrate your English ability.

PTE is becoming more popular in the USA as an alternative to TOEFL and IELTS. Many US universities now list PTE on their admissions pages.

7. How to Prepare for PTE Academic — A Complete Study Plan

Preparing for PTE Academic does not have to be overwhelming. With the right strategy and consistent practice, you can achieve your target score. Here is a comprehensive preparation plan that works for most test takers.

Week 1–2: Understand the Test and Build Foundations

  • Take a practice test: Start with a full-length practice test to understand your current level. Pearson offers official practice tests on their website. This will show you your strengths and weaknesses.
  • Learn the format: Familiarise yourself with all 20 question types. Understand how each question is scored and what the examiner (AI) is looking for.
  • Build vocabulary: Read academic articles from sources like The Guardian, BBC News, and Scientific American. Note down new words and their meanings. Aim to learn 10–15 new words daily.
  • Work on pronunciation: Use online dictionaries to check the correct pronunciation of words. Record yourself speaking and compare with native speakers.

Week 3–4: Targeted Practice by Section

  • Speaking section: Practise Read Aloud daily. This is the highest-scoring speaking question. Read academic passages aloud for 20 minutes every day. Focus on fluency, not speed. For Describe Image, practise with real graphs, charts, and maps. Use the template: "This image shows... The main features are... In conclusion..."
  • Writing section: Practise Summarise Written Text by reading articles and writing one-sentence summaries. For Essay, practise writing 200–300 word essays on common topics like education, technology, environment, and society. Use a clear structure.
  • Reading section: Practise Fill in the Blanks and Re-order Paragraphs daily. Read academic passages and identify topic sentences, linking words, and logical flow. Build your vocabulary through extensive reading.
  • Listening section: Listen to academic podcasts, TED talks, and university lectures. Practise Write from Dictation by listening to sentences and typing them. This is one of the highest-scoring question types.

Week 5–6: Full Practice Tests and Refinement

  • Take full practice tests: Complete at least 2–3 full-length practice tests under timed conditions. Simulate the real test environment — use headphones, sit at a desk, and avoid distractions.
  • Anse your mistakes: After each practice test, review every wrong answer. Understand why you got it wrong. Was it a vocabulary issue? A time management problem? A listening comprehension error?
  • Focus on weak areas: Spend more time on the question types where you score the lowest. If your Reading score is low, do more reading practice. If your Speaking score is low, record yourself more often.
  • Time management: Practice working within time limits. The Reading section has a single timer for all questions. Learn to move on from difficult questions and come back to them later.

General Preparation Tips

  • Use official materials: Pearson's official practice tests and study materials are the most reliable. They are designed by the same people who create the real test.
  • Join online communities: There are many PTE study groups on Facebook, Reddit, and YouTube. Join them to get tips from other test takers.
  • Record yourself speaking: This is especially important for the Speaking section. Listen back to your recordings and identify areas for improvement.
  • Practise with a timer: Always practice under timed conditions. This builds your speed and helps you manage the real test better.
  • Get feedback: If possible, work with a tutor or study partner who can give you feedback on your Speaking and Writing.

A focused 4–6 week study plan is usually enough to improve your PTE score by 10–15 points. For a detailed guide on preparing for English tests, check our IELTS complete guide which includes many tips that apply to PTE as well.

8. PTE Question Types — Detailed Strategies

Let us go deeper into the most important question types and share specific strategies for each one.

Read Aloud — The Highest-Scoring Speaking Task

Read Aloud is one of the most important question types in PTE. It contributes to both your Reading and Speaking scores. Here is how to approach it:

  • Read the text in your head first during the 30–40 second preparation time.
  • Identify difficult words and practise saying them silently.
  • When you start speaking, read at a natural pace. Do not rush.
  • Focus on pronunciation and fluency. The AI scores these heavily.
  • If you make a mistake, keep going. Do not stop and restart.
  • Punctuation matters. Pause slightly at commas and periods.

Describe Image — Mastering Visual Data

For Describe Image, you need to describe a visual in 40 seconds. Use this structure:

  • Introduction (5 seconds): "This image shows..." or "This chart illustrates..."
  • Key features (25 seconds): Describe the main trends, data points, or features. Use specific numbers if available.
  • Conclusion (10 seconds): "In conclusion, the image shows that..." or "Overall, the trend is..."

Practise with different types of images: bar charts, line graphs, pie charts, tables, maps, and process diagrams. The more you practise, the more comfortable you will be on test day.

Re-tell Lecture — Active Listening

For Re-tell Lecture, take notes while listening. Write down key points, examples, and any numbers mentioned. After the audio ends, you have 10 seconds to organise your notes. Then speak for 40 seconds, covering the main ideas in order.

Summarise Written Text — One Sentence Only

This task requires you to write a single sentence that captures the main idea of a passage. The sentence must be between 5 and 75 words. Here are some tips:

  • Read the passage carefully. Identify the main idea and supporting details.
  • Use a complex sentence with clauses to combine ideas. For example: "Although the passage discusses X, the main argument is that Y because Z."
  • Do not include minor details. Focus on the core message.
  • Check your grammar and spelling before submitting.

Essay — Structured Writing

For the Essay task, use this structure:

  • Introduction (2–3 sentences): Paraphrase the question and state your position.
  • Body Paragraph 1 (4–5 sentences): Present your first argument with examples.
  • Body Paragraph 2 (4–5 sentences): Present your second argument with examples.
  • Conclusion (2–3 sentences): Summarise your position and restate your main point.

Aim for 250 words. Use linking words like "however," "moreover," "in contrast," and "therefore" to connect your ideas. Avoid using informal language or contractions.

Fill in the Blanks — Vocabulary and Context

For both the Reading and Listening versions of Fill in the Blanks, the key is vocabulary. Read the surrounding text carefully to understand the context. The correct word must fit grammatically and make sense in the sentence.

Re-order Paragraphs — Finding the Logic

For Re-order Paragraphs, look for:

  • Topic sentences: The first paragraph usually introduces the main idea.
  • Linking words: Words like "however," "therefore," "in addition," and "for example" show the order of ideas.
  • Pronouns: If a paragraph starts with "he," "she," "they," or "it," it likely follows a paragraph that introduces the person or thing being referred to.
  • Specific to general: The order usually goes from specific information to general conclusions.

Write from Dictation — Accuracy Is Key

For Write from Dictation, you earn points for each correct word. Even if you miss one word, you still get points for the words you wrote correctly. Here are some tips:

  • Listen carefully to the entire sentence before you start typing.
  • Type the sentence from memory.
  • Check your spelling and grammar before moving to the next question.
  • If you miss a word, do not panic. Continue with the rest of the sentence.

9. Common PTE Mistakes to Avoid

Many test takers lose marks due to avoidable mistakes. Here are the most common errors and how to avoid them:

1. Speaking too fast: Speed is not the goal. Fluency and clarity are. Speaking too fast leads to mispronunciation and makes it harder for the AI to understand you. Speak at a natural, comfortable pace.

2. Pausing too long: Long pauses in Speaking reduce your Oral Fluency score. If you need to think, pause briefly (1–2 seconds) and continue. Avoid dead air.

3. Not describing all parts of an image: In Describe Image, many test takers only describe the most obvious feature. You must cover all key features — axes, labels, trends, and data points.

4. Writing too short a summary: In Summarise Written Text, your summary must be a complete sentence between 5 and 75 words. Many test takers write too little. Aim for 30–50 words.

5. Not checking spelling: Spelling errors cost marks in both Writing and Listening sections. Always proofread your answers before moving on. Use simple, correct words rather than complex ones you might misspell.

6. Spending too long on one question: In the Reading section, all questions share one timer. If you spend too long on one question, you will rush through the others. If a question is too difficult, mark your best guess and move on.

7. Not using a template for essays: Many test takers waste time thinking about essay structure. Use a pre-practised template. Introduction, two body paragraphs, conclusion. This saves time and ensures a well-organised essay.

8. Ignoring negative marking: In Multiple Choice Multiple Answer questions, wrong answers receive negative marks. If you are not sure about an option, it is better to leave it unselected rather than guess.

9. Not adjusting to headphones: In the Listening section, use the volume control to adjust the audio to a comfortable level. Too loud or too soft can affect your comprehension.

10. Rushing through practice tests: Quality matters more than quantity. Take your time to review each practice test thoroughly. Learn from your mistakes rather than just completing more tests.

Frequently asked questions

What is PTE Academic?+

PTE Academic (Pearson Test of English Academic) is a computer-based English language test that assesses speaking, writing, reading and listening skills. It uses AI scoring, so results are objective and free from human bias. It is accepted by over 3,500 institutions worldwide including UK, Australia, New Zealand and Canadian universities.

How is PTE different from IELTS?+

PTE is entirely computer-based and scored by AI, while IELTS has human examiners for Speaking and Writing. PTE results come in 2-5 days (IELTS takes 3-13 days). PTE uses a 10-90 score scale (IELTS uses 0-9). PTE Speaking is recorded on microphone, not face-to-face. Both are accepted for study and visa purposes.

How long is the PTE Academic test?+

PTE Academic is approximately 2 hours long, making it shorter than IELTS (2 hours 45 minutes). The test has three sections: Speaking and Writing (54-67 minutes), Reading (29-30 minutes), and Listening (30-43 minutes). There is no separate Speaking test day — everything is completed in one sitting.

What is a good PTE score?+

For university admission, most institutions accept PTE 50-65 overall. Competitive programmes require 65-75. For visa purposes: UK Student visa requires PTE Academic UKVI (equivalent to IELTS 5.5-6.5), Australia requires PTE 42-50 for student visa, Canada accepts PTE 60+ for SDS. PTE 79+ is considered an excellent score.

How much does PTE cost in 2026?+

PTE fees vary by country: India INR 14,000-17,000, Pakistan PKR 42,000-55,000, UK GBP 160-200, USA USD 180-220, Australia AUD 410-460, Canada CAD 330-380. PTE is generally cheaper than IELTS in most countries.

Which countries accept PTE for student visa?+

PTE Academic is accepted for student visas in UK (PTE Academic UKVI), Australia, New Zealand, Canada, and many European countries. The USA accepts PTE for university admission but not all visa routes. Germany accepts PTE for university admission. Always check the specific visa authority for confirmation.

How do I prepare for PTE?+

Use official Pearson practice tests, focus on timed practice for each section, practise speaking clearly into a microphone, build vocabulary through academic reading, and use AI-powered practice platforms. A 4-6 week focused study plan is recommended. Score practice tests to track improvement.

What is PTE Academic UKVI?+

PTE Academic UKVI is the version of PTE specifically approved for UK visa applications. It is taken at a UKVI-approved test centre with additional security protocols. The test content is identical to standard PTE Academic, but the centre meets UKVI requirements. You need this version for UK Student, Skilled Worker and settlement visas.

How are PTE scores calculated?+

PTE uses AI scoring across three categories: content (what you said), oral fluency (how smoothly you spoke), and pronunciation (how clearly). Each skill (Speaking, Writing, Reading, Listening) is scored on a 10-90 scale. Your overall score is the average of all communicative skills. The AI analysis is more detailed than human scoring.

Can I retake PTE if I fail?+

Yes. You can retake PTE as many times as you need. There is no waiting period between tests. You can book your next test as soon as results from the previous one are available (2-5 days). You can also use PTE Core for immigration purposes in Canada.
