Scholarships for Pakistani Students to Study Abroad 2026: Complete Guide

Every year, thousands of Pakistani students dream of pursuing higher education abroad, but financial constraints remain the biggest barrier. The good news is that 2026 brings an extensive range of fully and partially funded scholarships specifically available to Pakistani nationals — from the prestigious Chevening and Fulbright programs to government-funded scholarships in Turkey, Hungary, China, Japan, South Korea, Germany, and beyond. This guide is the most comprehensive, country-by-country resource you will find on scholarships for Pakistani students to study abroad in 2026, covering eligibility, funding value, application windows, and practical tips to maximize your chances.

Whether you are a fresh graduate looking for a master's scholarship in the UK, a PhD aspirant targeting Germany, an undergraduate hoping to study in Turkey on a full ride, or a medical student eyeing China's government scholarships — this article has you covered. We have also included comparison tables, document checklists, HEC-specific programs, and warnings about common scams. Bookmark this page and check it regularly as deadlines approach.

Scholarship Landscape Overview: What Pakistani Students Should Know in 2026

The global scholarship ecosystem for Pakistani students is broader than ever. Governments of the UK, USA, Germany, Turkey, Hungary, China, Japan, South Korea, Australia, Canada, the Netherlands, Italy, Malaysia, and Russia all maintain dedicated funding programs for international students. Many of these were explicitly designed to strengthen ties with Pakistan, and Pakistani nationals are among the top recipient nationalities for several of them.

Before diving into country-specific programs, here are the universal truths every Pakistani applicant must understand:

  • Start early: Most scholarships have application windows 12–18 months before the program begins. If you want to start in September 2026, you should have begun applications by late 2025.
  • University admission first: Nearly every scholarship requires proof of acceptance or a letter of admission from a recognized institution. Apply to universities before or simultaneously with your scholarship applications.
  • HEC is the gatekeeper: For many UK and Commonwealth scholarships, HEC Pakistan nominates candidates. You apply through HEC's portal first, and HEC shortlists candidates before forwarding names to the scholarship body.
  • English proficiency is non-negotiable: IELTS, TOEFL iBT, or PTE Academic scores are required for English-medium programs. Plan to take your test by September–October of the year before your intended start.
  • Documents matter: Statement of Purpose, recommendation letters, transcripts, HEC attestation, passport copy, research proposal (for graduate/PhD), and sometimes a medical certificate — start assembling these months in advance.
  • No agent needed: Every legitimate scholarship listed in this guide has a free, official online application portal. Paying agents or consultants does not improve your chances and may even disqualify you if the scholarship body discovers it.

United Kingdom Scholarships for Pakistani Students 2026

The UK remains the most popular study-abroad destination for Pakistani students, and for good reason — British universities rank among the world's best, the academic year is compact (12 months for most master's programs), and several prestigious scholarships are tailored to Pakistani applicants. The UK government and individual universities collectively fund hundreds of Pakistani scholars each year.

Chevening Scholarships

Chevening is the UK government's flagship international scholarship program, funded by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO). It targets emerging leaders with the potential to make a positive impact in their home countries. For Pakistani students, Chevening is one of the most competitive and prestigious opportunities available — it is fully funded and covers tuition fees, a monthly living allowance, travel costs to and from the UK, and an arrival allowance.

Eligibility for Pakistani applicants:

  • You must be a Pakistani citizen currently residing in Pakistan.
  • You must have at least two years of work experience (this can include internships, volunteering, and part-time work — approximately 2,800 hours total).
  • You must hold a bachelor's degree with a minimum of an upper second-class (2:1) honours, or its Pakistani equivalent (typically a CGPA of 3.0 or above from a recognized university).
  • You must apply to three different UK university courses through the Chevening course search tool, and receive an unconditional offer from at least one by the deadline specified.
  • You must return to Pakistan for at least two years after your scholarship ends.

Application timeline for 2026 entry: The Chevening application typically opens in early August and closes in early November the year before entry. For 2026 entry, the application window was approximately August 2025 – November 2025. Shortlisted candidates are interviewed in early 2026, and final results are announced in June 2026. Programs begin in September 2026.

Read our detailed Chevening scholarship complete guide for a step-by-step walkthrough of the application process, essay tips, and interview preparation.

Commonwealth Scholarships (via HEC Nomination)

The Commonwealth Scholarships are funded by the UK's Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office and are available to students from Commonwealth countries, including Pakistan. These cover master's and PhD programs at UK universities and are among the most generous scholarships in the world.

What is covered:

  • Full tuition fees at the chosen UK university.
  • A personal monthly stipend (approximately £1,347/month for 2025–26, expected to increase slightly for 2026).
  • Economy-class return airfare.
  • Thesis grant for doctoral students.
  • Warm clothing allowance (for cold-climate placements).
  • Arrival allowance and excess baggage allowance.

How to apply from Pakistan: Unlike Chevening, Commonwealth Scholarships are administered through HEC Pakistan. HEC opens its nomination window annually (typically October–December), and shortlisted candidates are forwarded to the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission (CSC) in the UK. You must apply through HEC's online portal first, not directly to the CSC.

Eligibility:

  • You must be a Pakistani national permanently residing in Pakistan.
  • You must hold at least an upper second-class bachelor's degree (CGPA 3.0+).
  • For PhD, you must hold a master's degree with strong research credentials.
  • You must be unable to fund your studies from your own resources.
  • Age limit: typically under 35 for master's and under 40 for PhD (check HEC's specific call).

HEC usually announces its call for Commonwealth Scholarship nominations in October or November. Monitor the live scholarship board for the exact dates.

Rhodes Scholarship (University of Oxford)

The Rhodes Scholarship is one of the oldest and most prestigious international scholarships, providing full funding for study at the University of Oxford. While not Pakistan-specific, Pakistani students are eligible and several have won the Rhodes in recent years. The scholarship covers all university fees, a personal stipend, health insurance, and economy-class airfare.

Eligibility:

  • You must be a citizen of Pakistan (Rhodes has regional quotas — Pakistan falls under the 'Pakistan' constituency).
  • You must be between 19 and 27 years of age at the time of application.
  • You must hold a bachelor's degree (or be in your final year) with an outstanding academic record — typically a CGPA of 3.5+ or equivalent.
  • You must demonstrate leadership, commitment to service, and energy to use your talents to the full.

Timeline: Applications typically open in June–July and close in October. Interviews are held in November–December, and the scholarship is awarded for the following October. For 2026 entry, applications would have closed around October 2025.

GREAT Scholarships

The GREAT Scholarships are a joint initiative of the British Council and UK universities, offering partial scholarships of at least £10,000 towards tuition fees for students from Pakistan and other countries. These are not fully funded but significantly reduce the cost of a UK master's degree.

Key details:

  • Available at participating UK universities — each university offers a limited number of GREAT Scholarships per year.
  • Typically for one-year taught master's programs.
  • You must hold a Pakistani passport and have received an offer from a participating university.
  • Deadline varies by university — most fall between January and April 2026.
  • Some universities require a separate application; others automatically consider you based on your admission application.

Check the British Council's GREAT Scholarships page for the 2026 list of participating universities and Pakistan-specific awards.

Scotland's Saltire Scholarships and Scotland-Pakistan Scholarship

Scotland offers two relevant programs for Pakistani students:

Scotland's Saltire Scholarships: These are available to citizens of Canada, China, India, Pakistan, and the USA for postgraduate study at Scottish universities. They cover £8,000 towards tuition fees for one-year master's programs in science, technology, creative industries, healthcare, and renewable energy.

Scotland-Pakistan Scholarship for Young Women: Funded by the Scottish Government, this is specifically for Pakistani women to pursue a master's degree at a Scottish university. It is fully funded, covering tuition, living expenses, and airfare. This scholarship is administered through the British Council Pakistan and typically opens in January–February for the following academic year.

Eligibility:

  • You must be a Pakistani woman permanently residing in Pakistan.
  • You must hold a bachelor's degree with a strong academic record.
  • You must apply to and receive an offer from a Scottish university.
  • Priority is given to STEM, education, and sustainable development fields.

United States Scholarships for Pakistani Students 2026

The USA is the second most popular destination for Pakistani scholars, and the funding landscape is excellent. The flagship program is the Fulbright, but university-funded graduate assistantships, research positions, and several other programs also provide substantial financial support.

Fulbright Foreign Student Program (via USEFP Pakistan)

The Fulbright Foreign Student Program is the US government's premier international exchange program, administered in Pakistan by the United States Educational Foundation in Pakistan (USEFP). It provides full funding for Pakistani students to pursue master's or PhD degrees at American universities.

What is covered:

  • Full tuition and fees at the host US university.
  • Monthly living stipend (adjusted for city cost of living).
  • Round-trip economy airfare.
  • Health insurance (mandatory and fully covered).
  • Book and settling-in allowance.
  • Visa processing support.
  • Pre-departure and in-country orientation.

Eligibility for Pakistani applicants:

  • You must be a Pakistani citizen currently residing in Pakistan.
  • You must hold at least a 4-year bachelor's degree or a master's degree from a recognized Pakistani university.
  • For master's applicants: minimum CGPA of 3.0/4.0 (or equivalent).
  • For PhD applicants: a master's degree with strong research credentials.
  • You must demonstrate English proficiency — USEFP accepts IELTS (6.5+) and TOEFL iBT (80+). USEFP also administers its own English test (TSE) for shortlisted candidates.
  • You must commit to returning to Pakistan after completing your studies.
  • Age limit: typically under 35 for master's and under 40 for PhD (as per USEFP's guidelines).

Application timeline for 2026 entry: USEFP typically opens the Fulbright application in February–March of the year before entry and closes in May–June. So for September 2027 entry, applications would open around February 2026. For those targeting September 2026, the application cycle would have already closed by mid-2025.

Important note: Fulbright is extremely competitive. USEFP receives thousands of applications and selects approximately 150–200 Pakistani scholars annually. A strong Statement of Purpose, excellent academic record, relevant work experience, and leadership potential are critical.

University Graduate Assistantships (RA/TA Positions)

While not a named scholarship, graduate research assistantships (RA) and teaching assistantships (TA) at US universities effectively provide full funding for master's and PhD students. Many Pakistani students secure these positions directly from their departments.

How it works:

  • RA positions involve working on a professor's research project. You receive a monthly stipend and often a full tuition waiver.
  • TA positions involve assisting with undergraduate courses. You also receive a stipend and tuition waiver.
  • These positions are typically offered for the duration of your program (2 years for master's, 4–6 years for PhD).
  • You usually need to contact professors directly before applying to express interest in their research and inquire about available positions.

Tip for Pakistani students: Identify professors whose research aligns with your interests, send them a well-crafted email with your CV and research statement, and apply to the university's graduate program. Having a professor willing to fund you from their grant dramatically increases your admission and funding chances.

Other US Scholarships and Programs

Several additional US programs are relevant for Pakistani students:

  • Hubert H. Humphrey Fellowship: A non-degree program for mid-career professionals (not students), but worth mentioning as it provides a year of professional development at a US university. Applications through USEFP.
  • Stanford Knight-Hennessy Scholars: A fully funded master's/PhD program at Stanford University. Pakistani students are eligible and several have been selected. The scholarship covers tuition, stipend, travel, and academic enrichment. Deadline is typically in October. Highly competitive — requires exceptional academics, leadership, and civic mindset.
  • University-specific merit scholarships: Many US universities (MIT, Harvard, Yale, Columbia, etc.) offer their own international merit scholarships that can cover partial to full tuition. These are typically considered automatically upon admission or require a separate application.
  • AAUW International Fellowships: For women from outside the US to pursue graduate study. Covers up to $50,000 for master's/PhD. Deadline typically in November.

Germany Scholarships for Pakistani Students 2026

Germany has emerged as a top destination for Pakistani students thanks to its tuition-free public universities (even for international students) and the strength of programs in engineering, computer science, natural sciences, and medicine. While tuition is free at most public universities, students still need to cover living expenses, which is where scholarships come in.

DAAD Scholarships

The Deutscher Akademischer Austauschdienst (DAAD) — the German Academic Exchange Service — is the world's largest funding organization for international academic exchange. DAAD offers numerous scholarship programs for Pakistani students at the master's and PhD levels.

Key DAAD programs for Pakistan:

  • DAAD Scholarship for Development-Related Postgraduate Courses (EPOS): This targets professionals from developing countries (including Pakistan) who want to pursue a master's in development-related fields such as engineering, public health, agriculture, economics, and environmental sciences. The scholarship covers a monthly stipend of approximately €934 (master's) or €1,300 (PhD), health insurance, travel allowance, and study/research allowance. Applications typically close in October 2025 for programs starting in 2026.
  • DAAD Research Grants (PhD and Postdoc): For Pakistani researchers who want to conduct doctoral or postdoctoral research at a German university or research institution. The monthly stipend ranges from €1,300 to €2,670 depending on the level. Deadline is typically in October–November.
  • DAAD Helmut Schmidt Scholarship (Public Policy and Good Governance): A fully funded master's program for future leaders from developing countries. Covers tuition, monthly stipend, travel, health insurance, and study allowance. Deadline is usually in August.

General DAAD eligibility:

  • Strong academic record (typically CGPA 3.0+).
  • Relevant academic background matching the chosen program.
  • At least 2 years of professional experience (for EPOS and some other programs).
  • English proficiency (IELTS 6.0+ or equivalent) for English-taught programs; German language skills (TestDaF or DSH) for German-taught programs.
  • Pakistani citizenship and residence.

KAAD Scholarships (Catholic Academic Exchange Service)

The KAAD (Katholischer Akademischer Ausländer-Dienst) provides scholarships for students and academics from developing countries to study or research in Germany. KAAD covers tuition (which is mostly free anyway at public universities), a monthly stipend, health insurance, and travel costs.

Important caveat: KAAD is a Catholic organization. While it does not require you to be Catholic, it does prioritize applicants who are affiliated with the Catholic Church or who have a demonstrated connection to a Christian community. Pakistani Catholics or Christians from minority communities may find this particularly relevant. Muslim applicants should be aware of the religious orientation and apply accordingly.

Eligibility:

  • Applicants from developing countries (Pakistan qualifies).
  • Strong academic record.
  • German language skills (typically B1–B2 level) are required for most KAAD programs, though some English-taught exceptions exist.
  • A commitment to intercultural and interreligious dialogue is valued.

KAAD applications typically close in September–October for programs starting the following year.

Deutschlandstipendium

The Deutschlandstipendium (Germany Scholarship) is a merit-based scholarship available to both German and international students at participating German universities. It provides €300 per month for at least two semesters (often longer). While not exclusively for Pakistani students, international students — including those from Pakistan — are eligible.

How to apply: You apply directly to the university you are attending. Each university selects its own recipients based on academic merit, social engagement, and personal achievements. Some universities also consider financial need. Deadline is set by each institution, typically at the start of the academic year.

Tuition-Free Universities and the Blocked Account Requirement

It is important to understand that most public universities in Germany charge no tuition fees for international students, including Pakistani nationals. Students pay only a small semester contribution (typically €150–€350) that covers administrative costs and a public transport ticket.

However, to obtain a German student visa, Pakistani students must demonstrate sufficient financial resources by depositing approximately €11,208 per year (as of 2025) into a blocked account (Sperrkonto). This money is released to you in monthly installments (approximately €934/month) once you arrive in Germany. This is a visa requirement, not a scholarship, but it is essential to know about when planning your budget.

Turkey Scholarships for Pakistani Students 2026

Turkey has become one of the most popular study-abroad destinations for Pakistani students, thanks in large part to the Turkiye Burslari (Turkey Scholarships) program — one of the world's most generous fully funded scholarship programs.

Turkiye Burslari (Turkey Scholarships)

Turkiye Burslari is the Turkish government's flagship scholarship program, administered by the Presidency for Turks Abroad and Related Communities (YTB). It provides fully funded scholarships at all levels — undergraduate, master's, and PhD — at Turkish universities.

What is covered:

  • Full tuition fees at the host Turkish university.
  • A monthly stipend: approximately 800 TRY for undergraduate, 1,100 TRY for master's, and 1,600 TRY for PhD students (amounts may be adjusted for 2026 — check official site).
  • Free accommodation in university dormitories.
  • Round-trip airfare (once at the beginning and once at the end of the program).
  • Health insurance.
  • One year of free Turkish language training (andatory for all scholarship holders).

Eligibility:

  • Must be a non-Turkish citizen (Pakistanis qualify).
  • Undergraduate: must be under 21 years of age and have a strong high school record (typically 75%+ or equivalent).
  • Master's: must be under 30 and hold a bachelor's degree with CGPA 3.0+.
  • PhD: must be under 35 and hold a master's degree.
  • No previous study in Turkey at the same level is allowed.
  • Turkish proficiency is not required — the program includes a one-year language course.

Application timeline: Applications for Turkiye Burslari open in January and close in February each year. For 2026 entry, the application window is typically January–February 2026. Results are announced by July–August, and programs begin in September–October.

Tip: Turkiye Burslari is extremely popular among Pakistani students, with thousands applying each year. A well-written motivation letter and strong academic record are critical. The program also considers your fit with Turkish universities and your post-graduation plans.

Hungary Scholarships for Pakistani Students 2026

Hungary offers one of the best fully funded scholarship programs for Pakistani students through the Stipendium Hungaricum, a government-funded initiative administered through bilateral agreements between Hungary and partner countries, including Pakistan. See our Stipendium Hungaricum guide..

Stipendium Hungaricum

The Stipendium Hungaricum is a scholarship program funded by the Hungarian government and administered by the Tempus Public Foundation (TPF). Pakistan is a partner country, and Pakistani students can apply for undergraduate, master's, and PhD programs.

What is covered:

  • Full tuition fees (Hungarian public universities charge no tuition to Stipendium Hungaricum scholars).
  • Monthly stipend: approximately HUF 43,700 (~€110–120) for master's students and HUF 140,000 (~€360) for PhD students (2025 figures — expect slight increases for 2026).
  • Free dormitory accommodation or a housing contribution.
  • Health insurance.

Eligibility:

  • Must be a Pakistani citizen residing in Pakistan.
  • Undergraduate: strong secondary school record (typically 75%+ in HSSC or equivalent).
  • Master's: bachelor's degree with CGPA 3.0+.
  • PhD: master's degree with strong research potential.
  • You must be nominated by the Pakistani sending partner — for Pakistan, this is typically HEC or the relevant government body.
  • You must not have a previous degree from a Hungarian institution at the same level.

Application timeline: The Stipendium Hungaricum application typically opens in November and closes in January. For 2026 entry, applications would have opened in November 2025 and closed in January 2026. Pakistani students apply through the designated Pakistani partner (HEC or the relevant body), and the Hungarian side makes the final selection.

China Scholarships for Pakistani Students 2026

China has become a major destination for Pakistani students, particularly for medical (MBBS), engineering, and computer science programs. The Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC) is the primary funding mechanism.

Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC)

The China Scholarship Council (CSC) manages the Chinese Government Scholarship program, which provides fully funded scholarships for international students at Chinese universities. Pakistani students are among the top recipients.

What is covered:

  • Full tuition fees.
  • Free accommodation on campus (or a housing allowance).
  • Monthly stipend: approximately CNY 2,500/month for bachelor's, CNY 3,000/month for master's, and CNY 3,500/month for PhD (2025 figures).
  • Comprehensive health insurance.

How to apply from Pakistan: Pakistani students can apply through two channels:

  1. Via the Embassy of China in Pakistan: The Chinese Embassy in Islamabad accepts applications directly. You submit your application to the embassy, which shortlists and forwards candidates to CSC.
  2. Via HEC Pakistan: HEC sometimes runs a parallel selection process for CSC scholarships. Check HEC's website for the latest call.
  3. Directly to Chinese universities: Some Chinese universities have their own quotas and may accept direct applications.

Eligibility:

  • Non-Chinese citizen (Pakistanis qualify).
  • Under 25 for bachelor's, under 35 for master's, under 40 for PhD.
  • Strong academic record (typically 75%+ in previous degree).
  • Good health (medical certificate required).
  • English proficiency: IELTS 5.5–6.0 or equivalent (some universities accept Duolingo or internal English tests).

Application timeline: CSC applications typically open in January and close in April each year. For 2026 entry, the window is approximately January–April 2026. Results are announced by July.

Provincial and University Scholarships in China

Many Chinese provinces and individual universities offer their own scholarships separate from the CSC program. These may cover partial to full tuition, and sometimes include a monthly stipend. Examples include the Shanghai Government Scholarship, Beijing Government Scholarship, and university-specific awards from Tsinghua, Peking, Zhejiang, and Fudan universities. These are often less competitive than CSC and can be a good backup option. Check individual university websites for 2026 availability.

Japan Scholarships for Pakistani Students 2026

Japan's MEXT (Monbukagakusho) scholarship is one of the most coveted fully funded opportunities for Pakistani students, covering everything from tuition to living expenses to airfare. Read our MEXT scholarship guide for details..

MEXT Scholarship (Japanese Government Scholarship)

The MEXT Scholarship is funded by the Japanese Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT). It is administered in Pakistan through the Embassy of Japan in Islamabad. Pakistani students can apply for undergraduate, master's, and PhD programs at Japanese universities.

What is covered:

  • Full tuition fees at a Japanese university.
  • Monthly stipend: approximately ¥117,000 (~$800) for research students (master's/PhD), ¥120,000 for undergraduate, and ¥145,000 for PhD-level students (2025 figures — expect minor increases for 2026).
  • Round-trip airfare.
  • 6 months of intensive Japanese language training before the academic program begins (for most categories).

Eligibility:

  • Pakistani citizenship (or other eligible nationality).
  • Under 35 for research students, under 25 for undergraduate.
  • Strong academic record (CGPA 3.0+ or equivalent).
  • Good health.
  • Japanese language proficiency is not required for initial application but is developed during the preparatory period. Some programs (especially at the graduate level) are taught in English.

Application timeline: MEXT applications in Pakistan typically open in March–April and close in May–June. The Embassy of Japan in Islamabad administers the process, which includes document screening, written examinations (mathematics, English, Japanese), and interviews. Results are announced by early the following year, and programs begin in April or October.

Tip: The MEXT application process is rigorous. The written exam covers mathematics and English at a high level, and the Japanese language test is challenging for beginners. Prepare thoroughly and consider taking Japanese language classes before applying.

South Korea Scholarships for Pakistani Students 2026

South Korea's Global Korea Scholarship (GKS) — formerly known as the Korean Government Scholarship Program (KGSP) — is a fully funded opportunity for Pakistani students at all academic levels.

Global Korea Scholarship (GKS)

The GKS is funded by the Korean government through the National Institute for International Education (NIIED). In Pakistan, it is administered through the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Islamabad. It covers undergraduate, master's, and PhD programs at Korean universities.

What is covered:

  • Full tuition fees.
  • Monthly stipend: approximately ₩1,000,000 (~$750) for master's/PhD students; ₩900,000 for undergraduate.
  • Free on-campus housing or a housing allowance.
  • Round-trip airfare.
  • Health insurance (National Health Insurance).
  • Korean language training (1 year for undergraduate, 6 months for graduate).
  • Settlement allowance and completion grant.

Eligibility:

  • Pakistani citizenship.
  • Under 40 for graduate programs, under 25 for undergraduate.
  • Strong academic record: CGPA of at least 80% (or 2.64/4.0) out of 100%. Higher CGPA gives better chances.
  • Korean language proficiency (TOPIK Level 2+) is a plus but not mandatory — you will receive language training.
  • Good health.
  • Not a Korean citizen or descendant of Korean nationals (unless eligible under specific categories).

Application timeline: GKS applications in Pakistan typically open in February–March and close in March–April. The Embassy of Korea in Pakistan manages the first round of selection. Final results are announced by August–September, and programs begin in September (for university track) or March (for embassy track).

For a comprehensive application guide, see our GKS complete guide.

Australia Scholarships for Pakistani Students 2026

Australia offers several scholarship programs for Pakistani students, with the Australia Awards being the most prominent fully funded option.

Australia Awards Scholarships

The Australia Awards Scholarships (AAS) are funded by the Australian Government through the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT). They provide full funding for Pakistani students to pursue master's or PhD programs at Australian universities, with a focus on fields that contribute to Pakistan's development.

What is covered:

  • Full tuition fees.
  • Return airfare.
  • Establishment allowance (one-time payment for arrival costs).
  • Contribution to living expenses (approximately AUD 3,000/month).
  • Introductory academic program.
  • Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC).
  • Supplementary academic support (if required).
  • Fieldwork allowance (for select programs).

Eligibility:

  • Pakistani citizenship and residence.
  • At least 2 years of professional work experience.
  • Strong academic record (typically CGPA 3.0+).
  • Not a current or former Australian Government scholarship holder (for some categories).
  • Must be applying to an eligible master's or PhD program at an Australian university.
  • Fields of study: typically aligned with Pakistan's development priorities — agriculture, health, education, governance, infrastructure, and water resources.

Application timeline: Australia Awards applications in Pakistan typically open in February–March and close in April–May. Applications are submitted through the Australian Awards Pakistan online portal. Shortlisted candidates are interviewed, and results are announced by the end of the year.

Canada Scholarships for Pakistani Students 2026

Canada is an increasingly popular destination for Pakistani students, though it has fewer government-funded scholarships compared to the UK or USA. The main funding avenues are university-specific awards and the prestigious Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarship.

Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarships

The Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarship is a Canadian government-funded award for doctoral students who demonstrate academic excellence, research potential, and leadership. It provides CAD 50,000 per year for three years — making it one of the most generous PhD scholarships globally.

Eligibility:

  • Open to Canadian and international students (including Pakistani nationals).
  • You must be nominated by the Canadian university where you are applying (you cannot apply directly).
  • You must be pursuing a PhD program (or combined MA/PhD) at a Canadian university.
  • Strong academic record (typically in the top 10% of your class).
  • Demonstrated research excellence and leadership potential.

Application timeline: Vanier applications open in July and close in November. You must be nominated by your university, so contact the graduate studies office early.

University Scholarships and Funding in Canada

Most Canadian universities offer their own scholarships for international graduate students, including:

  • University of Toronto: Lester B. Pearson International Scholarship (undergraduate), various graduate funding packages.
  • McGill University: McCall MacBain Scholarships (graduate), and departmental funding.
  • University of British Columbia: International Doctoral Fellowships (CAD 3,200/year + tuition).
  • University of Alberta, University of Waterloo, McMaster, etc.: Department-specific funding and international student awards.

For master's programs, many Canadian departments offer graduate teaching and research assistantships that provide tuition waivers and stipends. Contact the department directly for details.

Quebec Exemptions

The province of Quebec has a unique system where international students at Quebec universities pay a different fee structure. Some Quebec universities and the provincial government offer exemptions or reduced tuition for international students through programs like the Quebec Merit Scholarship for International Students (PBEEE). This is worth investigating if you are targeting universities in Montreal or other Quebec cities.

Other Countries: Netherlands, Italy, Malaysia, Russia, Kazakhstan

Beyond the major destinations listed above, several other countries offer meaningful scholarship opportunities for Pakistani students in 2026.

Netherlands

The Orange Knowledge Programme (OKP) and the Holland Scholarship are the main Dutch government-funded options for Pakistani students. OKP provides fully funded master's programs for mid-career professionals from developing countries, including Pakistan. However, the OKP program has undergone changes in recent years — check the official Nuffic website for the latest 2026 cycle availability. The Holland Scholarship provides a one-time grant of €5,000 for non-EEA international students.

Dutch universities also offer their own scholarships: the University of Amsterdam Excellence Scholarship, University of Groningen Talent Grant, and Delft University of Excellence Scholarship are all worth exploring.

Italy

Italy offers several scholarship programs for international students, including those from Pakistan:

  • DSU (Diritto allo Studio Universitario) Scholarships: Regional scholarships that cover tuition fees and provide a living stipend. Available at most Italian public universities. Application deadlines vary by region — typically June–September for the following academic year.
  • Italian Government Scholarships (Invest Your Talent in Italy): A fully funded master's program for students from partner countries. Pakistan is included. Covers tuition and a monthly stipend.
  • University-specific scholarships: Bocconi, Politecnico di Milano, Sapienza, and other top Italian universities offer merit-based scholarships for international students.

Note: Many Italian university programs are taught in Italian, but an increasing number are offered in English, especially at the graduate level. IELTS/TOEFL is required for English-taught programs.

Malaysia

Malaysia has become a popular affordable study destination for Pakistani students, and several funding options exist:

  • Malaysian International Scholarship (MIS): Funded by the Malaysian government through the Ministry of Education. Fully funded master's and PhD scholarships for international students, including Pakistanis. Covers tuition, living allowance, and airfare.
  • University-specific scholarships: Universiti Malaya (UM), Universiti Putra Malaysia (UPM), and other top Malaysian universities offer tuition fee reductions and partial scholarships for international students.

Russia

The Russian government offers scholarships to Pakistani students through the Rossotrudnichestvo (Federal Agency for CIS Affairs, Compatriots Living Abroad, and International Humanitarian Cooperation). The scholarship covers tuition, a monthly stipend, and dormitory accommodation. Applications are made through the Russian cultural center or embassy in Pakistan. The Russian government typically allocates a specific quota of scholarships for Pakistani nationals each year.

Key details:

  • Covers tuition, dormitory, and a monthly stipend (approximately 1,500–3,000 RUB/month, which is modest but supplemented by low living costs).
  • Programs are mostly in Russian — you may need to complete a 1-year preparatory Russian language course first.
  • Some English-taught programs exist at top universities (Moscow State, SPbSU, etc.).
  • Applications typically close in October–November for the following year.

Kazakhstan

Kazakhstan is an emerging destination for Pakistani students, particularly in medical and engineering fields. The Bolashak International Scholarship is the Kazakh government's flagship scholarship for international students, though it primarily targets Kazakh citizens. Pakistani students are more likely to find opportunities through direct university applications, where many Kazakh universities offer reduced tuition fees for international students. Costs are generally lower than in Western countries, making Kazakhstan an attractive option even without a full scholarship.

HEC Pakistan Scholarships for Studying Abroad

The Higher Education Commission (HEC) of Pakistan plays a central role in facilitating Pakistani students who wish to study abroad. While HEC itself does not fully fund overseas study (it primarily funds domestic scholarships), it serves as the nominating body for many international scholarships and administers its own programs.

HEC Overseas Scholarships

HEC periodically announces Overseas Scholarship Schemes that provide partial to full funding for Pakistani students to pursue master's and PhD degrees abroad. These are typically announced on a year-by-year basis, so check HEC's website regularly for the latest announcements.

HEC Indigenous Scholarships (for Domestic Study)

While not for studying abroad, HEC's Indigenous PhD Fellowship Program and MS leading to PhD programs are worth mentioning as alternatives for students who decide to pursue their graduate studies in Pakistan. These provide full tuition, stipend, and research funding at Pakistani universities.

HEC's Role in International Scholarships

HEC serves as the Pakistan-level administrator for several international scholarship programs:

  • Commonwealth Scholarships: HEC nominates Pakistani candidates.
  • Stipendium Hungaricum: HEC coordinates the Pakistani selection process.
  • CSC (China Government Scholarship): HEC sometimes runs parallel selection rounds.
  • DAAD and other programs: HEC may provide NOCs (No Objection Certificates) required for Pakistani students to study abroad.

Important: To study abroad on any scholarship, you will likely need an HEC NOC. This is a mandatory document that HEC issues to confirm that you are not blacklisted and that your academic credentials are verified. Apply for the NOC well in advance — processing can take 4–8 weeks.

PEEF (Punjab Educational Endowment Fund)

The PEEF is a Punjab-government initiative that provides scholarships for students from Punjab to study at top universities in Pakistan and abroad. PEEF offers:

  • Master's Scholarships: For students from Punjab to pursue master's degrees at top-ranking HEC-recognized Pakistani universities.
  • PhD Scholarships: For students from Punjab to pursue PhD degrees at Pakistani or select international universities.
  • Shahbaz Sharif Merit Scholarship: A fully funded scholarship for top-performing students from Punjab to study at world's top 200 universities (as per QS/THE rankings).

PEEF scholarships are primarily for Punjab residents, but some categories are open to students from other provinces. Eligibility typically requires domicile of Punjab, a strong academic record, and enrollment or admission at a recognized university.

General Eligibility Requirements for Pakistani Students

While each scholarship has its own specific requirements, the following are the universal eligibility criteria that most international scholarships share:

Academic Requirements

  • CGPA: Most scholarships require a minimum CGPA of 3.0 out of 4.0 (or approximately 75% in the Pakistani percentage system). Elite scholarships like Rhodes, Gates Cambridge, and Knight-Hennessy typically expect 3.5+.
  • Degree level: For master's scholarships, you must hold a bachelor's degree (4-year program or equivalent). For PhD, you must hold a master's degree. Some programs accept students in their final year of study.
  • HEC recognition: Your degree must be from an HEC-recognized institution. If your university is not on the HEC list, you may face rejection.

English Language Requirements

  • IELTS Academic: Most scholarships require an overall band score of 6.5, with no individual band below 5.5 or 6.0. Some require 7.0+.
  • TOEFL iBT: Typically 80–100+ depending on the scholarship and university.
  • PTE Academic: Accepted by many scholarships as an alternative to IELTS. Typical requirement: 58–65+.
  • Exceptions: Scholarships in non-English countries (Turkey, China, Japan, Korea) may not require IELTS if the program is in the local language, or they may accept their own English proficiency tests. However, for English-taught tracks in these countries, IELTS or equivalent is usually required.

Age Limits

  • Undergraduate: Typically under 21–25 years.
  • Master's: Typically under 30–35 years.
  • PhD: Typically under 35–40 years.
  • Some scholarships (Chevening, Fulbright) have specific age guidelines that may be flexible for exceptional candidates.

Work Experience

  • Chevening: Minimum 2 years (2,800 hours) of work experience.
  • Fulbright: Not strictly required but strongly recommended; relevant work experience improves chances significantly.
  • DAAD EPOS: Minimum 2 years of professional experience.
  • Australia Awards: Minimum 2 years of professional experience.
  • Most other scholarships do not require work experience but value it highly.

Documents You Need to Prepare

Regardless of which scholarship you apply for, you will need a standard set of documents. Start assembling these months in advance — HEC attestation and document preparation can take weeks.

Essential Documents

  • Valid Pakistani passport: Must be valid for at least 18 months beyond your intended travel date. Apply or renew early.
  • Academic transcripts and degree certificates: All bachelor's, master's (if applicable), and any other degree certificates.
  • HEC attested documents: All academic documents must be attested by HEC Pakistan. This is mandatory for most international scholarships. Apply through HEC's online attestation portal — processing takes 2–6 weeks.
  • Statement of Purpose (SOP): A compelling essay explaining your academic background, career goals, why you chose the specific program/university, and how the scholarship will help you serve Pakistan. Typically 500–1,000 words. Tailor this for each scholarship — do not use a generic SOP.
  • Recommendation letters (LORs): At least 2–3 letters from professors, supervisors, or employers who know your academic and professional capabilities. Give your recommenders at least 4–6 weeks' notice.
  • IELTS/TOEFL/PTE score report: Official score report sent to you (or the scholarship body). Test scores are valid for 2 years.
  • Research proposal (for PhD/research programs): A detailed proposal outlining your research objectives, methodology, and expected outcomes. Typically 2,000–3,000 words.
  • CV/Resume: An up-to-date academic CV, including education, work experience, publications, skills, and extracurricular activities.
  • Medical certificate: Some scholarships require a medical fitness certificate from a recognized hospital.
  • Passport-sized photographs: Typically 2–4 recent photographs with white or blue background.
  • University admission letter: An unconditional offer from a recognized university. Most scholarships require this before you can finalize your application.

Tips for Winning a Scholarship from Pakistan

Competition for scholarships is fierce, especially for Pakistani students — thousands apply for each program. Here are practical, field-tested tips to maximize your chances:

1. Start 12–18 Months Early

The biggest mistake Pakistani students make is starting too late. If you want to study abroad in September 2026, you should have begun preparing in early 2025. This means taking IELTS, arranging LORs, drafting your SOP, and researching programs well in advance.

2. Tailor Every Application

Do not copy-paste the same SOP across multiple scholarships. Each scholarship has different values and selection criteria. Chevening values leadership and networking. Fulbright values academic excellence and service to Pakistan. DAAD values development relevance. Read the scholarship's mission statement and tailor your essays accordingly.

3. Get Your IELTS Score as High as Possible

IELTS is often the differentiator. Aim for 7.0+ overall, with no band below 6.5. A higher IELTS score signals English proficiency and makes your application stronger. Take the test multiple times if necessary — many scholarships accept your best score.

4. Secure Strong Recommendation Letters

Choose recommenders who know you well and can speak specifically about your abilities, character, and potential. A generic letter from a professor who barely knows you is useless. Give your recommenders your CV, SOP draft, and a list of your achievements so they can write detailed, personalized letters.

5. Apply to Multiple Scholarships Simultaneously

Do not put all your eggs in one basket. Apply to 3–5 scholarships simultaneously. The timelines overlap for most programs, so you can manage multiple applications with proper planning.

6. Get University Admission First

Most scholarships require proof of admission or at least an application to a recognized university. Apply to universities early — many have rolling admissions and respond within 4–8 weeks.

7. Attend Pre-Departure and Networking Events

If you are shortlisted for an interview, attend any preparation sessions offered by the scholarship body. USEFP (Fulbright) and the British Council (Chevening) often hold workshops in Karachi, Lahore, and Islamabad. These are invaluable for interview preparation.

8. Be Honest and Authentic

Scholarship committees read thousands of applications. They can spot generic, inflated, or dishonest claims easily. Be genuine about your achievements, honest about your challenges, and specific about your goals.

9. Demonstrate Impact on Pakistan

Almost every scholarship for Pakistani students wants to know how you will use your education to benefit Pakistan. Be specific: name the sector you want to work in, the problems you want to solve, and the communities you want to serve.

10. Follow Instructions Exactly

If the application asks for a 500-word SOP, do not submit 1,000 words. If it requires three LORs, do not submit two. If it asks for IELTS 6.5, do not submit a 6.0. Following instructions precisely shows attention to detail and respect for the process.

Scholarship Scams to Avoid

Unfortunately, scholarship scams are rampant in Pakistan. Unscrupulous agents and consultants prey on students' dreams and charge exorbitant fees for services that are either worthless or harmful. Here is what you need to know:

  • Never pay for a scholarship application: Every legitimate scholarship listed in this guide has a free application portal. If someone asks you to pay a fee to apply, it is a scam.
  • Never give your passport or bank details to an agent: Official scholarships will ask for these directly through their own secure portals — never through a third party.
  • Beware of guaranteed scholarships: No one can guarantee you a scholarship. Anyone who claims they can is lying.
  • Do not pay for SOP writing services: While it is acceptable to get feedback on your SOP from a trusted mentor or professor, paying a service to write it for you is both unethical and risky — scholarship committees can tell when an SOP is not written by the applicant.
  • Use official websites only: Apply directly through the scholarship's official website or through HEC/USEFP/British Council Pakistan. Do not use third-party application portals.
  • Report scams: If you encounter a scholarship scam, report it to HEC, USEFP, or the relevant embassy.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Can Pakistani students get full scholarships abroad?

Yes. Pakistani students regularly win fully funded scholarships from major programs like Chevening (UK), Fulbright (USA), Turkiye Burslari (Turkey), Stipendium Hungaricum (Hungary), CSC (China), MEXT (Japan), GKS (South Korea), and Australia Awards. In 2024–25 alone, hundreds of Pakistani students received fully funded scholarships across these programs. The key is applying early, preparing strong documents, and meeting all eligibility requirements.

What CGPA is required for a scholarship from Pakistan?

The minimum CGPA varies by scholarship, but the general baseline is 3.0 out of 4.0 (or approximately 75% in the Pakistani system). Elite programs like Rhodes, Gates Cambridge, and Knight-Hennessy typically expect 3.5+. Some scholarships like DAAD EPOS or Australia Awards may accept slightly lower CGPAs if you have strong professional experience. Always check the specific scholarship's requirements, as they vary.

Do Pakistani students need IELTS for these scholarships?

Almost every English-medium scholarship requires IELTS Academic (typically 6.5 overall, with no band below 5.5–6.0). TOEFL iBT and PTE Academic are accepted as alternatives by most programs. Some scholarships in non-English countries (Turkey, China, Japan, Korea) may waive IELTS if the program is taught in the local language, or if you can demonstrate English proficiency through other means. However, for English-taught tracks in these countries, IELTS or equivalent is usually required.

Are there scholarships for MBBS/MD students from Pakistan?

Yes, but options are more limited than for other fields. China's CSC scholarship fully funds MBBS programs at Chinese medical universities. Turkey's Turkiye Burslari also funds medical programs. Russia's government scholarship and several Kazakh universities offer medical scholarships. Germany's DAAD funds medical research at the graduate/PhD level, but undergraduate medical programs in Germany are primarily in German. Fulbright covers medical fields at the graduate/PhD level, not undergraduate MBBS.

How do I apply for a scholarship from Pakistan?

The process varies by scholarship but generally follows these steps: (1) Research scholarships and eligibility. (2) Take IELTS/TOEFL and score 6.5+. (3) Apply to universities and secure an admission offer. (4) Prepare your documents: SOP, LORs, transcripts, HEC attestation, passport. (5) Submit your application through the official portal (Chevening, Fulbright, USEFP, HEC, embassy, etc.). (6) Attend interviews if shortlisted. (7) Accept the scholarship and apply for a visa. Start this process 12–18 months before your intended program start date.

The Role of HEC Attestation and NOC

One of the most overlooked steps for Pakistani students is HEC attestation and the No Objection Certificate (NOC). Here is what you need to know:

  • HEC Attestation: All academic documents (transcripts, degree certificates, equivalency certificates) must be attested by HEC before submission to international scholarship bodies. Apply through HEC's online portal. You will need to submit original documents or attested copies. Processing takes 2–6 weeks.
  • HEC Equivalency: If your degree is from a Pakistani university, HEC may issue an equivalency certificate confirming that your qualification is equivalent to the international standard (e.g., a 4-year bachelor's being equivalent to an honours degree).
  • HEC NOC: To study abroad on a scholarship, you must obtain an NOC from HEC. This confirms that you are not blacklisted, that your credentials are verified, and that HEC has no objection to your overseas study. Apply at least 6–8 weeks before your intended travel date.
  • Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA) attestation: Some scholarships or visa processes may additionally require attestation from Pakistan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Check the specific requirements of your scholarship and the destination country's embassy.

Different scholarships are better suited to different fields. Here is a quick reference:

  • Engineering and Technology: DAAD (Germany), CSC (China), MEXT (Japan), GKS (Korea), university RA/TA (USA, Canada).
  • Business and Management: Chevening (UK), Fulbright (USA), Stipendium Hungaricum (Hungary).
  • Medicine and Health Sciences: CSC (China), Turkiye Burslari (Turkey), Russia government scholarship, DAAD (Germany — graduate level).
  • Computer Science and IT: DAAD (Germany), CSC (China), MEXT (Japan), university RA/TA (USA, Canada).
  • Social Sciences and Humanities: Chevening (UK), Fulbright (USA), Commonwealth (UK), Stipendium Hungaricum (Hungary).
  • Agriculture and Environmental Sciences: DAAD EPOS (Germany), Australia Awards, Turkiye Burslari (Turkey).
  • Education: Fulbright (USA), Chevening (UK), Commonwealth (UK), Australia Awards.
  • Law and Human Rights: Chevening (UK), Fulbright (USA), Stipendium Hungaricum (Hungary).

Special Scholarships for Pakistani Women

Several scholarships have specific provisions or preferences for Pakistani women:

  • Scotland-Pakistan Scholarship for Young Women: Fully funded master's at Scottish universities. Open only to Pakistani women. Administered by the British Council Pakistan.
  • Australia Awards: While open to all genders, AAS actively encourages applications from Pakistani women and has gender-equality targets.
  • AAUW International Fellowships (USA): Exclusively for women from outside the US to pursue graduate study. Provides up to $50,000.
  • Chevening and Fulbright: Both programs strongly encourage applications from Pakistani women and have seen increasing female representation in recent years.
  • Turkiye Burslari and GKS: Both programs have a strong track record of funding Pakistani women, particularly in STEM and education fields.

Post-Scholarship: Returning to Pakistan

Many scholarships — including Chevening, Fulbright, Commonwealth, and Turkiye Burslari — require you to return to Pakistan for a specified period (usually 2 years) after completing your studies. This is a binding commitment, not just a suggestion. Here is why this matters and how to prepare:

  • Chevening: You must return to Pakistan for at least 2 years after the scholarship ends. Your visa is tied to this commitment.
  • Fulbright: You must return to Pakistan for at least 2 years. Fulbright alumni are expected to contribute to Pakistan's development.
  • Turkiye Burslari: You must return to Pakistan for at least 2 years.
  • Practical tip: Returning to Pakistan is not a punishment — it is an opportunity. Pakistani professionals with international degrees and experience are in high demand in academia, government, NGOs, and the private sector. Use your international education to make a real impact.
