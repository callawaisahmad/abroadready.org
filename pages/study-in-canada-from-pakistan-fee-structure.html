Introduction: Why Canada Is the Top Choice for Pakistani Students

Canada has emerged as the single most popular study abroad destination for students from Pakistan, and the numbers tell a compelling story. In 2025, Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) issued over 500,000 study permits to international students, and Pakistani nationals consistently rank among the top five source countries. The appeal is straightforward: world-class universities, affordable tuition compared to the United States and United Kingdom, generous post-graduation work rights, and a clear pathway to permanent residency through Express Entry and Provincial Nominee Programmes.

But behind the aspiration lies a question that every Pakistani family must answer honestly: can we actually afford it? The answer is nuanced. Canada is not cheap, but it is far more accessible than many students assume once you understand the full cost picture. Tuition at a public college can be as low as CAD 10,000 per year. Living costs in smaller cities like Winnipeg or Halifax can drop below CAD 1,000 per month. And the combination of part-time work during studies, co-op placements, and the post-graduation work permit means that many students earn back a significant portion of their investment while still in Canada.

This guide is the most comprehensive resource available for Pakistani students planning to study in Canada. It covers tuition fees for every programme level, living costs broken down by Canadian city, the SDS and regular visa pathways, scholarships specifically available to Pakistani nationals, the GIC requirement, health insurance, work rights, post-graduation pathways, and a complete application timeline. Whether you are a student with a 2.5 CGPA looking at college diplomas or a high achiever targeting the University of Toronto, this guide gives you the numbers and the strategy to plan with confidence.

All figures in this guide are for the 2026–27 academic year and are presented in Canadian dollars (CAD) unless otherwise noted. Exchange rate references use the approximate rate of 1 CAD = 200 PKR, though you should check current rates before making financial decisions. For a broader look at studying abroad, visit our cost and budget guide.

Tuition Fees by Programme Type

Tuition is the single largest component of your study abroad budget. It varies dramatically based on whether you attend a college or university, the specific province, and the programme you choose. Below are detailed breakdowns for the 2026–27 academic year.

Diploma and Certificate Programmes (Colleges)

Public colleges in Canada offer diploma and certificate programmes that are the most affordable pathway for Pakistani students. Annual international tuition typically ranges from CAD 10,000 to 18,000. These programmes last one to three years and are designed with direct employer input, which means the curriculum is practical and job-focused. Most include mandatory co-op work placements where you earn money while gaining Canadian work experience.

Popular institutions in this category include Seneca College in Toronto, Humber College, Conestoga College in Kitchener, George Brown College in downtown Toronto, Centennial College, and Langara College in Vancouver. A two-year diploma at Seneca College in business or computer systems costs approximately CAD 16,000 per year for international students. A one-year graduate certificate at Humber College in a technology field costs approximately CAD 14,000 to 16,000.

College diplomas are particularly attractive for students with lower CGPAs because admissions requirements are more flexible than universities. Many colleges accept students with a CGPA of 2.5 or higher, and some accept students with even lower grades if they have relevant work experience. English requirements are also slightly lower, with some colleges accepting IELTS 5.5 overall for conditional admission.

The pathway advantage is significant. Many colleges have articulation agreements with universities, allowing you to transfer diploma credits toward a bachelor's degree. This means you can start at a college at CAD 12,000 per year, complete two years, transfer to a university, and earn a bachelor's degree at lower total cost than four years at a university directly. For more on this pathway, see our low CGPA scholarship guide.

Bachelor's Degree Programmes (Universities)

Undergraduate tuition at Canadian universities for international students ranges from CAD 15,000 to 38,000 per year. The range depends heavily on the institution and the field of study. Humanities and social science programmes tend to be at the lower end, while engineering, computer science, business, and natural sciences command higher tuition.

At the University of Manitoba, international undergraduate tuition is approximately CAD 17,000 to 22,000 per year, making it one of the most affordable research universities in the country. Memorial University of Newfoundland charges approximately CAD 11,000 to 20,000. The University of Saskatchewan charges approximately CAD 18,000 to 25,000. These prairie and Atlantic universities offer excellent education at a fraction of what you would pay in Ontario or British Columbia.

At premium research universities, tuition climbs significantly. The University of Toronto charges CAD 28,000 to 58,000 depending on the programme, with engineering and computer science at the top end. McGill University in Montreal charges CAD 20,000 to 35,000. The University of British Columbia charges CAD 22,000 to 45,000. The University of Waterloo, famous for its co-op programmes, charges CAD 30,000 to 52,000 for engineering and computer science.

A bachelor's degree in Canada typically takes four years. Over four years, the total tuition cost ranges from CAD 60,000 at an affordable prairie university to CAD 150,000 or more at a top-tier Ontario institution. However, co-op programmes at Waterloo, Simon Fraser, and others allow you to earn during your degree, which substantially reduces the net cost.

Master's Programmes

International master's tuition ranges from CAD 15,000 to 40,000 per year for most programmes, with MBA programmes being the exception. Research-based master's programmes sometimes include funding through teaching assistantships (TA) and research assistantships (RA) that can cover tuition and provide a monthly stipend. These positions are competitive but widely available at research-intensive universities.

A master's degree at the University of Alberta in engineering costs approximately CAD 16,000 to 22,000 per year, with potential TA funding that can reduce this by CAD 5,000 to 15,000. At McMaster University, a master's in computer science costs approximately CAD 18,000 to 25,000. At Western University, a master's in political science costs approximately CAD 15,000 to 20,000.

Professional master's programmes are more expensive. An MBA at the Rotman School of Management (University of Toronto) costs approximately CAD 65,000 to 75,000 per year for two years. At the Ivey Business School (Western University), it is approximately CAD 55,000 to 65,000. The Schulich School of Business (York University) charges approximately CAD 50,000 to 60,000. Some smaller universities offer MBA programmes in the CAD 20,000 to 35,000 range, including the University of New Brunswick and Vancouver Island University.

PhD Programmes

PhD tuition ranges from CAD 8,000 to 20,000 per year, but most funded PhD positions include full tuition waivers plus an annual stipend of CAD 15,000 to 25,000. Canadian universities actively recruit international PhD students, and funding packages are standard at research-intensive institutions. The Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarship, the most prestigious doctoral award in Canada, provides CAD 50,000 per year for three years and is open to international students.

Exchange Rate and Budgeting for Pakistani Families

The Canadian dollar to Pakistani rupee exchange rate directly affects how affordable Canada is for your family. As of early 2026, the approximate rate is 1 CAD = 195 to 210 PKR. This means:

  • CAD 10,000 tuition = approximately PKR 19.5 to 21 lakh
  • CAD 20,000 living costs = approximately PKR 39 to 42 lakh
  • CAD 20,635 GIC = approximately PKR 40 to 43 lakh

Exchange rate fluctuations matter. The PKR has experienced significant volatility over the past several years. A sudden depreciation can increase your costs by 10 to 15 percent when converted. Smart budgeting strategies include:

  • Lock in tuition early. Many Canadian institutions allow you to pay a deposit months in advance, and tuition is fixed once you are admitted. Paying early can protect against exchange rate movements.
  • Use bank drafts or wire transfers, not informal channels. The State Bank of Pakistan allows remittances for education through official banking channels. Keep all receipts for your study permit financial documentation.
  • Consider the GIC as forced savings. The CAD 20,635 GIC is released monthly after you arrive, providing approximately CAD 1,720 per month for the first year. This covers a significant portion of living costs.
  • Explore education loans. Major Pakistani banks including HBL, MCB, UBL, and Bank Alfalah offer education financing products specifically for study abroad. Some international lenders like Prodigy Finance and MPOWER Finance also serve Pakistani students heading to Canada.
  • Start a savings plan early. Families who begin saving 2 to 3 years before the intended start date have significantly less financial stress during the application process.

How to Pay Tuition: GIC and the Student Direct Stream

The payment process for Pakistani students heading to Canada involves several steps, and understanding the GIC is critical:

The Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC)

The GIC is a mandatory pre-paid investment for Pakistani students applying through the SDS route. Here is exactly how it works:

  1. Choose a participating bank: Scotiabank, CIBC, or ICICI Bank Canada.
  2. Apply for the GIC programme online through the bank's international student portal.
  3. Transfer CAD 20,635 plus any applicable fees (typically CAD 100 to 200) from Pakistan using a bank draft or wire transfer.
  4. The bank issues a GIC certificate, which you include in your SDS study permit application.
  5. After you arrive in Canada and activate your bank account, the funds are released in monthly instalments of approximately CAD 1,720 over 12 months.

The GIC serves two purposes: it proves to IRCC that you have sufficient funds for living costs, and it ensures you have accessible money after arriving. The monthly release schedule prevents you from spending everything upfront.

Direct Tuition Payment

In addition to the GIC, you must pay your first-year tuition before applying through the SDS route. Most Canadian institutions accept international wire transfers through banks like HBL, MCB, UBL, and Standard Chartered. Some institutions also accept credit card payments or flywire, a third-party payment service used by many Canadian universities and colleges.

Payment deadlines vary by institution but are typically between April and June for September intake. Once you pay, the institution issues a receipt and an official acceptance letter, both of which are required for your study permit application.

Regular Stream Financial Requirements

If you apply through the regular study permit stream instead of SDS, you do not need to purchase a GIC or pay tuition upfront. Instead, you must provide evidence of sufficient funds through:

  • Bank statements showing sufficient balance for at least 6 months
  • Sponsor letters from family members with their financial documentation
  • Scholarship award letters
  • Education loan sanction letters from recognized Pakistani or international banks

The regular stream requires you to demonstrate that you can cover tuition plus CAD 20,635 per year for living costs (or CAD 10,325 if studying in Quebec) for yourself and any accompanying family members.

Health Insurance for Pakistani Students in Canada

Healthcare in Canada is publicly funded, but coverage for international students varies by province:

Provinces that cover international students in their provincial health plan:

  • Alberta: Alberta Health Care covers international students from the start. No additional insurance needed.
  • British Columbia: MSP covers international students after a wait period of up to 3 months. Many universities provide interim coverage.
  • Manitoba: Manitoba Health covers international students. No additional insurance needed.
  • Saskatchewan: Saskatchewan Health covers international students.
  • New Brunswick: Covers international students after 3 months.

Provinces that require separate health insurance:

  • Ontario: International students are not covered by OHIP. Most universities and colleges offer mandatory health insurance plans (UHIP, ASC, GHIP) costing CAD 600 to 900 per year. Some allow you to waive the plan if you have equivalent private coverage.
  • Quebec: RAMQ covers international students after they have been in Quebec for more than 6 months. Many universities provide interim coverage through group plans.

In provinces without public coverage, university health plans typically cover doctor visits, hospital stays, emergency care, prescription drugs, and mental health services. Dental and vision are often included at a lower level or require a supplementary plan. The cost of these plans is usually added to your tuition bill automatically.

Regardless of provincial coverage, you should have private insurance from the day you arrive until your provincial coverage activates. Some universities offer arrival health coverage for the first few weeks. Always confirm your coverage status with your institution's international student office before arriving.

Part-Time Work While Studying in Canada

Work rights are one of the strongest financial incentives for Pakistani students choosing Canada:

On-campus work: You can work at any on-campus employer from your first day of classes, with no hour limit. On-campus positions include library assistants, research assistants, teaching assistants, campus bookstore staff, food services, and administrative roles. The average wage for on-campus work is CAD 15 to 20 per hour.

Off-campus work: You can work up to 24 hours per week off campus during regular academic sessions. During scheduled breaks (winter break, summer break, reading week), you can work full-time. Off-campus work includes retail, food service, customer service, warehouse work, and many other sectors. The federal minimum wage applies, but most provinces have higher minimums (Ontario: CAD 16.55, British Columbia: CAD 17.40, Alberta: CAD 15).

Co-op and internships: If your programme includes a mandatory co-op or internship component, you need a co-op work permit in addition to your study permit. This allows full-time work during the placement period. Co-op wages typically range from CAD 15 to 30 per hour depending on your field, with engineering and computer science co-ops paying the most.

Earning potential: At 24 hours per week at CAD 16 per hour, you can earn approximately CAD 1,500 to 2,000 per month during the academic year. During a four-month summer break working full-time, you can earn CAD 6,000 to 10,000. Over a four-year degree, total earnings from part-time and co-op work can reach CAD 30,000 to 60,000, which significantly offsets your total investment.

Important rules: You must have a valid study permit and be enrolled full-time at a Designated Learning Institution to maintain work eligibility. Working more than the permitted hours is a serious violation that can result in loss of status and future inadmissibility. Keep records of your hours and ensure your employer understands the conditions of your work authorization.

Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)

The PGWP is arguably the most valuable benefit of studying in Canada. It allows you to gain Canadian work experience after graduation, which is a critical component of permanent residency applications.

Eligibility: You must have completed a programme of at least 8 months at a Designated Learning Institution. The programme must lead to a degree, diploma, or certificate. You must apply for the PGWP within 180 days of receiving your final transcript or completion letter.

Duration:

  • Programme of 8 months to 2 years: PGWP valid for the length of the programme
  • Programme of 2 years or more: PGWP valid for 3 years
  • Master's programme (1 to 2 years): PGWP valid for 3 years regardless of programme length
  • PhD programme: PGWP valid for 3 years

Key advantages: The PGWP is an open work permit, meaning you can work for any employer in any location in Canada. There is no restriction on your job type or field. This is in contrast to employer-specific work permits, which tie you to a single employer.

Strategic value: Canadian work experience gained through the PGWP is highly valued in permanent residency applications. Under the Express Entry system, one year of skilled Canadian work experience (NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3) earns 40 to 80 CRS points depending on your other profile factors. Combined with your Canadian education, this can be enough to receive an Invitation to Apply (ITA) for permanent residency.

Many Pakistani students use the following pathway: study a 2-year college diploma or university degree, receive a 3-year PGWP, work for 1 to 2 years in their field, and then apply for permanent residency through Express Entry or a Provincial Nominee Programme. This is one of the most reliable immigration pathways available to any international student in the world. For more on immigration pathways, see our study in Canada overview.

Express Entry and Permanent Residency Pathway

Canada's Express Entry system is the primary pathway to permanent residency for skilled workers, and studying in Canada gives you significant advantages in this system.

The Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) scores candidates out of 1,200 points based on age, education, work experience, language ability, and other factors. As of 2026, the minimum CRS score for an ITA has typically ranged from 470 to 510 points.

How Canadian education helps:

  • Canadian education credential: A post-secondary credential of 3 or more years from a Canadian institution earns 15 additional CRS points. A credential of 1 to 2 years earns 30 additional CRS points.
  • Canadian work experience: One year of skilled Canadian work experience earns 40 CRS points (NOC TEER 0 or 1) or 80 CRS points (NOC TEER 2 or 3).
  • Provincial Nominee Programme (PNP): A provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points, virtually guaranteeing an ITA. Many provinces have streams specifically for international graduates, including the Ontario Immigrant Nominee Programme (OINP), the British Columbia PNP, the Alberta Advantage Immigration Programme, and the Manitoba Provincial Nominee Programme.

Typical Pakistani student PR pathway:

  1. Complete a 2-year diploma or 4-year degree at a Canadian institution (15 to 30 CRS points for education)
  2. Obtain a 3-year PGWP and work in Canada for 1 to 2 years (40 to 80 CRS points for work experience)
  3. Apply to a PNP stream for international graduates (600 CRS points if nominated)
  4. Receive an ITA through Express Entry within weeks of nomination
  5. Receive permanent residency within 6 to 12 months

This pathway is well-established and has been successfully used by thousands of Pakistani students. The total timeline from starting your Canadian education to receiving permanent residency is typically 3 to 5 years. For more on visa and immigration, visit our visa guidance page.

Documents and Financial Proof for the Study Permit

Preparing a complete and accurate application is critical. Here are the documents you need:

Core documents:

  • Valid Pakistani passport (valid for at least 6 months beyond your intended stay)
  • Letter of acceptance from a Designated Learning Institution
  • Proof of tuition payment (receipt from the institution)
  • GIC certificate (for SDS applicants) or bank statements showing sufficient funds (for regular stream applicants)
  • Language test results (IELTS, TOEFL, or other accepted tests)
  • Medical examination results from an IRCC-approved panel physician in Pakistan
  • Police clearance certificate from your local police station or the National Database and Registration Authority (NADRA)
  • Two recent passport-sized photographs meeting IRCC specifications
  • Study permit application form (IMM 1294)

Financial documentation for the regular stream:

  • Bank statements for the past 4 to 6 months showing consistent balance above CAD 20,635 plus tuition
  • Sponsor letter if a family member is funding your education, along with their bank statements, employment letter, and tax returns
  • Education loan sanction letter from a recognized bank (HBL, MCB, UBL, Bank Alfalah, or international lenders like Prodigy Finance)
  • Scholarship award letter if applicable

Additional documents that strengthen your application:

  • Statement of purpose explaining why you chose Canada and your specific programme
  • Proof of ties to Pakistan (property ownership, family connections, employment offer for return)
  • Previous academic transcripts and degree certificates
  • Employment letters and pay stubs if you have work experience

The IRCC website has a document checklist tool that generates a personalized list based on your specific situation. Use it to ensure you submit everything required. Incomplete applications are a common cause of delays and refusals. For a step-by-step walkthrough, see our visa guidance page.

Application Timeline: Month-by-Month for September Intake

Planning ahead is essential. A well-organized timeline prevents last-minute stress and strengthens your application. Here is a detailed month-by-month guide for September 2027 intake:

18 months before (March 2026): Research Canadian institutions and programmes. Begin IELTS preparation. Identify 3 to 5 institutions to apply to. Start saving and planning finances with your family.

15 months before (June 2026): Take the IELTS test. A score of 6.0 with no band below 6.0 qualifies you for the SDS route. If you score below this, consider retaking the test or applying through the regular stream.

12 months before (September 2026): Submit applications to Canadian institutions. Most college and university applications for September intake open in the fall. Apply through the institution's direct portal or through Education in Canada. Submit your transcripts, English test scores, statement of purpose, and recommendation letters.

10 months before (November 2026): Receive offer letters. Compare offers based on tuition, location, programme content, and career outcomes. Accept your offer and pay any required deposit (typically CAD 500 to 2,000, which is deducted from your tuition).

8 months before (January 2027): Begin the GIC process. Open an account with Scotiabank, CIBC, or ICICI Bank Canada's international student programme. Transfer CAD 20,635 from Pakistan through your bank. This process takes 2 to 4 weeks.

7 months before (February 2027): Pay first-year tuition. Use a bank wire transfer through HBL, MCB, or UBL. Keep the payment receipt. Some institutions accept payment through flywire or other online services.

6 months before (March 2027): Apply for the SDS study permit. Submit your application online through the IRCC portal. Upload all required documents. Pay the study permit fee (CAD 150) and biometrics fee (CAD 85).

5 months before (April 2027): Complete your biometrics appointment at the Visa Application Centre in Islamabad, Karachi, or Lahore. Bring your passport and biometric instruction letter.

4 months before (May 2027): Complete the medical examination at an IRCC-approved panel physician. The examination includes a physical exam, chest X-ray, and blood tests. Results are sent directly to IRCC.

3 months before (June 2027): Receive your study permit decision (if applying via SDS, this should arrive within 20 calendar days of a complete application). Book your flight. Begin searching for accommodation near your institution.

2 months before (July 2027): Book accommodation. Apply for on-campus residence (deadlines vary) or search for off-campus housing through platforms like Kijiji, Facebook Marketplace, and university housing boards. Join Pakistani student WhatsApp and Facebook groups at your institution for housing advice.

1 month before (August 2027): Attend pre-departure orientation if offered by your institution or by the Canadian High Commission in Pakistan. Prepare your arrival logistics: pack essential items, arrange airport pickup, and familiarize yourself with your city's transit system. Exchange some CAD for immediate expenses upon arrival.

Arrival (September 2027): Activate your GIC account at a Canadian bank branch. Obtain your Social Insurance Number (SIN) for work eligibility. Attend international student orientation at your institution. Set up your phone, transit card, and other essentials.

Tips to Save Money as a Pakistani Student in Canada

Reducing your costs while studying in Canada is possible with smart planning:

  • Choose a smaller city. Winnipeg, Halifax, Saskatoon and Moncton offer 30 to 50 percent lower rent than Toronto or Vancouver. Even a mid-sized city like Edmonton or Calgary saves thousands per year compared to Ontario or BC.
  • Share accommodation. Living with roommates reduces rent from CAD 1,500+ per month to CAD 600 to 900. Many Pakistani students form shared housing groups before arriving.
  • Cook at home. A meal at a restaurant costs CAD 15 to 25. Cooking Pakistani food at home costs CAD 5 to 8 per meal. Grocery stores like No Frills, Food Basics, Walmart and local ethnic grocery stores offer affordable options.
  • Buy used textbooks. Purchase used textbooks from senior students, university bookstores, or online platforms like Amazon.ca and AbeBooks. Many courses also use free open-source textbooks.
  • Use student discounts. Your student ID unlocks discounts on transit, software (including free Microsoft Office and Adobe), gym memberships, and entertainment. Many retailers offer student pricing year-round.
  • Apply for scholarships. Even small scholarships of CAD 1,000 to 2,000 add up. Apply to every award you are eligible for. Visit our scholarships page for current opportunities.
  • Start working early. On-campus jobs are available from day one. Build relationships with professors to secure research or teaching assistant positions, which pay CAD 18 to 25 per hour and provide relevant experience.

Choosing Between College and University: Which Is Right for You?

This is one of the most important decisions Pakistani students face. Both options have clear advantages:

Choose a college if: you want lower tuition (CAD 10,000 to 18,000 per year), shorter programmes (1 to 2 years), practical training with co-op placements, lower English requirements, or a stepping stone to university transfer. Colleges are also the better choice if your CGPA is below 2.5 and university admission is challenging. Seneca, Humber, Conestoga, George Brown, and Centennial are the most popular choices among Pakistani students.

Choose a university if: you want a bachelor's or master's degree (higher prestige and earning potential), research opportunities, access to larger scholarship pools, a 3-year PGWP for master's and doctoral programmes, or professional licensing in fields like engineering, medicine or law. The University of Toronto, UBC, McGill, Waterloo, Alberta, Western, Queen's, and McMaster are the most popular universities among Pakistani students.

The college-to-university transfer pathway is increasingly popular. You complete a 2-year diploma at a college (at lower tuition), gain Canadian work experience, and then transfer to a university for a bachelor's degree with credit for your diploma. Some institutions like Seneca and York University or Humber and Lakehead University have formal transfer agreements. This pathway can save CAD 15,000 to 30,000 compared to four years at a university from the start.

Final Thoughts

Studying in Canada from Pakistan is a life-changing investment that requires careful financial planning, but it is far more accessible than many students assume. The combination of relatively affordable tuition (especially at colleges and prairie universities), generous work rights, and a clear path to permanent residency makes Canada the most strategic choice for many Pakistani families. The total first-year investment of CAD 30,000 to 60,000 is significant, but when you factor in part-time earnings, co-op income, and the long-term earning potential of a Canadian credential plus Canadian work experience, the return on investment is substantial.

The key steps are straightforward: choose the right programme for your budget and goals, prepare a strong SDS or regular stream application, budget realistically, and take advantage of the work and scholarship opportunities available to you. Whether you are an engineer targeting the University of Waterloo, a business student heading to McGill, or a college student starting at Seneca, the pathway from Pakistan to Canada to career to permanent residency is well-documented and achievable. Start planning today, and make the numbers work for your future.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost for Pakistani students to study in Canada in 2026?+

Total first-year costs range from CAD 30,000 to 60,000 depending on programme and city. This includes tuition (CAD 10,000 to 40,000), living expenses (CAD 10,000 to 20,000), health insurance (CAD 600 to 900), and visa-related costs including the GIC of CAD 20,635 for the SDS route. Budget-conscious students who choose college diplomas in smaller cities can bring the total closer to CAD 30,000, while students at top-tier universities in Toronto or Vancouver may spend CAD 55,000 to 60,000 in their first year.

What is the SDS programme and do Pakistani students qualify?+

The Student Direct Stream (SDS) is an expedited study permit processing route that guarantees a decision within 20 calendar days. Pakistani students qualify if they have an acceptance letter from a Designated Learning Institution, purchase a Guaranteed Investment Certificate of CAD 20,635 from Scotiabank, CIBC, or ICICI Bank Canada, pay first-year tuition in full, and meet language requirements (IELTS 6.0 overall with no band below 6.0, in either academic or general training). The SDS route is faster and more predictable than the regular stream, which takes 8 to 12 weeks from Pakistan.

Can I work while studying in Canada as a Pakistani student?+

Yes. International students can work up to 24 hours per week off campus during regular academic sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks (winter break, summer break, reading week). On-campus work has no hour limit and is available from your first day of classes. If your programme includes a mandatory co-op or internship, you need a co-op work permit for full-time work during the placement. Part-time earnings of CAD 1,500 to 2,000 per month during term and CAD 6,000 to 10,000 during summer breaks significantly offset your living costs.

What scholarships are available for Pakistani students in Canada?+

Major scholarships include the Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarships (CAD 50,000 per year for 3 years for doctoral students), the Lester B. Pearson Scholarship at the University of Toronto (fully funded for 4 years), the Trudeau Foundation Scholarships (up to CAD 60,000 per year for doctoral students), and university-specific entrance scholarships ranging from CAD 2,000 to 40,000. Colleges like Seneca and Humber also offer entrance awards of CAD 1,000 to 5,000. The key is to apply early and to as many awards as possible. Visit our scholarships page for a comprehensive list.

How long does the Canada student visa take from Pakistan?+

Via the SDS route, decisions are guaranteed within 20 calendar days of a complete application. The regular study permit stream takes 8 to 12 weeks from Pakistan, though processing times can vary based on application volume and completeness. To ensure the fastest processing, apply through SDS if you meet the requirements, submit all documents in a complete application, and complete your biometrics and medical examination promptly. Avoiding incomplete applications is the most important factor in getting a fast decision.

What is the GIC requirement for the Canada study permit?+

The Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) is a mandatory pre-paid investment of CAD 20,635 with a participating Canadian bank (Scotiabank, CIBC, or ICICI Bank Canada) for students applying through the SDS route. You transfer the funds from Pakistan before applying for your study permit. After arriving in Canada and activating your bank account, the funds are released in monthly instalments of approximately CAD 1,720 over 12 months to help cover your living expenses. The GIC is not required for the regular study permit stream, where you provide bank statements or other financial documentation instead.

Which Canadian cities are cheapest for Pakistani students?+

Winnipeg, Halifax, Saskatoon, Moncton, and Sherbrooke offer the lowest living costs in Canada. Monthly total expenses for a student sharing accommodation in these cities range from CAD 1,000 to 1,600, compared to CAD 1,650 to 2,500 in Toronto or Vancouver. Edmonton and Calgary are also more affordable than Ontario or British Columbia, with the added benefit of Alberta's no-sales-tax environment and free provincial health insurance for international students. Montreal offers big-city amenities at significantly lower cost than Toronto, with rent 30 to 40 percent cheaper.

How does studying in Canada help with permanent residency?+

Studying in Canada gives you multiple advantages for permanent residency. A Canadian post-secondary credential earns 15 to 30 additional CRS points in the Express Entry system. A post-graduation work permit allows you to gain Canadian work experience worth 40 to 80 additional CRS points. A Provincial Nominee Programme nomination adds 600 points, virtually guaranteeing an Invitation to Apply. The typical pathway is: 2-year diploma or 4-year degree, 3-year PGWP with 1 to 2 years of work, PNP nomination or direct Express Entry application, and permanent residency within 3 to 5 years of starting your Canadian education. Thousands of Pakistani students have successfully completed this pathway.
