Immigrate to France: The 2026 Talent & Settlement Guide

France's main skilled route is the Talent card (renamed from "Talent Passport"): a 4-year work+residence permit for qualified employees earning ≥1.5× SMIC, with no labour-market test and no French required initially. Around it sit the EU Blue Card, an entrepreneur route, family reunification, and a citizenship system that just got harder (B2 French + a new civic exam).

The French System in One Paragraph

France is employer-friendly for the highly skilled and otherwise restrictive. If you have a French job offer paying ≥1.5× SMIC (≈€39,582/yr) and a master's degree or equivalent, the Talent route gets you a 4-year renewable card with your family's work rights included and no labour-market test. Settlement comes after ~5 years of residence (10-year carte de résident and/or naturalisation), and since January 2026 citizenship needs B2 French plus a 40-question civic exam.

Biggest leverage for graduates: a French master's (or 5 years' experience) qualifies you for the Talent and Blue Card routes — and a French degree earns a reduced path to naturalisation (2 years instead of 5). Studying in France is genuinely the fastest door to French permanent life.

Self-Employment & Entrepreneurship

Two flavours: the VLS-TS entrepreneur/profession libérale (1 year, renewable into a multi-year card) for freelancers, and the Talent – Business creation route (invest ≥€30,000 + master's/5 yrs experience, 4 years, family from day one). Since June 2025 the entrepreneur route needs a prior opinion on economic viability from the foreign-workforce service, plus an EU/ACPR-approved bank account and evidence of business premises.

  • Micro-entrepreneur registration via URSSAF is free and online once you arrive (you get a SIRET number)
  • No dedicated "digital nomad" visa exists — freelancers use the entrepreneur route
  • No family derivative on the basic entrepreneur route — family reunification after 18 months, or use Talent – Business creation

Immigration FAQs

Do I need to speak French to work in France?+

Not to get in — the Talent and Blue Card routes require no French for the initial visa. You'll need A2 at your first multi-year renewal and B1+ toward permanent residence/citizenship. In practice, English-only roles exist mainly in tech, research and finance; French dramatically widens your options.

Is the EU Blue Card or Talent route better?+

Talent has the lower bar (€39,582 + master's, no labour-market test) and is the default for most professionals. The Blue Card requires €59,373 but adds intra-EU mobility after 12 months — valuable if you might move within Europe. Both count toward the same 5-year settlement clock.

Can I work remotely in France as a digital nomad?+

There's no dedicated digital-nomad visa. Freelancers use the entrepreneur/profession libérale route, which since June 2025 requires a favourable economic-viability opinion plus bank-account and premises evidence. For employees, Talent or Blue Card sponsorship is the standard path.

How long does French citizenship take?+

Plan 18–24 months from application (official deadline is 18 months, faster if you've resided 10+ years). The bar is now B2 French + the new NAT civic exam + stable income. Meeting the criteria doesn't guarantee approval — naturalisation is discretionary.

Does studying in France speed up settlement?+

Yes. A French master's reduces the naturalisation residence requirement from 5 to 2 years, and student time counts toward the 5-year residence requirement at reduced weight. Graduates also find the Talent route easy to meet. This makes France one of the best countries for the study-to-settlement path.
