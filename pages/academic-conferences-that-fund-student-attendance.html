Academic Conferences That Fund Student Travel

🗓️ Aug 5, 2026⏱️ 9 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Academic conferences are where research gets seen, networks get built, and opportunities get created, but the cost of registration, flights, and hotels keeps many students away. The good news is that a surprising number of conferences fund student attendance directly. Between student travel grants, fee waivers, registration discounts, and volunteer programs, most students can attend at least one major conference a year without paying full price.

This guide covers the best-known conferences and programmes that fund student attendance, and, just as importantly, the funding mechanisms that exist inside almost every conference. The strategy is simple: find a conference that accepts your work, get your paper or poster accepted, and apply for the student support that most organisers provide.

Key takeaways

  • IEEE and ACM both run student travel grant programmes that support conference attendance across dozens of technical fields.
  • Many major conferences, including NeurIPS, ICML, ACL, CHI, and SIGGRAPH, offer dedicated student travel grants and volunteer registration discounts.
  • Field-specific meetings such as AGU, ASCB, and large medical conferences fund student travel through society grants and competitive awards.
  • University sources, supervisor grants, and national funding bodies fill the gaps when a conference itself has no student fund.
  • Apply as early as possible: grants are usually announced months ahead and close before the conference begins.

Conferences with strong student travel funding

Computer science and AI

The AI and machine learning community is the most generous when it comes to funding students. NeurIPS and ICML run major travel award programmes that cover registration, flights, and accommodation for selected students, with special programmes for underrepresented groups. ACL (computational linguistics) offers the same model, and CHI (human-computer interaction) and SIGGRAPH (computer graphics) fund student volunteers and travel. The IEEE and ACM student travel grant programmes are broader, supporting any accepted paper at an affiliated conference.

Engineering and applied sciences

IEEE conferences across power, electronics, communications, and robotics routinely offer student travel grants, often funded by IEEE societies such as the Computer Society and the Communications Society. ACM similarly funds students attending its flagship conferences. In many cases you need only an accepted paper and a faculty recommendation to qualify.

Natural sciences and medicine

The American Geophysical Union (AGU) supports student and early-career travel to its fall meeting. The American Society for Cell Biology (ASCB) funds student travel with competitive awards. Large medical and public health conferences frequently offer trainee travel awards and registration fee waivers for first authors, so check the funding page of any conference in your discipline before assuming you cannot afford it.

How student funding at conferences actually works

When a conference lists a travel grant, the mechanism is usually one of four types:

  • Registration fee waiver. Your registration is reduced or eliminated, which saves the biggest single expense.
  • Fixed travel award. You receive a set amount, often a few hundred to over a thousand dollars, toward flights, hotels, and food.
  • Reimbursement. You pay costs up front and claim them back after the conference with receipts.
  • Volunteer programme. You work a few hours at the event and your registration fee is waived or heavily discounted.

Most competitive grants require an accepted paper or poster and expect you to present it, so acceptance comes first and funding second.

Where to find the funding

1. The conference itself

Almost every major conference has a travel grant or student support page. Search the conference site for words like "travel grant", "student travel", "fee waiver", and "diversity support".

2. Professional societies

IEEE and ACM run the two largest student travel grant systems in the technical world. National academies and field societies (such as the Royal Society or your national physics, chemistry, or medical societies) also fund student conference travel.

3. Your university

Graduate schools, departments, and faculty research grants are a major source of conference funding. Most PhD supervisors have travel money in their grants and simply need to be asked with a clear budget.

4. National bodies and foundations

Some national research agencies and non-profit foundations fund student conference attendance in specific fields. These tend to be competitive and aligned with research priorities.

How to apply for a conference travel grant

  • Get accepted first. Apply to the conference, and use the acceptance letter in your grant application.
  • Start early. Travel grant deadlines are often two to three months before the conference.
  • Prepare a one-page case. State your accepted paper or poster, your costs, and how attending benefits your research; combine this with the advice in our profile guide.
  • Ask your supervisor. Faculty grants are the most flexible funding of all; a short request with a realistic budget often succeeds.
  • Apply to several sources. You can combine a fee waiver with a university grant, and if two sources over-fund you, ask one to redirect the balance.

Conference papers lead to more funding

Presenting at a conference is itself a strong item on a scholarship application. Reviewers look for students who already operate at research level, so a conference presentation strengthens your case for major awards like the ones on our scholarship results page. If you are new to research, start with the poster track or a regional conference, then work up to flagship events, and let each presentation fund the next.

Frequently asked questions

Can students really get conferences to pay for attendance?+

Yes. Major conference organisers, professional societies such as IEEE and ACM, and many universities offer travel grants, fee waivers, and registration discounts specifically for students, especially those presenting papers or posters.

Do I need to present a paper to get conference funding?+

Not always. Many student travel grants require a paper, poster, or presentation, but some cover general attendees, and volunteer programs trade registration fees for help with the event.

What do conference travel grants typically cover?+

Awards vary widely: some cover the full registration fee, others reimburse flights and hotel costs, and many provide a fixed dollar amount (for example, a few hundred to over a thousand dollars) toward total costs.

Where do I find conference travel grants?+

Start with the conference's own website, then check professional societies, your department and graduate school, your supervisor's research grants, and national funding bodies in your field.

When should I apply for conference funding?+

As soon as you know your paper was accepted. Most grants open months before the event and close four to eight weeks before it, so apply early and keep the acceptance letter handy.
