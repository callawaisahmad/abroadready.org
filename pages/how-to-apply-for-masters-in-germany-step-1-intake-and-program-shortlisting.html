Masters in germany intake and shortlisting: the decision before every document

Most applicants searching how to apply for a masters in germany want a form, a portal link or a checklist. What they need first is a decision, because the intake you choose sets every deadline, translation and appointment that follows.

This guide covers what most people call Step 1 of the process: the intake decision, the longlist, and the tiered shortlist you will genuinely submit to.

Get the intake right and the rest is administration. Get it vague and you lose a full cycle to programmes that closed while you were still deciding, because German deadlines are early and competitive courses are assessed in rounds.

Germany is also a federal system, with 16 states and hundreds of universities, each running its own portal, deadline habits and transcript expectations. There is no national admissions office. The shortlist you build yourself from public databases is the piece most applicants give a week when it deserves a month.

This is the masters in germany complete guide to intake planning: winter against summer, the real deadlines, DAAD and Hochschulkompass, a five to eight university shortlist in three tiers, and the spreadsheet that holds it together. The method works anywhere, whether you are comparing masters in germany from usa, masters in germany from asia or masters in germany from pakistan.

It works the same whether your target is an mphil in germany, an ms in germany, or a thesis-heavy research master's. The intake calendar is set by the university, not by the degree title, so the shortlisting work barely changes between them.

What the intake decision covers in a masters in germany application

The three deliverables, and what this stage excludes

This stage has three deliverables and nothing else. An intake decision: winter semester, summer semester, or one of the small number of rolling programmes. A longlist of roughly fifteen to thirty programmes meeting your subject requirements. And a tiered shortlist of five to eight you will actually submit.

None of this includes writing a letter, gathering transcripts, booking a test or applying for a visa. If you are writing a motivation letter in February for a programme that closed the previous January, you did not do the intake work. You skipped it, and you paid for it with a year.

That is also why a masters in germany guide that opens with document checklists is often read at exactly the wrong moment. Documents are cheap to start and impossible to apply for once the portal has closed.

How much time the shortlisting stage deserves

Budget three to four weeks of real work, spread across two or three months. Week one is intake research and database setup, week two is longlist building, week three is eligibility screening against official pages, week four is tiering plus the tracking sheet.

If you are still weighing whether to study masters in germany at all, this stage is the cheapest possible test of that decision. Two weeks of structured searching will tell you more than a year of vague research.

Winter intake masters in germany vs summer intake masters in germany: making the call

Can I start a masters in germany in summer?

Yes, but know what you are choosing. Germany runs two semesters: the winter semester, abbreviated WS or Wintersemester, and the summer semester, SS or Sommersemester. Winter is the main intake. Summer is real, but far fewer master's programmes run in English.

In many subjects nothing in English starts in April. What does exist in summer tends to be German-taught continuation degrees, teacher training programmes, part-time formats, and a handful of English-taught courses in business and some STEM fields.

If summer is your only realistic option, that is fine, and the shortlist gets smaller rather than worse. But choosing between an English-taught winter programme at a strong university and a German-taught summer programme at a similar one deserves more thought, because language is a hard gate.

German-taught master's programmes usually want C1, not B2. That is a year of language study folded silently into your timeline, and it is the most common reason a summer shortlist quietly collapses in February.

Official semester dates and lecture periods

The winter semester runs officially from 1 October to 31 March. The summer semester runs from 1 April to 30 September. Those are administrative semesters, not teaching periods, and confusing the two is how people arrive a month late.

Lecture periods sit inside them and vary by university. Winter lectures usually start in the second half of October and finish in mid-February. Summer lectures begin around mid-April and end in mid-July.

Which intake should I choose if I want a DAAD scholarship?

Target the winter semester, almost certainly. Most DAAD funding lines, including the popular development-related postgraduate courses and university-administered scholarships, are built around the winter cycle, and far fewer routes exist for an April start.

Scholarship deadlines fall earlier than admission deadlines, not later, so treat the scholarship deadline as your real deadline. Our guide to DAAD scholarships for Germany covers which lines Pakistani students can use, and how to study in Germany for free covers the cost side.

For the country-level picture, study in Germany from Pakistan 2026 is the right starting point. Funding, not academics, is what quietly eliminates most otherwise strong shortlists.

Year-round and every-other-year programmes

Both exist in small numbers, and both are misunderstood. Some professional master's programmes, particularly part-time and continuing-education formats, accept applications year-round with several start dates, and a few joint degrees run on a rolling basis.

More disruptive is the every-other-year programme, offered by a few specialist degrees in fields with heavy infrastructure plus some part-time variants. The page says applications for winter 2027 are accepted and says nothing at all about 2028.

If that is your target, the shortlisting stage changes completely. You are planning around a fixed launch year, so decide whether a gap year is acceptable and check whether an equivalent course runs the following year before you invest anything.

When does summer intake start in germany, and when do deadlines actually fall?

Is the 15 July deadline real, or just a guideline?

It is real for the large majority of programmes with no earlier date of their own, and in most states binding for courses with restricted admission. But calling it the German deadline is the most common error applicants make.

15 July is a framework, not a fact about your programme. Faculties publish earlier dates and are entitled to, so you will meet 15 May, 31 March and 1 December on real programme pages, plus rolling admissions where the only deadline is when the programme is full.

Is 15 July a target or a cliff?

A cliff. Applications are assessed in rounds, and in restricted subjects the later you apply the worse your chances, because better-prepared candidates apply first and are assessed first.

If your goal is a competitive programme, your personal deadline is the day applications open, or within a fortnight of it. Write that date into the spreadsheet as your submission date and treat the published deadline as an outer limit you will never approach.

What happens if you miss 15 July?

For restricted-admission programmes you are usually finished for that cycle. For open-admission programmes, called zulassungsfrei or nc-frei in German, you can often still apply, because the course is not capacity-constrained and admits anyone meeting the formal requirements.

Many universities keep those portals open through late summer, and some run a waiting list or a lottery for oversubscribed courses. Missing the deadline is survivable only for programmes that were never competitive anyway, which is exactly why the safety tier matters.

The most common German deadline mistakes

Assuming a time zone you are not in comes first. German portals usually close at 23:59 or sometimes 12:00 CET, and a late-night Karachi submission can land a full day behind without any warning.

Working backwards from your earliest deadline

Work backwards from your earliest deadline, not your target one, because the earliest decides whether you have time to fix a problem. A missing reference letter takes a week to rescue. A missing transcript can take a month.

Searching for german universities for masters with DAAD and Hochschulkompass

Hochschulkompass first, then the DAAD database

Start with Hochschulkompass for the authoritative list, then use the DAAD international programmes database for the international-student view and the funding information. The order matters less than finishing on the university's own page.

The DAAD database, reachable from daad.de under Studying in Germany, is built for applicants from outside Germany. It is narrower than Hochschulkompass, but it shows what you need on one card: language, intake, deadline, application process and entry requirements.

That layout makes it the fastest triage tool, and it is where a german universities shortlisting normally begins. Always finish on the programme page itself, because that is what counts when a deadline is disputed.

Building the official longlist in Hochschulkompass

Hochschulkompass is the official portal for German higher education and holds the complete set of degree programmes in the country. At hochschulkompass.de you can filter by degree type, subject, state, language of instruction, admission type and start of the degree programme.

Use the advanced filters rather than free text, because free-text search in German will not find an English description of a German-taught programme. Set the degree type to master's, set the subject using the discipline you want rather than the department name you assume exists, and set language to English if you are not German fluent.

Third-party tools, and how long the longlist should be

Aggregators and ranking sites are useful for one thing: showing you what else exists. They are dangerous for three. Rankings of German master's programmes are weak, because most are built on research output rather than teaching quality.

Aggregator listings go out of date, showing last year's deadline or a discontinued course. And tuition-free pages rarely distinguish states that charge international students from those that do not, which matters directly if you are building a list of german universities for masters on price.

How do I build a german universities shortlisting of 5 to 8 options in three tiers?

Why five to eight applications, and not twenty?

Because each serious German application costs hours. A motivation letter written properly is a focused afternoon, a customised certified and translated document pack is a week of logistics, and reference management adds more on top.

Five to eight well-executed applications, aimed at programmes where your profile sits inside the admitted range, beat twenty scattershot ones. A list of three leaves you with nothing at all if all three refuse.

The dream tier: one or two low-probability bets

One or two programmes where you sit below the typical admitted range, or where competition is fierce, but the fit is so good that the payoff justifies the odds. Apply expecting rejection, and do not emotionally rehearse acceptance.

The realistic tier: three or four that should work

Three or four programmes, and this is the heart of the list. The average admitted applicant's profile looks like yours, the language and degree requirements are clearly satisfied, and the deadline is achievable with your document pipeline.

Test each candidate honestly. If the university publishes its admitted range, are you inside the lower bound, the middle, or above it? Is the required subject credit actually printed on your transcript, or only described in your syllabus?

The safety tier: one or two near-certain options

One or two programmes where admission is close to certain and the budget works. In Germany this has a particular flavour: open-admission programmes, often at smaller or less internationally known universities.

The main test is whether you hold a relevant bachelor's degree with a suitable grade and can prove language proficiency. They are not consolation prizes, and a German public university degree is a degree.

But an open-admission programme must not be a money trap. Open admission does not mean affordable if the course sits in a high-cost state or city, which is exactly where research into low fee universities in germany for masters pays for itself.

Deciding between two programmes you both want

Score them on the same five criteria, in the same order, with the same weights, and let the total break the tie. Never break a tie on prestige alone, because prestige is the criterion that most reliably turns a good application into a wasted one.

If two still tie, ask which one you would attend if money were free. A useful backup is a second programme at the same institution, and some faculties will consider an application to one programme for another if you ask early and in writing.

Matching your background to requirements for masters in germany

Grade conversion and the Eignungsfeststellungsverfahren

Many programmes convert your grade onto the German scale, where 1.0 is best and 5.0 is worst, using a published formula, and some use the raw percentage against a stated minimum. Where a minimum is set, it means it.

Pakistani students should expect a degree certificate, a transcript showing subjects and credit hours, and often a professor's statement describing relative performance, because there is no universal national scale to convert from. If your result sits near a threshold, ask the admissions office in writing before investing in the application.

Many programmes also run a formal suitability assessment, the Eignungsfeststellungsverfahren, combining a grade-based score with a short aptitude questionnaire and sometimes an interview.

Do I need work experience, and does my degree field have to match?

For a straight-from-undergraduate applicant, relevant work experience is a differentiator, not a gate. What matters is specificity, not length: what you did, with what data or equipment, and what changed because of it.

An internship, a research assistantship, a final-year project that produced something, or documented volunteering all count. Our resume versus CV guide covers the format, and the motivation letter guide shows how to turn a thin experience section into an argument.

On subject match, Germany is stricter than the US or UK. Programme pages often distinguish degrees that must be fachlich verwandt, meaning subject-related, from those that must be fachlich einschlägig, meaning subject-specifically relevant. Those are not the same requirement.

If your background is adjacent rather than identical, use a logical transition story and demonstrate subject knowledge through coursework or thesis. Some universities will examine your existing credits for equivalence when you ask, which sometimes converts a rejection into an offer for the price of one email.

uni-assist, the central service for restricted programmes

uni-assist is the central application service used by many universities for programmes with restricted admission. It handles the preliminary review of your documents, grades and language certificates on behalf of each institution.

If a programme says application via uni-assist, you cannot apply directly to the university, and a direct application there does nothing at all. Registration is at uni-assist.de.

You submit separately to each university, each with its own handling fee: roughly EUR 75 for the first university and about EUR 30 for each additional one. A six-programme list therefore means six submissions and six separate fees.

Checking the language of instruction for a masters in germany

If the programme says English, is it really taught in English?

Usually, but not always, and the difference matters more than students expect. Some officially English-taught programmes hold examinations in German or offer a portion of modules in German.

Some master's programmes are German-taught with a minority of English modules, which is fine if you plan a life in Germany and a serious problem if you do not. In a German-taught programme your thesis supervisor, your internship and your first job will all be in German.

Check three things: the language of the taught modules, the language of the examination, and the language expected for the master's thesis. If any of the three is German and you are not fluent, either plan to become fluent through a structured course or drop the programme.

Language requirements: German levels, IELTS and TOEFL

For German-taught master's programmes, C1 is the usual expectation and B2 is occasionally accepted where a language component is built in. The certificate normally must be a recognised one, such as a Goethe-Zertifikat, a telc certificate or a university language certificate.

It also has to be no older than two years at application. If you are starting from zero, an A1 or A2 course is a better use of six months than a desperate application. Studying abroad without IELTS covers the German route, and IELTS scores by country sets out what Germany expects.

Book the English test in your first month of shortlisting, not after you have decided where to apply, because availability in Pakistan can be tight and a result you already hold turns a shortlist from a projection into facts.

Typical requirements are IELTS 6.5 overall with 6.0 or 6.5 per component, and TOEFL iBT 80 to 95, with competitive programmes asking for 7.0 or above. Take the target from the programme page, not from a country table. Our TOEFL versus IELTS guide explains the differences in how speaking and writing are assessed.

Many universities now also accept the Duolingo English Test, but the list is institution-specific, and a test a university accepts is not always one the visa authority accepts. Most guidance written for a master in germany for international students audience treats language as a checkbox; it is a hard gate on your entire list.

For masters in germany for international students with no English test at all, the German-taught route is often faster than a test-prep sprint. Test the waters in a language course before committing either way.

Low fee universities in germany for masters: the filters that should decide your shortlist

Is tuition really free, and what does the semester contribution include?

Public universities in most states charge no tuition for standard degree programmes, and the country's free-study reputation rests on that. Tuition free universities in germany for masters is an accurate description for the large majority of public institutions.

The important exception is Baden-Württemberg, which charges non-EU and non-EEA international students about EUR 1,500 per semester. A few public institutions elsewhere also levy international tuition, particularly in art, music and some professional fields.

Verify fee status on the programme page, because Germany is free is a generalisation rather than a fact about your application. Our cost and budget guide sets out what else to budget, and study abroad loans in Pakistan covers financing a gap.

The semester contribution, or Semesterbeitrag, is what every enrolled student pays each semester, and it bundles administration with the semester ticket, the local transport pass. In most states that pass covers the regional transport association, which across a large state can mean unlimited trains, buses, trams and metro for the full semester.

Expect roughly EUR 150 to EUR 390 depending on the university and state, and treat it as compulsory, because enrolment is not complete until you have paid it. Budget it as a fixed cost in your shortlist scoring, not as a surprise in October.

Do the city, the state and my funding options change the choice?

City and state decide how useful the international office actually is, and support varies enormously between them. If part-time work is part of your plan, see part-time jobs for international students, and for the longer view the post-study work visa comparison.

Work rules matter for budget planning: students are generally allowed 20 hours a week, or 140 full days a year, and the minimum wage sits around EUR 13.90 an hour. Health insurance for under-30s typically costs EUR 128 to EUR 140, and it sits on top of the semester fee rather than inside it.

Scholarships change the ranking of your options more than the list itself, so note before tiering which realistic-tier programmes are scholarship-eligible and where self-funding is comfortable.

A slightly weaker academic fit that is fully funded can beat a stronger one you cannot afford, and some German funding is not a scholarship at all but a research assistantship. The DAAD scholarship guide and our scholarships for Pakistani students overview both belong here.

Setting alerts, and what to do when a new programme appears

Set reminders at thirty days, fourteen days, seven days and forty-eight hours before the official cut-off, and treat your real submission date as a week earlier than the deadline. For competitive programmes, add the portal opening date as a milestone with an alert two days before it opens.

When a new programme appears mid-search, apply the brief you wrote for the longlist, not a new and more enthusiastic one. Check the deadline first, then the academic requirements, then the language, then the cost.

If it passes all four and the deadline is at least six weeks away, add it and tier it properly. If the deadline is closer, add it only if it is open-admission. Set a hard final date for the shortlist, or you will never stop adding.

Common mistakes in a masters in germany shortlist

Confusing the course start date with the application deadline

Students read winter semester 2027 and assume they can apply in 2027. They cannot. A winter 2027 intake has an application deadline in the first half of 2026, often earlier, because the university needs time to decide and to issue confirmations so students can apply for the visa.

Shortlisting by ranking and nothing else

Ranking reflects research reputation, which is not teaching quality, student support or fit with your career. A mid-ranked programme with a live industry placement and an international office that answers emails may suit you far better than a top-ranked one where nobody replies.

The subtler mistake is letting the list grow indefinitely. Every extra programme is ten to twenty more hours of work and a weaker letter for all of them, so growth is a real cost rather than a safety margin.

What happens after the shortlist stage?

The document list, and the visa timeline after you apply

Assume you will need your bachelor's certificate and full transcript, a passport valid beyond your course, a detailed module handbook for the target programme, a language certificate, a motivation letter, one or two academic references, a CV in the German academic format, a passport photograph, and certified plus translated documents where required.

For a full-time degree you will also need proof of funds for the residence permit, usually a blocked account covering the study period plus a buffer. For 2026 that is EUR 11,904, which works out at EUR 992 a month, and the figure is updated annually by the federal government.

On top of that sits the visa itself: a EUR 75 visa fee, and a processing period that realistically runs 6-12 weeks. That is Step 2 territory, but booking the blocked account and the APS appointment early is what keeps the shortlist you built on schedule. Our blocked account and proof of funds guide covers the money side.

For Pakistani applicants, plan around a document verification process run by the academic verification office. It must be booked as an appointment rather than walked into, and the APS certificate costs around PKR 35,000 to 40,000 with four to six weeks of normal processing, or eight to ten weeks at peak.

Frequently asked questions about winter and summer intake in Germany

What intakes do German universities have for masters programmes?

Germany has two standard intakes: a winter semester starting in October, and a summer semester starting in April. Winter is the main intake and carries the large majority of programmes, especially the English-taught degrees international applicants want.

Summer intake is real but smaller, with far fewer English-taught options, concentrated in business, teacher education and some STEM fields. A small number of programmes admit every other year only.

For anyone counting on DAAD funding, the choice comes down to a wide October market against a narrow April one. For everyone else weighing winter intake masters in germany options against summer intake masters in germany options, winter is the default and summer is the exception.

When are the application deadlines for German universities?

The standard framework is 15 July for the following winter semester and 15 January for the summer semester. But many competitive programmes close three to six months earlier than that.

Some publish entirely different dates, such as 15 May, 31 March or 1 February. Restricted-admission programmes running through uni-assist add their own earlier cut-off on top of the university's.

How do I find masters programmes in Germany?

Use two official sources together. The DAAD international programmes database at daad.de is built for international applicants and shows language, intake, deadline and application process in one place, which makes it fast for triage.

Hochschulkompass at hochschulkompass.de is the complete official list of degree programmes in Germany, with the most reliable filters by subject, state, degree type, language and start of programme.

How many German universities should I apply to?

Five to eight, structured in three tiers: one or two dream options where you are plausible but low probability, three or four realistic options where your profile sits comfortably inside the admitted range, and one or two safety options you would genuinely be happy to attend.

More than eight usually means weaker documents across the board, and fewer than five leaves you exposed if several decisions go against you. Masters in germany requirements for international students are not the binding constraint here; your own capacity is.

Your shortlist checklist before the document stage

You are done with shortlisting when you can answer all of the following without hesitation. You know whether you are targeting a winter or a summer intake, and why. You have a longlist of fifteen to thirty programmes and a tiered shortlist of five to eight.

Every shortlisted programme has a verified deadline, language requirement, academic requirement, cost figure and a saved direct link. Your spreadsheet is live with alerts set and a next action dated, and your language test is booked or deliberately skipped.

Close anything still open before you write a single sentence of motivation letter. A focused application to a programme you have genuinely researched will beat a rushed application to a better name every time, and the research you do now is what makes the writing fast later.

This stage is not the boring administrative part of a masters in germany application. It is the part that decides what the rest of the application is even possible, and it is where a germany masters degree starts to become real rather than theoretical.

It is also what separates a serious shortlist from a browser full of masters in germany universities you liked because of a rankings page. One has verified deadlines behind it. The other has not.
