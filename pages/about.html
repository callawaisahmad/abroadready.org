Scholarship Types We Cover

Our global scholarships database includes every major type of funding available for international students. Understanding the differences helps you choose the right opportunities for your situation.

Fully Funded Scholarships

Fully funded scholarships cover your full tuition costs, monthly living stipend, health insurance, travel costs, and sometimes additional allowances for research, books, or family members. Examples include DAAD Scholarships, Chevening Scholarships, Commonwealth Scholarships, Fulbright Program, Gates Cambridge Scholarship, Rhodes Scholarship, and the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS). These are the most competitive but also the most rewarding — they allow you to focus entirely on your studies without financial worry.

Partial Scholarships

Partial scholarships cover a portion of your tuition fees or living costs. A university might offer a 50% tuition-fee waiver, a department might provide a graduate assistantship that pays a monthly salary in exchange for teaching or research work, or a foundation might award a fixed grant of several thousand dollars. Partial scholarships are often easier to win than fully funded ones, and you can combine multiple partial awards to build a funding stack that covers most of your expenses.

Tuition-Fee Waivers

Many universities, especially public universities in Germany, offer tuition-fee waivers that reduce or eliminate your tuition costs. A tuition-fee waiver does not usually cover living expenses — you will need a separate source of funding for housing, food, and health insurance — but it removes the single largest cost of studying abroad.

Graduate Assistantships & Research Fellowships

Graduate assistantships and research fellowships are common at the Master's and PhD levels. You work part-time as a teaching assistant, research assistant, or laboratory supervisor, and in exchange your tuition is waived and you receive a monthly stipend. These positions also provide valuable academic experience that strengthens your CV and future career prospects.

Need-Based Grants

Need-based grants are awarded based on your financial circumstances. Universities and foundations require you to submit proof of your family's income and assets. If your financial need is demonstrated, the award can range from a partial contribution to full coverage of tuition and living costs.

Merit-Based Scholarships

Merit-based scholarships are awarded based on your academic record, test scores, research experience, and extracurricular achievements. These are the most common type of scholarship for undergraduate scholarships and Master's scholarships. Strong grades, high IELTS or TOEFL scores, and a compelling statement of purpose are essential for merit-based awards.

Our database also covers exchange programs, summer school funding, and postdoctoral fellowships. No matter what stage of your academic journey you are at — from applying to undergraduate UK courses to seeking PhD funding for a doctoral research project — you will find relevant options on AbroadReady.

Scholarships by Destination

Different countries offer different types of funding for international students. Here is an overview of the major scholarship destinations we cover in our global scholarships database, with specific programmes and keywords you need to know.

Germany — DAAD Scholarships and Tuition-Free Universities

Germany is one of the most popular destinations for international students seeking fully funded scholarships and high-quality education. The country is known for its tuition-free universities Germany system, where public universities Germany charge little to no tuition fees regardless of your nationality. You only need to cover your living costs, which are estimated at approximately 11,208 EUR per year as of 2026, and you must show proof of sufficient funds through a German blocked account (Sperrkonto) to obtain your student visa Germany.

The most prominent funding source in Germany is the DAAD Scholarships programme, administered by the Deutscher Akademischer Austauschdienst (German Academic Exchange Service). DAAD offers a wide range of fully funded scholarships for Master's and PhD study at German universities. The DAAD EPOS programme (Development-related Postgraduate Courses) is specifically designed for applicants from developing countries and funds postgraduate courses that are relevant to development policy. DAAD EPOS scholarships cover tuition, a monthly stipend of 934 EUR for Master's students (2026 rate), health insurance, travel allowance, and a study and research allowance.

Other German scholarship providers include the Deutschlandstipendium (Germany Scholarship), which provides 300 EUR per month to high-achieving students at participating German universities; KAAD (Catholic Academic Exchange Service), which funds postgraduate study for students from developing countries; the Heinrich Böll Stiftung (Heinrich Böll Foundation), which offers scholarships for international students committed to environmental and social justice; and the Konrad Adenauer Stiftung (Konrad Adenauer Foundation), which funds politically and socially engaged students from around the world.

Germany also offers many international degree programmes Germany taught entirely in English, particularly at the Master's level. These English-taught Master's Germany programmes allow international students to study without needing a German language certificate, though learning basic German is strongly recommended for daily life. Some programmes do require proof of German proficiency through the TestDaF or DSH language certificate. Applications for most German Master's programmes go through Uni-Assist, a centralized application service that evaluates your documents and forwards them to universities.

If you are planning to study in Germany, you will need to navigate the university admission in Germany process, which may require credential evaluation of your previous degrees. You must also open a German blocked account (Sperrkonto) to prove you have sufficient funds for your first year of living costs. The student visa Germany application process typically takes 6 to 12 weeks, so apply early.

Tip for applicants: DAAD Scholarships are highly competitive. Start your application at least 8 to 10 months before the deadline. Focus on writing a strong research proposal or motivation letter that clearly explains how your studies relate to your home country's development needs.

United Kingdom — Chevening, Commonwealth, Gates Cambridge, and More

The United Kingdom is home to some of the world's most prestigious scholarship programmes and universities. The Chevening Scholarships programme is the UK government's flagship global scholarship scheme, funded by the Foreign and Commonwealth Office (FCO) and partner organisations. Chevening offers fully funded Master's scholarships to outstanding emerging leaders from around the world. The award covers full tuition fees at any UK university, a monthly living stipend, return economy airfare, an arrival allowance, a thesis or dissertation grant, and access to the global Chevening alumni network. Chevening is one of the most competitive scholarships globally, with an acceptance rate of around 2 to 3 percent.

The Commonwealth Scholarships programme is another major funding source for international students from Commonwealth countries. Funded by the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO), Commonwealth Scholarships are awarded to students from Commonwealth countries for Master's and PhD study at UK universities. The scholarship covers full tuition fees, airfare, a living stipend, and a warm clothing allowance. Commonwealth Master's Scholarships and Commonwealth PhD Scholarships are among the most sought-after postgraduate UK courses for international students.

The Gates Cambridge Scholarship is a fully funded scholarship for international students to pursue a postgraduate degree at the University of Cambridge. Established by the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation, the scholarship covers the full cost of study at Cambridge, including tuition fees, a maintenance allowance, airfare, and discretionary funding for research and professional development. Gates Cambridge is highly selective, with approximately 80 scholarships awarded each year from an applicant pool of thousands.

The Rhodes Scholarship is one of the oldest and most prestigious international scholarship programmes. Rhodes Scholarships are awarded to exceptional students from selected countries to study at the University of Oxford. The scholarship covers Oxford University tuition fees, a monthly living stipend, airfare, and a settling-in allowance. The selection process evaluates academic excellence, leadership potential, physical vitality, and a commitment to making a positive impact on the world.

Scotland offers the Saltire Scholarships programme, which provides partial tuition fee funding (up to 8,000 GBP) for international students to study at Scottish universities. Saltire Scholarships cover any subject area and are awarded based on academic ability, leadership potential, and a clear connection to Scotland.

International students applying to UK universities must understand the international student tuition fees UK structure, which varies by university and programme. Most undergraduate and postgraduate programmes charge higher fees for international students. The UCAS application UK system is used for undergraduate programmes, while postgraduate applications go directly through each university's admissions portal. Most UK universities require English language evidence through IELTS for UKVI (IELTS for UK Visas and Immigration), the Secure English Language Test (SELT) accepted by the UK Home Office.

After completing your studies, the Graduate Route UK (also known as the post-study work visa UK) allows international graduates to remain in the UK for two years (three years for doctoral graduates) to work or look for work at any skill level. This is a valuable pathway to gaining professional experience and building a career in the UK. The Student Route visa (formerly Tier 4 Student Visa UK) is the visa you need for study programmes longer than six months. You will need to show proof of sufficient funds, a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) from your university, and valid English language test results.

Visa and Immigration Guide

Winning a scholarship is just the first step. You also need to navigate the visa and immigration process for your chosen destination. AbroadReady provides practical guidance on the most common visa routes for international students.

Student Visas

Every country has a student visa category for international students. For Germany, you apply for a student visa Germany at the German Embassy in Islamabad or the Consulate in Karachi. You will need your university admission letter, proof of health insurance, a German blocked account (Sperrkonto) with sufficient funds (11,208 EUR for 2026), a valid passport, and your previous academic certificates. The visa processing time is typically 6 to 12 weeks. For the UK, you apply through the Student Route (formerly Tier 4 Student Visa UK) online, attend a biometric appointment at a visa application centre, and provide your CAS letter (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies), proof of funds, and IELTS for UKVI scores.

Post-Study Work Visas

Many countries now offer post-study work options that allow international graduates to stay and work after completing their degree. The UK's Graduate Route and post-study work visa UK policies are among the most generous, offering two to three years of work eligibility. Germany offers an 18-month job seeker visa for graduates, which can lead to a skilled worker visa and eventually permanent residency pathways. Australia offers a Temporary Graduate Visa (subclass 485) that allows graduates to work for two to four years after study. Canada's Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) allows graduates to work for up to three years, which is a common stepping stone to permanent residency through Express Entry.

Work Visas and Immigration Pathways

If you are considering working abroad after your studies, it is important to understand the work visa sponsorship system in your destination country. The UK's Skilled Worker Visa allows employers to sponsor qualified professionals in eligible occupations. Germany offers an EU Blue Card for highly skilled workers. Canada's Express Entry system is a points-based immigration pathway that values Canadian education and work experience. Australia's General Skilled Migration programme uses a points system for skilled workers. The job seeker visa options in Germany and the UK give you time to find employment after graduation. Some countries also offer dependent visa options that allow your spouse or children to join you during your studies or work.

For remote workers and digital professionals, certain countries offer a digital nomad visa that allows you to live and work remotely while based in that country.

Essential Visa Documentation

No matter which country you choose, the visa application process requires careful preparation. Key documents you will need include: valid passport with sufficient blank pages, university admission letter or CAS, proof of scholarship or financial means (settlement funds proof), health insurance certificate, academic transcripts and degree certificates, English language test results (IELTS, TOEFL), a moving abroad checklist to track your tasks, and your visa application form and fee receipt. You may also need to attend a visa interview at the embassy or consulate.

Understanding the cost of living abroad in your destination country is essential for your visa application and budget planning. Monthly living costs vary significantly: approximately 934 to 1,200 EUR in Germany, 1,000 to 1,500 GBP in the UK, 1,500 to 2,500 CAD in Canada, and 1,500 to 2,500 AUD in Australia. Your scholarship may cover these costs, or you may need to show personal funds through a German blocked account (Sperrkonto) or equivalent mechanism.

Credential Evaluation

Many universities and immigration authorities require your academic credentials to be evaluated by a recognized body. Credential evaluation services such as WES evaluation (World Education Services) assess your degrees and transcripts against the educational standards of your destination country. This is commonly required for university admission in Germany (through Uni-Assist), Canadian immigration (through WES or IQAS), and US university applications (through WES or ECE). Start the credential evaluation process early, as it can take 4 to 8 weeks to complete.

What is the difference between a DAAD EPOS and a regular DAAD scholarship?

The DAAD EPOS programme (Development-related Postgraduate Courses) is a specific funding line within DAAD Scholarships that supports students from developing countries to pursue postgraduate courses that are relevant to the development of their home countries. EPOS-funded programmes are selected for their development focus and typically include a practical component or field research. The application process for EPOS is similar to other DAAD scholarships, but the programme must be one of the designated EPOS courses. Other DAAD scholarship lines fund study in a wider range of programmes and may have different eligibility criteria.

Do I need to know German to apply for a DAAD scholarship?

Not necessarily. Many international degree programmes Germany are taught entirely in English, especially at the Master's level. For these English-taught Master's Germany programmes, you need to provide proof of English proficiency (typically IELTS 6.5 or TOEFL 90) rather than German. However, some DAAD Scholarships may fund German-taught programmes or include a German language preparation component. A basic knowledge of German is always helpful for daily life and integration, even if your programme is in English. If you plan to stay in Germany after graduation for a job seeker visa or skilled worker visa pathway, learning German to at least B1 level will significantly improve your employment prospects.

What is a Sperrkonto and how do I open one?

A German blocked account (Sperrkonto) is a special bank account required for your student visa Germany application. It proves to the German authorities that you have sufficient funds to cover your living costs for your first year of study. In 2026, the required amount is 11,208 EUR. You deposit this amount into the blocked account, and you can only withdraw a monthly allowance (approximately 934 EUR) from it during your stay. Several providers offer blocked accounts for international students, including Fintiba, Expatrio, Coracle, and Deutsche Bank. You can open the account online before your visa appointment, transfer the funds, and receive a certificate to submit with your visa application.

How does the UK Graduate Route work for international students?

The Graduate Route UK (also called the post-study work visa UK) allows international students who have completed a degree at a UK university to stay in the UK for two years (three years for PhD graduates) to work or look for work. You do not need a job offer to apply. During this period, you can work at any skill level, switch into a skilled worker visa if you find a qualifying job, and gain valuable professional experience. The Graduate Route is a key pathway for international students who want to build an international career after their studies. To be eligible, you must have completed your degree at a UK university that is a licensed Student Sponsor, have a valid Student visa (formerly Tier 4 Student Visa UK), and apply from within the UK before your Student visa expires.

What is the difference between a Tier 4 Student Visa and the new Student Route?

The UK's student visa system changed in October 2020. The old Tier 4 Student Visa UK was replaced by the Student Route under the new points-based immigration system. The Student Route is simpler and more flexible. Key differences include: you can apply up to six months before your course starts (previously three months), you can switch into the Student Route from most other visa categories, and you have more flexibility around work placements and internships. The application process remains similar — you need a CAS from your university, proof of funds, and valid IELTS for UKVI scores.

Do I need a WES evaluation for my degree?

A WES evaluation (World Education Services credential evaluation) is often required for university admission in some countries and for immigration applications such as Canada's Express Entry. Whether you need it depends on your destination country and institution. For university admission in Germany, your documents are typically evaluated through Uni-Assist or directly by the university, and a WES evaluation may not be required. For UK universities, most institutions assess your international qualifications internally. For Canada, a WES evaluation is commonly required for both university admission and immigration. For US universities, many require a credential evaluation from WES, ECE, or a NACES member organization. Check the specific requirements of your target institutions and visa category before proceeding with credential evaluation.

What are the costs involved in studying abroad beyond tuition?

Beyond tuition, the main costs include: cost of living abroad (housing, food, transportation, utilities), health insurance, visa application fees, airfare, German blocked account (Sperrkonto) deposit (for Germany), settlement funds proof for visa applications, textbook and study materials, and personal expenses. For Germany, budget approximately 934 EUR per month for living costs. For the UK, budget 1,000 to 1,500 GBP per month outside London and 1,300 to 1,800 GBP in London. A fully funded scholarship typically covers most or all of these costs, while a partial scholarship or tuition-fee waiver may leave you responsible for living expenses and other fees. Create a detailed budget using a relocation guide or moving abroad checklist to plan your finances before you leave.

What is the difference between undergraduate and postgraduate funding?

Undergraduate scholarships are generally less common and more competitive than postgraduate funding because many governments and universities prioritize funding for higher-level study. Master's scholarships and PhD funding are more widely available, particularly through government scholarship programmes like DAAD Scholarships, Chevening Scholarships, Commonwealth Scholarships, and Fulbright. Postgraduate funding often covers full tuition and living costs, while undergraduate scholarships may be partial. Postdoctoral fellowship opportunities are available for researchers who have completed their PhD and want to continue their research at an international institution. Exchange programs and summer school options can also provide short-term funding for specific periods of study abroad.
