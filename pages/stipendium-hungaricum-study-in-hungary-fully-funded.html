Stipendium Hungaricum: Study in Hungary for Free

🗓️ Apr 6, 2026⏱️ 11 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Stipendium Hungaricum is Hungary's flagship international scholarship programme and one of the most accessible routes to fully funded study in Europe. Funded by the Hungarian government, it supports students at bachelor, master, one-tier master and doctoral level across a wide range of Hungarian universities. If you want to study in the European Union with tuition covered and a monthly stipend, Stipendium Hungaricum deserves a close look.

The programme is built on bilateral agreements between Hungary and partner countries, which means availability and available fields depend partly on your nationality. This guide explains, in question-and-answer form, what the programme covers, who can apply, what documents you need, and how to move through the application step by step.

Key takeaways

What are the key facts about the Stipendium Hungaricum programme?

If you only read one section, read this one. The five points below determine almost everything else about your application, and each of them is expanded on in the questions that follow.

  • Fully funded tuition: your tuition is covered for the duration of your programme.
  • Monthly stipend: a living contribution, with a higher amount for doctoral students than for bachelor and master students.
  • Accommodation and health cover: dormitory placement or a housing contribution, plus medical insurance.
  • Bilateral basis: eligibility and available programmes depend on the agreement between Hungary and your home country.
  • Two-step nomination: your home country sending partner nominates you, and Hungarian universities make the final academic decision.

Put simply, Stipendium Hungaricum removes tuition fees, adds a monthly payment, helps with housing and health cover, and asks you to satisfy two separate gatekeepers. Nothing is decided by a single central office on the Hungarian side, which is the single most important thing to internalise before you begin.

What is Stipendium Hungaricum?

Who runs the Stipendium Hungaricum programme?

Stipendium Hungaricum is a scholarship programme run by the Hungarian state through Tempus Public Foundation, the public foundation responsible for international higher education cooperation. Applications are handled through an official online platform, and the final academic decisions are made by the universities themselves rather than by the foundation.

Why was Stipendium Hungaricum created?

It was established to increase the number of international students in Hungary and to strengthen cultural and academic ties between Hungary and partner countries around the world. That development purpose explains a great deal about how the programme is designed, including its emphasis on fields listed in bilateral agreements and its expectation that scholars eventually take their knowledge home.

Why do two applicants from different countries see different options?

Because it operates on bilateral agreements, the specific countries, quotas and available study fields are set by cooperation between Hungary and each partner government. This is why two applicants from different countries may see different options, and why checking your own country's arrangement is an essential first step. If your nationality is not covered by a current agreement, no amount of academic strength will make the programme available to you.

What does Stipendium Hungaricum cover?

Is tuition really free on a Stipendium Hungaricum scholarship?

Yes. The scholarship is a comprehensive package designed to make study genuinely affordable, and tuition is the first major cost it removes. For the full standard duration of your programme, your tuition fees are fully covered at the university that admits you.

What else does the Stipendium Hungaricum package include?

Beyond tuition, the package typically includes:

  • Tuition-free study: your tuition fees are fully covered.
  • Monthly stipend: a contribution toward living costs. Non-doctoral students receive a set monthly amount, while doctoral students receive a higher amount reflecting their longer, research-based study.
  • Accommodation: a place in a university dormitory, or a monthly contribution toward the cost of private accommodation.
  • Medical insurance: health care coverage in line with the relevant Hungarian regulations, plus supplementary cover.

Is the Stipendium Hungaricum stipend enough to live on?

It is worth being realistic: the stipend is a contribution rather than a lavish salary, and living costs vary between Hungarian cities. Many students supplement it modestly, but the overall package removes the largest barriers to studying abroad. If you live in a dormitory in a smaller university city and cook for yourself, the funding is genuinely workable; if you expect to rent a private flat in central Budapest and eat out daily, you will need savings or family support as well.

Who is eligible?

What are the basic Stipendium Hungaricum eligibility conditions?

Eligibility rests on a few core conditions, and you must satisfy all of them:

  • You are a citizen of one of the partner countries that has an agreement with Hungary.
  • You meet the minimum age requirement, generally being at least 18 in the year you begin.
  • You meet the academic entry requirements for your chosen programme and level.
  • You are not already a beneficiary of the same scholarship for the same level.

Am I eligible for Stipendium Hungaricum?

Because participation is country-specific, the very first thing to check is whether your country is a sending partner and what fields are open to you. If your country participates, you then compete on academic merit and fit. That is the honest answer: eligibility is a country question first and an academic question second, and checking the country question costs you an hour and saves you a wasted application season.

Is there a minimum age for Stipendium Hungaricum?

Generally the requirement is that you are at least 18 in the year you begin your studies. This rule mostly affects applicants taking a second degree after a career, so if that describes you, check the annual call for the precise wording rather than assuming an exception exists.

Available study levels and programmes

Which study levels does Stipendium Hungaricum cover?

The programme spans the full range of higher education, which is one of its real strengths compared with many European scholarships:

  • Bachelor programmes for school leavers.
  • Master programmes for those with a bachelor degree.
  • One-tier master programmes in fields such as medicine, where the degree combines undergraduate and graduate study.
  • Doctoral programmes for research-focused candidates with a masters.
  • Non-degree and preparatory options in certain cases, including Hungarian language preparation.

Which languages are Stipendium Hungaricum programmes taught in?

Programmes are offered in English and in Hungarian, with a broad spread of disciplines. The number of English-taught options, particularly at master and doctoral level, is considerably wider than at bachelor level, so language of instruction is one of the constraints that shapes your choice list.

Required documents

What documents do I need to apply for Stipendium Hungaricum?

Applications are submitted online, and you will need clear scans of several documents. Requirements vary slightly by level and programme, but you should prepare:

  • A valid passport or identity document.
  • Your school leaving certificate, or your degree and transcripts for postgraduate study, with certified translations where required.
  • A motivation letter explaining why you want to study your chosen programme in Hungary.
  • A medical certificate confirming you are in good health.
  • Language proficiency evidence appropriate to your programme, where required.
  • For doctoral applicants, a research plan and often proof of contact or acceptance from a supervisor.
  • Any additional programme-specific documents, such as a portfolio or entrance test results for certain fields.

Do my documents need certified translations?

Where the issuing language of a document is not English, certified translations are normally required. This is where many otherwise strong applications fail: a clean scan of an untranslated transcript is treated as an incomplete document. Check each university's stated translation policy, because some accept notarised translations and others insist on a specific certifying body.

How the application works: step by step

The programme involves a distinctive two-step process, so understanding the flow prevents confusion. Each of the five stages below is administered by a different party, and missing one does not necessarily disqualify you, but it will delay you or cost you the place.

Step 1: How do I find my country's sending partner?

Every partner country has a designated sending partner, usually a government ministry or agency, that manages nominations. Find yours and note its specific requirements and deadlines, which can differ from the general Hungarian timeline. Your sending partner is the body that will formally nominate you, so an application that never reaches them is an application that cannot be considered.

Step 2: How does the online application work?

Register on the official Stipendium Hungaricum online application platform and complete your profile. You select your study level, then choose your programme preferences from those available to your country. You can usually list more than one programme choice, so use your options wisely. The platform is the Hungarian record of your application; the sending partner has its own separate requirements on top of it.

Step 3: How do I choose my programmes carefully?

Research each programme, its university and its city. Pick options that genuinely match your background and goals rather than choosing on prestige alone. Aligning your choices with your motivation letter makes your whole application coherent, and a panel reading a consistent application is far more likely to believe the reasoning behind it than one reading a random list of five institutions.

Step 4: Why must I submit to both the platform and my sending partner?

You typically submit your application online and also complete any separate application required by your home country's sending partner. Missing the sending-partner step is a common error, so confirm exactly what your national agency expects. Ask the agency directly if their instructions are unclear, because an email confirmation that you did the right thing is valuable evidence if a deadline is later disputed.

Step 5: What happens during nomination and the university decision?

Your sending partner nominates eligible candidates, and Hungarian universities then assess nominated applicants academically. Some programmes include an entrance examination or interview. Final decisions rest with the universities and the programme coordinators, so the university you ranked highly may still assess you strictly if it admitted you.

Remember the two gatekeepers: your home country nominates you, and the Hungarian university admits you. A strong application needs to satisfy both.

What kinds of entrance exams or interviews might I face?

Depending on the programme, you may face an online entrance test, a written assignment or an interview. These assess your academic readiness and, for research degrees, the strength of your proposed project. Some programmes assess nothing beyond your documents, so check each programme page rather than assuming an interview is part of every Stipendium Hungaricum application.

How should I prepare for the assessment?

Prepare by revisiting the fundamentals of your field and by being ready to discuss your motivation clearly. Preparation for the subject content protects your chances; preparation for the conversation protects your confidence, and confidence is audible. Tips for a competitive application

What makes a Stipendium Hungaricum application competitive?

Five habits separate the applications that get read seriously from those that get filtered out:

  • Confirm your country participates before investing time, as availability is country-specific.
  • Match your choices to your profile. Coherent applications beat ambitious but mismatched ones.
  • Invest in your motivation letter. It is one of the few places you can stand out.
  • Respect both deadlines. The platform deadline and your sending partner's deadline may differ.
  • Prepare for an exam or interview in case your programme requires one.

Which of these tips matters the most?

If you can only do one thing well, spend your effort on the motivation letter. Grades and transcripts are largely fixed by the time you apply, but the letter is the one component you fully control, and the one that lets a reviewer understand why you are in the room. Everything else on the list is discipline; the letter is persuasion.

Living and studying in Hungary

What is studying in Hungary like for international students?

Beyond the funding, Hungary offers an appealing student experience: a central European location with easy travel, historic university cities, and a relatively affordable cost of living compared with much of Western Europe. Day-to-day life revolves around university teaching, libraries, dormitories and student societies, and because the country is compact, most cities are easy to reach and explore on a weekend.

Is Stipendium Hungaricum only about funding?

Studying in the European Union also broadens your academic and professional horizons. For many students, Stipendium Hungaricum is not only a scholarship but a gateway to the wider continent: the qualification is recognised across the EU, the network is genuinely international, and the experience of studying abroad changes how you approach your own career.

Choosing your city and university

What should I look at when ranking my programme preferences?

Hungary has universities in the capital and in a number of vibrant regional cities, each with its own character. When you rank your programme preferences, look beyond the name of the institution to the specific department, the language of instruction, and the city itself. Rank the options against your own priorities, because the best university for a specific department may sit in a city that suits you poorly.

Should I choose the capital or a regional city?

The capital offers the largest range of programmes and the biggest international community, but regional university cities can provide a lower cost of living, a close-knit student environment and strong departments in particular fields. Since accommodation support is part of the package, weigh the overall lifestyle rather than location alone. A cheaper city effectively increases what your stipend buys, which matters more than most applicants expect.

How do I match the programme to my goals?

Read the curriculum, check the language of instruction, and confirm that the programme aligns with your academic background and career plans. A coherent connection between your past studies, your chosen programme and your stated goals strengthens your whole application, and it is the same coherence that panels look for when they judge motivation.

Writing a standout motivation letter

What does a strong Stipendium Hungaricum motivation letter answer?

The motivation letter is one of the few places where you can distinguish yourself from other qualified applicants, so it deserves real effort. Maintaining and renewing your scholarship

How long is Stipendium Hungaricum funding awarded for?

Stipendium Hungaricum is awarded for the standard duration of your programme. For a bachelor or master degree that normally means the full length of the course, so funding is not something you have to re-apply for year by year provided that you remain in good standing.

What do I have to do to keep my scholarship?

Continued funding depends on your remaining a student in good standing. That means enrolling on time, meeting your programme's academic progress requirements, and completing each stage of your studies as expected. Understanding these conditions from the outset helps you keep your scholarship secure throughout your degree, and the main practical risk is administrative rather than academic, since missed re-registration deadlines are a common and entirely avoidable problem.

After you are selected: enrolment and arrival

What practical steps do I complete after selection?

Once you are nominated and admitted, there are practical steps to complete before you begin. You will need to accept your place, arrange your residence permit or visa, organise your travel, and prepare for enrolment and, where relevant, orientation. Each of these has its own lead time, and some depend on documents issued by your sending partner, so request them early.

When should I start my residence permit process?

Start your residence permit process early, keep copies of all your documents, and watch for communications from your host university. The permit is normally applied for after arrival within a set deadline, but the supporting paperwork can take weeks to obtain from home, so gathering it before you travel is the difference between a smooth first week and a difficult one.

How should I use the time before term starts?

Planning these final steps carefully means you arrive in Hungary ready to focus on your studies. Many students also use the period before term to research their city, connect with other incoming scholars, and prepare for life in a new country. Contacting your future classmates before arrival is underrated: it turns an unfamiliar city into a familiar one much faster.

Students in dormitories and smaller cities generally find it more comfortable than those renting privately in the capital.

Which Hungarian cities are cheapest to live in?

Cost of living tracks the size of the city, so a dormitory room in Szeged, Pecs or Debrecen stretches the monthly payment considerably further than a rented flat in Budapest. If your funding is the main constraint on your choice list, ranking cheaper cities first can be the single most effective way to make the scholarship work comfortably.

What should I include in my budget?

Plan for essentials such as food, transport, study materials and occasional travel, and keep a modest buffer for unexpected costs, especially in your first weeks before payments settle into a routine. Stipendium Hungaricum for Pakistani students: how the bilateral agreement works

How does the bilateral agreement work for Pakistani students?

Pakistan is one of the bilateral partner countries of the Stipendium Hungaricum programme, which means Pakistani students have a dedicated allocation of scholarships each year. Understanding this structure helps you plan your application strategy effectively. The programme operates through bilateral agreements between Hungary and partner countries. Each partner country has an agreed number of scholarships available per year, and the selection process is managed jointly by the Hungarian side, through Tempus Public Foundation, and the nominating body on the Pakistani side. The exact quota for Pakistan is not always publicly announced in advance, but it has historically been sufficient to make the programme competitive but accessible for strong applicants.

How do I apply for Stipendium Hungaricum as a Pakistani student?

As a Pakistani applicant, your application goes through a nominating authority in Pakistan before it reaches the Hungarian side. This means there is essentially a two-stage selection: first you are nominated by your home country, and then the Hungarian side makes the final selection. Understanding this structure matters because it means you need to satisfy both the Pakistani nominating body and the Hungarian selection panel, so your preparation has to satisfy two different sets of expectations at once.

Which study fields are available to Pakistani applicants?

The bilateral agreement also determines which study fields are available to Pakistani students. Not all programmes at all Hungarian universities are open under the Stipendium Hungaricum scheme for every partner country. Check the current list carefully before applying, as it may differ from what was available in previous years. Applicants who rely on last year's list often find that the programme they had built their motivation letter around is no longer on it.

Am I eligible for Stipendium Hungaricum as a Pakistani student?

Another important aspect is the return-home requirement. Stipendium Hungaricum scholars are generally expected to return to their home country after completing their studies, at least for a period specified in the scholarship agreement. If you intend to stay in Hungary or Europe after graduation, discuss this obligation carefully before accepting the scholarship. Agreeing to something you have not thought through can complicate both your visa position and your relationship with the programme.

What are the Stipendium Hungaricum deadlines and timeline?

Pakistani students should also be aware that the application process typically opens in November or December each year, with a deadline in January or February. Monitor the official Stipendium Hungaricum website and the website of the Pakistani nominating authority regularly starting in the autumn. The national deadline can fall earlier than the platform deadline, so the earlier date is the one that should govern your planning.

Preparing for the Stipendium Hungaricum entrance exam and interview

Is there an entrance exam in the Stipendium Hungaricum application?

Yes. The Stipendium Hungaricum application includes an entrance examination or interview stage, and preparing for this stage properly is one of the strongest things you can do to improve your chances of selection. It is a genuine selection tool rather than a formality, and candidates who treat it as the final hurdle after everything else is submitted tend to perform noticeably worse than those who prepare alongside their documents.

What format does the assessment take?

The format varies by university and study level. For undergraduate programmes, it may take the form of a written or online exam in your chosen field. For master's and doctoral programmes, it is more commonly an interview conducted by a faculty panel, either in person at the Hungarian embassy or online via video call. Because panels schedule short slots, answer clearly and keep your response to the question asked rather than delivering a prepared speech.

How do I prepare for a written entrance exam?

For written entrance exams, review the specific topics covered by the programme you are applying to. Hungarian universities typically publish their entrance exam syllabi on their websites. Focus on foundational knowledge rather than advanced topics. Past papers, if available, are the most useful preparation material, and working through them under timed conditions is the closest thing to a realistic rehearsal of the real thing.

What Stipendium Hungaricum interview questions should I prepare for?

For interview-based assessments, prepare to discuss your motivation for studying in Hungary, your academic background, and your plans after graduation. The panel will want to understand why you chose Hungary specifically, how the programme fits your career goals, and whether you are likely to succeed in the academic environment. Research the programme structure, key faculty members, and any unique features of the university, because these details are exactly what distinguish a prepared candidate from a rehearsed one.

What will the panel ask about Hungary and Hungarian culture?

A common area of focus is your understanding of Hungary and Hungarian culture. You do not need to be an expert, but showing that you have researched the country, its education system, and basic cultural norms demonstrates commitment. A few informed sentences about the education system, the language situation and the cost of living do more for you than a long list of facts, because they show genuine rather than recited interest.

How should I practise my interview answers?

Practice answering questions aloud in English, as interviews are typically conducted in English for English-taught programmes. Record yourself, review the recording, and focus on speaking clearly and confidently without reading from notes. Listening back is uncomfortable and useful, and it is normally the fastest way to discover that you speak more quickly than you think or trail off at the end of your answers.

How do I answer "why this university and programme"?

Finally, prepare a concise, structured response to the question of why you chose this particular university and programme. A strong answer connects your academic interests to specific features of the programme and links your future career plans to what the programme offers. Structure it in three moves: what you want to study, why this institution can teach you that, and what you will do with it afterwards.

Stipendium Hungaricum versus other European scholarship programmes

How does Stipendium Hungaricum compare with Erasmus Mundus?

Stipendium Hungaricum versus Erasmus Mundus: Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters offer the unique experience of studying in at least two European countries and receiving a scholarship of up to 1,400 euros per month. However, Erasmus Mundus is highly competitive. Stipendium Hungaricum is generally less competitive, and its fixed country allocation means Pakistani students face a defined pool of competitors rather than the entire global applicant pool.

How does Stipendium Hungaricum compare with DAAD?

Stipendium Hungaricum versus DAAD: Germany's DAAD scholarship is one of the most prestigious in Europe, offering generous monthly stipends and access to tuition-free universities. However, DAAD is extremely competitive. Stipendium Hungaricum has a broader range of available study fields at the undergraduate level, which DAAD does not typically fund, so for a school leaver the Hungarian option is often the only realistic route to a funded European degree.

How does Stipendium Hungaricum compare with Turkiye Burslari?

Stipendium Hungaricum versus Turkiye Burslari: These two programmes are structurally similar: both are government-funded, both cover tuition and living expenses, and both include a language preparatory year. The main difference is the destination country. Turkey offers a larger economy, while Hungary places you in the heart of the EU with easier access to the broader EU job market, which is a decisive advantage for graduates planning to work in Europe.

How does it compare with Chevening or Commonwealth?

Stipendium Hungaricum versus Chevening or Commonwealth: Chevening and Commonwealth are the top-tier UK government scholarships, but they are limited to master's level and are extraordinarily competitive. Stipendium Hungaricum is open at the undergraduate, master's, and doctoral level, so it is one of the few European government scholarships that supports a full academic pathway from school-leaver to doctorate.

What is the smartest strategy for Pakistani students?

The wisest strategy for Pakistani students is to apply to multiple programmes simultaneously. The application timelines overlap but are not identical, and the documents required are similar enough that preparing for one helps with the others. Build one strong core set of documents, then adapt the motivation letter for each programme rather than writing every letter from scratch.

Health insurance, accommodation, and practical logistics in Hungary

What health insurance do Stipendium Hungaricum scholars receive?

Stipendium Hungaricum scholars receive health insurance coverage through the Hungarian social security system, known as TAJ card access. This covers visits to public healthcare facilities, hospital stays, and emergency treatment. Many scholars choose to supplement the basic coverage with a private health insurance policy that provides faster access to English-speaking doctors. The additional cost is modest, typically a few hundred euros per year, and for a family with pre-existing conditions it is usually worth the peace of mind.

What accommodation is included in the scholarship?

The scholarship includes a place in a university dormitory for scholars who want it. Dormitory rooms are typically shared, and the cost is deducted from your monthly stipend at a subsidised rate. If you prefer to live off campus, the scholarship provides a housing contribution. Budapest has a well-developed rental market, but popular areas near universities fill up quickly, so begin your search by July.

How much does it cost to live in Hungary?

Hungary is one of the most affordable countries in the EU for students. Monthly expenses for a student living modestly in Budapest typically range from 500 to 800 euros, including rent, food, transport, and social activities. Outside Budapest, costs can be twenty to thirty percent lower, so a student in a dormitory in a regional city may spend less than half of that figure.

How do I get around in Hungary?

Transport is one of the easiest parts of the practical picture. Budapest has an excellent public transport system, and students can purchase a discounted monthly pass. For intercity travel, Hungarian railways offer student discounts, which makes visiting other European destinations between terms genuinely affordable on a scholar's budget.

How do I open a Hungarian bank account?

You will need a Hungarian bank account to receive your stipend. Several banks offer student accounts with no monthly fees. You will need your passport, residence permit, and proof of enrollment, so apply as soon as you have all three rather than waiting until your first payment is due.

When do I apply for a residence permit?

After arriving, you must apply for a residence permit within thirty days. Your university's international office will guide you through this process, and they will want to see that you have already enrolled, so complete your registration before you file the permit application.

How can my Hungarian degree contribute to Pakistan's development?

Finally, consider how your experience in Hungary can contribute to Pakistan's development in your specific field. Why do strong Pakistani applicants get rejected?

That is frustrating but useful news, because every one of the mistakes below is within your control and can be eliminated before you submit anything. Work through them in order, because the first three filter applications out before any human being reads them seriously.

Mistake 1: Applying to the wrong study field or university

The first major mistake is applying to the wrong study field or university. The available programmes for Pakistani students are specified in the bilateral agreement and may not include every programme offered by Hungarian universities. Always check the current official list, and confirm that the specific programme and the specific university both appear on it rather than assuming a university's overall partnership covers everything it teaches.

Mistake 2: Submitting a generic motivation letter

The second mistake is submitting a generic motivation letter. The motivation letter is one of the most heavily weighted components. Your letter should explain why you chose Hungary, why you chose the specific university and programme, and how the scholarship fits your career plan, and it should do so using details that could not be copied into an application to another country.

Mistake 3: Incomplete or inconsistent documents

The third mistake is incomplete or inconsistent documents. If your passport name differs from your academic records, or if your transcripts are not translated, your application may be filtered out at an early stage. Create a checklist from the official requirements, and then check every name, date and number against the original documents rather than against your memory.

Mistake 4: Ignoring interview preparation

The fourth mistake is ignoring the interview preparation. Some applicants treat the entrance interview as a formality. The interview is a genuine selection stage used to assess motivation, communication skills, and academic readiness, and applicants who have never spoken about their own research aloud tend to show it immediately.

Mistake 5: Missing deadlines

The fifth mistake is missing deadlines. The application window is typically open for only two to three months, and late submissions are not accepted. Set calendar reminders well in advance, and treat your sending partner's earlier deadline as the one that governs, even if the platform remains open afterwards.

Mistake 6: Failing to prove English proficiency adequately

The sixth mistake is failing to demonstrate English proficiency adequately. Even if the programme is taught in English, some applicants do not provide sufficient evidence of their language ability. A strong English score signals to the panel that you can handle academic study in English, and it removes one obvious risk from an interview that may be your only opportunity to prove yourself in person.

How do I make sure I avoid these mistakes?

Avoiding these six mistakes does not guarantee selection, but it ensures that your application is evaluated on its merits rather than being eliminated for administrative reasons. Build your checklist at the start of the season, assign one person to proofread documents and another to review the motivation letter, and do a final date check the week you intend to submit rather than on the day.

Writing a standout motivation letter for Stipendium Hungaricum

How heavily weighted is the motivation letter?

The motivation letter is often the most heavily weighted document in the Stipendium Hungaricum application. A strong letter can compensate for a borderline GPA, while a weak letter can undermine an otherwise excellent profile. Understanding what the selection committee is looking for and structuring your letter accordingly is essential, because the committee uses the letter to test whether you actually want the specific programme in front of you.

How long should a Stipendium Hungaricum motivation letter be?

The motivation letter for Stipendium Hungaricum should be between 500 and 1,000 words, depending on the specific instructions in the annual call. Within this space, you need to address three core questions: why this programme, why Hungary, and what you will do with your education after returning home. Answering those three questions in a few hundred well-chosen sentences is better than filling the full limit.

How should I open the letter?

Begin with a clear opening that states your purpose. Avoid generic openings like I am writing to express my interest in the Stipendium Hungaricum scholarship. Instead, start with a specific statement about your academic or professional goal and how this programme connects to it. For example, if you are applying for a programme in water resource management, open with a statement about the water challenges Pakistan faces and your specific interest in addressing them through Hungarian expertise in this field.

What belongs in the middle section?

The middle section should address why Hungary and why this specific university. Do not write generically about Hungary being a beautiful country with a rich culture. Instead, reference specific academic strengths of the university, particular professors whose work interests you, unique research facilities, or programme features that are not available at Pakistani universities. This specificity demonstrates that you have researched the programme thoroughly and are applying with genuine intent rather than casting a wide net.

What should the final section explain?

The final section should explain your plans after returning to Pakistan. The selection committee wants to invest in people who will make a tangible impact in their home country. Describe the specific problem you intend to address, the skills you expect to gain in Hungary, and how you plan to apply them. Concrete examples are more persuasive than general statements about wanting to contribute to Pakistan's development.

What tone should the letter use?

Throughout the letter, maintain a professional but personal tone. The committee reads thousands of letters, and those that stand out are the ones that feel genuinely personal rather than formulaic. Use specific anecdotes from your own experience to illustrate your points. If a particular experience sparked your interest in the field, describe it, and describe it with the details that make it unmistakably yours.

How should I proofread the letter?

Finally, proofread meticulously. Spelling errors, grammatical mistakes, and awkward phrasing undermine even the strongest content. Have at least two people review your letter: one who knows you well enough to confirm that the letter sounds like you, and one with strong English skills who can catch language issues. Reading the letter aloud is also surprisingly effective at exposing sentences that are technically correct but impossible to follow.

Choosing the right Hungarian city and university for your Stipendium Hungaricum application

Why does my city and university choice matter so much?

Your choice of university and city significantly affects your experience as a Stipendium Hungaricum scholar. Making this decision thoughtfully, rather than defaulting to the most well-known institution, leads to a more satisfying and productive study period. The decision also shapes your budget, your social life and the kind of network you build, so it deserves more attention than most applicants give it before pressing submit.

Is Budapest the right city for me?

Budapest is the largest city and home to several major universities, including Eotvos Lorand University, Budapest University of Technology and Economics, Semmelweis University, and Corvinus University. Budapest offers the most diverse social life, the largest international student community, and the widest range of part-time work opportunities. However, it is also the most expensive city in Hungary, and the cost of living outside the dormitory can be higher than in other cities.

Is Debrecen a good choice?

Debrecen is Hungary's second-largest city and home to the University of Debrecen, one of the country's oldest and most comprehensive universities. Debrecen has a strong international student community, particularly in medical and engineering programmes. The cost of living is significantly lower than Budapest, and the city offers a more intimate, community-oriented experience, which many scholars prefer during a master's degree.

What about studying in Szeged?

Szeged is a university city in southern Hungary, home to the University of Szeged, which ranks among Hungary's top research universities. Szeged is known for its strong programmes in science, medicine, and the arts. The city is compact and walkable, with a lower cost of living than Budapest or Debrecen, so a larger share of the stipend is genuinely available for daily life.

What about Pecs?

Pecs is a cultural hub in southern Hungary with a long academic tradition, centred on the University of Pecs. The city has a vibrant arts scene, a well-preserved historical centre, and a welcoming atmosphere for international students. Pecs is particularly strong in the humanities, social sciences, and medical programmes, so it is worth checking even if you had not considered southern Hungary before.

Which three factors matter more than rankings?

When choosing your university, consider three factors beyond rankings: the availability of your specific programme in English, the strength of the department in your field, and the city's cost of living and quality of life. A slightly less well-known university with a strong department in your field may provide a better academic experience than a prestigious university where your programme is a secondary priority.

How do I research the faculty members?

Research the specific faculty members at each university. For master's and doctoral students, the quality of your supervisor matters more than the university's overall ranking. Look for professors whose research interests align with yours and who have a track record of supervising international students successfully, and make contact before you apply wherever the institution welcomes it.

How should I think about daily life?

Finally, consider the practical aspects of daily life. If you are accustomed to the bustle of a large city like Lahore or Karachi, Budapest may feel comfortable, while a smaller city may feel quiet. If you prefer a quieter, more focused environment, Debrecen or Szeged may suit you better. Visit the university's international student pages and, if possible, connect with current or former students to get an honest perspective on daily life.

Frequently asked questions

Is Stipendium Hungaricum really fully funded?

Yes, in the sense that matters most for applicants: your tuition fees are fully covered for the standard duration of the programme, and you receive a monthly stipend, accommodation support and health insurance. It is not fully funded in the sense that a salary would be, so budget for the gap using the guidance above.

Who can apply for Stipendium Hungaricum?

Citizens of partner countries that have an agreement with Hungary, who meet the minimum age requirement and the academic entry requirements of their chosen programme, and who are not already benefiting from the same scholarship at the same level. Nationality comes first, because it determines whether the programme is open to you at all.

Do I need an IELTS or TOEFL score?

Language proficiency evidence is required where the programme asks for it, which is common for English-taught master's and doctoral programmes. Bachelor programmes taught in Hungarian may instead require Hungarian language evidence, and Hungarian language preparatory options exist for some applicants who need to build that level first.

Can I apply for more than one programme?

Yes. The online platform normally allows you to list several programme preferences open to your country, usually within the same study level. Choose carefully, though, because a shorter and more coherent list is more persuasive than an ambitious one, and each preference should be something you would genuinely attend.

Is there an application fee?

There is no application fee for the scholarship itself. The costs you will meet are ordinary application costs, such as certified translations, medical certification, or courier and document preparation charges, so set aside a modest budget for document handling in advance.

How long does the whole process take?

From the November or December opening to an enrolment date, expect several months that include your sending partner's nomination stage, Hungarian platform processing, university assessment, and any entrance exam or interview. Building in a buffer is wise, because delays on either side are common and are outside your control.

What language are the programmes taught in?

Both English and Hungarian, with the widest choice in English at postgraduate level. Check the language of instruction for every programme you consider, because a change in teaching language between years is possible and would affect both your preparation and your funding assumptions.

Is the monthly stipend the same at every level?

No. Non-doctoral students receive one set monthly amount, while doctoral students receive a higher amount, as shown in the 2026 table earlier on this page. The doctoral figure reflects the longer, research-based nature of a PhD, and the exact figures are confirmed in the annual call.

Do I have to return to Pakistan after I graduate?

Generally yes. The scholarship agreement includes a return-home expectation for at least the period it specifies, which is part of the bilateral arrangement rather than a discretionary condition. If your plan involves staying in Europe, raise it with the scholarship administration before you accept the offer.

How do I check whether my country participates?

Check the official scholarship website and the website of your national sending partner, and confirm that your nationality appears on the current list of partner countries and that your intended field is among the available programmes. This five-minute check is the most valuable five minutes in the whole application process.

Frequently asked questions

Is Stipendium Hungaricum fully funded?+

Yes, in the sense that it covers your tuition in full and provides a monthly stipend, dormitory accommodation or a housing contribution, and medical insurance. The stipend is a contribution toward living costs rather than a large salary, and doctoral students receive a higher monthly amount than bachelor and master students.

Can anyone apply for Stipendium Hungaricum?+

No. The programme is based on bilateral agreements, so you must be a citizen of a partner country that has an arrangement with Hungary. Available fields and quotas depend on your country's agreement, so your first step is to confirm that your country participates and see which programmes are open to you.

What is a sending partner?+

A sending partner is the designated authority in your home country, usually a government ministry or agency, that manages nominations for Stipendium Hungaricum. You complete the Hungarian online application and also satisfy any separate requirements your sending partner sets, and it nominates eligible candidates to the Hungarian universities.

Do I need to speak Hungarian?+

Not for English-taught programmes, which make up a large share of the offer and set their own English requirements. Some programmes are taught in Hungarian and require Hungarian proficiency, and preparatory language options exist in certain cases. Always check the language of instruction and the requirement for your chosen programme.

Is there an entrance exam or interview?+

It depends on the programme. Many programmes require an online entrance test, a written assignment or an interview, especially for competitive or research-based fields. These assess your academic readiness and, for doctoral applicants, the strength of your research plan, so prepare to discuss your field and motivation clearly.
