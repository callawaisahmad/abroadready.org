Immigrate to the USA: The Honest 2026 Guide

The US offers the world's highest salaries — but also its most competitive immigration system: an H-1B lottery, per-country green card caps and backlogs measured in years for some nationalities. Here's the straight path: every work visa, every green card route, the real fees and exactly where the waits are.

The Reality of US Immigration

Compared to Canada, Australia or the UK, the US has no points-based system. Green cards are limited to ~140,000 employment-based and ~226,000 family-based per year, with a 7% per-country cap. For applicants from India — who make up ~75% of EB-2/EB-3 demand — this means waits that can stretch past a decade. For most other countries, employment green cards are achievable in 1–4 years.

This guide is not designed to discourage you. It's designed so you know the exact route, the exact costs and the exact waits — and can decide which pathway fits your profile, or whether a different country is a smarter bet.

Started in the US already? International students with OPT experience are the most common H-1B candidates — see the study-side path in Study in the USA.

Route 1 — H-1B Work Visa (Specialty Occupation)

The workhorse visa for foreign professionals. Your employer sponsors you; you compete in an annual electronic lottery.

Cap-exempt escape hatch: universities, non-profits and government research organisations can hire on H-1B with no lottery — a realistic strategy is working at a university or non-profit while your green card processes.

Route 2 — Employment Green Cards (EB-1, EB-2, EB-3)

The actual path to permanent residence. Three tiers, three different eligibility bars — and per-country backlogs you must plan around.

EB-1 · Extraordinary Ability Fastest

For top researchers, executives and outstanding professors. EB-1A can even be self-petitioned — no employer, no job offer. Meet 3 of 10 criteria (major awards, published media about you, judging, high salary…). Fee: I-140 $665. Processing ~2–3 months. No PERM needed. India/China currently wait ~2+ years; most countries are current.

EB-2 · Advanced Degree / NIW

For master's+ holders or bachelor's + 5 years' experience. The NIW (National Interest Waiver) is a self-petition with no job offer and no PERM. Fee: I-140 $665. Wait: most countries ~1 year; India ~12–14 years; China ~4–6 years.

EB-3 · Skilled Workers & Professionals

For bachelor's holders and skilled workers (2+ years training). Requires employer PERM labour certification + I-140. Wait: most countries ~1 year; China ~5 years; India ~12 years. Note: India EB-3 is sometimes faster than EB-2 — a "downgrade" strategy.

EB-5 · Investor High cost

Invest $800,000 in a US business that creates 10 jobs (or $1,050,000 standard; rising to $1.4M for high-employment areas). A July 2026 proposed rule starts automatic inflation adjustment from Jan 2027. Source-of-funds scrutiny is intense.

What is a priority date?

Your priority date is your place in line, set when your PERM or I-140/I-130 is filed. Each month the Visa Bulletin publishes cutoffs per country and category. If your date is earlier than the cutoff, you can file for adjustment of status (I-485). The per-country cap of 7% is why India and China wait so long.

Straight talk on India backlogs: as of Feb 2026, EB-2 India's cutoff is 15JUL13 — CATO estimates the queue could take 195+ years to clear at current rates. If you're Indian-born, treat the US as a high-risk option unless you qualify for EB-1 or EB-5, or are willing to build a career in another country first.

Route 3 — Other Work Visas Worth Knowing

O-1 · Extraordinary Ability No lottery

No cap, no lottery. Meet 3 of 8 criteria or hold a major international award. Used by startup founders, AI researchers, executives and athletes as the H-1B alternative. Processing 2–6 months (15 days premium). Fees ~$1,055 + asylum fee.

L-1 · Intracompany Transfer

For managers, executives (L-1A) and specialised-knowledge employees (L-1B) transferring to a US office after 1 year abroad with the same company. Max 7 years (L-1A) / 5 years (L-1B). Direct path to EB-1C.

E-2 · Treaty Investor

For nationals of ~80 treaty countries who invest substantial capital (typically $100K+) in a real US business. Renewable indefinitely, spouses can work — but it does not directly lead to a green card.

J-1 · Exchange Visitor

Researcher, scholar, au pair, intern categories. Check your DS-2019 for the two-year home-residency rule (212e) — it can block H/L/immigrant visas until you serve 2 years at home or obtain a waiver.

More Work Visas: TN, H-2B & Specialist Routes

Beyond H-1B and the EB system, the US runs a long menu of quota-based and niche work visas. Most don't lead straight to a green card — treat them as stepping stones or short-term tools.

TN · USMCA Professional No cap

For Canadian and Mexican citizens in ~60 listed professions (engineers, scientists, IT, accountants, teachers). Up to 3 years, renewable indefinitely. Canadians apply at the border with no visa fee; Mexicans need the $185 visa. It carries no immigrant intent — but you can later move to an EB route.

H-2B · Seasonal Non-Agricultural 66,000/yr cap

For temporary seasonal or peak-load work in hospitality, landscaping, seafood and construction. The employer must first prove no US workers are available (DOL certification). Stays capped at 3 years; no direct green card path. Petition ≈ $340 (I-129 $190 + anti-fraud $150).

H-2A · Agricultural Workers

The seasonal agricultural counterpart to H-2B — with no annual cap. Covers planting, harvesting, orchard and livestock work. Employer files I-129 ($190) and must provide housing, meals and transport. Max 3 years in the US per stay, then 3 months abroad.

H-1B1 & E-3 No lottery

Treaty-based cousins of H-1B. H-1B1: 6,800 visas a year reserved for Chile & Singapore, outside the lottery. E-3: 10,500 a year for Australians, filed at the consulate with no USCIS petition — and E-3 spouses can work.

R-1 · Religious Workers

For ministers and religious occupations serving a qualifying US religious organisation. I-129 $190; valid up to 5 years (2 + 3 extension). Not itself an immigrant route, but ministers can later use the EB-4 special-immigrant category.

EB-1C · Multinational Managers No PERM

Green card for managers/executives transferred from a foreign affiliate. Requires 1 year of managerial work for the company abroad within the last 3 years plus a qualifying US entity. I-140 $665, no labour certification — the natural endgame for L-1A holders.

Also notable: the Q cultural exchange visa (up to 15 months for international cultural-exchange programs) and the I visa for foreign journalists. Both are strictly temporary — no path to a green card.

Route 4 — Family Green Cards & the DV Lottery

💍 Spouse / Family Green Card

Immediate relatives of US citizens (spouse, parents, under-21 children) have no cap — typically 8–18 months total. Spouses of green card holders wait ~35+ months. Needs I-130 ($625), I-485 or consular processing, medical exam and the I-864 affidavit of support (income ≥125% of the poverty line).

🎲 Diversity Visa Lottery Free entry

Free online entry each October for nationals of low-immigration countries. DV-2027: ~14.7M entries, 55,000 visas, ~1 in 267 odds. Requirements: high-school diploma or 2 years' qualifying work. From DV-2028 a valid passport scan is mandatory. Only entry is free — any "agent" charging you is a scam.

Asylum & refugee status (brief): if you're physically in the US and fleeing persecution based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion or membership in a social group, you can file the free I-589 asylum application. It's a protection route, not a queue-based one — decisions take years and an attorney is strongly advised. Resettlement through the US Refugee Admissions Program requires a UNHCR or NGO referral from outside the US.

Visitors, Tourists & Short Stays (ESTA & B-2)

No green card needed for a visit — but you can never work on a visitor status, and in most cases you can't switch to a work visa from inside the country.

B-2 process steps

  1. Complete the DS-160 form online
  2. Pay the $185 fee and schedule an interview at the nearest US embassy or consulate
  3. Bring your passport, DS-160 confirmation, appointment letter and evidence of ties home (job, family, property)
  4. Attend the interview — officers often decide the same day; approved visas typically issue within days
Hard rule: tourist status (ESTA or B-2) strictly prohibits work. ESTA can be neither extended nor changed to another status. If you're offered a job while visiting, the employer must petition for a work visa from abroad — you leave, then return.

Business Visitors (B-1)

The B-1 covers short business trips: meetings, conferences, negotiations, site visits, consultations and contract signing. Same $185 fee and typically the same 6-month stay as the B-2 — most travellers receive the combined B-1/B-2 visa.

Allowed B-1 activities

Attending meetings and conferences, negotiating contracts, site visits and inspections, consulting with business partners, attending trade shows, and participating in training while remaining paid by your home employer.

What B-1 forbids

You cannot be paid by a US source, cannot perform productive local work, cannot fill a US position and cannot be employed by the host company. The visitor is paid abroad and must leave at the end of the trip.

Recommended documents: a formal invitation letter from the US host company stating purpose and dates, your employer's letter confirming salary and role, an itinerary, and evidence you'll return home (return ticket, ongoing job).

ESTA business trips: VWP nationals can carry out the same B-1 business activities on ESTA — meetings, conferences and negotiations are fine for up to 90 days without a visa.

Investors & Entrepreneurs (E-2, L-1A → EB-1C, EB-5)

E-2 · Treaty Investor Renewable

For citizens of ~80 treaty countries who invest "substantial" capital in a real, operating US business they direct and control. There's no statutory minimum, but ~$100K+ is the practical bar. Renewable indefinitely; spouses may work. It does not itself lead to a green card — but a scaled-up business can later fund an EB-5 or EB-1C.

L-1A → EB-1C No PERM

The classic executive route: work 1 year for a foreign company as a manager/executive, transfer to its US office on L-1A (max 7 years), then the employer files EB-1C — a green card with no labour certification. The full cycle typically takes 3–5 years.

EB-5 · Immigrant Investor $800K+

Covered under Route 2: invest $800,000 (or $1.05M standard) in a business creating 10 US jobs for a conditional green card — the fastest immigrant route for the wealthy. Expect intense source-of-funds scrutiny, with a price rise to $1.4M in high-employment areas proposed from Jan 2027.

Investor caution: USCIS checks that investments are genuine and "at risk". Buying a small franchise or a minority stake rarely qualifies for E-2 — the investor must develop and direct the enterprise, and the money must truly be at risk.

From Green Card to US Citizenship

Permanent residence is the finish line for many — but naturalisation is open to most green card holders.

  • Timeline: 5 years as a green card holder (3 years if married to and living with a US citizen), with continuous residence and physical presence for roughly half that time
  • Requirements: good moral character, English ability and a pass on the US history/civics test (exemptions for older long-term residents)
  • Cost: N-400 naturalisation ~$710 online / $760 paper
  • Dual citizenship: allowed — most nationalities can keep their original passport
Why naturalise? Only citizens can vote, run for office, sponsor certain family members, hold federal jobs and travel with a US passport. Green cards can also be lost (abandonment, long absences, serious crime) — citizenship is permanent.

From Student to Green Card: The Most Common Path

  1. F-1 study in a STEM field (12–36 months OPT work right after)
  2. OPT job at an E-Verify employer for the STEM extension
  3. H-1B lottery entry (employer files; ~35% odds — multiple years' attempts allowed)
  4. EB-2/EB-3 sponsorship — employer files PERM + I-140; your priority date starts
  5. Wait for the cutoff (varies by nationality — current for most, years for India/China)
  6. I-485 adjustment → green card → citizenship after 5 years of permanent residence (3 for spouses)

For the student-side details — F-1 admission, OPT rules, the 24-month STEM extension and choosing a degree that converts into sponsorship — see Study in the USA.

Immigration FAQs

What's the fastest legal way to get a US green card?+

For most people: EB-1A self-petition (if you meet extraordinary-ability criteria — no employer, no backlog for most countries), or immediate-relative family sponsorship. EB-5 is fast but costs $800K+. For the average professional, H-1B → EB-2/EB-3 with a current priority date is the realistic route (~3–5 years total).

Can I immigrate without a job offer?+

Yes — EB-1A (extraordinary ability), EB-2 NIW (national interest waiver), family sponsorship and the DV lottery all allow self-application. Everything else (H-1B, standard EB-2/EB-3, L-1) requires a sponsoring employer.

Is the H-1B really a lottery?+

Yes. ~200,000–470,000 registrations compete for 85,000 visas each year, and selection is randomised (with higher wage levels now weighted). You can re-enter every year while your status permits. Some people win on the first try; others take 2–4 attempts.

Do F-1 students really get a green card?+

Yes, it's the most common immigrant profile — but it's a long game: OPT (1–3 yrs) → H-1B (3–6 yrs) → EB-2/EB-3 (1–4 yrs for most nationalities). Total commonly 6–10 years. Canadian, Irish and Australian students often find their home countries faster; see all guides.

How can I check my chances / status?+

Check the monthly Visa Bulletin for your category/country cutoff, and use USCIS processing times at egov.uscis.gov/processing-times. For a personal assessment, get admission guidance.

Can I switch from a tourist visa to a work visa while in the US?+

Generally no. B-1/B-2 visitors cannot change to most employment-based statuses from inside the US — the employer must petition for a work visa from abroad, and you leave and re-enter once approved. ESTA travellers cannot extend, change status or adjust at all; they must depart and apply for the proper visa.

Can I do business meetings on a tourist visa or ESTA?+

Yes. Meetings, conferences, negotiations and site visits are permitted B-1 activities and are allowed on ESTA too — as long as you are not paid by a US source and do no productive local work. Anything resembling employment requires a work visa.

ESTA or B-2 — which should I use?+

If your nationality is in the Visa Waiver Program, ESTA is cheaper ($21), instant and fine for up to 90-day visits — no interview. Use a B-2 visa if you need a longer stay (up to 6 months), want to visit for medical treatment, or were previously refused ESTA. Note ESTA cannot be extended or changed to another status.
