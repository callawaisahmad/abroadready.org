Study in Italy: The Complete 2026 Guide

Italy pairs Europe's most affordable public-university tuition with world-famous art, architecture, fashion and food — and, increasingly, strong English-taught programmes in engineering, design, economics and medicine. Here's everything: Universitaly, the student visa, real costs and what happens after you graduate.

Why Study in Italy?

Italy's public universities charge remarkably low tuition — typically €1,000–4,000/year for international students (even less with income-based reductions through the ISEE system). You get Europe's richest cultural heritage, a growing catalogue of English-taught degrees, and a student life that's more affordable than France, Germany or the Nordics. Architecture, design, engineering, economics, arts and medicine are the standouts.

Key 2026 change: non-EU students MUST complete pre-enrolment on the Universitaly portal (universitaly.it) before any visa application. Skipping it means no visa — even with an admission letter from a private university. See the visa section.

The Italy Student Visa (Type D) — Step by Step

Non-EU students studying for more than 90 days need a national Type D study visa from the Italian embassy or consulate in their home country.

Universitaly — the mandatory first step

  1. Secure admission to an Italian university (public or private)
  2. Create your Universitaly account and submit pre-enrolment (one university, one course, per academic year)
  3. The university validates your application; the consulate verifies within 4–6 weeks
  4. Receive the "green" confirmation → book your Type D visa appointment
  5. Pre-enrolment deadlines typically close July–September for the September intake

Required documents

  • Valid passport, admission letter, Universitaly confirmation
  • Dichiarazione di Valore (DoV) or CIMEA statement of comparability for your previous degree/diploma
  • Financial proof (~€6,947+/year — personal account or sponsor letter)
  • Health insurance (min €30,000 coverage) for visa + Italian SSN registration
  • Proof of accommodation; FBI/background check (required by many consulates for certain nationalities)
Most common rejection cause: mismatched names across documents. Make sure your passport, degree certificates, University letter and Universitaly form use the exact same name and date of birth.

Affordable & Easy-Entry Universities

Italy's public universities are a genuine bargain for internationals: income-based (ISEE) fees that can drop to €0–1,000/year, flat fees as low as €306 for non-EU students, and many programs with open admission (libero accesso) — no entrance exam. The private picks (Pegaso, eCampus) are the easiest route for online learners. Apply through the national Universitaly portal.

Degrees, Intakes & Admission

🎓 Bachelor's (Laurea) — 3 years

Italian-taught for most; English-taught options growing (engineering, economics). Application windows generally open in autumn/spring for the September intake. You need a high-school diploma + DoV/CIMEA comparability.

📘 Master's (Magistrale) — 2 years

A large and growing English-taught selection, especially at Politecnico di Milano, Bologna, Padua, Turin. B2 English or Italian typically required. Some programmes need a portfolio or entrance exam (medicine, architecture).

🩺 Medicine & surgery

English-taught (IMAT exam), 6 years, extremely competitive — but Italian public tuition makes it far cheaper than the UK or US. Only a small quota per year for non-EU students.

🎨 Design, fashion & arts

NABA, Polimoda, IED and Politecnico di Milano lead. Portfolio + interview required; these private schools cost €12K–20K/yr but are globally recognised.

After Graduation: Stay & Work

Italy wants to keep its international graduates. After your degree you can apply for the 12-month job-search permit (Permesso di Attesa Occupazione), then convert your student residence permit into a work permit. Under reforms (Legislative Decree 20/2023), conversion no longer depends on the annual Decreto Flussi quota windows — you can switch to a work permit any time you have a job offer. A related field matters: work must match your qualification.

  • Students can work up to 20 hrs/week (1,040 hrs/yr) during study
  • Working on a student permit requires a Codice Fiscale (Italian tax code)
  • After 5 years of legal residence → long-term residence permit (Permesso di Soggiorno UE per Soggiornanti di Lungo Periodo)
  • After 10 years → citizenship (4 years for EU citizens); dual nationality allowed

For the full work-visa picture (EU Blue Card, Decreto Flussi, digital nomad), see Immigrate to Italy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is study in Italy expensive?+

No — public universities charge €1,000–4,000/year (income-scaled via ISEE), far below the US/UK/Australia. Private business/design schools cost €12K–20K. Living costs run €430–1,200/month. Total realistic budget: €12K–18K/year outside Milan, €18K–25K in Milan/Rome.

Can I study in Italy in English?+

Yes — there are hundreds of English-taught degree programmes, concentrated at master's level and in engineering, economics, computer science and medicine. B2 English (IELTS 6.0–6.5) is typically required. Bachelor's programmes in English exist but are fewer.

Do I need Universitaly for every Italian university?+

Yes — pre-enrolment on Universitaly is mandatory for ALL non-EU students needing a study visa, including at private universities. It's free, and it's the only route to the visa. One application per academic year.

Can I work while studying in Italy?+

Yes — up to 20 hrs/week during term and full-time during holidays (1,040 hrs/year cap). You'll need a Codice Fiscale and, for salaried work, a valid permit. English teaching, tourism and IT are the most accessible sectors.

Can I stay in Italy after my degree?+

Yes — 12-month job-search permit, then convert to a work permit when you land a job. Since 2023 you can convert any time (not tied to the Decreto Flussi windows). After 5 years' residence you can apply for long-term EU residence.
