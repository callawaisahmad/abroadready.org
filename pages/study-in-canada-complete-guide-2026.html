Study in Canada: Complete 2026 Guide (Admissions, Visa, Costs & PR)

🗓️ Aug 17, 2026⏱️ 28 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Studying in Canada is one of the smartest decisions an international student can make in 2026. Canada offers world-class universities, affordable tuition compared to the US and UK, and the clearest pathway to permanent residence of any English-speaking country. With the Student Direct Stream (SDS) guaranteeing visa decisions in 20 days, the Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) giving up to 3 years of open work rights, and Express Entry turning Canadian education and work experience into a direct route to PR, Canada is not just a study destination — it is a life strategy.

This comprehensive guide covers everything: how the Canadian education system works, exact tuition fees for every level, the top 10 universities and top colleges, the SDS vs regular visa streams, step-by-step application processes, PGWP details, Express Entry and PR pathways, scholarships, living costs, work rights, and a month-by-month application timeline. Whether you are applying from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria or anywhere else, this is the only Canada guide you need.

Key Takeaways

  • Canada hosts over 800,000 international students and is the second most popular study destination in the world.
  • The SDS programme guarantees a study permit decision in 20 calendar days for eligible applicants.

  • The PGWP gives 8 months to 3 years of open work after graduation — any employer, any job.

Why Study in Canada?

Canada is the world's most popular destination for international students who want a degree and a realistic path to permanent residence. More than 800,000 international students chose Canada in 2025, and the number continues to grow. But popularity alone is not the reason — the reason is that Canada's entire immigration system is designed to turn international graduates into permanent residents and eventually citizens.

The PR pathway is the strongest among English-speaking countries. Unlike the US, UK or Australia, Canada has a dedicated immigration stream — the Canadian Experience Class (CEC) — that specifically rewards graduates of Canadian institutions who gain work experience in Canada. Combine that with a Provincial Nominee Programme (PNP) nomination worth an extra 600 CRS points, and the path from student to permanent resident is genuinely predictable and achievable.

SDS delivers visa decisions in 20 calendar days. The Student Direct Stream is an expedited processing route for students from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, China, Brazil and other eligible countries. You get a decision in under 3 weeks, not the 8 to 12 weeks that regular processing takes. This speed matters because it gives you certainty — you know your visa outcome early enough to book flights, arrange housing and plan your move.

The PGWP gives you 1 to 3 years of open work. The Post-Graduation Work Permit lets you work for any employer in any occupation across Canada. There is no employer sponsorship required, no job offer needed, and no restriction on the type of work. This is the bridge between studying and settling permanently.

Quality of life is among the highest in the world. Canada consistently ranks in the top 10 globally for safety, healthcare, personal freedom and quality of life. Cities like Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal and Ottawa are diverse, multicultural and welcoming. International students are protected by provincial consumer protection laws and have access to campus health services, counselling and legal support.

You can work while studying. International students with a valid study permit and a Social Insurance Number (SIN) can work up to 24 hours per week off campus during regular academic sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks like winter and summer holidays. On-campus work has no hour limit. This means you can earn CAD 5,000 to 12,000 per year to offset living costs.

Tuition is genuinely affordable. Compared to the US (where international tuition averages USD 40,000 to 60,000), Canada's fees are significantly lower across every level — undergraduate, postgraduate, college diploma and MBA. And with scholarships ranging from CAD 2,000 to 50,000 per year, the net cost can be remarkably competitive.

The Canadian Education System

Canada's education system is managed at the provincial and territorial level, not by the federal government. This means standards, curricula and funding vary by province. However, all provinces maintain high quality — Canadian institutions consistently rank among the best in the world, and the system is recognised globally by employers and other universities.

Universities are degree-granting institutions. Canada has over 100 universities, of which roughly 94 are Designated Learning Institutions (DLIs) eligible to host international students. Universities focus on academic research and grant bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees. The University of Toronto, McGill University and the University of British Columbia are among the world's top 50 universities.

Colleges (also called polytechnics or institutes of technology) focus on applied education. They offer certificates (1 year), diplomas (2 years), advanced diplomas (3 years) and some offer applied bachelor's degrees. Colleges are an excellent pathway for students who want practical, job-ready skills. Top colleges include Seneca, Humber, Conestoga, George Brown, Langara and Algonquin. Many college programmes include co-op terms — paid work placements built into the curriculum.

CEGEP is a uniquely Quebec institution. Students in Quebec complete secondary school at age 16, then attend a CEGEP for 2 years (pre-university or technical programmes) before entering university. This means Quebec bachelor's programmes are typically 3 years instead of 4. International students do not need to attend CEGEP unless they want to study in Quebec's specific education pathway.

Degree types and durations:

  • Certificate: 1 year — focused vocational training in a specific skill area
  • Diploma: 2 years — broader applied education, common at colleges
  • Advanced Diploma: 3 years — more in-depth applied study, sometimes includes co-op
  • Bachelor's Degree: 4 years (3 years in Quebec) — comprehensive academic education
  • Master's Degree: 1 to 2 years — specialised research or professional training
  • PhD: 3 to 5 years — original research under faculty supervision

Co-op programmes are one of Canada's biggest advantages. A co-op programme alternates study terms with paid work terms, typically 4 months each. Students in co-op programmes at the University of Waterloo, for example, complete 6 paid work terms during their degree, earning CAD 30,000 to 50,000 over the course of their studies while building a professional network in Canada.

The academic year has three semesters: Fall (September to December), Winter (January to April) and Summer (May to August). Most programmes start in September, with fewer options for January intake and limited summer starts.

Tuition Fees — Detailed Breakdown

Tuition fees in Canada vary significantly by institution type, programme and province. Below are the 2025-2026 international student rates, which represent the most current data available for planning a 2026-2027 start.

Undergraduate Tuition

International undergraduate tuition ranges from CAD 20,000 to 35,000 per year at most institutions. However, top-tier universities charge considerably more:

  • University of Toronto: CAD 45,000 to 65,000/year depending on programme
  • McGill University: CAD 30,000 to 55,000/year
  • University of British Columbia: CAD 42,000 to 58,000/year
  • University of Alberta: CAD 30,000 to 40,000/year
  • University of Waterloo: CAD 42,000 to 60,000/year
  • Brandon University (affordable): approximately CAD 16,900/year

Postgraduate Tuition

International postgraduate tuition ranges from CAD 15,000 to 40,000 per year for most master's programmes. Research-based master's programmes may offer funding through teaching or research assistantships, which can cover tuition and provide a stipend.

College Diploma Tuition

College diploma programmes are the most affordable option: CAD 10,000 to 18,000 per year. This makes colleges an excellent choice for students on tighter budgets who still want a Canadian credential and PGWP eligibility.

MBA Tuition

MBA programmes in Canada range from CAD 30,000 to 80,000 per year. Top-ranked MBA programmes at Rotman (University of Toronto), Ivey (Western University), and Desautels (McGill) are at the higher end. More affordable MBA options exist at universities like the University of Calgary, Wilfrid Laurier and Brock University.

Engineering and Computer Science

Engineering and computer science programmes are among the most popular and typically cost CAD 25,000 to 45,000 per year at the undergraduate level. The University of Waterloo, University of Toronto and UBC charge at the top of this range.

Medicine

Medical programmes for international students are expensive: CAD 50,000 to 80,000 per year. Limited seats are available to international students, and competition is extremely intense.

Living Costs by City

Living costs are a significant part of your budget and vary dramatically by city:

Toronto and Vancouver: The most expensive cities. Expect CAD 15,000 to 20,000 per year for rent, food, transport and personal expenses. Rent for a room in a shared apartment runs CAD 800 to 1,500 per month.

Montreal and Ottawa: More affordable. CAD 12,000 to 15,000 per year. Montreal is one of the best-value student cities in the world — high quality of life with relatively low rents. Ottawa, as the national capital, offers excellent amenities at moderate cost.

Alberta and Manitoba (Calgary, Edmonton, Winnipeg): The most affordable major cities. CAD 10,000 to 13,000 per year. Edmonton and Winnipeg have some of the lowest rents of any Canadian city, and Alberta has no provincial sales tax, which saves you money on everyday purchases.

Atlantic Canada (Halifax, St. John's, Fredericton): Very affordable at CAD 10,000 to 14,000 per year. These provinces actively recruit international students and offer generous provincial nominee programmes for PR.

SDS vs Regular Stream — Which Should You Choose?

The Student Direct Stream (SDS) and the regular study permit stream are the two routes for obtaining a Canadian study permit. Understanding the difference is critical for planning your application timeline and choosing the right documents.

SDS (Student Direct Stream)

SDS is an expedited processing route that guarantees a study permit decision within 20 calendar days. It is available to students from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, China, Brazil, Colombia, Costa Rica, Indonesia, Morocco, Peru, the Philippines, Senegal, Trinidad and Tobago, and Vietnam.

SDS requirements:

  • Acceptance letter from a Designated Learning Institution (DLI)
  • A Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) of CAD 20,635 from a participating Canadian bank
  • First-year tuition paid in full (receipt required)
  • IELTS 6.0 overall with no band below 6.0 (academic or general training)
  • Medical exam (if required for your country)
  • Police clearance (if required)

SDS applications are processed online through the IRCC portal. The 20-day clock starts when IRCC receives a complete application with all documents. In practice, most SDS decisions come within 2 to 3 weeks.

Regular Stream

The regular study permit stream takes 8 to 12 weeks for South Asian applicants and 4 to 8 weeks for most other countries. It is more flexible on financial proof — you can show bank statements instead of a GIC, and there is no strict minimum for language test scores (though you still need to meet your institution's requirements).

Regular stream requirements:

  • Acceptance letter from a DLI
  • Proof of financial support: bank statements, GIC, scholarship letter, or sponsor's financial documents
  • Language test results (per institution requirements)
  • Medical exam (if required)
  • Police clearance (if required)

Which should you choose? If you are from an SDS-eligible country, SDS is almost always the better option. The speed alone — 20 days versus 8 to 12 weeks — makes it worth preparing the GIC and paying tuition upfront. The GIC is not an extra cost; it is your living expense fund that you get back in monthly instalments after arriving in Canada.

Application Process — Step by Step

The Canadian study permit application process has clear, sequential steps. Here is the complete process from research to landing in Canada:

Step 1: Choose your DLI. Check the IRCC DLI list to confirm your institution is eligible. Not all Canadian institutions can host international students.

Step 2: Apply to your chosen institution. Submit your application directly through the university or college's admissions portal. You will need transcripts, language test scores, a statement of purpose, and possibly letters of recommendation. Allow 4 to 8 weeks for a decision. See our statement of purpose guide for help.

Step 3: Receive your acceptance letter. Once accepted, the institution will send an official acceptance letter. For SDS, you need this letter to confirm your DLI number, programme name and start date.

Step 4: Buy your GIC (for SDS). Purchase a Guaranteed Investment Certificate of CAD 20,635 from a participating bank. The main options are ScotiaBank (via SBI Canada), CIBC, and ICICI Bank Canada. You transfer the funds, and the bank issues a GIC certificate that you include in your visa application.

Step 5: Pay your first-year tuition. Transfer your first year's tuition to the institution and obtain a receipt. This is required for SDS applications.

Step 6: Take your IELTS. Book and take the IELTS exam. For SDS, you need a minimum of 6.0 overall with no band below 6.0. Most universities require 6.5 overall. Book your test at least 2 to 3 months before you plan to apply, as test slots fill up quickly. Our IELTS vs PTE vs Duolingo comparison covers all accepted tests.

Step 7: Apply for your study permit. Apply online through the IRCC portal. Create an account, complete the application form, upload your documents and pay the fees.

Step 8: Biometrics. After submitting your application, you will receive a Biometric Instruction Letter (BIL). Visit your nearest Visa Application Centre (VAC) to give your fingerprints and photograph. This must be done within 30 days of receiving the BIL.

Step 9: Medical exam. If required for your country, undergo a medical examination with an IRCC-approved panel physician. Schedule this early — results take 1 to 2 weeks to reach IRCC.

Step 10: Wait for your decision. SDS applications: 20 calendar days. Regular stream: 4 to 12 weeks depending on country and time of year. You can check your status online through the IRCC portal.

Student Visa — Study Permit Requirements

A study permit is the official document that allows you to study in Canada for more than 6 months. It is not a visa — you will also receive a visitor visa (temporary resident visa) or an electronic travel authorisation (eTA) to enter Canada. The study permit itself is what lets you stay and study.

Core Requirements

  • Acceptance letter from a DLI: Your official letter from a Designated Learning Institution confirming your admission to a full-time programme.
  • Proof of financial support: Either a GIC of CAD 20,635 (for SDS) or bank statements showing you can cover tuition and living expenses. The minimum living expense threshold is CAD 20,635 per year outside Quebec.
  • Tuition payment receipt: Proof that you have paid your first year's tuition (required for SDS, recommended for all).
  • Language test results: IELTS 6.0+ for SDS; institution-specific scores for regular stream. Canada accepts IELTS, PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, Duolingo English Test and Cambridge C1 Advanced.
  • Medical exam: Required for students from certain countries. An IRCC-approved panel physician conducts the exam. Results are sent directly to IRCC.
  • Police clearance: Required if you have a criminal record or have lived in certain countries for more than 6 months.
  • Valid passport: Your passport must be valid for the entire duration of your intended study. If it expires during your studies, you will need to renew it before applying.
  • Two passport-sized photographs: Meeting IRCC specifications.
  • Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL): Required for most undergraduate programmes starting in 2026. Your institution requests this from the provincial government on your behalf. Master's and PhD students are exempt.

Fees

  • Study permit application fee: CAD 150 (non-refundable)
  • Biometrics fee: CAD 85 (valid for 10 years)
  • Medical exam: approximately CAD 200 to 300
  • Total upfront cost: approximately CAD 435 to 535 for the permit itself, plus tests and translations

Processing Times

  • SDS: 20 calendar days guaranteed
  • Regular stream from South Asia: 8 to 12 weeks
  • Regular stream from other countries: 4 to 8 weeks

Country-Specific Notes

  • Pakistan: Apply through IRCC Islamabad. SDS processing is efficient. Pakistani students are one of the largest groups in the SDS programme.
  • India: Apply through IRCC Delhi, Mumbai, Chandigarh, or other visa offices. High volume means peak-season delays are possible — apply early.
  • Bangladesh: Apply through IRCC Dhaka. SDS is available and processing times are competitive.
  • Nigeria: Apply through IRCC Abuja or Lagos. Regular stream processing typically takes 6 to 8 weeks.

PGWP — Post-Graduation Work Permit

The Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) is one of the most valuable immigration benefits Canada offers to international students. It allows graduates of eligible Canadian programmes to work in Canada after completing their studies, building the work experience needed for permanent residence.

Eligibility

  • You must have completed a programme at an eligible DLI that is at least 8 months long
  • You must have maintained full-time student status during every semester of your programme
  • You must apply within 180 days of receiving your final marks or graduation confirmation
  • You must have a valid study permit at the time of application (or have held one within the last 180 days)

Duration

The PGWP duration matches your programme length:

  • Programme 8 months to less than 2 years: PGWP valid for the same length as your programme
  • Programme 2 years or more: PGWP valid for 3 years
  • Minimum PGWP duration: 8 months
  • Maximum PGWP duration: 3 years

For the full 3-year PGWP, your programme must be at least 2 years (4 semesters) in length. This is an important consideration when choosing between a 1-year and a 2-year programme — the extra year of study can give you an extra 2 years of work rights.

Key Rules

  • The PGWP is an open work permit — you can work for any employer in any occupation
  • You can work full-time immediately while your PGWP application is processing if you applied before your study permit expired
  • The PGWP is available only once — you cannot get a second PGWP after completing another programme
  • The PGWP costs CAD 255
  • Processing time: typically 80 to 180 days

Strategic Considerations

If your goal is permanent residence, choosing a 2-year programme is almost always better than a 1-year programme. The 3-year PGWP gives you more time to gain work experience, settle into the Canadian job market, and build your Express Entry profile. It also provides a safety buffer if your first job search takes a few months.

Express Entry — The PR Pathway

Express Entry is Canada's primary system for managing permanent residence applications for skilled workers. It covers three programmes, and for international graduates, the Canadian Experience Class (CEC) is the most relevant.

Canadian Experience Class (CEC)

The CEC is designed for people who have worked in Canada. The requirements are:

  • At least 1 year of skilled work experience in Canada (NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3) within the last 3 years
  • The work experience must be gained while holding a valid work permit (your PGWP counts)
  • Language proficiency: CLB 7 for TEER 0 or 1 jobs, CLB 5 for TEER 2 or 3 jobs
  • Plan to live outside the province of Quebec

Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS)

Express Entry uses the CRS score to rank candidates. Points are awarded for:

  • Age: Maximum points at ages 20-29; declining after 30
  • Education: Points for Canadian and foreign education; Canadian education gets bonus points
  • Language: Points for English and/or French proficiency; higher scores earn more points
  • Work experience: Points for Canadian and foreign work experience; Canadian experience is weighted more heavily
  • Additional factors: Provincial nomination (+600 points), Canadian education, French language proficiency, arranged employment

Typical CRS scores for CEC draws range from 430 to 520 points. A young graduate with a master's degree, strong IELTS scores (CLB 9+), and 1 year of Canadian work experience can expect a CRS score in the range of 460 to 510, which is competitive in most draws.

Provincial Nominee Programmes (PNPs)

Every Canadian province and territory has its own immigration programme that can nominate you for permanent residence. A PNP nomination adds 600 CRS points to your Express Entry profile, effectively guaranteeing an invitation to apply for PR.

Many provinces have dedicated streams for international graduates:

  • Ontario (OINP): International Student Stream for master's and PhD graduates
  • British Columbia (BC PNP): International Graduate stream with priority processing
  • Alberta (AAIP): Alberta Opportunity Stream for international graduates
  • Manitoba (MPNP): International Education Stream for international graduates
  • Saskatchewan (SINP): Student Category for graduates of Saskatchewan institutions
  • Atlantic provinces: Atlantic Immigration Programme — one of the most accessible PR pathways for international graduates

The Complete Study-to-PR Timeline

The most common pathway looks like this:

  1. Study in Canada for 2 to 4 years
  2. Obtain a PGWP (up to 3 years)
  3. Work in Canada for at least 1 year in a skilled occupation
  4. Apply through Express Entry (Canadian Experience Class) or a Provincial Nominee Programme
  5. Receive permanent residence — processing typically takes 6 months through Express Entry

From the start of your studies to landing PR, the total timeline is typically 4 to 6 years. This is the most straightforward and predictable PR pathway available to international students anywhere in the world.

Scholarships in Canada

Canada offers a wide range of scholarships for international students. While fully funded scholarships are competitive, even partial awards can significantly reduce your costs. Here are the major opportunities:

Federal and National Scholarships

  • Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarships: CAD 50,000 per year for 3 years. For doctoral students demonstrating academic excellence, research potential and leadership. One of the most prestigious graduate scholarships in the world.
  • Canada Graduate Scholarships — Master's (CGS-M): CAD 12,000 to 27,000 for 1 to 2 years. For master's students at Canadian institutions. Highly competitive — only Canadian citizens and permanent residents are eligible.
  • Banting Postdoctoral Fellowships: CAD 70,000 per year for 2 years. For postdoctoral researchers at Canadian institutions.

University-Specific Scholarships

  • Lester B. Pearson Scholarship (University of Toronto): Fully funded for 4 years of undergraduate study. Covers tuition, books, incidental fees, and full residence support. One of the most prestigious undergraduate scholarships globally. Requires a nomination from your school.
  • UBC International Leader of Tomorrow Award: Up to CAD 40,000 per year for undergraduate study. Based on academic merit and demonstrated leadership.
  • McGill Entrance Scholarships: CAD 3,000 to 12,000 for automatic merit-based awards. Additional named scholarships available.
  • University of Waterloo International Student Entrance Scholarships: CAD 10,000 to 25,000 for first-year international students.
  • University of Alberta International Scholarships: Range from CAD 5,000 to 9,000 per year for undergraduate students.

College Scholarships

Many colleges offer scholarships of CAD 2,000 to 5,000 for international students. These are less competitive than university scholarships and can be a valuable offset for college tuition. Check individual college websites for current offerings.

Living in Canada as an International Student

Moving to Canada means adjusting to a new country, climate and culture. Here is what daily life looks like for international students.

Housing

  • On-campus housing: CAD 600 to 1,200 per month. Includes furnished rooms, meal plans (optional), utilities and internet. On-campus housing is convenient but limited — apply early. First-year students usually get priority.
  • Off-campus shared housing: CAD 800 to 1,500 per month for a room in a shared apartment. Use platforms like Kijiji, Facebook Marketplace, and Rentals.ca to find listings. Most cities have student-specific housing boards.
  • Homestay: CAD 800 to 1,400 per month. Live with a Canadian family, usually including meals. Good for cultural immersion and building English/French skills.

Food

Expect to spend CAD 300 to 500 per month on groceries and eating out. Cooking at home saves significantly — a weekly grocery shop at No Frills, Walmart or a local supermarket costs CAD 50 to 80. Campus cafeterias and fast food cost CAD 10 to 20 per meal.

Transportation

Public transport costs CAD 80 to 150 per month for a student transit pass. Most cities offer discounted student fares. Some universities include a transit pass in your tuition fees — check with your institution. Biking is popular in warmer months, and cities like Vancouver, Montreal and Ottawa have extensive cycling networks.

Health Insurance

Healthcare varies by province. Some provinces cover international students under their provincial health plan (Ontario's OHIP, for example, covers international students after 3 months). Others require you to buy private health insurance through your institution (UHIP at UofT, for example). Budget CAD 600 to 1,200 per year if your institution requires mandatory health insurance.

Banking

Open a Canadian bank account as soon as you arrive. The major banks — RBC, TD, Scotiabank, CIBC and BMO — all offer student accounts with no monthly fees and free transactions. You will need your study permit, passport and proof of enrolment to open an account. A Canadian debit card is essential for everyday transactions.

Phone and Internet

Mobile phone plans cost CAD 30 to 50 per month for a prepaid or student plan with data. Major providers include Rogers, Bell, Telus and their budget brands (Fido, Koodo, Virgin Plus). Campus Wi-Fi is usually included in tuition fees.

Working in Canada While Studying

International students in Canada have generous work rights that help offset living costs and build Canadian work experience — a critical factor for permanent residence.

Off-Campus Work

With a valid study permit and a Social Insurance Number (SIN), you can work up to 24 hours per week off campus during regular academic sessions (September to April). During scheduled breaks — winter break, summer break and reading weeks — you can work full-time (40 hours per week).

On-Campus Work

On-campus work has no hour limit. You can work at your university's library, bookstore, cafeteria, administration offices or research labs. On-campus jobs are convenient, flexible and relevant to your studies.

Co-op Work Permits

If your programme includes a mandatory co-op or internship component, you need a co-op work permit in addition to your study permit. This is a separate application but is usually processed quickly. Co-op work is full-time and typically pays CAD 15 to 25 per hour.

Typical Student Jobs and Wages

  • Food service (cafeteria, restaurant): CAD 15 to 18/hour
  • Retail (shops, malls): CAD 15 to 17/hour
  • Campus jobs (library, admin, tutoring): CAD 15 to 20/hour
  • Teaching or research assistant: CAD 18 to 25/hour
  • Freelance and online work: Variable

The minimum wage varies by province. Ontario's minimum wage is CAD 16.55/hour (2025-2026); British Columbia is CAD 17.40/hour; Alberta is CAD 15.00/hour. Most student jobs pay at or slightly above minimum wage.

Application Timeline — Month by Month

Planning is everything. Here is a month-by-month timeline for students targeting a September 2027 start. Adjust dates if you are targeting January or May intake.

12 to 18 Months Before (March to September 2025)

  • Research universities and colleges — compare tuition, programme content, co-op options and city cost of living
  • Take the IELTS exam — book early, as test dates fill up. Aim for 6.5+ overall to keep all options open
  • Start preparing your statement of purpose and gathering transcripts, recommendation letters and other documents
  • Research scholarships and their deadlines — many require early applications

10 to 12 Months Before (November 2025 to January 2026)

  • Apply to institutions — submit applications to 3 to 6 universities/colleges. Most application deadlines for September intake are January to March
  • Apply for scholarships — many institutional scholarships have deadlines aligned with admission deadlines
  • Request official transcripts and language test score reports to be sent to institutions

8 to 10 Months Before (March to May 2026)

  • Receive offer letters — accept your best offer and decline others
  • If applying via SDS: buy your GIC from a participating Canadian bank
  • Pay your first-year tuition and obtain a receipt
  • Begin the PAL application if required (your institution initiates this for undergraduates)

6 to 8 Months Before (May to July 2026)

  • Apply for your study permit — submit your application online through the IRCC portal
  • Gather all supporting documents: acceptance letter, GIC certificate, tuition receipt, language test scores, passport photos
  • Schedule your medical exam with an IRCC-approved panel physician if required for your country

4 to 6 Months Before (July to September 2026)

  • Biometrics appointment — visit your nearest Visa Application Centre within 30 days of receiving your Biometric Instruction Letter
  • Follow up on your medical exam results if applicable
  • Begin researching housing options — apply for on-campus housing or start searching for off-campus rooms

2 to 3 Months Before (October to November 2026)

  • Receive your visa decision — if via SDS, this should already be decided. If regular stream, follow up with IRCC if it has been more than 12 weeks
  • Book your flight to Canada — book early for better prices
  • Arrange health insurance if your province or institution requires private coverage
  • Contact your institution's international student office for orientation information

1 to 2 Months Before (November to December 2026)

  • Finalise your housing arrangements
  • Prepare your arrival budget — bring enough cash or arrange a Canadian bank account for initial expenses
  • Attend pre-departure orientations offered by your institution or education agents
  • Pack for Canadian weather — winters are cold, especially in Ontario, Quebec and the prairies

Arrival in Canada

  • Present your study permit and documents at port of entry (airport or land border)
  • Get your study permit stamped at the port of entry (if not already issued)
  • Apply for a Social Insurance Number (SIN) — required for working and filing taxes
  • Open a Canadian bank account
  • Attend your institution's international student orientation
  • Set up your phone plan and get a Canadian SIM card

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to study in Canada?+

International undergraduate tuition ranges from CAD 20,000 to 35,000 per year. Postgraduate tuition ranges from CAD 15,000 to 40,000 per year. College diploma programmes are CAD 10,000 to 18,000 per year. Living costs are CAD 10,000 to 20,000 per year depending on city.

What is the SDS programme?+

The Student Direct Stream (SDS) is an expedited study permit processing route that guarantees a decision within 20 calendar days. You need an acceptance letter from a Designated Learning Institution (DLI), a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) of CAD 20,635, first-year tuition paid, and IELTS 6.0 overall with no band below 6.0.

Can I work while studying in Canada?+

Yes. International students can work up to 24 hours per week off campus during regular academic sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks. On-campus work has no hour limit. You need a Social Insurance Number (SIN) to work.

What is the PGWP?+

The Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) allows graduates of eligible Canadian programmes to work in Canada for 8 months to 3 years, depending on the length of their programme. It is an open work permit, meaning you can work for any employer. This is the main pathway to permanent residence through Express Entry.

How do I get permanent residence in Canada after studying?+

The most common pathway is: complete a Canadian programme, obtain a PGWP, gain 1 year of skilled work experience in Canada, then apply through Express Entry (Canadian Experience Class). Provincial Nominee Programmes (PNPs) offer additional pathways. Some provinces have dedicated streams for international graduates.

What are the best universities in Canada?+

Top universities include University of Toronto, McGill University, University of British Columbia, University of Alberta, McMaster University, University of Waterloo, University of Calgary, and Western University. For college programmes, top choices include Seneca, Humber, Conestoga, and George Brown.

Do I need IELTS to study in Canada?+

For SDS, you need IELTS 6.0 overall with no band below 6.0. Most universities require IELTS 6.5 overall. Canada also accepts PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, Duolingo English Test, and Cambridge C1 Advanced. Some institutions offer conditional admission with later language proof.

How long does the Canada student visa take?+

Via SDS: 20 calendar days guaranteed. Regular study permit processing from South Asia: 8 to 12 weeks. From other countries: 4 to 8 weeks. Apply at least 3 to 4 months before your program start date.

What is a GIC and how do I get one?+

A Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) is a Canadian investment product that proves you have funds for living expenses. For SDS, you need CAD 20,635. You buy it from a Canadian bank (ScotiaBank, CIBC, ICICI Bank Canada) and the funds are released in monthly instalments after you arrive in Canada.

What scholarships are available in Canada?+

Major scholarships include Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarships (CAD 50,000/year for 3 years), Lester B. Pearson Scholarship (University of Toronto, fully funded), Trudeau Foundation Scholarships, and university-specific entrance scholarships ranging from CAD 2,000 to 40,000.
