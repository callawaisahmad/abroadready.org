CAS vs COE vs COA: Study Visa Letters

🗓️ Sep 14, 2026⏱️ 17 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

For most Pakistani students, the study abroad journey starts before you ever touch a visa application form: a university or a government sends you a letter that proves you have a confirmed place to study. Three acronyms dominate this stage, and they get confused all the time: CAS vs COE vs COA. The Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies runs the UK system, the Certificate of Eligibility runs Japan, and the Confirmation of Acceptance runs Australia. E

ach is a different document from a different institution, carrying different data and following different timelines. Understand one, and the other two become variations on the same idea. Confuse them, and you can waste months, pay visa fees twice, or lose an intake completely. This guide explains what each letter means, the data it carries, how quickly it arrives, how to fix errors in it, and what proof of funds you need to place alongside it. A

ll figures are as of 2026, and you should always verify current details on the official portal before you pay anything.

There is a family likeness here that makes the letters easy to compare. In every case, the letter is the bridge between an acceptance and a visa. It exists because a visa officer cannot personally call every university in the world to check whether you are really enrolled. The letter does that verification for them: it is a trusted machine readable record issued by an authority the officer already trusts. T

hat is why the letter is not a luxury or a formality. It is the load-bearing document of your whole application, and a single error in it can sink a file that took you months to build. Treat it that seriously from the first email you receive.

What each letter means: CAS, COE, COA and the two neighbours you will also meet

The UK issues a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies, universally shortened to CAS. It is not a certificate you download from the government. Your university sponsor generates it inside the Home Office online system and gives it to you as a reference number plus a supporting statement, usually as a PDF. The CAS number is a fourteen digit code, and you type that number into your UK Student visa application form. W

ithout a valid CAS, you cannot even begin a Student visa application, because the entire form depends on it. A fresh CAS is normally issued to new students after they accept an offer and pay the required deposit. A CAS can also be issued for a pre sessional English course, in which case the course dates on the letter will reflect that shorter programme, and you will receive a second CAS later for the main degree if the conditions require it.

Japan works differently but sits in the same family. Instead of a CAS, you receive a Certificate of Eligibility, the COE, from the Immigration Services Agency of Japan. There is usually an extra step: your school or university files the COE application on your behalf with Japanese immigration, and only when that application is approved does Japanese immigration issue the COE to you. The COE is a printed certificate, not a number you type. Y

ou submit the original with your visa application at the Japanese embassy or consulate, and the visa officer uses it to issue your visa. The COE carries its own reference number, your name in the exact order shown on your passport, your university, the course, and the period of stay you are permitted. B

ecause immigration runs the check before the embassy sees you, a person holding a COE is usually granted the visa quickly, and the COE is also what you must show at the airport when you land.

Australia uses the Confirmation of Acceptance for overseas students, abbreviated COA. When you accept an offer from an Australian university, it sends you a COA through the online provider portal. The COA contains a unique code that goes directly into your student visa form, much like a CAS number. A

ustralia also relies on a separate enrolment check: the university reports to the Department of Home Affairs through an electronic system, so a visa officer does not have to trust only a paper document. This is why an Australian change of course or extension of stay sometimes updates your record before you ever hold a new certificate, and why the record itself matters as much as the paper.

You will also meet two neighbours of these three letters. Germany does not issue a national study visa letter in quite the same way. German universities send an admission letter, the Zulassung, under the university's own letterhead, and that document is what you attach to your visa application at the German embassy. It is trustworthy because German institutions are registered and enrolled on official lists, and because the applicant's records can be checked electronically. T

he United States issues the Form I 20, the Certificate of Eligibility for Nonimmigrant Student Status. The I 20 is produced by the SEVP certified school and carries a SEVIS number that the school must keep active, and a school can even cancel an I 20 if you stop attending. So across all five destinations, the underlying rule is identical: an approved institution vouches for you through a document that a visa officer can verify. For Pakistani applicants, that shared logic is more useful than memorising each acronym separately.

The data inside the letter, and why visa officers check every single field

A confirmation letter only protects you if the data it carries is exactly right. Visa officers across the world are trained to cross-check the letter against your passport, your application form, and, where the system allows, the university's own electronic record. Any mismatch becomes a red flag, and red flags become delays, extra documents, or refusal. So what actually sits inside these letters?

  • Your full legal name, in the exact order and spelling shown on your passport. Pakistani passports sometimes split a compound given name, and the university may have kept the order you wrote on the application rather than the order on the passport.
  • The course title, level, and start and end dates. A letter dated for the wrong intake, or showing an expired course, makes the whole file untrustworthy, and a short course window can mean a shorter visa than you budgeted for.
  • The name of the institution and its campus, plus a sponsor reference. For the UK this is the sponsor licence number, which tells an officer which university vouches for you, and whether that licence is current.
  • The qualifying requirements attached to the letter. In the UK this can be a condition, for example passing an English test or completing a previous degree certificate, that you must satisfy before enrolment, and officers read unmet conditions carefully.
  • The amount of tuition and accommodation you have already paid, which some letters state explicitly and which officers read against your proof of funds.

Why does the visa officer care about a line like "tuition already paid"? Because every study visa system is designed to prove two things at once: that you are a genuine student, and that you are not planning to enter the country mainly to work or to stay indefinitely. The letter shows the first. The money you paid and the funds you hold shows the second. A CAS that says your course costs 22,000 pounds and a bank statement that shows only 300,000 rupees will be read together, and loans, gifts and sudden deposits all have to be explained, not just declared on a form.

cas vs coe vs coa visa process

Japan is the strictest on name matching because the COE is a physical certificate that travels with you. When you show it to an officer at Narita or Haneda, every character must match your passport and your residence record. A

ustralia reads your COA code electronically, but the same principle applies: the officer pulls up your enrolment record, and if one letter of your name differs, the record does not match the passport, and you wait at the airport while they reconcile the two. The practical lesson for a Pakistani student is to treat the letter as a reflection of your passport and nothing else. I

f the letter and the passport ever disagree, the letter is wrong, not the passport, and the correction has to be made before you go anywhere near a visa form.

How each letter is issued, and how long it takes

The biggest practical difference between the three letters is who starts the process. In the UK, the university acts quickly and the CAS normally lands within ten to fifteen working days after you meet the conditions and pay any required deposit. Many UK universities issue CAS guidance first, then the actual CAS reference, and there is usually a fixed window to apply for your visa after the CAS is issued. A

pplying too early is impossible anyway, because the visa can only be granted 90 days before your course start date. Applying too late is risky, because Pakistani applicants already face longer UK processing windows: the separate guide on UK student visa delays from Pakistan explains the realistic timelines you should budget for, and why the CAS window is only the start of the waiting game.

Japan takes longer because the school first files a COE application with immigration, and Japanese immigration runs a background assessment before issuing anything. This is not a quick form. It involves the school submitting your passport copy, your certificate of eligibility application, your financial records, and sometimes a study plan. From the moment the school submits, you should plan for one to three months of waiting, and during the January to April peak the workload gets heavier. F

or scholarship students the route is different and faster: MEXT students, covered in detail in the MEXT scholarship guide for study in Japan, have the COE and visa arranged through the Japanese embassy in Islamabad rather than through an individual school application. If you hold a MEXT award, you do not chase a COE independently; the coordination follows the scholarship channel.

Australia is once again the closest to the UK in spirit. The COA is generated almost immediately once you accept an offer and the university confirms your enrolment details, often within a week of your deposit. Australian visa processing is a separate matter, and offshore student visa processing from Pakistan has been slow in 2025 and 2026, so the gap between receiving your COA and receiving a decision can be weeks or months depending on the season. The COA is not a guarantee of a quick visa; it is the green light to apply, and nothing more.

Germany and the United States add their own wrinkles. In Germany, the Zulassung arrives on university letterhead and its speed depends entirely on the university's own admission office; some send it within two weeks, others only after the semester filing closes. In the United States, the I 20 is created by the school, and you then pay the SEVIS fee and book an F 1 interview. T

he full F 1 process from Pakistan is documented step by step in the US F1 student visa guide from Pakistan, but the key timing point is that the school controls the I 20, only the school can cancel it, and an interview slot can cost you weeks in peak season no matter how fast the school moves.

The letter versus the visa: what each document actually does

A confusion that quietly ruins applications is the belief that the letter is the visa. It is not. The letter is evidence that you qualify; the visa is the decision. Between the two sit the application fee, the biometrics, the photograph, the interview in some countries, and the visa officer's judgement of everything you submitted. T

hink of the letter as the key ingredient and the visa as the meal: you can hold the best ingredient in the world and still fail if you cook it wrong, and "cook it wrong" here means signing the form with mismatched details, missing the proof of funds, or booking biometrics after the course start date.

The practical difference shows up in your travel planning. With a UK CAS in hand you have not yet bought a ticket; with a granted Student visa you are free to fly, and the usual rule is that you can enter the UK up to one week before your course starts for courses of six months or more. W

ith a Japanese COE you still need the visa sticker in your passport before you can book anything, and with an Australian COA you can purchase flights the moment the visa grant letter arrives. Each country prints its entry window differently, so anchor your flight booking to the visa grant date, not to the letter issuance date.

There is one more difference that matters most in an emergency. Some letters can be cancelled by their issuer at any time. A UK CAS can be withdrawn by the university if you miss a condition or fail to enrol, and a cancelled CAS takes your pending application down with it. An I 20 can be cancelled by the school if you fall off full time status, and an Australian COA can be revoked if your enrolment lapses. N

o officer at the border is going to ask you which happened; the decision simply turns against you. So the letter deserves maintenance throughout the process, not just on the day it arrives: keep your offer conditions met, keep your course enrolment active, and reply to every university email about your enrolment within a day.

Mistakes in letters: name order, course dates, and how to fix them

Most letter errors are not glamorous. They are small, annoying discrepancies that appear because a student typed something different on the application form than what appears on the passport. The three most common, and how to handle each, are below.

  1. Name mismatch. Your passport shows "MUHAMMAD ALI KHAN" but the CAS shows "MUHAMMAD ALI" only, or your F.Sc certificate spells your name differently, or your name order follows the English convention of given name first. Fix: email the university admissions office with a passport copy and ask for a corrected letter or a system name update. For the COA and the I 20, the school updates its record and reissues the electronic document. For the COE, the school must request an amendment through Japanese immigration, which takes time, so check your name the moment you receive the COE, not on the day before your visa appointment.
  2. Course dates. Your letter shows a January start when you applied for September, or the UK CAS shows course end dates that do not include your dissertation extension. Fix: ask the university to reissue with the correct period of study. Visa officers check dates against the study visa rules, and if the dates are too short you may receive a visa that expires before you finish, forcing an expensive extension. If they are too long, an officer may question whether the letter is genuine.
  3. A conditional requirement left unresolved. A UK CAS that still carries an unmet English condition can be refused, and an Australian COA linked to an offer that depends on final transcripts will fail the genuine student check if the condition is not met. Fix: meet the condition first, provide the evidence to the university, then request the updated record, and only then start your visa application.

Whatever the error, never try to correct a letter yourself. Do not white out text, do not edit a PDF, and do not reorder your name in the visa form to match the letter while your passport says something else. Any of those creates a mismatch that a trained eye will catch, and an edited document invites a refusal for misrepresentation rather than for a simple error, which is a far heavier outcome. I

nstead, get corrected documentation in writing from the issuing office, keep the email chain safe, and attach a short explanation note if the correction happened mid-application. Officers are used to corrections; they are not used to silent edits.

Proof of funds and the blocked account alongside the letter

A visa letter proves you belong in the system, but it does not prove you can pay. Every serious study destination asks for proof of funds as a separate requirement, and the two documents are always read together. For the UK, you generally need one month of held money within the 28 day window before the visa application, at set amounts for fees and living costs, with the exact figures shown on the official gov.uk pages. F

or Japan, the COE application itself usually requires proving funds at the school level, so by the time you hold a COE the money question has partly been checked, but the embassy can still review bank statements at the visa stage. For Australia, you need genuine access to funds evidence, and officers can query the source of deposits, especially large or sudden ones made a week before you apply.

Germany deserves a special mention because it uses a blocked account, the Sperrkonto, and combines it with the admission letter. You open the blocked account, deposit the required amount, and receive a confirmation certificate that is released back to you monthly or on a defined schedule. The embassy checks the admission letter together with the blocked account certificate and cross-checks the details electronically. F

or Pakistani students, the practical advice is the same everywhere: start the money process as early as the letter process. Do not let the university confirmation arrive and then spend three weeks assembling bank statements, because in the UK and Australia the two are read as one file, and a missing piece halts the whole application. A loose but complete file beats a perfect letter with no funds behind it.

What happens to the letter after the visa is granted

Once the visa lands, most students close the file and never think about the letter again, which is a mistake. The letter keeps working inside your system for months. In the UK, the CAS reference travels with your visa record, and when you arrive you pull your BRP or create your digital share code, both of which trace back to the same enrolment record that the CAS created. I

n Japan, the COE reference becomes the anchor of your residence card, your work permission, and your re entry permit, which is why losing the original COE means a real administrative trip to immigration, not a shrug. In Australia, the COA code underpins your enrolment confirmation audit, and your right to work, to renew, and eventually to apply for a temporary graduate visa all reference the same provider record. S

o the practical rule is simple: keep the original letter, the reference number, and the issuing email archived for the entire duration of your studies, not just for the visa phase.

Renewals are where the letter comes back to life. A UK Student visa extension normally needs a new CAS from your university, and a Japanese extension of stay uses your COE references as part of the file, and an Australian visa renewal revolves around a current COA confirming your ongoing enrolment. Every one of these renewals assumes you kept the chain of documents from the first letter to the latest one. S

tudents who threw away the original CAS PDF, or who let a COE print copy get faded and unreadable, find themselves paying for replacement documents and delay at the exact moment they can least afford it. Print everything twice, scan everything into a folder with a clear name, and store the folder on two devices plus a USB stick, because the cost of losing this one file is measured in weeks of waiting, not in rupees.

Dependants add a second layer. If you are planning to bring your spouse or children, study rules in most countries link the dependant visa holders to the principal student's visa, and the same confirmation references feed into their applications. A UK dependant, an Australian dependent, and a Japanese family visa all trace back to the student's enrolment record, so the accuracy of the original letter directly shapes what your family can and cannot do. I

f the principal letter is shorter than the course, the dependant permissions inherit that shortness, which is a quiet and painful way to find out. Double-checking the course end date on the original letter is therefore not just a visa stage chore; it is a family planning decision.

Finally, changing your course or university mid studies creates a new letter event. Universities treat a change of course as a fresh acceptance, which means a new CAS, a new COA on the new provider, or a new COE application through the new school, and the old letter's records are closed in parallel. R

ule of thumb: never assume that switching programmes keeps your existing letter valid, because the visa conditions of the new course may differ (shorter, longer, different level), and in some countries switching below degree level ends your work rights entirely. Ask the new university for a written confirmation of how the letter is reissued before you change anything, and archive both the old and the new letters side by side.

Three common refusal scenarios that trace back to the letter

If you want to imagine the worst cases before they happen, these three scenarios cover the practical majority of letter-linked refusals we see reported from Pakistan each year. Each one is preventable with the habits this guide has been building.

  1. The multi-year CAS gap. A student accepts a two year pathway offer, but the CAS is issued only for the first year, and no one tells them a second CAS will be needed. The visa arrives for one year, and the second year application collapses into a scramble. Fix: ask in writing how many letters your course will need and when each one arrives.
  2. The unsynchronised name. The passport, the intermediate certificate and the letter each spell the name slightly differently, and the student submits without reconciling them. The officer flags the mismatch and asks for an explanation, and the whole season is lost to correspondence. Fix: consolidate every document to the passport spelling before submitting.
  3. The condition never closed. The offer still carries a band requirement, the CAS is issued conditionally, and the officer reads the unmet condition as a signal that enrolment itself is in doubt. Fix: never start a visa application while any qualifying condition in the letter remains open.

Notice what all three have in common: they are not failures of marks, money or intent. They are failures of document maintenance, the boring work that this article keeps returning to. Do the maintenance, and the letter becomes what it is supposed to be: the strongest single document in your application, instead of the quiet reason it falls apart.

A pre-submission checklist before you press submit

Every successful applicant we have studied has a private routine before submitting anything, and you should build the same habit. Run this checklist on the letter itself, then on the application that uses it.

  1. Read the letter against your passport, character by character, twice: once when it arrives and once the night before you submit.
  2. Confirm the course dates match your offer letter, not your memory of it, and confirm the course title matches the one on your visa form.
  3. Confirm the money on your bank statement covers the fee and living figure that the letter implies, with the correct currency conversion and the correct holding period.
  4. Upload the letter in the exact format the portal expects, and keep both a PDF and a printed copy, because the interview officer may ask to see it.
  5. Set a reminder for the university's enrolment deadline, not the visa deadline: a visa granted three weeks into your course is useless if the university withdraws your place first.

This list looks obvious, which is exactly why it is skipped. In real applications, the difference between a smooth file and a mess is almost never talent and almost always order: the letter checked twice, the funds sitting for the right number of days, the name spelled identically in four places. Build the checklist into your calendar and you will almost never be the applicant who is rejected on a clerical detail, which is sadly the most common rejection of all.

Building your personal timeline around the letter

Here is a worked example to make the logic concrete. Say you are a Pakistani student applying to a UK university for a September intake. You accept the offer in March, get your degree transcripts checked, and pass your English requirement. In late July you pay the deposit, and within ten working days the university issues your CAS. Y

our visa appointment then happens in early August, your visa is approved in mid August, and you travel at the end of August, comfortably inside the entry window. Every stage depends on the one before it, which is why the CAS is not the last step but the centre of the chain: it triggers the application, which triggers the funds check, which triggers the biometrics, which triggers the decision.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a CAS and a COE?+

The CAS, Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies, is issued by a UK university inside the Home Office system, and you type its 14 digit number into your UK visa form. The COE, Certificate of Eligibility, is issued by Japanese immigration through your school, and you submit the original certificate with your visa application.

Which countries use a COA instead of a CAS?+

Australia uses the COA, which stands for Confirmation of Acceptance, issued by your university for overseas student visas. The COA carries a unique code that goes into the Australian visa form, much like the UK CAS number does. Germany and the United States use close cousins: the Zulassung and the I 20.

What should I do if my name is misspelled on the CAS, COE or COA?+

Contact the issuing office immediately, with a copy of your passport, and ask for a corrected letter or a system update. Never edit a PDF yourself. For Japan, corrections can take weeks because they pass through immigration, so check your COE name as soon as it arrives, not on the day of your appointment.

Can a scholarship letter replace a CAS or COE?+

No. A scholarship award letter proves funding but never replaces the confirmation letter itself. It can, however, replace part or all of the proof of funds requirement, since awards count as income in most systems. Submit the award letter together with the CAS, COE or COA, and confirm what it covers on the official portal.
