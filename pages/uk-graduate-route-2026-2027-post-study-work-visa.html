UK Graduate Route 2027: Cut to 18 Months

🗓️ Sep 28, 2026⏱️ 27 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team✅ Verified against official sources

If you want the full two-year version of the uk graduate route, the clock is running. You must apply before 31 December 2026, and after 1 January 2027 the same application gives you 18 months instead of 2 years.

As of late September 2026, that leaves roughly 94 days to apply for two years. The cut-off is a fixed calendar date, not a rolling window, so every week of delay moves you closer to the shorter allowance.

What is not changing is eligibility itself. Same course, same university, same completion report, same rules about who can accompany you. Only the length of the visa you are handed is being cut.

This guide explains who qualifies, what it costs, how to apply from inside the UK, what you can and cannot do on it, and how to switch to a sponsored route before your time runs out.

What Is the uk graduate route?

The uk graduate route is a visa that lets international graduates who have finished an eligible course at a UK university stay in the country and work without an employer sponsoring them. It is a post study work visa uk in the most literal sense: you can take almost any job you can find.

It launched in July 2021, when the earlier two-year post-study work route for international students was replaced. Almost five years later it is still one of the most applied-for UK visa categories for students from Pakistan, India, Nigeria and beyond.

Search terms for the graduate visa uk are among the most competitive in the sector, because the route solves three problems at once: staying, working and delaying a sponsorship decision.

Because there is no certificate of sponsorship and no job offer, this route is fundamentally different from the Skilled Worker visa. You are not applying for a job. You are applying for permission to go and find one.

The rules are route-specific in one important way: this time is not settlement time. It does not count towards the five-year qualifying period for indefinite leave to remain, which surprises applicants who assume any year in the UK counts.

It is also not an extension route. When the uk graduate route visa expires you must leave, unless you have switched to another category first. Planning that switch early is the single most important thing graduates do.

For most Pakistani applicants the sequence looks the same: finish the degree, receive your completion confirmation, apply quickly, and use the 2 years or 18 months to turn a UK job offer into Skilled Worker sponsorship.

If you have not started your degree yet, begin with our UK student visa guide so your course timing leaves room for this application.

The 2027 Change: What 18 Months Means for the uk graduate route

The single most important fact on this page is the shortening. Applications submitted on or before 31 December 2026 still receive a two-year graduate route for bachelor and master graduates. Applications submitted on or after 1 January 2027 receive 18 months.

This is settled policy, not a consultation. It applies to the date you apply, not to your course start date, enrolment date or graduation date. What matters is the day you submit the uk graduate route application online.

Doctoral graduates are the exception. If your award is a PhD or another doctoral-level qualification you keep three years whether you apply in 2026 or 2027. The cut affects non-doctoral graduates only, which means bachelors and masters.

The practical difference is six months of legal working time. Six months is enough to lose a contract that was still in probation, or to reach the end of your permission while a Skilled Worker application is still being decided.

This is why people keep searching for the last day to apply for two years. The answer is fixed and there is no discretion: 31 December 2026. It is a calendar date, not a rolling 24-month count from your course start.

Do not plan to file in the last week of December. Documents, payment and biometrics all have to happen, and an application started on 30 December may not be submitted until January, which is 18 months instead of 2 years for you.

If you submit on 31 December 2026 and the decision lands in January 2027, you still get two years. The law looks at the application date, not the decision date, so a slow decision does not cost you the longer route.

The same logic applies to dependants. A partner or child applying at the same time as you on the right date gets the longer period, and a late application drags the whole family into the shorter window.

Quick answer for anyone searching: the last day to apply for two years is 31 December 2026, and to keep the longer visa you must apply before 31 december 2026, because after that you get 18 months instead of 2 years.

Am I Eligible for the uk graduate route?

Eligibility is short and specific. You need a completed eligible course, valid Student permission covering it, and the ability to apply from inside the UK before that permission expires.

You must have completed an eligible UK course

The uk graduate route is only for people who have finished something. Your university must confirm successful completion to the Home Office through the student record system before you can apply at all.

That confirmation step is why some graduates apply late. The provider reports completion first, the Home Office updates your electronic record, and only then does the graduate route application appear in the online system.

Eligible awards include bachelor degrees, taught master degrees, MBAs, PG diplomas and certificates that meet the course level rule, and doctoral degrees. Short courses, language-only programmes and some professional training routes do not qualify.

You must have held Student permission for long enough

If your course is 12 months or less, you must have held Student permission for the full length of the course. If the course is longer than 12 months, you must have held it for at least 12 months.

This is the rule that catches people who transferred courses, changed universities, or arrived late on a visa extension. If you did not have valid Student permission for the required period, your application is likely to be refused even though you passed.

Time held on a different category, such as a spouse or work visa, does not count towards the 12-month requirement. If that describes you, get advice before you pay a fee.

These graduate route requirements 2026 checks are unchanged from previous years, but they are enforced strictly, and a refusal resets your position to almost nothing.

You must apply from inside the UK

There is no out-of-country application for this route and no overseas visa appointment. You must be physically in the UK and applying before your Student visa expires.

Your existing permission must be valid at the moment you apply. If it has already run out, or you have overstayed, you are no longer eligible and you need an alternative route immediately.

One useful side effect: you can stay in the UK while the decision is made, as long as your current permission is valid. Most decisions come within 8 weeks, so a valid Student visa buys real breathing room.

No sponsor, no job offer, no certificate of sponsorship

The uk graduate route requires no sponsor. You do not need a job offer, a CoS, a salary threshold, a sponsor licence or a recruitment letter. That is the entire point of the route.

People often mix this up with work rules during study. Our guide to working during university holidays on a student visa covers the separate 20-hour weekly limit that applies before you graduate.

Check every line of this list before you submit:

  • You have successfully completed a course at a UK higher education provider that qualifies for the graduate route.
  • Your university has reported that successful completion to the Home Office.
  • You held Student permission for the full length of the course, or for at least 12 months if it is longer.
  • You are physically inside the UK on the day you apply.
  • Your current Student visa is valid and unexpired.
  • You are filing on or before 31 December 2026 if you want the full two years.
  • You have no sponsor, job offer or certificate of sponsorship to provide.
  • You can pay the application fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge in one go.

If every line above is true you are in a strong position. Refusals are rarely mysterious, so treat the graduate route requirements 2026 as a checklist rather than a formality.

If your course record looks unusual, read student visa refusal reasons first, because a clean record is what makes this application straightforward.

Your university is the source of truth for completion, not the Home Office. If the two records disagree, the university record is queried first, and that query can add weeks to a deadline you cannot move.

There is also no minimum grade and no minimum salary attached to eligibility. A 2:2, a pass or a first all qualify equally, which is why the graduate route is used by graduates whose marks were not exceptional.

Language evidence is not normally required for the uk graduate route itself, unlike the Student visa you already hold. Some employers still ask for English proof when they sponsor you later.

Finally, do not assume the course must be at a Russell Group university. Any UK higher education provider that is licensed to sponsor students and that reports completion correctly is enough.

The graduate route requirements 2026 are also unchanged from the last several years, so most of your checking is about your own record rather than about new paperwork or new financial tests.

What Does the uk graduate route Cost in 2026?

It is not free, but it is cheaper than almost any other UK work visa. For 2026 the application fee is £937 per applicant, and the Immigration Health Surcharge is charged at the full rate of £1,035 per year of permission.

One detail catches a lot of applicants: the surcharge here is not the cheaper student rate of £776 per year. The student rate applies while you hold a Student visa, but the graduate route uses the full adult rate.

Because the 2-year version costs more, the deadline is not purely a timing decision. Anyone filing on or after 1 January 2027 spends roughly £918 less, but gets 18 months instead of 2 years of working time to spend it on.

Payment is taken up front. There is no instalment plan across the life of the visa, and the fee is normally not refunded if the application is refused, so treat the total as a same-day budget line.

Dependants pay their own fee and their own surcharge for the full length of their permission. A family of three is a serious number to plan for, and it is the figure that catches applicants out most often.

Confirm current figures before paying anything. The official guidance sits at gov.uk graduate visa information, and it is the only place that will settle a fee dispute.

UKCISA publishes a plain-English summary of the rules and costs at UKCISA graduate route advice, which is worth reading before you submit.

Universities also publish their own advice. Imperial College London post-study work visa guidance is a good example of how the reporting and timing steps are explained locally.

If money is tight, ask your university about a bursary, hardship fund or international travel bursary. Many institutions hold back funding specifically for graduates who are about to lose their status.

Longer-term planning is covered in UK full scholarship options, which is the better place to reduce this cost in the first place.

What is the graduate route in uk in cost terms? It is a fixed, knowable number before you apply, and nothing is charged per year afterwards. The surcharge buys access to the NHS for the whole period at once.

That is why the fee feels steep next to a student visa, which has no application fee at all. You are not extending a student permission cheaply, you are buying an independent work permission from scratch.

Budget the surplus carefully rather than spending the full £3,007 on the first month. New graduates often arrive with almost nothing left, which turns a visa advantage into a financial emergency by week six.

How to Apply for the uk graduate route

The application is online, from inside the UK, and shorter than a Student visa application because there is no sponsor paperwork. Most of the work happens before you log in.

The university reports your successful completion

Your provider sends your completion record to the Home Office through the student system, including your award and course dates. Until it lands, your record will not show as completed online.

Ask your international office to confirm the record has been submitted and keep the email. If the qualification level or completion date is wrong, fix it before you apply, because you will otherwise pay to be refused.

You wait for your immigration account to update

Once the completion report reaches the Home Office, your account updates and the graduate route application becomes available. Many graduates simply look too early, which wastes the most valuable route they have.

If a Student visa extension is also in progress, read UK student visa delays so you do not lose the 31 December 2026 date to a queue you did not know existed.

You gather your documents

You need your current passport, your Student visa details, proof of your award or a completion letter, and a UK address. Some applicants are asked for the electronic Certificate of Acceptance for Studies.

Take a screenshot of your account showing the course, the provider and the dates. It is the single most useful document if the Home Office queries the 12-month Student permission rule.

You apply online while inside the UK

The application is made through the online immigration service, not a visa application centre. You answer the eligibility questions, confirm your completion and pay. There is no appointment and no paper form.

If you want two years, submit and pay on or before 31 December 2026. The payment date is treated as the application date, so do not submit late and hope a pending payment is dated earlier.

You pay the fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge

You pay the £937 fee and the surcharge for the whole length of the route in one session. Two years is £2,070 of surcharge, 18 months is £1,152.50, and a three-year doctoral route is £3,105.

You will be asked how long you want the visa for. If you are filing on or before 31 December 2026 and are non-doctoral, choose the two-year option, because that is the last day to apply for two years.

You give biometrics through the UKVCAS app

Biometric information is normally handled in the UKVCAS app as a photograph or face scan rather than an in-person appointment. You must respond to the request or the application is refused after a formal notice.

Check that the app is installed and that the passport in your application is the same one you scan. Mismatches between form and document are one of the most common avoidable delays.

You receive the decision

Most decisions on the uk graduate route are made within 8 weeks. You can stay while you wait as long as your Student visa is still valid, which is why the expiry date matters as much as the application status.

On approval your status changes and you receive a vignette or eVisa showing the new permission. Download it and carry it with your passport, because employers and landlords ask for it before they pay or sign.

Common mistakes that cost graduates their two years

The most expensive error is applying late out of caution. A rejection over a small paperwork problem can be fixed; a missed 31 December 2026 date cannot, so decide early and correct problems afterwards.

The second most common error is applying from abroad. Plenty of graduates fly home for a family event in the last week of December and quietly lose the longer allowance by doing it.

The third is paying before the completion record has landed. A submitted application with a wrong date is much harder to fix than a two-week wait, because the date itself is what was wrong.

The fourth is declining the UKVCAS biometric request. It looks like a formality and it is not. A formal request that goes unanswered turns into an automatic refusal, and refusals carry a cost to future applications.

The fifth is assuming the certificate of sponsorship rules apply here. If an employer or adviser sends you a CoS document for this route, something has gone wrong, because the graduate route does not use one.

What Can You Do on the uk graduate route?

In practical terms this is the most open work permission the UK gives to a newly graduated international student. There is no occupation restriction, no salary floor and no limit on hours.

You can work any job. Warehouse work, retail, a teaching assistant post, an IT contract, a marketing internship, a graduate scheme. Skill level is irrelevant here, which is exactly what makes it useful when the first role is not the dream role.

There is no hours cap, so full-time, part-time, nights, weekends and seasonal work are all fine. You can be employed and self-employed at the same time without breaking any conflict of interest rule.

You can be self-employed or a business owner. Many graduates use the uk graduate route to run an agency, freelance in design or software, sell online, or start a trading business, because there is no sponsor to report to.

You can change employers freely. There is no notice period, no reapplication and no second Home Office fee. Moving from a warehouse job to a sponsored analyst role is one new application, not a new visa.

You can also incorporate a limited company, appoint co-directors, take on contractors and invoice clients. It is the most underused feature of the route for anyone thinking about work in uk after masters.

You may do unpaid or voluntary work, study part-time, volunteer, and travel in and out while you hold it. This is permission to live and work, not a job.

You can leave and re-enter the UK while an application is pending, as long as your current permission is valid. Booking a flight home after graduation is one of the most expensive mistakes graduates make.

Work in uk after masters is not the only use, though. Plenty of people use this period to build a portfolio, learn an industry, or simply save enough to fund the next application.

Running a business or freelancing

Self-employment is expressly permitted, and there is no restriction on the type of work. If your uk post study work plan involves clients rather than an employer, this is the visa that allows it without a sponsor.

You can be both a director of your own company and an employee elsewhere. You can invoice UK clients, open a business bank account, and register for self-assessment tax once your income requires it.

There is no turnover target and no employer count, so a small side income and a full-time salary are equally fine. Nobody reports your earnings to the Home Office on this route.

One practical limit: because your permission is time-limited and non-settlement, lenders and landlords often ask for a longer visa history. Keep your contract and bank statements ready, because that is the evidence they will want.

Before accepting a contract, check the sponsor position of your client. A genuine employer who will sponsor you later is worth more than a higher-paying client who never will.

Health, banking and day-to-day life

Your Immigration Health Surcharge payment gives you access to the NHS for the full length of the visa. You also need a BRP or eVisa, a UK address, a bank account and a National Insurance number once you start work.

Getting a National Insurance number is a separate, quick process and employers often cannot pay you properly without one, so start it in your first fortnight rather than your first month.

None of this depends on the employer being a licensed sponsor. Banks will open accounts on this permission, which is a genuine advantage over applicants who are waiting on a Skilled Worker decision.

Understand what is permitted and check the graduate route for international students summary from the official graduate visa page before an employer describes a role to you.

Employment on this route is genuinely unrestricted, which is why the graduate visa uk is described by advisers as the most employer-friendly visa a new graduate can hold.

That freedom is also why discipline matters. There is no occupational code, so a job that would never qualify for Skilled Worker can still consume the whole 2 years without moving you closer to staying.

What the uk graduate route Does NOT Give You

The flexibility is real, and so are the hard edges. Knowing what the route does not do is what stops applicants building expensive assumptions about the next two years.

It does not count towards indefinite leave to remain. Time on this route is not part of the five-year qualifying period, so two years in the UK is two years of work, not two years closer to settlement.

It cannot be extended. When the visa ends you must leave or switch to another category beforehand. There is no further 18 months and no discretion for anyone who is nearly settled.

It does not give access to public funds. You cannot claim Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, income support or Jobseeker's Allowance while you hold it, and dependants on the route are in the same position.

It does not target skilled jobs. A shortage occupation is irrelevant to this route, and there is no built-in list of expected roles. Any salary counts, including none at all if you trade but draw no income.

It does not let you sponsor new dependants. Partners and children already in the UK can apply, but you cannot bring a new spouse or new children while holding only this permission.

It does not protect your tuition, settle a student loan, or carry points towards any future visa application. It is time-limited permission and a working window, nothing beyond that.

It does not guarantee that an employer will sponsor you afterwards. Many UK employers do sponsor Skilled Worker applicants, plenty of small ones do not, so treat every offer as something to check rather than a certainty.

It also does not make you a resident, and it offers nothing if your plans change. A deliberate exit plan or an early switch is your responsibility, and the timeline is short.

Switching From the uk graduate route to a Skilled Worker Visa

The Skilled Worker visa is the standard destination for most graduates and the switch is straightforward. You apply from inside the UK before your permission expires, and your existing status stays valid while you wait.

To qualify you need a job offer from a licensed sponsor at or above the required salary, generally the general threshold unless your occupation sits on the eligible occupation list, plus English evidence at the level your sponsor requires.

You must also show you meet the financial requirement, maintain that income, and pay a far larger Immigration Health Surcharge at the adult rate for the full length of the new visa. That is where budgets get tight.

Be very clear about one rule: time on the uk graduate route does not count towards the five-year ILR qualifying period. Two years here plus three on Skilled Worker means five years in the country but only three qualifying years.

That changes planning for anyone hoping to settle. If settlement is the goal, the honest calculation is to move onto Skilled Worker early and build the five-year clock as fast as your salary allows.

You can switch to other categories instead: Global Talent, an endorsement route, a spouse or partner visa, a business visa, or any other settlement category. This route is a good platform for testing which fits.

Experienced immigration advisers suggest starting employer hunting within the first six months, treating a Skilled Worker offer as a reason to stay rather than the plan itself, and watching salary thresholds, which are reviewed regularly.

Unsure whether a sponsor is genuine or the salary is realistic? Ask before you sign. Unregistered sponsors and unpaid trial work are the most common traps for new graduates in the first months.

That is the point of the uk graduate route in one sentence: it buys you time to find the right job, then hands you over to a system that pays you to stay.

How the two routes differ in practice

The single biggest difference is that a uk work visa after studying is temporary and non-settlement, while Skilled Worker is a path to staying. Everything else follows from that one fact.

On the graduate route you need no sponsor, no salary threshold, no English test and no CoS. On Skilled Worker you need all four, and you pay a larger surcharge for the length of the visa you are applying for.

On the graduate route you can change employer as often as you like. On Skilled Worker each new sponsor means a new application, a new fee and usually a new qualifying period for settlement.

That is why advisers often suggest taking the graduate route first, working without pressure for a few months, and only then sponsoring once you know the industry you actually want to stay in.

The risk of that plan is obvious: if the uk work visa after studying ends before a sponsor is found, you leave the UK. Build a buffer into your plans rather than assuming the switch will be quick.

Saving toward the Skilled Worker application also matters, because that fee and surcharge are considerably higher. A graduate who has spent a year earning on the graduate route arrives at the switch with a much easier budget.

Taken together, uk post study work planning is less about the visa itself and more about what you do with the window it gives you, which is why the 31 December 2026 date matters more than any other date on this page.

Dependants on the uk graduate route

A spouse, partner or children already in the UK can apply to join you on the uk graduate route. This is the dependants category attached to the Student route, applied for with you or after you.

A dependant applying under the 18-month route gets 18 months. A dependant applying with you on or before 31 December 2026 gets the longer period, so the deadline matters for the whole family.

Cost matters here too. Each dependant pays their own application fee and their own surcharge for the full length of their permission, and family totals routinely exceed what applicants budgeted for.

Dependants on this route can work, with no restriction on employment. That is a real advantage over some other categories where a dependant needs their own sponsor before they can take a job.

Dependants also cannot claim public funds, and their time on the route does not build the Skilled Worker five-year clock either. If one of you has a genuine settlement plan, model it before committing.

You cannot add new dependants while on this route. A new partner or new child arriving during your 2 years or 18 months would need their own category, which is hard to arrange from inside the UK.

If your dependant has their own degree and their own entitlement, they should apply separately. A dependant is not eligible for the graduate route as an individual, so apply twice if both of you qualify.

Some couples stagger their applications on purpose, because the first applicant can then be treated as the sponsor of the second. Your university immigration office can confirm whether that works for your situation.

Documents for dependants are the same set: passport, proof of relationship, your approval notice and a UK address. Missing marriage or birth certificates are the most common cause of dependant refusals.

What is the graduate route in uk for a dependant in plain terms? It is the same duration as the main applicant, on the same start date, with the same switching options and the same settlement exclusions.

Because of that, a family decision about switching to Skilled Worker is really a decision about two immigration applications and two large surcharges. Plan both, not just the cheaper one.

Frequently Asked Questions About the uk graduate route

Is the UK graduate route still 2 years in 2026?

Yes, but only for a short window. If you apply on or before 31 December 2026 you get the full two years, assuming a bachelor or master award. From 1 January 2027 the same application gives you 18 months. PhD and doctoral graduates keep three years either way, so the change affects non-doctoral graduates only.

Can I work any job on the UK graduate route visa?

You can. There is no occupation restriction, no salary floor, no hours limit and no sponsor requirement. Retail, hospitality, warehouse work, teaching assistant roles, IT, marketing, care work and self-employment are all allowed, and you can move between employers without applying again.

How much does the UK graduate route visa cost in total?

In 2026 the application fee is £937 per person and the Immigration Health Surcharge is £1,035 per year at the full rate, not the student rate. That makes two years £3,007, an 18-month application £2,089.50, and a three-year doctoral route £4,042, before dependants.

Can I switch from the graduate route to a Skilled Worker visa?

Yes, and most graduates do. You need a job offer from a licensed sponsor meeting the Skilled Worker requirements, plus English and financial evidence, and you apply online from inside the UK before your graduate route permission expires. Time on the graduate route does not count towards the five-year ILR period.

Everything above comes down to one decision, and the decision has a date attached. Check the graduate route for international students requirements now, ask your university when your completion record will be reported, and get your documents and funds ready so you are not the person who misses the deadline.
