What is the Canada study permit cap 2026 PAL exemption?

Canada study permit cap 2026 PAL exemption If you are planning to study in Canada in 2026–27, two numbers now define your entire application: 309,670 — the count of study permit application spaces for attestation-required applicants — and 1 January 2026 — the date Master's and doctoral applicants at publicly funded universities stopped needing an attestation letter at all.

Understanding which side of that line you sit on decides how fast your file moves, how much competition you face, and how much money you need to show. This guide explains both numbers in plain language and lays out the application path for students from Pakistan.

Is Canada's system getting easier or harder?

Canada's managed-intake era began in 2024 and has tightened every year since. The encouraging part of that tightening is that graduate researchers at public institutions have been deliberately carved out of the queue. A complete online application from an exempt applicant is often processed faster than you would expect.

For everyone else, the constraint is real: the window is finite and the paperwork rules are stricter. Knowing whether you need a PAL or qualify for the 2026 PAL exemption is therefore the first planning decision, not a detail to settle at the end.

Key Takeaways

What should Pakistani applicants know about the Canada study permit cap 2026 PAL exemption?

  • The 2026 cap sets aside 309,670 study permit application spaces for attestation-required applicants; IRCC expects roughly 408,000 permits in total when exempt groups and renewals are included.
  • Master's and doctoral students at publicly funded DLIs are PAL/TAL-exempt from 1 January 2026 — you do not need a provincial attestation to apply.
  • Primary/secondary students and certain government-scholarship holders are exempt too; college and most undergraduate applicants still need a PAL/TAL.
  • Proof of funds rose to CAD 23,448 (single applicant, outside Quebec) for applications on or after 1 September 2026, plus tuition and travel.
  • Provincial allocations can be exhausted — once a province's pool is gone, it stops issuing attestations, so timing is part of your strategy.
  • IRCC now evaluates the source and availability of funds in every case, not just high-risk ones — clean, explainable finance is a hard requirement.

What do these takeaways mean for my application?

They divide applicants into two practical groups. Public-university graduate applicants can usually move as soon as they hold the required offer, while college, undergraduate and other PAL-required applicants must fit inside a provincial allocation. Both groups still need a credible study plan, valid documents and sufficient funds.

Provincial allocations are governed by population share and past approval rates, so they are not equal across provinces. Your destination province therefore shapes your odds, while a qualifying public-university graduate applicant avoids that provincial queue altogether.

Does every applicant use one national pool?

No. The 309,670 spaces are divided among provinces and territories, and exempt applicants are counted outside that total. That is why a provincial allocation can close while a public-university Master's or doctoral applicant remains eligible to submit a complete study permit application.

The Master's & PhD PAL Exemption: What Changed on 1 January 2026

What changed for graduate applicants?

Until 1 January 2026, graduate students applying for a study permit from outside Canada needed the same provincial attestation as every other applicant. That made them dependent on a province's allocation rhythm even when they had already received an offer.

The 2026 reform removes that friction for the cohort Canada has explicitly flagged as a priority: researchers and advanced-degree holders at public institutions. Their applications sit outside the capped provincial-attestation process.

What does the exemption actually mean?

  • You can submit your study permit application as soon as you hold your offer — no waiting for a provincial attestation letter.
  • Your application is not deducted from a province's capped 309,670 spaces.
  • Processing consequences are real: a cleaner file with no provincial hop is typically reviewed on a tighter timeline.

What has not changed?

  • You still need an unconditional or conditional letter of acceptance from a DLI, valid finances, and (if applicable) consistent study history.
  • Quebec still requires its own acceptance certificate (CAQ) process on top of the federal permit.
  • You still need a study permit to study even though you are PAL-exempt — the exemption is from the attestation, not from the permit.

Does exempt status guarantee faster approval?

No. It removes the provincial bottleneck, but IRCC still assesses eligibility, purpose of study, finances and admissibility. A faster route works best when your online application is complete and every document supports the same credible study plan.

How Provincial Allocations Work — and Why Timing Is Strategy

How are the 309,670 spaces divided?

Each province receives a share based on population and past approval share. It then issues Provincial Attestation Letters against that share to its designated learning institutions. The result is several provincial pools rather than one pool that every applicant can draw from equally.

Can a provincial pool run out?

Yes. Applications cluster at the start of each round, and when a province's allocation is fully subscribed, it stops issuing attestations until a new allocation or an un-subscribed province is used. Determine your destination's remaining capacity before you commit.

What if I am considering several provinces?

Spread your risk. If you are applying at college or undergraduate level across several provinces, understand each province's pace. One province can be fast and another exhausted within weeks of the round opening, so a flexible second choice can be valuable.

Do exempt graduate applicants still wait for a provincial window?

No. This waiting period does not apply to a qualifying public-DLI Master's or PhD applicant. You no longer have a reason to wait for a provincial window, which is one of the practical advantages of the new graduate exemption.

No. These figures cover living expenses only. You must additionally show your first year's tuition and the cost of travel to and from Canada, so your available amount needs to cover all three categories rather than merely the living-expense line.

What does IRCC mean by available funds?

IRCC updated its guidance in July 2026 to assess the source and availability of funds in every case. A sponsor whose income cannot be traced, or a bank balance dressed up for the screenshot, is now a standard refusal trigger rather than an edge case.

Build a clear trail from documented income to funds that are genuinely available for tuition, travel and daily living. This is especially important when your application is supported by family, a scholarship or an education loan.

Step-by-Step Application for Students from Pakistan

What is the right application sequence?

  1. Confirm your DLI and PAL status. Master's/PhD at a public DLI: no attestation needed. Everything else: obtain the PAL/TAL from your province's system and keep it with your file.
  2. Gather proof of funds. CAD 23,448 (single, outside Quebec) or the family-size figure that fits you, plus tuition and travel; prefer a GIC or a traceable bank statement over last-minute cash deposits.
  3. Prepare the letter of acceptance and study plan. Pakistani applicants benefit from a short, specific study plan connecting your past study, your chosen programme and your return plans.
  4. Complete biometrics early. Application + biometrics fee paid up front beats a mid-file biometrics slip.
  5. Submit a complete online application. A missing document stops the clock or draws a request that adds weeks — submit everything in one pass.
  6. Track processing on the official IRCC page for your visa office, and keep funds untouched until the decision lands.

Why do these early steps matter?

Each step prevents a delay that is difficult to recover later. An early PAL request protects a PAL-required applicant from an exhausted pool, while traceable money and a complete biometrics instruction prevent avoidable requests for evidence.

For graduate applicants, the same discipline still applies even without a PAL. The exemption removes one queue; it does not remove the need for an eligible offer, a credible study plan or financially accessible funds.

What If the PAL Pool Is Already Exhausted?

What options remain when a provincial pool closes?

You have three realistic options:

  • Switch to a PAL-exempt route. If your target is a Master's or PhD at a qualifying publicly funded DLI, the exemption removes the provincial-attestation problem.
  • Apply to a province with remaining capacity. The national space total is shared unequally, so a full province is not the whole country.
  • Plan for the next admission window. Enrolment intakes recur, and a January or May start can avoid the packed winter queue.

What should I never do?

Never apply without the attestation if one is legally required. That file is refused, your fee is gone, and the record does not help the next attempt. Use another eligible route or a later intake instead.

Understanding the Provincial Attestation Letter Process Under the Canada Study Permit Cap 2026 PAL Exemption

What is a PAL, and why does it matter?

The Provincial Attestation Letter, commonly known as a PAL, is the single most important document for most undergraduate and non-degree master's applicants aiming at Canada in 2026. Since the federal government introduced intake caps in early 2024, the PAL has functioned as a gatekeeping mechanism: without one, your study permit application cannot proceed into processing.

A PAL is issued by the province or territory where your Designated Learning Institution is located. Each province receives a limited number of attestation spaces from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada every year, so admission and the federal permit are not the same gate.

How does the provincial process work?

  1. Apply to a DLI and receive an acceptance.
  2. Have the DLI submit your information to the provincial ministry responsible for higher education.
  3. Receive a PAL if spaces remain in the province's allocation.
  4. Include the PAL in your federal study permit application.

The main bottleneck is step three. Once a province exhausts its allocation, it issues no more PALs until the next cycle or capacity becomes available. Ontario and British Columbia, which host a large share of international students, tend to exhaust their allocations earliest in the year.

How can I improve my chance of obtaining a PAL?

Apply to DLIs in smaller provinces such as Manitoba, Saskatchewan, New Brunswick or Nova Scotia to face less competition for attestation spaces. Many of these provinces also have strong programs in engineering, computer science, health sciences and business that are well-regarded by employers.

Apply early in the admissions cycle, ideally between October and January for the following September intake. This gives you the best chance of receiving a PAL while allocations are still healthy.

How quickly does the DLI submit a PAL request?

It matters. Not all institutions are equally proactive about submitting requests. Some universities and colleges process attestation requests within days of issuing an acceptance letter, while others may take weeks.

Ask the admissions office directly how quickly it submits PAL requests. An institution that processes your request in three business days gives you an advantage over one that takes three weeks, particularly when provincial allocations are tightening.

What does the attestation letter contain, and who issues it?

The letter contains your name, the name of your DLI, the programme you will study, and the province that issued it. It is typically a single-page PDF. You do not apply for it directly to the federal government; the province handles issuance after receiving your information from the DLI.

Keep the copy safe and do not let it expire. Most provinces issue PALs with a validity window, and if you do not submit your study permit within that window, you may need to request a new one and return to the queue.

How should I check and store the PAL?

Your DLI obtains the letter through the provincial system and issues it with the reference details required on your study permit application. Keep the original file and a clear scan in the same folder as your acceptance letter because you will enter the PAL number and issue date on the IRCC form and may be asked to upload the letter with your proof of funds.

Double-check the letter against your passport name and date of birth as soon as it arrives. Correcting a small mismatch early is easier than explaining it during processing, and a simple typo is one of the most avoidable causes of a delayed decision.

How Provincial Allocations Are Distributed and Why Geography Is Your Strategy

How does the Canada study permit cap 2026 PAL exemption shape provincial choice?

Canada study permit cap 2026 PAL exemption rules make geography a core part of your strategy. The cap is not administered as one national pool. IRCC allocates spaces to each province and territory using a formula that considers population, international student enrolment trends and provincial immigration priorities.

Each province then decides which DLIs receive attestation capacity and how it is distributed among undergraduate, graduate and college programmes. Demand and supply can therefore vary sharply even when the national total appears unchanged.

Where is demand highest?

Ontario typically receives the largest share because it hosts the most international students, but demand in Ontario also far exceeds supply. Universities in the Greater Toronto Area are heavily oversubscribed, and PALs for popular college programmes can run out within weeks of the cycle opening. British Columbia faces similar dynamics, particularly around Vancouver.

Atlantic provinces such as Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island and Newfoundland and Labrador generally have more attestation space relative to demand. Their DLIs are often eager to recruit international students.

Which smaller provinces can help Pakistani applicants?

The geographic distribution creates a strategic opportunity. Memorial University of Newfoundland, University of New Brunswick, Cape Breton University, University of Prince Edward Island and Brandon University in Manitoba have strong programmes, welcoming international communities, and often more available PAL capacity than institutions in Toronto or Vancouver.

Living costs in many of these provinces are also substantially lower. That can directly help with the proof-of-funds requirement and your overall budget, although each applicant must still satisfy the applicable IRCC threshold and show the source of the money.

What do Alberta and Saskatchewan offer?

Alberta occupies a middle ground. International enrolment has grown rapidly and the province has tightened its approach, but it still offers more capacity than Ontario or British Columbia. Institutions such as the University of Alberta, University of Calgary and MacEwan University are worth considering.

Saskatchewan similarly has strong programmes and reasonable living costs. The University of Saskatchewan and University of Regina actively welcome international applicants, making them useful alternatives to the highest-demand markets.

How should I compare provincial options?

Treat province selection as carefully as university ranking. Research which provinces have historically exhausted allocations earliest, which announced increases for the coming year, and which DLIs align with your academic goals.

Contact international admissions offices directly and ask whether they expect PAL availability for your programme level. This information is rarely published in advance, but admissions teams often know their provincial allocation status weeks before it becomes public knowledge.

How can I monitor changing allocations?

Allocation levels can change within the year, and IRCC publishes the number of letters each province has issued in regular data releases. If your preferred province shows high utilisation, consider other provinces with more room or keep a second DLI ready.

No published figure guarantees a letter, and totals can be revised, but the trend shows whether you are early, on pace or late in the cycle. Checking monthly can make a large difference when your preferred DLI or province is not fixed.

Study Permit Refusal Rates and How to Avoid the Most Common Grounds for Rejection

Can a valid PAL still lead to refusal?

Yes. Even with a valid PAL and acceptance letter, your study permit can still be refused. IRCC refusal rates for Pakistani applicants have historically been higher than the global average, so a strong admission outcome does not remove the federal eligibility assessment.

The three leading grounds are insufficient proof of funds, failure to demonstrate the purpose of study, and concerns that you may not leave Canada after the permit expires. Each can be addressed before submission through stronger, consistent evidence.

How do I avoid a proof-of-funds refusal?

For 2026, show tuition for your first year plus the minimum living-cost threshold set by IRCC. The funds must also be in a form the officer considers credible.

Recent bank statements showing a consistent balance over several months are stronger than a large deposit made days before the application. Scholarship letters, education-loan sanctions and affidavits can help, but attach supporting tax returns, employment letters, business records or other proof of the underlying income.

What should my statement of purpose explain?

Visa officers want a clear, logical connection between your past education, your Canadian programme and your future career plans. Applications without that thread raise red flags. Explain why Canada, why this specific programme and what you plan to do with the qualification when you return home.

How can I show that I intend to leave Canada?

IRCC assesses your ties to Pakistan, including family relationships, property, employment prospects and the overall reasonableness of your study plan. Evidence of strong obligations, property ownership or a confirmed position with an employer after graduation directly addresses the departure concern.

Use evidence that is specific and verifiable rather than listing generic hopes. A documented return plan that fits your qualification is more persuasive than an unsupported promise to leave.

Which administrative errors cause problems?

Incomplete documents, inconsistent information across forms and missing items such as a police clearance certificate or medical examination can cause rejection even when your profile is strong. Create a complete checklist and ask someone unfamiliar with the application to review it before submission.

What should I do after a refusal?

Treat a refusal as information rather than a verdict. Order available assessment notes, review the exact ground cited and fix that issue before reapplying with stronger funds, a clearer study plan or more complete family information.

Many students succeed on a second or third application because their goals have not changed; the evidence has simply become sharper. Keep every document version, date each refusal clearly and do not resubmit an identical file, because an unchanged application is the fastest route to the same outcome.

Proof of Funds for 2026 to 27: Detailed Requirements and Strategies for Pakistani Applicants

How much total money must I prove?

The proof-of-funds requirement is one of the most rigid elements of a Canadian study permit application. For 2026 to 27, demonstrate access to funds covering your first year of DLI tuition plus the minimum annual living cost published by IRCC for the province where you will study.

The detailed living-cost component described here falls in the range of CAD 15,000 to CAD 20,000 per year for a single student. Whichever budget line you calculate, your total must satisfy the current IRCC threshold as well as your actual tuition and travel costs.

Which forms of financial proof are accepted?

Acceptable evidence can include personal or family bank statements, Guaranteed Investment Certificates from Canadian financial institutions, education loans from recognized banks and official scholarship award letters. Bank statements must show the required balance for the recent period before submission, and the funds must be readily available.

Long-term fixed deposits that you cannot access may not satisfy the requirement. If funds are held in several accounts, you can combine statements, but the cumulative available total must meet or exceed the threshold throughout the relevant review period.

How should a family sponsor's support be documented?

Family sponsorship is common among Pakistani applicants and accepted, but it must be carefully documented. A sponsor should provide a signed support letter, proof of the relationship, employment or income documents and recent bank statements.

Officers look for consistency. If a sponsor claims annual income of PKR 3 million but the statements show monthly balances of PKR 200,000, the discrepancy raises questions that additional unsourced paperwork may not resolve.

Is a Pakistani education-loan letter enough?

IRCC accepts education loans from Pakistani banks when the sanction letter comes from a recognized financial institution and clearly states the amount, disbursement conditions and terms. A sanction covering the full required amount is generally sufficient on its own, although combining it with personal savings strengthens the application.

How should I manage exchange-rate risk?

Because the required amount is in Canadian dollars while your funds may be in Pakistani rupees, exchange-rate movements can affect whether you technically meet the threshold. Build a buffer of at least ten to fifteen percent above the minimum to account for changes between preparing your finances and review.

How should I document the source of family funds?

Where parents or siblings provide support, attach their bank statements with a letter explaining in simple terms how the money relates to documented income such as salaries, rental income or business proceeds. If part of the funds comes from a property sale or prize, retain the correspondence trail that proves it.

What makes a financial story easy to verify?

Create one clear line from overall household means, to the money you personally hold, to the exact amount on your tuition schedule. A file that tells that coherent story in a few minutes is stronger than a larger stack of papers with muddled logic.

What Happens After You Receive Your Study Permit: Maintaining Status and Conditions

What conditions come with a study permit?

Receiving the permit is not the end of the administrative process. It comes with specific conditions that you must follow throughout your time in Canada. Violating them, even unintentionally, can result in loss of status.

The most important conditions concern full-time enrolment, work restrictions and valid travel and permit documents. A scholarship or a change in circumstances does not cancel those obligations.

What counts as full-time enrolment?

You must be registered for the full course load defined by your DLI each academic term. If you drop below full-time status, notify your DLI and potentially IRCC. Limited exceptions can include a final term with only one course remaining or an institution-authorized medical leave.

What work rights do I have?

Work rights under a Canadian study permit are generous but conditional. As of 2026, most full-time post-secondary students can work off-campus up to the set number of hours per week during academic terms and unlimited hours during scheduled breaks. On-campus work has no hour limit.

A co-op or internship position requires a separate co-op work permit in addition to the study permit. Confirm eligibility before beginning that work rather than assuming the study permit covers it.

What if I change school or programme?

You may need to notify IRCC or apply for a new study permit. Transferring from one DLI to another generally requires notifying IRCC. Before making changes, consult the international student office at both your current and target institutions.

What must I do before my permit expires?

Apply to extend the permit before expiry if your programme runs longer than its duration. When you complete your studies, use the defined window to apply for a Post-Graduation Work Permit, pursue another immigration stream or leave Canada. Missing these dates has serious consequences.

How can I keep track of my status?

If you switch institutions after the permit is issued, you generally need a new permit for a different DLI under Canada's rules. Notify your coordinator before any move and complete the transfer process in writing.

Likewise, report a semester break or an early programme end rather than assuming silence is acceptable. Keeping permits and enrolment documents in one secure file, with expiry dates in a calendar, turns these obligations into routine habits rather than last-minute problems.

Comparing the Canada Study Permit Cap 2026 PAL Exemption to Other Destination Countries

How does Canada compare with the United Kingdom?

Canada's managed-intake system for international students is not entirely unique, but its structure is more formalised than most. The United Kingdom does not have a formal cap on international student numbers. Instead, it manages intake through the points-based immigration system and financial requirements.

The main UK barriers are tuition cost and the financial threshold for visa purposes, rather than a provincial PAL queue.

How do Australia and the United States manage demand?

Australia uses the Genuine Student framework and caps new international enrolments at individual institutions, creating a soft cap. The United States does not cap international enrolment in the same way; the F-1 system focuses on sufficient funds and genuine intent to study.

How do Germany and other European options compare?

Germany and other European countries with tuition-free or low-tuition public universities manage demand primarily through programme capacity rather than visa caps. Canada's PAL system adds a layer of complexity that does not exist in Germany, although its post-study work pathways and immigration options remain among the most structured and generous in the world.

What should I compare beyond the permit cap?

Consider the complete package: tuition, living expenses, work rights during study, post-study work opportunities and the likelihood of obtaining permanent residence. Canada is more complex to navigate in 2026, but for students who plan carefully and apply early, the outcomes remain highly attractive.

Why is the cap only part of the cost equation?

The cap and PAL requirement are only one side. Canadian tuition varies sharply by programme and province, and living costs depend heavily on the city. The country that suits your budget may not offer the most comfortable application experience.

What else can change the destination decision?

Compare school fees in Canadian dollars, housing, work rights appropriate to your level and the time needed to qualify for post-study work. Applicants who treat the permit cap as the only deciding factor often discover that the real difference appears in monthly expenses and in the job market two years later.

Managing Your Application Timeline Around the PAL Calendar

Why is timing now a hard constraint?

Timing has always mattered in study-abroad applications, but the PAL has made it a hard constraint for most applicants. You now face two sequential gateways that each consume part of the year: the PAL before submission and federal permit processing after it.

Building a realistic timeline around both is the difference between arriving on time and missing your intake. A delay at the attestation stage can prevent you from reaching the permit stage at all.

How should I plan for September 2026?

Begin in late summer 2025, roughly fourteen months before your first class. This is the research window: choose a province, shortlist DLIs and begin language testing.

Have IELTS or another accepted exam score by October or November 2025. Popular test dates fill up, and most admissions decisions need the result. At the same time, assemble financial evidence showing a consistent balance over a sustained period rather than one sudden deposit.

What should happen from November through March?

Finalise university applications and submit them early. Universities assess in rounds, and an earlier offer lets the DLI submit its PAL request earlier. Between January and March 2026, the DLI submits your details and the province issues the PAL if capacity remains.

Ontario and British Columbia usually involve more competition, so the first round is especially important there. Smaller provinces may have a more forgiving pace, but the sequence stays the same: offer, DLI request, provincial decision, then federal application.

When should I apply after receiving the PAL?

Submit the study permit application immediately. Most provinces issue the PAL with a validity window, and waiting can mean needing a new letter after the allocation is exhausted. After submission, allow roughly four to eight weeks for standard processing, though peak season or extra verification can extend this.

Pakistani applicants should plan for the possibility of a longer window. Keep arrival arrangements flexible so a processing delay does not conflict irreversibly with your university start date.

How does the January 2027 timeline differ?

Shift the steps forward: research in early 2026, tests by mid-2026, applications by August, PAL by October and permit submission as soon as the letter arrives. The January cycle is smaller, so provincial competition may be lower, but the sequential discipline still applies.

Whichever intake you choose, put the full calendar on paper and mark every checkpoint. Under the cap system, a month of delay at one stage can remove you from that cycle.

Frequently Asked Questions About the Canada Study Permit Cap 2026 PAL Exemption

What questions do Pakistani applicants ask most?

Applicants from Pakistan repeatedly ask a small set of practical questions about the cap and attestation letter. The answers below address the most common issues in plain terms, based on how the system has operated since the cap was introduced.

Do I need a PAL for a public university master's or PhD?

No. Since 1 January 2026, master's and doctoral applicants at public universities in Canada do not need an attestation letter. This removes the biggest scheduling constraint for qualifying graduate applicants.

If you are applying at undergraduate or college level, or at certain non-degree or private institutions, the PAL requirement still applies.

Can I use one PAL for multiple universities?

No. The PAL is issued for a specific DLI and programme. If you change institutions or programmes after receiving it, you cannot reuse it; you need a new PAL through the new institution, subject to provincial capacity.

Being certain about your choice matters because switching schools during the process can reset your timeline.

Do strong grades guarantee a PAL?

No. The PAL depends on provincial allocation and the DLI recommendation, not simply academic merit. Two applicants may both receive offers, yet one can still wait if the institution limits requests or the province exhausts its allocation.

This is why province choice and early application deserve serious attention even for a high-achieving candidate.

What if my entry date falls after classes begin?

Contact your university immediately. Most institutions will defer your start to the next available term without losing your place, and some may arrange remote study until you arrive. Never travel or attempt to enter Canada before permit approval, and never assume an unapproved entry date.

Is a PAL the same as a study permit?

No. A PAL is a provincial document confirming that an attestation-required application can proceed under the cap. The study permit is the federal document that grants entry and study rights.

You need the PAL before applying for the permit when one is required, but the documents are separate steps issued by different levels of government.

What does the 2026 cap number mean for me?

It means the number of applications that can be processed for attestation-required applicants is fixed, and once filled, no more are accepted even if you are otherwise qualified. Academic strength alone is no longer enough for the capped cohort.

Being an early applicant to a province with remaining capacity is what can secure your place. Every applicant in the 2026–27 cycle should build that reality into the plan.

Canada set 309,670 study permit application spaces for 2026 for applicants who need a Provincial or Territorial Attestation Letter, distributed to provinces and territories by population and approval-rate share. Within that structure IRCC expects to issue roughly 180,000 permits to the PAL-required cohort and about 408,000 study permits in total when you add exempt groups (Master's/PhD, primary/secondary, etc.) and renewals.

Do Master's and PhD students need a PAL for 2026?+

No. From 1 January 2026, Master's and doctoral students at publicly funded designated learning institutions are exempt from the Provincial/ Territorial Attestation Letter requirement. Primary and secondary students and certain government-scholarship holders are also exempt. College and most undergraduate applicants still need a PAL/TAL and depend on provincial allocations.

How much money do I need to show for a Canada study permit in 2026?+

For applications submitted on or after 1 September 2026, a single applicant outside Quebec must show CAD 23,448 for living expenses for the first year (up from CAD 22,895), on top of first-year tuition and travel costs. The threshold increases with each accompanying family member, and Quebec sets its own higher amounts. IRCC now assesses the source and availability of those funds in all cases, so documentation matters as much as the number.

What happens if a province runs out of PAL allocations?+

Each province stops issuing attestation letters when its 2026 allocation is used up, and you cannot get a study permit that requires a PAL without one. The sensible moves are to apply as early as possible, consider universities in provinces with remaining capacity, and for Master's/PhD applicants use the PAL exemption rather than waiting on a provincial queue.

How long does the Canada study permit take in 2026?+

Processing times vary sharply by country and applicant profile, and IRCC updates the published estimate every month. From Pakistan, straightforward Master's/PhD applications (PAL-exempt) typically move faster than applications that depend on a province's attestation queue on top of the core processing. Apply online, pay biometrics fees early, and check the official IRCC processing-times page for your visa office.

Which documents does IRCC accept as proof of funds?+

IRCC accepts a Canadian bank account in your name, a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC), proof of a student/education loan, bank statements covering roughly four months, a family-room letter from a financial institution, or evidence of paid tuition and housing. Whatever you use, the source must be consistent and explainable — a sudden large deposit or an unverifiable sponsor is a refusal risk even when the math clears.
