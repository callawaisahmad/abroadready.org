Chevening Scholarship 2026: Eligibility, Essays & How to Win

🗓️ Aug 12, 2026⏱️ 12 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

The Chevening Scholarship is one of the most prestigious fully funded awards in the world, giving outstanding professionals from more than 160 countries the chance to study a one-year master's degree at any UK university. Funded by the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office and partner organisations, the Chevening Scholarship is designed for future leaders who want to build skills, expand networks and strengthen ties between their home country and the United Kingdom.

This 2026 guide walks through what Chevening covers, who is eligible, how the selection process works, and how to build an application that genuinely stands out. The application window typically opens in August and closes in early November, so the time to start preparing your essays is now, months before you press submit.

Key takeaways

  • Chevening is a fully funded UK government scholarship covering tuition within a fee cap, a monthly stipend, flights and other allowances for a one-year master's.
  • You need at least an undergraduate degree, typically around two years (2,800 hours) of work experience, and a genuine leadership track record.
  • The application centres on four essays: leadership and influence, networking, studying in the UK, and career plan.
  • You must apply to and receive an unconditional UK university offer before a set deadline in the year after applying.
  • For 2026–27, applications open around August and close in early November, with interviews the following spring.

What is the Chevening Scholarship?

Chevening is the UK government's international awards program. It funds ambitious individuals to complete a full master's degree in the UK and, in return, asks scholars to return home for at least two years afterwards to apply what they learned. It is not just financial support; it is a leadership development experience with a lifelong global network of more than 55,000 alumni.

What Chevening covers

The award is genuinely comprehensive: full tuition fees (within an annual cap), a monthly living stipend, a return economy flight, and arrival and departure allowances. It also covers a visa application fee and, in many cases, the cost of an English test. The exact figures are published each cycle, but the principle is that your master's year should not be financed by your savings.

Eligibility

You must be a citizen of a Chevening-eligible country and plan to return there for at least two years after the award. You need an undergraduate degree, and while there is no strict minimum, around two years of work experience (typically 2,800 hours) is the practical bar. Full-time, part-time, paid, voluntary and internship experience can usually be counted. Leadership is the theme that ties everything together, so evidence of leading teams, initiatives or communities matters as much as your job title.

The four essays

The application rises or falls on four questions. Leadership and influence asks how you lead and how you would influence people. Networking asks how you build and sustain professional relationships. Studying in the UK asks why the UK, why this course, and what you will do while there. Career plan asks what you will do with the degree and how you will give back. Each is a short essay, so every sentence must carry weight. Our statement of purpose guide and the dedicated Chevening interview guide cover the same skills in depth.

Applying to UK universities in parallel

You choose up to three eligible UK master's courses when you apply. You do not need offers upfront, but if you are shortlisted you must secure at least one unconditional offer from a UK university by a deadline the following year. Treat university applications as part of the Chevening process from day one, because the offer deadline arrives faster than it feels.

How to win

Use concrete examples, not adjectives: show a specific team you led, a network you built, a problem you solved. Connect every essay to your long-term plan and to your home country. Apply early enough to revise, and be honest, since panels interview thousands of candidates and spot generic answers instantly. If you are not selected, reapply, as many scholars succeed on a second or third attempt. Frequently asked questions

Is the Chevening Scholarship fully funded?+

Yes. Chevening covers full tuition within an annual cap, a monthly living stipend, return economy airfare, and arrival and departure allowances, plus a visa fee, making it one of the most comprehensive UK awards.

When do Chevening applications open and close in 2026?+

Applications typically open in August and close in early November for study starting the following year. Interviews run the following spring, so start preparing your essays months before the deadline.

How much work experience do I need for Chevening?+

You generally need around two years of work experience, often measured as 2,800 hours. Full-time, part-time, paid, voluntary and internship experience can usually be counted toward the total.

Do I need a university offer before I apply for Chevening?+

No. You apply to up to three eligible UK courses without needing offers upfront, but if selected you must provide at least one unconditional offer by a set deadline the following year.

Can I reapply if I am not selected?+

Yes. Many scholars succeed on a second attempt. Use any feedback, strengthen your leadership record, refine your essays and reapply in a future cycle.
