Scholarship Types We Cover Our global scholarships database includes every major type of funding available for international students. Understanding the differences helps you choose the right opportunities for your situation. Fully Funded Scholarships Fully funded scholarships cover your full tuition costs, monthly living stipend, health insurance, travel costs, and sometimes additional allowances for research, books, or family members. Examples include DAAD Scholarships, Chevening Scholarships, Commonwealth Scholarships, Fulbright Program, Gates Cambridge Scholarship, Rhodes Scholarship, and the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS). These are the most competitive but also the most rewarding — they allow you to focus entirely on your studies without financial worry. Partial Scholarships Partial scholarships cover a portion of your tuition fees or living costs. A university might offer a 50% tuition-fee waiver, a department might provide a graduate assistantship that pays a monthly salary in exchange for teaching or research work, or a foundation might award a fixed grant of several thousand dollars. Partial scholarships are often easier to win than fully funded ones, and you can combine multiple partial awards to build a funding stack that covers most of your expenses. Tuition-Fee Waivers Many universities, especially public universities in Germany, offer tuition-fee waivers that reduce or eliminate your tuition costs. A tuition-fee waiver does not usually cover living expenses — you will need a separate source of funding for housing, food, and health insurance — but it removes the single largest cost of studying abroad. Graduate Assistantships and Research Fellowships Graduate assistantships and research fellowships are common at the Master's and PhD levels. You work part-time as a teaching assistant, research assistant, or laboratory supervisor, and in exchange your tuition is waived and you receive a monthly stipend. These positions also provide valuable academic experience that strengthens your CV and future career prospects. Need-Based Grants Need-based grants are awarded based on your financial circumstances. Universities and foundations require you to submit proof of your family's income and assets. If your financial need is demonstrated, the award can range from a partial contribution to full coverage of tuition and living costs. Merit-Based Scholarships Merit-based scholarships are awarded based on your academic record, test scores, research experience, and extracurricular achievements. These are the most common type of scholarship for undergraduate scholarships and Master's scholarships. Strong grades, high IELTS or TOEFL scores, and a compelling statement of purpose are essential for merit-based awards. If you have a lower CGPA, there are still strategies to strengthen your application. Our database also covers exchange programs, summer school funding, and postdoctoral fellowships. No matter what stage of your academic journey you are at — from applying to undergraduate UK courses to seeking PhD funding for a doctoral research project — you will find relevant options on AbroadReady. Germany — DAAD Scholarships and Tuition-Free Universities Germany is one of the most popular destinations for international students seeking fully funded scholarships and high-quality education. The country is known for its tuition-free universities Germany system, where public universities Germany charge little to no tuition fees regardless of your nationality. You only need to cover your living costs, which are estimated at approximately 11,208 EUR per year as of 2026, and you must show proof of sufficient funds through a German blocked account (Sperrkonto) to obtain your student visa Germany. The most prominent funding source in Germany is the DAAD Scholarships programme, administered by the Deutscher Akademischer Austauschdienst (German Academic Exchange Service). DAAD offers a wide range of fully funded scholarships for Master's and PhD study at German universities. The DAAD EPOS programme (Development-related Postgraduate Courses) is specifically designed for applicants from developing countries and funds postgraduate courses that are relevant to development policy. DAAD EPOS scholarships cover tuition, a monthly stipend of 934 EUR for Master's students (2026 rate), health insurance, travel allowance, and a study and research allowance. Other German scholarship providers include the Deutschlandstipendium (Germany Scholarship), which provides 300 EUR per month to high-achieving students at participating German universities; KAAD (Catholic Academic Exchange Service), which funds postgraduate study for students from developing countries; the Heinrich Boll Stiftung (Heinrich Boll Foundation), which offers scholarships for international students committed to environmental and social justice; and the Konrad Adenauer Stiftung (Konrad Adenauer Foundation), which funds politically and socially engaged students from around the world. Germany also offers many international degree programmes Germany taught entirely in English, particularly at the Master's level. These English-taught Master's Germany programmes allow international students to study without needing a German language certificate, though learning basic German is strongly recommended for daily life. Some programmes do require proof of German proficiency through the TestDaF or DSH language certificate. Applications for most German Master's programmes go through Uni-Assist, a centralized application service that evaluates your documents and forwards them to universities. If you are planning to study in Germany, you will need to navigate the university admission in Germany process, which may require credential evaluation of your previous degrees. You must also open a German blocked account (Sperrkonto) to prove you have sufficient funds for your first year of living costs. The student visa Germany application process typically takes 6 to 12 weeks, so apply early. Tip for applicants: DAAD Scholarships are highly competitive. Start your application at least 8 to 10 months before the deadline. Focus on writing a strong research proposal or motivation letter that clearly explains how your studies relate to your home country's development needs. United Kingdom — Chevening, Commonwealth, Gates Cambridge, and More The United Kingdom is home to some of the world's most prestigious scholarship programmes and universities. The Chevening Scholarships programme is the UK government's flagship global scholarship scheme, funded by the Foreign and Commonwealth Office (FCO) and partner organisations. Chevening offers fully funded Master's scholarships to outstanding emerging leaders from around the world. The award covers full tuition fees at any UK university, a monthly living stipend, return economy airfare, an arrival allowance, a thesis or dissertation grant, and access to the global Chevening alumni network. Chevening is one of the most competitive scholarships globally, with an acceptance rate of around 2 to 3 percent. Check our Chevening interview preparation guide for tips on the selection process. The Commonwealth Scholarships programme is another major funding source for international students from Commonwealth countries. Funded by the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO), Commonwealth Scholarships are awarded to students from Commonwealth countries for Master's and PhD study at UK universities. The scholarship covers full tuition fees, airfare, a living stipend, and a warm clothing allowance. Commonwealth Master's Scholarships and Commonwealth PhD Scholarships are among the most sought-after postgraduate UK courses for international students. The Gates Cambridge Scholarship is a fully funded scholarship for international students to pursue a postgraduate degree at the University of Cambridge. Established by the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation, the scholarship covers the full cost of study at Cambridge, including tuition fees, a maintenance allowance, airfare, and discretionary funding for research and professional development. Gates Cambridge is highly selective, with approximately 80 scholarships awarded each year from an applicant pool of thousands. The Rhodes Scholarship is one of the oldest and most prestigious international scholarship programmes. Rhodes Scholarships are awarded to exceptional students from selected countries to study at the University of Oxford. The scholarship covers Oxford University tuition fees, a monthly living stipend, airfare, and a settling-in allowance. The selection process evaluates academic excellence, leadership potential, physical vitality, and a commitment to making a positive impact on the world. Scotland offers the Saltire Scholarships programme, which provides partial tuition fee funding (up to 8,000 GBP) for international students to study at Scottish universities. Saltire Scholarships cover any subject area and are awarded based on academic ability, leadership potential, and a clear connection to Scotland. International students applying to UK universities must understand the international student tuition fees UK structure, which varies by university and programme. Most undergraduate and postgraduate programmes charge higher fees for international students. The UCAS application UK system is used for undergraduate programmes, while postgraduate applications go directly through each university's admissions portal. Most UK universities require English language evidence through IELTS for UKVI (IELTS for UK Visas and Immigration), the Secure English Language Test (SELT) accepted by the UK Home Office. If you want to study in the UK without IELTS, there are alternative pathways available. After completing your studies, the Graduate Route UK (also known as the post-study work visa UK) allows international graduates to remain in the UK for two years (three years for doctoral graduates) to work or look for work at any skill level. This is a valuable pathway to gaining professional experience and building a career in the UK. Compare post-study work visa options across all countries. The Student Route visa (formerly Tier 4 Student Visa UK) is the visa you need for study programmes longer than six months. You will need to show proof of sufficient funds, a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) from your university, and valid English language test results. Visa and Immigration Guide Winning a scholarship is just the first step. You also need to navigate the visa and immigration process for your chosen destination. AbroadReady provides practical guidance on the most common visa routes for international students. Read our complete student visa guide for a step-by-step walkthrough. Student Visas Every country has a student visa category for international students. For Germany, you apply for a student visa Germany at the German Embassy in Islamabad or the Consulate in Karachi. You will need your university admission letter, proof of health insurance, a German blocked account (Sperrkonto) with sufficient funds (11,208 EUR for 2026), a valid passport, and your previous academic certificates. The visa processing time is typically 6 to 12 weeks. For the UK, you apply through the Student Route (formerly Tier 4 Student Visa UK) online, attend a biometric appointment at a visa application centre, and provide your CAS letter (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies), proof of funds, and IELTS for UKVI scores. Check our country-by-country IELTS requirements and English language requirements by country and visa type for the exact scores you need. Post-Study Work Visas Many countries now offer post-study work options that allow international graduates to stay and work after completing their degree. The UK's Graduate Route and post-study work visa UK policies are among the most generous, offering two to three years of work eligibility. Germany offers an 18-month job seeker visa for graduates, which can lead to a skilled worker visa and eventually permanent residency pathways. Australia offers a Temporary Graduate Visa (subclass 485) that allows graduates to work for two to four years after study. Canada's Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) allows graduates to work for up to three years, which is a common stepping stone to permanent residency through Express Entry. See our full post-study work visa comparison across 15 countries. Work Visas and Immigration Pathways If you are considering working abroad after your studies, it is important to understand the work visa sponsorship system in your destination country. The UK's Skilled Worker Visa allows employers to sponsor qualified professionals in eligible occupations. Germany offers an EU Blue Card for highly skilled workers. Canada's Express Entry system is a points-based immigration pathway that values Canadian education and work experience. Australia's General Skilled Migration programme uses a points system for skilled workers. The job seeker visa options in Germany and the UK give you time to find employment after graduation. Some countries also offer dependent visa options that allow your spouse or children to join you during your studies or work. For remote workers and digital professionals, certain countries offer a digital nomad visa that allows you to live and work remotely while based in that country. Essential Visa Documentation No matter which country you choose, the visa application process requires careful preparation. Key documents you will need include: valid passport with sufficient blank pages, university admission letter or CAS, proof of scholarship or financial means (settlement funds proof), health insurance certificate, academic transcripts and degree certificates, English language test results (IELTS, TOEFL, PTE, Duolingo), a moving abroad checklist to track your tasks, and your visa application form and fee receipt. You may also need to attend a visa interview at the embassy or consulate. If you have a low CGPA, a strong motivation letter can help offset weaker academic records. Understanding the cost of living abroad in your destination country is essential for your visa application and budget planning. Monthly living costs vary significantly: approximately 934 to 1,200 EUR in Germany, 1,000 to 1,500 GBP in the UK, 1,500 to 2,500 CAD in Canada, and 1,500 to 2,500 AUD in Australia. Your scholarship may cover these costs, or you may need to show personal funds through a German blocked account (Sperrkonto) or equivalent mechanism. Credential Evaluation Many universities and immigration authorities require your academic credentials to be evaluated by a recognized body. Credential evaluation services such as WES evaluation (World Education Services) assess your degrees and transcripts against the educational standards of your destination country. This is commonly required for university admission in Germany (through Uni-Assist), Canadian immigration (through WES or IQAS), and US university applications (through WES or ECE).
