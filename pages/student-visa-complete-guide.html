Student Visa Overview — What You Need to Know

Before diving into country-specific details, here are the universal principles that apply to almost every student visa application worldwide. Understanding these basics will make the entire process less intimidating and help you avoid the most common mistakes.

Every major study destination requires five core things from you as an applicant:

  • An unconditional university offer: You need a confirmed place at a recognised institution. Conditional offers (based on pending exam results) generally aren't enough — you need the final, unconditional acceptance letter or its equivalent (CAS in the UK, I-20 in the USA, CoE in Australia, etc.).
  • Proof of funds: You must demonstrate that you can pay for both tuition and living costs without working. Each country sets its own minimum, and the money must usually be held in your account (or your sponsor's account) for a specific period — typically 28 days or more.
  • English proficiency: Unless you're from an English-speaking country or studied in English, you'll need a test score. IELTS Academic is universally accepted; TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic, and Duolingo are also widely accepted. Scores range from IELTS 5.5 to 7.5 depending on the course and country.
  • Health insurance: Some countries bundle this into a visa fee (UK's IHS, Australia's OSHC), while others require you to arrange separate coverage. Either way, you cannot get a visa without it.
  • Clean criminal record: Most countries ask for a police clearance certificate, especially if you're over 18. Serious criminal convictions will result in automatic refusal.

Processing times vary dramatically — the UK can process a standard application in 3 weeks, while Canada's study permit routinely takes 8–16 weeks. Always check current wait times and apply at least 3 months before your programme starts.

Pro tip: Even if a country doesn't require an interview, consular officers can always request one. Prepare for it as if it were mandatory — know your university, your course, why you chose that country, and how you'll fund your stay.

UK Student Visa (Tier 4)

The UK Student visa is one of the most straightforward in the world — no interview for most applicants, a clear document checklist, and processing in as little as 3 weeks.

Application fee£558
Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS)£776/year (discounted student rate)
Maintenance funds — London£1,529/month × 9 months + first year's tuition
Maintenance funds — outside London£1,171/month × 9 months + first year's tuition
Funds holding period28 consecutive days before application
Processing time3 weeks standard; priority +£500 (~1 week); super-priority +£1,000 (~next working day)
Work during study20 hrs/week during term; full-time during holidays
Post-study workGraduate Route: 2 years (18 months from January 2027 for UG/PG)

Required Documents

  • CAS number: Your Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies, issued by your university after you accept the offer and pay the deposit. The CAS contains a reference number that links your visa application directly to your university's records. You cannot apply without it.
  • Valid passport: Must be valid for the duration of your stay plus at least one blank page for the visa vignette.
  • Financial evidence: Bank statements showing you've held the required funds (tuition + living costs) for 28 consecutive days. Accepted sources include your own funds, parents' funds (with a letter of consent and birth certificate), or an official scholarship letter.
  • TB test certificate: Required if you're applying from certain countries (most of South Asia, parts of Africa, parts of Southeast Asia). The test must be from a UK-approved clinic and is valid for 6 months.
  • ATAS certificate: Required for certain sensitive subjects (nuclear physics, advanced engineering, cybersecurity, etc.) at master's level and above. Your university will tell you if you need it — apply early as processing can take 4–6 weeks.
  • English language proof: IELTS UKVI (Secure English Language Test) at the level specified by your university, typically IELTS 5.5–7.5.
  • Passport-sized photographs: Meeting UK visa photo specifications.

You can apply online up to 6 months before your course starts (or 3 months if you're inside the UK). After applying, you'll attend a biometrics appointment at a visa application centre to provide fingerprints and a photo.

Key date: From 1 January 2027, the Graduate Route shortens to 18 months for bachelor's and master's graduates (PhD stays at 3 years). If you want the full 2-year post-study work period, apply before this date.

USA Student Visa (F-1)

The F-1 visa is the most common US student visa and the only one that allows you to work on campus and participate in OPT and CPT. It's also the most rigorous — you'll face a face-to-face embassy interview.

ItemDetails (2026)
Visa typeF-1 (Student Visa)
Application fee (MRV fee)$185
SEVIS fee$350 (I-901, paid before interview)
Total upfront cost$535
Processing time2–8 weeks depending on embassy wait times
Work during study20 hrs/week on-campus only (first year); on-campus + off-campus (after first year with justification)
OPT (post-study)12 months standard + 24-month STEM extension = up to 36 months for STEM graduates
CPT (internships)Available during study — part-time (20 hrs/week) or full-time (winter/summer)

Required Documents

  • I-20 form: Issued by your SEVP-certified school after you've been accepted and shown financial proof. The I-20 contains your SEVIS ID number and programme details — you cannot attend the visa interview without it.
  • DS-160 confirmation: The completed online Nonimmigrant Visa Application form. Print the confirmation page with the barcode — it's required at the embassy.
  • SEVIS fee receipt: Proof you've paid the $350 I-901 fee. Pay at fmjfee.com at least 3 days before your interview.
  • Financial documentation: Bank statements, scholarship letters, or sponsor affidavit of support (Form I-134) showing you can cover at least the first year of expenses. Consular officers want to see stable, recent funds — not a sudden large deposit.
  • Academic transcripts: Original or certified copies of your diplomas, transcripts, and standardised test scores (SAT, GRE, GMAT, etc.).
  • English proficiency: TOEFL iBT, IELTS Academic, or Duolingo scores as required by your university.
  • Valid passport: Must be valid for at least 6 months beyond your intended stay.
  • Passport-sized photo: Meeting US visa specifications (2×2 inches, white background).

The Embassy Interview

The F-1 interview is the single biggest difference between the US and other countries. It typically lasts 2–3 minutes. The officer will ask about your university choice, funding, ties to your home country, and post-graduation plans. Be direct, confident, and honest. Common questions include: "Why this university?", "How will you pay for it?", "What will you do after graduation?", and "Do you have family in the US?"

Common refusal reason: The officer must be convinced you intend to return home after your studies (the "non-immigrant intent" requirement). Strong ties to your home country — family, property, a job offer — help significantly. If refused under Section 214(b), you can reapply with stronger evidence of ties.

Canada Study Permit

Canada's study permit system is generous with work rights and offers one of the best post-study pathways in the world — the PGWP allows up to 3 years of open work after graduation.

ItemDetails (2026)
Permit typeStudy Permit
Application feeCAD 150
Biometrics feeCAD 85
Total upfront costCAD 235
Processing time8–16 weeks standard; SDS stream 4–6 weeks
Work during study20 hrs/week off-campus during term; full-time during scheduled breaks
PGWP (post-study)Up to 3 years depending on programme length

Student Direct Stream (SDS)

If you're from Pakistan, India, China, Brazil, Colombia, Costa Rica, Indonesia, Morocco, Peru, the Philippines, Senegal, or Vietnam, you may qualify for the SDS — a faster processing stream that typically delivers decisions in 4–6 weeks. You must prove you've paid your first year's tuition, purchased a GIC (Guaranteed Investment Certificate) of at least CAD 20,635, and met language requirements.

Required Documents

  • Letter of Acceptance (LOA): From a Designated Learning Institution (DLI). This is the Canadian equivalent of the CAS/I-20.
  • Proof of funds: GIC (CAD 20,635 minimum) from a participating Canadian financial institution + proof of tuition payment. Alternatively, bank statements showing at least CAD 20,635 for living costs plus tuition.
  • Valid passport
  • Police clearance certificate: Required for applicants over 18 from most countries. No criminal convictions should appear.
  • Medical examination: Required if you've lived in certain countries for more than 6 months in the past year. Must be done by an IRCC-designated panel physician.
  • Passport-sized photographs
  • Statement of Purpose: Explaining why you chose Canada, your study plan, and your intention to return home after graduation.
  • Language proficiency: IELTS Academic (minimum 6.0 per band for SDS), PTE Academic, or TEF.
PGWP advantage: A programme of 2+ years qualifies you for a 3-year PGWP. A 1-year programme gives a 1-year PGWP. Studying at a public college or university in a smaller province can also boost your Express Entry CRS points for permanent residency.

Australia Student Visa (Subclass 500)

Australia's Subclass 500 visa is one of the most expensive in the world, but it comes with generous work rights and a clear post-study pathway to permanent residency.

ItemDetails (2026)
Visa typeSubclass 500 (Student Visa)
Application feeAUD 710
Processing time4–12 weeks (varies by sector and country)
Work during study48 hrs per fortnight during term; unlimited during scheduled breaks
Post-study workTemporary Graduate visa (Subclass 485): 2–4 years depending on qualification level and location
OSHC requirementMandatory — Overseas Student Health Cover for the full duration of your visa

Required Documents

  • Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE): Issued by your Australian education provider after you accept the offer and pay the deposit. Required before you can apply for the visa.
  • Genuine Temporary Entrant (GTE) statement: A written statement (minimum 300 words) explaining why you want to study in Australia, how the course relates to your career goals, and your ties to your home country. This is the most critical document — it determines whether the officer believes you're a genuine student.
  • Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC): You must purchase OSHC for the entire duration of your stay before applying. Popular providers include Allianz, Bupa, Medibank, and OSHC Worldcare. Costs vary by provider and duration but typically run AUD 500–700/year.
  • Financial proof: Evidence of AUD 24,505/year for living costs + AUD 1,000/year for a partner + AUD 800/year per child + tuition + return airfare.
  • Academic transcripts and certificates
  • English proficiency: IELTS Academic (minimum 5.5 overall, 5.0 in each band) or equivalent.
  • Police check: National police clearance certificate from every country you've lived in for 12+ months in the past 10 years.
  • Medical examination: Required for applicants from most countries — done by a Bupa Medical Visa Services panel physician.
  • Valid passport
485 visa tip: Studying in regional Australia (outside Sydney, Melbourne, and Brisbane) can extend your 485 visa by 1–2 additional years and gives you access to regional migration pathways. Consider Adelaide, Perth, Gold Coast, or Hobart for both cost savings and immigration advantages.

Germany Student Visa

Germany offers tuition-free education at public universities for international students, making it one of the most affordable study destinations in the world — but you'll need to prove you can cover living costs through a blocked account.

ItemDetails (2026)
Visa typeNational Visa for Study Purposes (Type D)
Application fee€75
Blocked account (Sperrkonto)€11,904/year (€992/month) — proof of funds
Tuition€0 at most public universities (semester contribution €150–350); €10K–30K/year at private universities
Processing time4–12 weeks (apply early — German embassies have long appointment queues)
Work during study120 full days or 240 half days per year
Post-study work18-month job seeker visa after graduation

Required Documents

  • University admission letter (Zulassungsbescheid): From a German university or Studienkolleg. Some programmes require uni-assist verification of your foreign qualifications.
  • APS certificate: Required for applicants from China, India, Vietnam, and some other countries. The Akademische Prüfstelle (APS) verifies your academic credentials through a review process that can take 4–8 weeks. Apply for it well in advance.
  • Blocked account proof: A bank account (e.g., Expatrio, Fintiba, Deutsche Bank) with at least €11,904 deposited. You can only withdraw €992/month once in Germany.
  • Health insurance: Public or private health insurance valid in Germany. Students under 30 can join public insurance (TK, AOK, etc.) at approximately €110/month; those over 30 need private insurance.
  • CV and motivation letter: A detailed CV in tabular format and a letter explaining why you chose Germany and your specific programme.
  • Academic transcripts and certificates: Certified copies; some may need to be translated into German or English.
  • Language proficiency: German-taught programmes require TestDaF, DSH, or telc C1; English-taught programmes require IELTS Academic or TOEFL iBT.
  • Valid passport
Job seeker advantage: Germany's 18-month job seeker visa is one of the most generous in Europe. After finding a job, you switch to a work permit or EU Blue Card. Germany's strong economy and demand for skilled workers in engineering, IT, and STEM fields makes this a realistic pathway.

Netherlands Student Visa (MVV + Residence Permit)

The Netherlands uses a two-part system: an MVV (entry visa) for travel and a residence permit for staying. Your university typically handles the application on your behalf — one of the easiest processes in Europe.

ItemDetails (2026)
Visa typeMVV (entry visa) + VVR (residence permit)
Application fee~€210 (MVV + residence permit combined)
Processing time2–4 weeks (university submits on your behalf)
Work during study16 hours/week during term; full-time in June, July, and August
Post-study workOrientation year (Zoekjaar) visa — 1 year to find work

Required Documents

  • Nuffic certificate (Kijkin): Issued by Nuffic (the Dutch organisation for internationalisation in education) confirming your previous qualifications are sufficient for your chosen programme. Required for most non-EU applicants.
  • University admission letter: From a Dutch higher education institution (university or university of applied sciences).
  • Financial proof: Evidence of approximately €1,100/month (or a full year's tuition + living costs) held in a recognized bank account, or proof of a scholarship.
  • Valid passport
  • Health insurance: Dutch basic health insurance (basisverzekering) is mandatory once you're registered in the Netherlands. Cost: ~€120–140/month.
  • Criminal background check
  • Passport-sized photographs
University-handled process: Unlike most countries, the Dutch university's International Office handles most of the immigration paperwork. You'll receive instructions after accepting your offer. This makes the Netherlands one of the most hassle-free visa processes in Europe.

New Zealand Student Visa

New Zealand offers a welcoming environment, excellent universities, and a generous post-study work visa — plus the bonus of stunning natural scenery and a high quality of life.

ItemDetails (2026)
Visa typeStudent Visa (Fee Paying)
Application feeNZD 430
Processing time4–8 weeks
Work during study20 hrs/week during term; full-time during holidays
Post-study workPost-Study Work Visa: up to 3 years depending on qualification and study location

Required Documents

  • Offer of Place: From a New Zealand Qualifications Authority (NZQA)-approved institution. Your tuition must be paid in advance or you must show proof of a scholarship.
  • Proof of funds: At least NZD 20,000/year for living costs + tuition fees + return airfare. You'll need bank statements showing this has been held for at least 6 months, or a scholarship letter.
  • Medical and travel insurance: Mandatory for the duration of your stay. The policy must cover medical treatment, hospital care, and repatriation.
  • Police certificate: From every country you've lived in for 12+ months since turning 20.
  • Valid passport
  • Medical examination: May be required depending on your country of residence and the length of stay.
  • Proof of English proficiency: IELTS, TOEFL, or PTE as required by your institution.
Post-Study Work Visa tip: Studying in a regional area (outside Auckland) can extend your post-study work visa by up to 2 years. Combined with New Zealand's skills shortage list, this makes regional study a smart immigration strategy.

Ireland Student Visa

Ireland's student visa system is straightforward, with English as the primary language and a growing tech sector offering strong post-study employment opportunities.

ItemDetails (2026)
Visa typeStamp 2 (Student Permission)
Application fee€100
Processing time4–8 weeks
Work during study20 hrs/week during term (40 hrs during June–September and December 15–January 15)
Post-study workThird Level Graduate Programme: 12 months (Level 8 degree) or 24 months (Level 9/10 postgraduate)

Required Documents

  • Letter of Acceptance: From an Irish institution on the Internationalisation Register. The letter must confirm your course details, fees paid, and that you've been accepted for a full-time programme.
  • Proof of funds: At least €10,000 for living costs for the first year, plus proof of tuition fee payment for the full course duration. Bank statements showing consistent funds are essential.
  • Private medical insurance: Ireland does not provide public healthcare to international students. You must have private medical insurance covering your full stay — providers include Irish Life Health, Laya Healthcare, and VHI.
  • Academic transcripts and certificates
  • English proficiency: IELTS Academic, TOEFL iBT, or PTE Academic as required by your institution.
  • Valid passport
  • Proof of accommodation: Confirmation of where you'll be living during your studies.
Tech sector advantage: Ireland is home to European headquarters of Google, Meta, Apple, Microsoft, and Amazon. Graduates in tech, pharma, and finance are in high demand, making the Third Level Graduate Programme a strong launchpad for a career in Ireland's booming tech sector.

How to Prepare Financial Evidence

Financial evidence is the #1 reason for student visa refusals. Get this right and your chances of approval increase dramatically.

What Counts as Financial Evidence

  • Bank statements: The most common and accepted form. Must show a consistent balance (not a sudden large deposit) for the required holding period (28 days for UK, 6 months for Canada SDS). Show the account holder's name, account number, bank name, and transaction history.
  • Education loans: Official education loan sanction letters from recognised banks or financial institutions. Must specify the loan amount, disbursement schedule, and that it's specifically for your education expenses.
  • Scholarships: Official scholarship award letters specifying the amount, duration, and what expenses it covers (tuition, living, travel). Fully funded scholarships often eliminate the need for additional financial proof.
  • Sponsor letters: If a parent, guardian, or relative is funding your studies, you'll need a signed affidavit of support (or equivalent) along with their bank statements, employment letter, and sometimes their tax returns. Some countries also require proof of relationship (birth certificate for parents).

Common Mistakes That Cause Rejections

  • Sudden large deposits: A bank statement showing €10,000 deposited the day before the statement period starts is a red flag. Funds should show a natural, consistent balance over the holding period.
  • Wrong currency or amount: Double-check the exact amounts required in the local currency. Converting to your own currency doesn't count — the funds must be in the required currency or equivalent.
  • Missing account holder name or bank stamp: Bank statements must be on official bank letterhead, show the account holder's full name, account number, and bank contact details. Printouts from online banking without these details are often rejected.
  • Unqualified sponsor: If using a sponsor, ensure the relationship is documented (birth certificate) and the sponsor's financial documents are as complete as your own.
  • Expired documents: Financial evidence has a shelf life. UK bank statements older than 28 days from the application date are invalid. Always check the validity window.
  • Not enough for the full duration: Some countries want proof for the entire programme length, not just the first year. Canada requires GIC + tuition; Germany requires a full year in the blocked account.
Critical advice: Start preparing financial evidence 3–6 months before you plan to apply. Keep funds in a stable, traceable account. Avoid cash deposits, cryptocurrency conversions, or transferring money between multiple accounts — these all raise red flags with visa officers.

Visa Interview Tips

Not every country requires an in-person interview, but when they do, preparation is everything. Here's what to expect for each destination.

CountryInterview Required?FormatDurationKey Tips
UK Rarely Decision by mail (application submitted online) N/A If interviewed (rare), it's usually a phone call. Be ready to explain your course choice and financial plans.
USA Always In-person at US Embassy/Consulate 2–3 minutes Be clear, concise, and confident. Know your university, course, funding source, and post-graduation plans. Carry all documents organized in a folder. Speak English.
Canada Biometrics required; interview rare Biometrics at a Visa Application Centre; interview only if officer requests it 15–30 minutes (if interviewed) If asked for an interview (by phone or in-person), explain your study plan, why Canada, and your intent to return home.
Australia Generally no Online application (no interview for most applicants) N/A Your GTE statement is your "interview" — write it carefully. A strong GTE statement can substitute for an interview.
Germany Possible In-person at the German Embassy/Consulate 15–30 minutes Be prepared to discuss your academic background, why Germany, your German language skills, and your plans after graduation. Bring all documents in organized order.

Universal Interview Tips

  • Know your course: Be able to explain why you chose this specific programme, this university, and this country. "Because it's ranked high" is not enough — connect it to your career goals.
  • Know your finances: Be ready to explain who is paying, how the funds were accumulated, and that you have enough for the entire programme.
  • Show ties to home: Family, property, a job offer, or a clear plan to return after graduation. This is especially critical for the USA (214(b) refusal risk) and UK (genuineness test).
  • Dress professionally: Business casual is appropriate. First impressions matter — especially at US embassies where you're seen for only 2–3 minutes.
  • Bring everything: Even if you don't think they'll ask for a specific document, bring it. An organized folder with tabs for each document shows preparation and professionalism.
  • Don't memorize scripts: Speak naturally. Officers can spot rehearsed answers, and it raises suspicion. Be genuine and honest.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a student visa take to process?+

Processing times vary significantly by country. UK: 3–6 weeks standard (priority processing available for 1 week, super-priority for next working day). USA: 2–8 weeks depending on embassy wait times — some embassies (like Lagos or Mumbai) have 3–6 month interview wait lists, so apply as early as possible. Canada: 8–16 weeks for standard processing; the Student Direct Stream (SDS) reduces this to 4–6 weeks for eligible countries. Australia: 4–12 weeks depending on your sector and country of application. Germany: 4–12 weeks; German embassies often have long appointment queues, so book early. General rule: Apply at least 3 months before your programme starts, and 6 months for the USA and Canada.

Can I work while studying abroad on a student visa?+

Yes, most countries allow part-time work during the academic term. UK: 20 hrs/week during term, full-time during holidays (for degree-level study at a Higher Education Provider). USA: 20 hrs/week on-campus only during your first year; on-campus + off-campus (with approval) in subsequent years. Canada: 20 hrs/week off-campus during scheduled academic periods, full-time during breaks. Australia: 48 hrs per fortnight during term, unlimited during scheduled breaks. Germany: 120 full days or 240 half days per year. Netherlands: 16 hours/week during term, full-time in summer. New Zealand: 20 hrs/week during term, full-time during holidays. Ireland: 20 hrs/week during term (40 hrs during summer and Christmas holidays). Work rights are a privilege — exceeding your allowed hours can result in visa cancellation.

What happens if my student visa is refused?+

You can usually reapply with a stronger application. The most common reasons for refusal include insufficient financial evidence, missing documents, concerns about genuine student intent, and incomplete applications. UK: You can request an administrative review (if you believe the decision was wrong) or reapply (stronger option). USA: Refusals under Section 214(b) are common — you can reapply with stronger evidence of ties to your home country, but you must pay the visa fee again. Canada: You can request an immigration appeal or reapply with additional documents. Australia: You can request a merits review at the Administrative Appeals Tribunal (AAT). Germany: You can reapply or file an objection. Always address the specific reason for refusal when reapplying.

Do I need health insurance for a student visa?+

Yes, every country requires some form of health coverage. UK: The Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) of £776/year gives you access to the NHS — the same healthcare British citizens use. This is paid upfront for your entire visa length. Australia: Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) is mandatory and must be purchased before your visa is granted. Costs AUD 500–700/year depending on the provider. Germany: You must have health insurance — public insurance (TK, AOK) costs ~€110/month for students under 30; private insurance is available for older students. Canada and USA: You need proof of health insurance coverage, either through the university's student health plan or a private policy. Netherlands: Basic Dutch health insurance (basisverzekering) is mandatory, costing ~€120–140/month. New Zealand and Ireland: You must arrange your own private health insurance. Costs vary by country and duration — budget for this as a mandatory visa expense.

Can I bring my family on a student visa?+

Most countries allow dependents (spouse and children) on a student visa, but financial requirements increase. UK: Dependents are allowed, but since January 2024, only students on PhD/government-sponsored programmes or certain research programmes can bring dependants. Your maintenance funds increase by £680/month per dependent (London) or £520/month (elsewhere). USA: F-2 visas are available for spouses and unmarried children under 21, but F-2 dependents cannot work. Canada: Spouses can apply for an open work permit, and children can study. Proof of additional funds is required. Australia: Dependents are allowed, with additional financial requirements (AUD 1,000/year for partner + AUD 800/year per child). Spouses can work 48 hrs/fortnight. Germany: Dependents can join you; additional blocked account funds may be required. Netherlands, New Zealand, and Ireland: All allow dependents with additional financial proof. Always check the specific country's rules on dependent work rights and study rights.

When should I apply for my student visa?+

Apply as soon as you receive your university acceptance letter and have your financial documents ready. Most countries allow applications 3–6 months before your programme starts. UK: You can apply up to 6 months before your course starts (3 months if inside the UK). Apply 2–3 months before to allow for biometrics and processing. USA: You can apply up to 120 days before your programme starts. Apply as early as possible due to embassy interview wait times. Canada: Apply 3–6 months before — processing can take 8–16 weeks. Australia: Apply as soon as you have your CoE and OSHC. Germany: Apply early — German embassies have long appointment queues, especially in peak season (June–August). General advice: Applying early gives you time for any unexpected delays, additional document requests, or reapplication if refused. Never wait until the last minute.
