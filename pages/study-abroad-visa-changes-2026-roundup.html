Study Abroad Visa Changes 2026: The Rulebook

🗓️ Sep 10, 2026⏱️ 16 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

2026 is rewriting the international student rulebook, and the changes are not cosmetic. The United States is ending “duration of status” for F-1 students in favour of fixed admission periods; Canada is running a capped study permit system but carving its strongest applicants out of the attestation queue; the UK has reset its financial thresholds and dependant rules; and Australia has a fully settled Genuine Student framework. If you are applying for 2027 entry, every one of these changes touches your documents, your budget and your timeline.

Reading four country-specific policy changes separately is exactly how students miss the pattern. This roundup puts them side by side — what changed, when it changed, who it hits, and what to do about it — so you can plan a single, coherent application strategy.

United States: The F-1 Fixed-Time Rule

On 17 July 2026 the Department of Homeland Security published a final rule — Establishing a Fixed Time Period of Admission and an Extension of Stay Procedure — that dismantles the decades-old “duration of status” (D/S) framework for F nonimmigrants. From the 15 September 2026 effective date:

  • F-1 students are admitted for a fixed period set by the programme length on their Form I-20, not to exceed four years, plus a 30-day arrival period and a 30-day departure period.
  • Your admission is stamped as an Admit Until Date (AUD) on your Form I-94 instead of “D/S” — the AUD is the operative number, not the visa in your passport.
  • Students who need more time — to finish a programme, start a new programme, or do post-completion or STEM OPT — must apply for an extension of stay (EOS) with USCIS or depart the US and obtain a new period of admission on re-entry.
  • New restrictions limit changing majors and transferring: undergraduates can change programmes or schools only after one full academic year, and graduate students generally cannot change educational objectives at all under the rule.

There is transition protection: students already in the US in valid F-1 status on 15 September 2026 keep their D/S admission until their I-20 programme end or the end of authorised OPT/STEM OPT, capped at 14 November 2030. And the four-year figure is a cap on a single period of admission, not a lifetime limit — a five-year PhD is still possible via an EOS or readmission. Note that a university coalition has filed suit to block the rule, so the effective date may shift; keep reading our dedicated US F-1 fixed time limit guide for updates.

Canada: A Tightened Cap with a Master's & PhD Exemption

Canada continues its managed-intake era. For 2026 the national picture is:

  • A cap of 309,670 study permit application spaces for applicants who need a Provincial or Territorial Attestation Letter, with provincial allocations distributed by population and historical approval rates — the pool can be exhausted, at which point a province stops issuing attestations.
  • Within the cap plus exempt cohorts, IRCC expects to issue roughly 408,000 study permits in 2026 (new arrivals and renewals together), a continued decline meant to shrink the temporary-resident population.
  • The headline 2026 reform for strong applicants: Master's and doctoral students at publicly funded DLIs are exempt from the PAL/TAL requirement from 1 January 2026. Primary/secondary students and certain government-scholarship holders are also exempt.
  • Proof of funds rose again: from 1 September 2026 a single applicant outside Quebec must show CAD 23,448 for living expenses (up from CAD 22,895), plus first-year tuition and travel costs.

For Pakistani applicants the practical change is large. A Master's or PhD applicant no longer depends on a province's attestation allocation — they can apply as soon as they hold an offer, which removes one of the chronic delay points of the last two cycles. College and undergraduate applicants face the opposite: an earlier race for a shrinking pool. See our Canada study permit cap and PAL guide for the step-by-step.

United Kingdom: Costs, Conditions and the Dependant Question

The UK's 2026 Student route is more expensive and more selective in who it lets bring family:

  • Financial requirement: £1,529 per month (up to 9 months) for London (£13,761) or £1,171 per month (£10,539) outside London, plus any unpaid first-year tuition on your CAS, held 28 consecutive days ending within 31 days of application.
  • Fees and health surcharge: application fee is £558 (from 8 April 2026) and the Immigration Health Surcharge is £776 per year — both form a real upfront budget line.
  • Dependants: only PhD, research-based postgraduate, and government-sponsored students can generally bring a partner or child under 18; taught-master's students cannot.
  • Post-study: the Graduate Route still offers 2 years (master's) or 3 years (PhD) of UK work rights after a degree — the main reason funding a UK degree remains worth the upfront cost.

English-language thresholds matter more for transition points: towards the B2 end for study-to-work status changes, and courses in sensitive fields need an ATAS certificate before a visa is issued. Read the full picture in our UK student visa from Pakistan guide.

Australia: The Genuine Student Framework Is Now Business as Usual

Australia replaced the Genuine Temporary Entrant (GTE) requirement with the Genuine Student (GS) requirement in March 2024 and has been tightening around it since. For 2026 applicants the practical rules are:

  • GS assessment is structured into targeted questions on study intent, course choice and personal circumstances — answer plainly and with evidence, not with a formulaic statement.
  • English: IELTS 6.0 minimum for most Subclass 500 pathways (and comparable equivalents on other accepted tests); the Subclass 485 skilled graduate visa sits at 6.5 baseline.
  • Funds: living-costs evidence of AUD 29,710 plus tuition and travel; recent rounds have scrutinised the source and availability of funds in all cases, not only high-risk ones.
  • Dependants and work rights remain conditional, and processing is multi-stage — offer, CoE, then the visa — which is why Australia keeps counselling the earliest possible applications.

How to Build a Visa-Proof Application in 2026

  • Anchor your calendar to the EARLIEST date — a Canadian attestation pool, a December US PhD deadline, or a visa-processing runway — not the advertised admission deadline.
  • Prepare financial evidence the way an officer reads it: 28-day holding periods (UK), source explanations, and the exact current threshold for your destination. Our proof of funds guide covers each country's numbers.
  • Check your I-94 and I-20 dates if you are US-bound, and add the extension-of-stay calendar to your programme plan.
  • Stay current: US litigation, Canadian allocations and UK fee changes all shift fast — verify against official government pages, not social media.

The students who plan this properly will find that the crowd beside them in the visa queue is much smaller than the crowd who intended to apply. Be on the right side of that filter.

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest student visa change in 2026?+

The biggest structural change is the US fixed admission rule effective 15 September 2026: F-1 and F-2 students are admitted for a fixed period (up to the programme length on the I-20, capped at four years, plus a 30-day departure period) instead of “duration of status”, with a formal extension of stay process for students who need more time. Canada's 2026 cap and the new Master's/PhD PAL exemption are the other headline changes.

Does the 4-year F-1 limit mean I can only study in the US for four years total?+

No — the four-year cap applies to a single period of admission, not a lifetime limit. The authoritative read is: you are admitted for the shorter of the programme end date on your I-20 and four years (plus a 30-day departure period); students whose programmes run longer (for example a five-year PhD) apply for an extension of stay with USCIS or depart and re-enter for a new period. Check your I-94 and coordinate with your DSO well before your admit-until date.

Does Canada still have a study permit cap in 2026?+

Yes. For 2026 Canada set 309,670 study permit application spaces for applicants who require a Provincial or Territorial Attestation Letter, within a wider plan to issue roughly 408,000 study permits overall (including exempt groups and extensions). Master's and doctoral students at publicly funded DLIs no longer need a PAL from 1 January 2026, and neither do primary/secondary students and certain government-scholarship holders.

How much does the UK require in maintenance funds now?+

For 2026/27, UK student visa applicants must show £1,529 per month for up to 9 months if studying in London (£13,761 total) or £1,171 per month outside London (£10,539 total), on top of any unpaid first-year tuition shown on their CAS. Funds must be held for 28 consecutive days ending within 31 days of the application. The application fee rose to £558 from 8 April 2026 and the Immigration Health Surcharge is £776 per year.

What replaced the GTE requirement in Australia?+

The Genuine Temporary Entrant (GTE) requirement was replaced by the Genuine Student (GS) requirement in March 2024, and it is now a standard part of Subclass 500 assessments in 2026. The GS questions target genuine study intent, course choice and personal circumstances rather than a single statement. English minimums are 6.0 overall for most Subclass 500 pathways and 6.5 for the Subclass 485, with a funds requirement of AUD 29,710 for living costs.

Which of these changes affect existing students?+

Almost none retroactively. Students already in valid F-1 status before 15 September 2026 keep their duration-of-status admission until their I-20 programme end (or post-completion/STEM OPT end), with a maximum transition cap of 14 November 2030. Canada's 2026 cap and PAL rules apply prospectively to applications, not to continuing students. The main thing to watch is travel: an F-1 who leaves and re-enters the US on or after 15 September 2026 will receive a fixed-admission I-94 on return.
