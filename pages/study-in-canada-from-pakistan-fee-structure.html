Study in Canada from Pakistan: Fee Structure, Scholarships & Visa 2026

🗓️ Aug 17, 2026⏱️ 14 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Canada has become the most popular study abroad destination for Pakistani students, and for good reason. The country offers high-quality education, post-graduation work opportunities, and a clear pathway to permanent residency. But behind the appeal lies a practical question that every family asks: how much does it actually cost?

The answer depends on your programme, your city, and your pathway. A diploma at a Toronto college costs differently from a master's at the University of British Columbia. Living in Vancouver is more expensive than living in Manitoba. And the Student Direct Stream (SDS) visa route can cut your processing time from months to weeks. This guide breaks down every cost category so you can plan with clarity, not guesswork.

Key takeaways

  • Tuition ranges from CAD 10,000 to 40,000 per year depending on programme level.
  • Living costs range from CAD 10,000 to 20,000 per year depending on city.
  • The SDS route gets your visa in 20 days, but requires IELTS 6.0 with no band below 6.0 and a GIC of CAD 20,635.
  • International students can work 24 hours per week during term and full-time during breaks.
  • The Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) allows 1 to 3 years of open work after graduation.
  • Total first-year budget: CAD 30,000 to 60,000 depending on city and programme.

Tuition by programme type

Tuition is the largest single cost. It varies significantly by institution type and programme level. Below are realistic ranges for the 2026–27 academic year:

Diploma and certificate programmes at public colleges typically cost CAD 10,000 to 18,000 per year. Institutions like Seneca College, Humber College, Conestoga College, and Centennial College fall in this range. These programmes are popular with Pakistani students because they are more affordable, have lower English requirements, and provide practical training with co-op work placements.

Bachelor's degrees at universities range from CAD 15,000 to 35,000 per year. Smaller universities like the University of Manitoba or Memorial University of Newfoundland are at the lower end, while large research universities like the University of Toronto, McGill University, and the University of British Columbia charge more. Engineering, computer science, and business programmes tend to cost more than humanities.

Master's programmes typically cost CAD 15,000 to 40,000 per year. Research-based master's sometimes include funding through teaching or research assistantships, which can reduce your out-of-pocket cost. Professional programmes like the MBA are significantly higher.

MBA programmes at top Canadian schools range from CAD 30,000 to 60,000 per year. Rotman (University of Toronto), Ivey (Western University), Schulich (York University), and Desautels (McGill) are among the most competitive and expensive. Some smaller universities offer MBA programmes in the CAD 20,000 to 30,000 range.

Living costs breakdown

Canada requires proof of sufficient funds for living costs as part of the study permit application. The amounts below reflect the proof-of-funds requirement and realistic monthly expenses:

  • Accommodation: CAD 600 to 1,500 per month. On-campus residence is typically CAD 800 to 1,200. Shared off-campus housing is CAD 600 to 900. Private studios in major cities can reach CAD 1,500 or more.
  • Food: CAD 300 to 500 per month. Grocery costs vary by city, with Toronto and Vancouver being the most expensive. Cooking at home saves significantly compared to eating out.
  • Transportation: CAD 80 to 150 per month. Most cities offer discounted student transit passes. Some smaller cities are walkable or have free transit for students.
  • Health insurance: CAD 600 to 900 per year. Most provinces cover international students through their health plans, but some require private insurance. Alberta, British Columbia, and Manitoba include international students in their provincial plans.
  • Books and supplies: CAD 500 to 1,000 per year. Used textbooks and digital resources can reduce this significantly.
  • Total annual living costs: CAD 10,000 to 20,000 depending on city and lifestyle.

Smaller cities like Winnipeg, Halifax, Saskatoon, and Moncton offer substantially lower living costs than Toronto and Vancouver. For a detailed budgeting guide, see our cost and budget guide.

SDS vs regular study permit stream

Pakistani students have two pathways to a Canadian study permit. Understanding the difference is critical for planning:

Student Direct Stream (SDS) is an expedited processing route that guarantees a decision within 20 calendar days. To qualify, Pakistani students must:

  • Have an acceptance letter from a Designated Learning Institution (DLI)
  • Purchase a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) of CAD 20,635 from a participating Canadian bank
  • Pay first-year tuition in full
  • Score IELTS 6.0 overall with no band below 6.0 (academic or general training)
  • Have a clean background check and medical exam

The GIC is purchased before you apply. The funds are held in a Canadian bank and released to you in monthly instalments after you arrive, helping cover living costs during your first year.

Regular study permit stream does not require a GIC or upfront tuition payment, and accepts more flexible financial proof (bank statements, sponsor letters, scholarship letters). However, processing takes 8 to 12 weeks from Pakistan, and there is no guaranteed timeline. If your SDS application is denied, you can reapply through the regular stream.

Work rights for international students

Canada offers generous work rights that make it financially viable for many Pakistani students:

  • On-campus work: No hour limit. You can work at any on-campus employer from the first day of your programme.
  • Off-campus work: Up to 24 hours per week during regular academic sessions. Full-time during scheduled breaks (winter, summer, reading weeks).
  • Co-op and internships: If your programme includes a mandatory co-op or internship component, you need a co-op work permit in addition to your study permit. This allows full-time work during the placement period.
  • Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP): After completing a programme of at least 8 months, you can get an open work permit valid for the length of your programme (up to 3 years). This is not employer-specific, meaning you can work for any employer in Canada. The PGWP is widely considered one of Canada's strongest advantages for international students.

Many Pakistani students use the PGWP as a pathway to permanent residency through the Express Entry system, where Canadian education and work experience earn significant points.

Scholarships for Pakistani students in Canada

While Canadian scholarships are competitive, several major awards are open to Pakistani applicants:

Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarships are Canada's most prestigious doctoral awards, offering CAD 50,000 per year for three years. They are open to both Canadian and international students and are awarded based on academic excellence, research potential, and leadership.

Lester B. Pearson Scholarship at the University of Toronto is a fully funded undergraduate award covering tuition, books, incidentals, and residence for four years. It is one of the most competitive international scholarships in the world, with approximately 37 scholars selected globally each year.

Trudeau Foundation Scholarships provide up to CAD 60,000 per year for doctoral students who demonstrate engagement in social issues and leadership.

University-specific entrance scholarships are also widely available. The University of British Columbia's International Leader of Tomorrow Award, McMaster University's Global Excellence Scholarship, and the University of Waterloo's International Master's and Doctoral Awards offer CAD 2,000 to 40,000. Many colleges like Seneca and Humber also offer entrance scholarships of CAD 2,000 to 5,000 for strong applicants.

Application timeline

A well-planned timeline is the difference between a smooth application and a stressful one. Here is a month-by-month guide for September 2027 intake:

  • 12–18 months before (March–September 2025): Research programmes and institutions. Take the IELTS (or equivalent). Start building your application documents.
  • 10–12 months before (September–November 2025): Apply to Canadian institutions. Most college and university applications open in the fall. Submit your application with official transcripts, English test scores, and a statement of purpose.
  • 8–10 months before (November–January 2026): Receive offer letters. Accept your offer and pay any required deposit. Begin the GIC process if applying via SDS.
  • 6–8 months before (January–March 2026): Apply for the SDS study permit. Submit your biometrics. Complete the medical examination.
  • 4–6 months before (March–May 2026): Receive your study permit decision. Book accommodation. Begin planning your travel and arrival logistics.
  • 2–3 months before (June–July 2026): Book flights. Attend pre-departure orientations. Join Pakistani student groups at your institution for community support.

Choosing between college and university

One of the most common decisions for Pakistani students is whether to apply to a college or a university. Both have advantages:

Colleges (like Seneca, Humber, Conestoga, and George Brown) offer diploma and certificate programmes that are more affordable, have shorter durations (1–2 years), include co-op placements, and have lower English requirements. They are an excellent pathway for students who want to enter the Canadian job market quickly or use the diploma as a stepping stone to a university degree through a transfer agreement.

Universities (like Toronto, UBC, McGill, Alberta, and Waterloo) offer bachelor's and master's degrees that are more expensive but carry higher prestige, provide access to research opportunities, and lead to longer PGWP validity (3 years for master's and doctoral programmes). They are the better choice if your long-term goal is research, academia, or professional licensing.

Many successful Pakistani students start at a college, gain Canadian work experience, and then transfer to a university for a bachelor's or master's degree. This pathway can reduce overall costs while building a strong Canadian profile.

Final thoughts

Canada offers Pakistani students a compelling combination of quality education, affordable tuition (compared to the US and UK), generous work rights, and a clear path to permanent residency. The key to a successful application is understanding the true cost, choosing the right programme and pathway, and planning your timeline well in advance. Whether you choose SDS or the regular stream, college or university, the investment is one that can transform your career trajectory. Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost for Pakistani students to study in Canada?+

Tuition ranges from CAD 15,000 to 35,000 per year for undergraduate programmes, CAD 10,000 to 25,000 for diploma/certificate programmes, and CAD 15,000 to 40,000 for master's programmes. Living costs are CAD 10,000 to 20,000 per year depending on city.

What is the SDS programme and do Pakistani students qualify?+

The Student Direct Stream (SDS) is an expedited study permit processing route that guarantees a decision within 20 calendar days. Pakistani students qualify if they have an acceptance letter from a Designated Learning Institution, purchase a Guaranteed Investment Certificate of CAD 20,635, pay first-year tuition, and meet language requirements (IELTS 6.0 overall with no band below 6.0).

Can I work while studying in Canada?+

Yes. International students can work up to 24 hours per week off campus during regular academic sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks. On-campus work has no hour limit. The post-graduation work permit (PGWP) allows 1 to 3 years of open work after graduation.

What scholarships are available for Pakistani students in Canada?+

Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarships (CAD 50,000/year for 3 years), Lester B. Pearson Scholarship (University of Toronto, fully funded), Trudeau Foundation Scholarships, and university-specific entrance scholarships ranging from CAD 2,000 to 40,000.

How long does the Canada student visa take from Pakistan?+

Via the SDS route, decisions are guaranteed within 20 calendar days. Regular study permit processing from Pakistan typically takes 8 to 12 weeks. Apply at least 3 to 4 months before your program start date.
