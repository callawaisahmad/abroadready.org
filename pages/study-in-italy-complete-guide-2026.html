Study in Italy: Complete 2026 Guide (Admissions, Fees, Visa & Scholarships)

🗓️ Aug 17, 2026⏱️ 28 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Italy is one of the most popular study abroad destinations in Europe, and for good reason. Public university tuition can be as low as EUR 0 per year, the DSU scholarship covers tuition and living costs for low-income students, and over 500 English-taught programmes are available across the country.

With 300,000+ international students already studying in Italy, the country has built a strong infrastructure for welcoming foreign students. From world-class universities like Bologna and Politecnico di Milano to affordable living costs and a central European location, Italy offers a study abroad experience that is hard to beat.

This guide covers everything you need to know about studying in Italy in 2026: tuition fees, university profiles, the DSU scholarship, admission requirements, the Universitaly portal, student visa process, living costs, and working after graduation.

1. Why Study in Italy?

Italy consistently ranks among the top 5 study abroad destinations in Europe. Here is why thousands of international students choose Italy every year.

Extremely low tuition fees. Public universities in Italy charge tuition based on family income through the ISEE system. Many international students pay between EUR 0 and EUR 4,000 per year. This is dramatically lower than the UK, US, or even other European countries like the Netherlands or Ireland.

DSU scholarship for living costs. The DSU (Diritto allo Studio Universitario) scholarship is available to low-income students, including non-EU nationals. It covers tuition fees, provides a meal allowance of EUR 2,000 to 5,000 per year, and offers free or discounted university housing. Some students effectively study in Italy for free while receiving money for food and accommodation.

3-year bachelor's degrees. Italy follows the Bologna Process, which means bachelor's degrees (Laurea) are completed in 3 years instead of 4. Master's degrees (Laurea Magistrale) take 2 years. This saves you one full year of tuition and living costs compared to the American or British system.

500+ English-taught programmes. Italian universities offer hundreds of degree programmes entirely in English. You do not need to speak Italian to study in Italy. Popular English-taught fields include engineering, computer science, business, economics, political science, medicine, and architecture.

World-class universities. The University of Bologna, founded in 1088, is the oldest university in the world. Politecnico di Milano is ranked among the top 20 universities globally for design and engineering. Sapienza University of Rome has produced 13 Nobel laureates. Italy's academic tradition is unmatched.

Rich culture and quality of life. Italy is home to more UNESCO World Heritage Sites than any other country. The food is world-famous. The weather is excellent. The cost of living is lower than in Northern Europe. You get to live in a country that people spend thousands of euros to visit as tourists.

Central European location. Italy sits in the heart of the Mediterranean, with easy access to France, Switzerland, Austria, Spain, and Greece. Budget airlines connect Italian cities to all of Europe for as little as EUR 20 to 50. Weekend trips to Barcelona, Paris, or Vienna are entirely realistic.

300,000+ international students. Italy has a large and growing international student community. You will find student groups, expat communities, and support services in every major university city.

2. Italian Education System

Understanding how the Italian education system works is essential before you apply. Here is a clear breakdown of the structure.

Public universities (Università Pubbliche). These are state-funded institutions that charge low tuition fees based on family income. They include the majority of Italy's top-ranked universities: Bologna, Sapienza, Padua, Milan, Pisa, Florence, and many more. Public universities in Italy offer the best value for money in European higher education.

Private universities (Università Private). These are funded by tuition fees and private endowments. They tend to be smaller, more internationally oriented, and more expensive. Examples include Bocconi University (business), Luiss (political science), and John Cabot University (American-style liberal arts in Rome). Private university tuition ranges from EUR 6,000 to 25,000 per year.

Polytechnics (Politecnici). Italy has several technical universities that specialise in engineering, architecture, and design. The two most famous are Politecnico di Milano and Politecnico di Torino. These institutions are world leaders in their fields and offer many English-taught programmes.

Degree structure. Italian universities follow the Bologna Process, which standardises degrees across Europe:

  • Laurea (Bachelor's): 3 years, 180 ECTS credits. Entry after upper secondary school (12 years of education).
  • Laurea Magistrale (Master's): 2 years, 120 ECTS credits. Entry after a Laurea degree.
  • Dottorato (PhD): 3 years. Entry after a Laurea Magistrale.
  • Laurea a Ciclo Unico (Integrated Master's): 5 or 6 years. Used for medicine, law, pharmacy, and architecture.

ECTS credit system. Each course is assigned ECTS (European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System) credits. One ECTS equals 25 hours of student work. A full academic year is 60 ECTS. This system makes it easy to transfer credits between European universities.

Academic calendar. The academic year runs from October to September, divided into two semesters. The first semester typically starts in mid-September or early October and ends in December or January. The second semester starts in February or March and ends in June or July. Exams are held after each semester, not just at the end of the year.

MIUR oversight. The Italian Ministry of University and Research (MIUR) oversees the entire higher education system. MIUR sets accreditation standards, approves degree programmes, and manages the national university ranking system (CINECA).

3. Tuition Fees — Real Numbers for 2026

One of the biggest advantages of studying in Italy is the low cost of tuition. Here are the real numbers you can expect.

Public University Tuition

Italian public universities do not charge a flat fee to all students. Instead, tuition is calculated based on your family's economic situation using the ISEE (Indicatore della Situazione Economica Equivalente) or ISEEU (for international students) system. This means two students in the same programme can pay very different amounts.

For non-EU international students, the typical range at public universities is:

  • EUR 0 to EUR 1,500/year for students with low family income (ISEEU below EUR 25,000)
  • EUR 1,500 to EUR 3,000/year for students with middle family income
  • EUR 3,000 to EUR 4,000/year for students with higher family income

Some universities charge a flat annual fee regardless of income. For example, the University of Bologna charges non-EU students approximately EUR 2,500 to EUR 3,500 per year for most programmes. Politecnico di Milano charges around EUR 3,800 per year for non-EU master's students.

Medicine and surgery programmes at public universities typically cost between EUR 1,000 and EUR 4,000 per year, regardless of whether the instruction is in Italian or English. This is remarkably affordable compared to medical school tuition in the US (EUR 40,000+) or the UK (EUR 25,000+).

Private University Tuition

Private universities in Italy charge significantly more than public ones, but they are still cheaper than equivalent institutions in the US or UK.

  • Bocconi University (Milan): EUR 12,000 to 16,000 per year for bachelor's and master's programmes
  • Luiss Guido Carli (Rome): EUR 10,000 to 14,000 per year
  • John Cabot University (Rome): EUR 12,000 to 14,000 per year
  • IESE Business School (campuses in Italy): EUR 20,000 to 30,000+ per year
  • IULM University (Milan): EUR 8,000 to 12,000 per year
  • Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore (Milan): EUR 7,000 to 12,000 per year

Many private universities offer merit-based scholarships that can reduce tuition by 30% to 100%. Bocconi, for example, offers full tuition waivers to top applicants.

Living Costs by City

Your monthly living costs in Italy depend heavily on the city you choose:

  • Milan: EUR 800 to 1,200 per month (highest cost of living in Italy)
  • Rome: EUR 750 to 1,100 per month
  • Florence: EUR 650 to 950 per month
  • Bologna: EUR 600 to 900 per month
  • Turin: EUR 550 to 850 per month
  • Pisa: EUR 500 to 750 per month
  • Palermo, Naples, Catania: EUR 450 to 700 per month

These costs include rent, food, transport, and basic expenses. Living in a smaller city like Pisa or Bologna can save you EUR 200 to 400 per month compared to Milan, and these cities also have excellent universities.

5. DSU Scholarship — Complete Guide

The DSU scholarship is the most important financial aid programme for international students in Italy. Here is everything you need to know to apply and win it.

What is DSU? DSU stands for Diritto allo Studio Universitario (Right to University Study). It is a regional scholarship programme funded by the Italian government and regional authorities. The scholarship is awarded based on economic need, not academic merit alone. It is available to both Italian and international students, including non-EU nationals.

Who is eligible? To qualify for the DSU scholarship, you must:

  • Be enrolled or have applied to an Italian university
  • Meet the ISEEU (Indicatore della Situazione Economica Equivalente per gli Studenti Universitari) income threshold, which varies by region but is typically below EUR 25,000 to 28,000
  • Not have a degree from the same level of study in Italy
  • Meet the academic requirements (minimum ECTS credits from the previous year, typically 25-30 ECTS for renewal)

What does it cover? The DSU scholarship provides several benefits:

  • Tuition fee waiver: Full or partial exemption from university tuition fees
  • Meal allowance: EUR 2,000 to 5,000 per year, paid as a lump sum or through a meal card
  • University housing: Free or highly discounted accommodation in university dormitories (EUR 100 to 300 per month instead of EUR 400 to 800)
  • Additional benefits: Some regions offer extra funding for study abroad periods, books, and transport

How to apply. The application process varies slightly by region, but the general steps are:

  1. Check the DSU call for applications (bando) on your university or regional DSU website
  2. Complete the online application form
  3. Submit required documents: passport, income certificates from your home country (translated and legalised), family composition certificate, tax code (codice fiscale), and academic transcript
  4. Attend the in-person verification if required
  5. Wait for the results (typically announced 2-3 months after the deadline)

Required documents for non-EU students:

  • Passport copy
  • ISEEU calculation (your university's student office can help you calculate this)
  • Income certificate from your home country (translated into Italian and legalised by the Italian embassy)
  • Family composition certificate
  • Italian tax code (codice fiscale)
  • Enrolment receipt or pre-enrolment confirmation

Deadlines. DSU application deadlines vary by region. In most regions, applications open between July and September for the following academic year. Northern regions like Lombardy (Milan) and Emilia-Romagna (Bologna) typically have earlier deadlines than southern regions.

Important tips:

  • Apply every year. The DSU scholarship must be renewed annually. You need to maintain minimum academic progress (usually 25-30 ECTS credits per year) to keep it.
  • Start early. Gather your documents from your home country before you arrive in Italy. Getting income certificates translated and legalised can take weeks.
  • Use your university's help. Most universities have a DSU office (ufficio DSU) that guides international students through the process. Do not hesitate to ask for help.
  • Apply even if unsure. The worst that can happen is your application is rejected. The best case is that your entire education is funded.

6. Admission Requirements

Admission requirements in Italy depend on the level of study and the programme. Here is a detailed breakdown.

Bachelor's Degree (Laurea)

  • High school diploma: You need a secondary school diploma representing at least 12 years of education. If your country has an 11-year system, you may need to complete a foundation year or provide additional documentation.
  • Pre-enrolment via Universitaly: Non-EU students must submit a pre-enrolment application through the official Universitaly portal. This is a mandatory step for the visa process.
  • Language certificate: For English-taught programmes: IELTS 6.0-6.5, TOEFL iBT 79-90, or Cambridge B2 First. For Italian-taught programmes: Italian B2 (CILS, CELI, or PLIDA). See our guide to studying abroad without IELTS for alternatives.
  • SAT or ACT: Some universities (especially Bocconi) require SAT or ACT scores for bachelor's admission.
  • Passport copy and passport-size photos.
  • Admission test: Some programmes require an admission test. Medicine requires the IMAT (International Medical Admissions Test). Engineering and architecture programmes may require a test at the university level.

Master's Degree (Laurea Magistrale)

  • Bachelor's degree: A completed bachelor's degree (minimum 3 years, 180 ECTS) in a relevant field.
  • Academic transcript: Official transcript from your previous university with grades and courses.
  • Curriculum vitae (CV): A detailed CV in European format (Europass preferred).
  • Motivation letter: In Italian, this is called lettera di motivazione. Explain why you want to study this programme, your academic background, and your career goals. Keep it to 1-2 pages.
  • Letters of recommendation: Typically 1-2 academic references from professors at your previous university.
  • Portfolio: Required for design, architecture, and fine arts programmes. Include your best work with descriptions.
  • Language certificate: Same requirements as bachelor's programmes.
  • GRE/GMAT: Required by some programmes (especially at Bocconi and Politecnico di Milano).

Medicine and Surgery

Medicine is one of the most competitive programmes in Italy. Admission is through the IMAT (International Medical Admissions Test), held annually in September. The test consists of:

  • 60 multiple-choice questions covering biology, chemistry, physics, mathematics, and logical reasoning
  • 90 minutes to complete the test
  • No negative marking for wrong answers
  • Results used for ranking: Your IMAT score determines your position in the admission ranking. Top scorers get their first-choice university.

Key English-taught medicine programmes are available at the University of Bologna, University of Padua, University of Milan, Sapienza University of Rome, and the University of Pavia. Tuition at public universities for medicine is typically EUR 1,000 to 4,000 per year.

7. Universitaly — Step-by-Step Application Process

Universitaly is the official Italian government portal for international student pre-enrolment. If you are a non-EU student, using this portal is mandatory for your university application and visa process.

Step 1: Create an account. Visit universitaly.it and click "Register" (Registrati). You will need to provide your email address, personal information, and create a password. You will receive a verification email to confirm your account.

Step 2: Choose your programme. Browse the catalogue of available programmes. You can filter by language (English or Italian), field of study, university, and city. Take your time to research programmes and check the admission requirements on the university's website.

Step 3: Upload documents. For each programme application, you will need to upload:

  • Passport copy
  • Academic transcripts (translated and legalised if not in Italian)
  • Diploma or degree certificate
  • Language certificate
  • CV in Europass format
  • Motivation letter
  • Any additional documents required by the specific programme

Step 4: Submit your pre-enrolment application. Review everything carefully before submitting. Once submitted, you cannot make changes. You will receive a confirmation email and a PDF of your pre-enrolment receipt.

Step 5: Embassy validation. After submission, your pre-enrolment application is forwarded to the Italian embassy or consulate in your country. The embassy will contact you for an interview and to process your visa. You must attend this appointment in person.

Step 6: Wait for confirmation. The embassy will issue a pre-enrolment confirmation once your application is approved. This document is required for your student visa application. Processing times vary by embassy but typically take 2 to 6 weeks.

Key deadlines:

  • Portal opens: Usually January or February
  • Application deadline: Varies by programme, typically between February and May
  • Embassy validation: May to July
  • Visa application: June to August
  • Programme start: September or October

For the most up-to-date deadlines, always check the Universitaly portal and your university's international office website.

8. Student Visa Process

If you are a non-EU student planning to study in Italy for more than 90 days, you need a Type D study visa (Visto Nazionale). Here is the complete process.

Required Documents

  • Valid passport: Must be valid for at least 3 months beyond your planned stay in Italy
  • Pre-enrolment receipt: The confirmation from Universitaly showing your pre-enrolment application
  • Admission letter: The official acceptance letter from your Italian university
  • Financial proof: Evidence that you can support yourself financially. You need to show access to at least EUR 6,000 to 10,000 per year (the amount varies by region and university). This can be a bank statement, scholarship letter, or sponsor's financial guarantee
  • Health insurance: Health insurance covering medical expenses in Italy. This can be Italian SSN (national health service) registration or private health insurance with a minimum coverage of EUR 30,000
  • Accommodation proof: A confirmed place to stay in Italy — university dormitory booking, rental contract, or a letter from a host
  • Language certificate: Proof of language proficiency in the language of instruction (IELTS for English programmes, CILS/CELI for Italian programmes)
  • Passport-size photos: Two recent passport-size photographs
  • Visa application form: Completed and signed visa application form (available from the Italian embassy website)
  • Visa fee: EUR 50 (non-refundable)

Application Process

  1. Book an appointment at the Italian embassy or consulate in your country. Many embassies now use online booking systems.
  2. Attend the appointment in person with all required documents. Bring original documents and copies.
  3. Submit biometric data (fingerprints and photograph) at the embassy.
  4. Pay the visa fee (EUR 50) at the embassy or before the appointment.
  5. Wait for processing. Visa processing typically takes 2 to 4 weeks, but can take longer during peak season (July-September). Apply as early as possible.
  6. Collect your passport with the visa sticker once approved.

After Arrival in Italy

Once you arrive in Italy with your student visa, you must apply for a permesso di soggiorno (residence permit) within 8 days of arrival. Here is how:

  1. Go to a post office (Poste Italiane) that handles permesso di soggiorno applications
  2. Fill out the kit with your personal information, visa details, and accommodation information
  3. Submit the kit with your passport copy, visa copy, passport-size photo, and the marca da bollo (EUR 16 tax stamp)
  4. You will receive a receipt (ricevuta) — this is your temporary residence permit
  5. Schedule an appointment at the local police questura for final verification
  6. The physical permesso di soggiorno card will be mailed to you within a few months

Your residence permit is valid for the duration of your studies and can be renewed annually. Keep your ricevuta (receipt) safe — it serves as proof of legal residence while your permit is being processed.

9. Living in Italy — Daily Life Guide

Moving to Italy means adjusting to a new culture, food, and way of life. Here is what to expect as an international student.

Accommodation

Finding accommodation is one of the first things you should do after receiving your admission letter. Options include:

  • University halls of residence (residenze universitarie): EUR 200 to 500 per month. These are the cheapest option and often include meals. Apply through your university's housing office or EDISU/DSU housing. Spaces fill up fast — apply early.
  • Shared flat (affitto condiviso): EUR 300 to 600 per month. This is the most popular option among international students. You share an apartment with other students or young professionals. Use platforms like Subito.it, Immobiliare.it, or student Facebook groups.
  • Private flat (appartamento privato): EUR 400 to 800 per month. A studio or one-bedroom apartment for yourself. More expensive but offers privacy.
  • Host family (famiglia ospitante): EUR 400 to 700 per month. Live with an Italian family. Good for language immersion and cultural exchange.

Tip: In Italy, rental contracts typically require a deposit (cauzione) of 2-3 months' rent. Budget for this upfront cost.

Food

Italian food is famous worldwide, and living in Italy means eating well at affordable prices. Monthly food costs are typically EUR 200 to 350.

  • University mensa (canteen): Meals for EUR 2 to 5 with the student meal card (DSU holders often get free meals)
  • Supermarket shopping: Coop, Conad, Lidl, and Eurospin are the most popular chains. A weekly grocery shop costs EUR 30 to 50
  • Eating out: A pizza and drink at a restaurant costs EUR 8 to 15. A full meal costs EUR 12 to 25
  • Aperitivo: Many Italian cities have an "aperitivo" tradition where you pay EUR 7 to 12 for a drink and get free access to a buffet of snacks and food. This can essentially replace dinner.

Transport

  • Student monthly pass: EUR 20 to 50 per month (heavily discounted for students in most cities)
  • Regional trains: Trenitalia and Italo offer cheap connections between cities. Book in advance for the best fares.
  • Bicycles: Many Italian cities are becoming more bike-friendly. Bologna, Florence, and Turin have good cycling infrastructure.
  • Budget flights: Ryanair, EasyJet, and Wizz Air connect Italian cities to all of Europe from EUR 10 to 50 one way.

Healthcare

Italy has a national health service (SSN — Servizio Sanitario Nazionale) that international students can register for:

  • Register at your local ASL (Azienda Sanitaria Locale) office with your permesso di soggiorno and codice fiscale
  • You will receive a tessera sanitaria (health card) that gives you access to the same healthcare as Italian citizens
  • SSN registration costs approximately EUR 700 to 1,500 per year (varies by region) or may be free for DSU scholarship holders
  • Alternatively, you can purchase private health insurance from providers like SafetyWing, Allianz Care, or UniSalute

With the SSN, you can visit public hospitals and clinics (ambulatorio) for free or at very low cost. Prescriptions are subsidised.

Banking and Money

  • Open an Italian bank account to receive DSU scholarship payments and pay rent. Popular banks include UniCredit, Intesa Sanpaolo, and online banks like N26 or Revolut
  • Codice fiscale: You need an Italian tax code (codice fiscale) for banking, renting, and many other activities. You can get one from the Agenzia delle Entrate or your local Italian embassy before you travel
  • Cash: Italy is still a cash-heavy country, but card payments are becoming more common. Always carry some cash for small purchases

Phone and Internet

  • Mobile plans: EUR 10 to 20 per month from providers like TIM, Vodafone, WindTre, or Iliad. Most plans include unlimited calls and several GB of data
  • Student SIM discounts: Some providers offer special student tariffs
  • WiFi: Most university campuses and student housing offer free WiFi

10. Working in Italy as an International Student

Working while studying in Italy is allowed and can help offset living costs. Here are the rules and opportunities.

Working During Studies

  • Work limit: You can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and up to 1,040 hours per year (approximately 20 hours per week for the entire year)
  • Work permit: You need a nulla osta al lavoro (work permit) from the immigration office and a contract from an employer
  • No employer restrictions: You can work in any field, not just your area of study
  • Registration: After getting your work permit, register at the local employment office (Centro per l'Impiego)

Popular Part-Time Jobs for Students

  • Tutoring: Teaching English or other subjects to Italian students. EUR 10 to 25 per hour
  • Restaurant and bar work: Italy's tourism industry creates constant demand. EUR 8 to 12 per hour
  • Tourism and hospitality: Hotels, hostels, and tour companies in tourist cities hire international students
  • Campus jobs: Some universities hire students as research assistants or administrative helpers
  • Freelance and remote work: Online tutoring, translation, content writing, and graphic design

After Graduation

Italy offers clear pathways for international students to stay and work after completing their studies:

  • Job search residence permit: After graduation, you can convert your student residence permit to a permesso di soggiorno per ricerca lavoro (job search permit) for up to 12 months
  • Work permit conversion: Once you find a job, you can convert your job search permit to a work residence permit without leaving Italy
  • EU Blue Card: If you find a job with a salary of at least 1.5 times the national average, you may qualify for the EU Blue Card, which offers faster permanent residency
  • Permanent residence: After 5 years of legal residence in Italy, you can apply for permanent residence (carta di soggiorno CE)
  • Citizenship: After 10 years of legal residence (reduced to 4-5 years for certain categories), you can apply for Italian citizenship

  • Apply via Universitaly: Submit your pre-enrolment applications. You can apply to multiple programmes at different universities.

  • Buy flight tickets: Book your flight to Italy. Aim to arrive by mid-September.

September — October 2026

  • Travel to Italy: Arrive at least 1-2 weeks before classes start.
  • Apply for permesso di soggiorno: Within 8 days of arrival, submit your residence permit application at the post office.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to study in Italy?+

Public university tuition in Italy is based on family income and ranges from EUR 0 to 4,000 per year for non-EU students. Private university tuition ranges from EUR 6,000 to 25,000 per year. Many universities offer fee waivers through the DSU scholarship for low-income international students.

What is the DSU scholarship?+

DSU (Diritto allo Studio Universitario) is an Italian scholarship based on economic need. It covers tuition fees, provides a meal allowance (EUR 2,000-5,000/year), and free or discounted university housing. Non-EU students can apply if they meet income criteria. Applications typically open in summer for the following academic year.

Do I need IELTS to study in Italy?+

For English-taught programmes, most universities accept IELTS 6.0-6.5, TOEFL iBT 79-90, or Cambridge B2 First. For Italian-taught programmes, you need Italian B2 (CILS, CELI, or PLIDA). Some universities accept Duolingo. Many universities do not require IELTS if your previous degree was taught in English.

What are the best universities in Italy?+

Top universities include University of Bologna (oldest in the world), Politecnico di Milano, Sapienza University of Rome, University of Padua, University of Milan, Politecnico di Torino, University of Pisa, and University of Florence. Italy also has excellent private universities like Bocconi and Luigi Bocconi.

How do I apply to Italian universities?+

Non-EU students apply through Universitaly (the Italian government portal) or directly to universities. Some universities have their own application portals. You need to pre-apply for a visa at the Italian embassy before the programme starts. The main intake is September/October, with some spring programmes available.

Can I work while studying in Italy?+

Yes. International students can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and up to 1,040 hours per year. You need a work permit (nulla osta) and a contract from an employer. Many students find part-time work in tourism, hospitality, and tutoring.

What is the Italian student visa process?+

You need a Type D study visa for programmes longer than 90 days. Apply at the Italian embassy with your pre-enrolment receipt from Universitaly, admission letter, financial proof (EUR 6,000-10,000), health insurance, accommodation proof, and language certificate. The visa fee is EUR 50. Processing takes 2-4 weeks.

What is Universitaly?+

Universitaly is the official Italian government portal for international student pre-enrolment. Non-EU students must create an account, choose a programme, and submit their pre-enrolment application through the portal. The embassy then processes your visa based on this pre-enrolment. The portal opens annually (usually January-May).

Can I stay in Italy after graduation?+

Yes. After graduation, you can convert your student visa to a residence permit for job search (up to 12 months). During this period, you can work while looking for a qualified job. Once employed, you can switch to a work permit. After 5 years of legal residence, you can apply for permanent residence.

What is the 3-year Italian bachelor's system?+

Italian bachelor's degrees (Laurea) are 3 years (180 ECTS), followed by 2-year master's degrees (Laurea Magistrale, 120 ECTS). This is the Bologna Process standard used across Europe. Some programmes like medicine, law and architecture are longer (5-6 years).
