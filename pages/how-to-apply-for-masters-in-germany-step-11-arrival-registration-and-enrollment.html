What apply for masters in germany step 11 arrival actually covers

Apply for masters in germany step 11 arrival is the stage where a visa becomes a life. Steps 1 to 10 were a file, a fee, an interview and a stamp in a passport. Step 11 is the part where the stamp stops being enough: you land, you hold a document that expires in about ninety days, and you must convert it into a residence permit, a university registration, a bank account and an address the German state recognises as real. Everything here is domestic administration, conducted in German, in person, usually with an appointment you booked online three weeks ago. Almost nothing about it is difficult in principle. Almost all of it goes wrong because students treat it as a soft landing rather than a deadline-driven process with statutory clocks running.

Three of those clocks are real and non-negotiable. You must register your address within 14 days of moving in. You must enrol at the university before the semester starts, and in most cases before the first assessment of the semester. And you must apply for your residence permit before your entry visa expires, typically within the first three months after landing. Miss any one of them and the consequences escalate quickly: an unregistered address means no bank account, no insurance, no enrolment, no permit. This chapter covers the whole sequence in the order that actually unblocks things, plus the tasks that are less urgent but still need to happen in month one. For the wider context, see how to study in Germany from Pakistan in 2026.

How long does the whole step take?

Four to eight weeks, and the spread is explained by appointment availability rather than by your own effort. The Anmeldung takes one morning once you have a slot. The bank account takes one to three weeks of waiting for a mailed card. Enrolment can be online in a single evening or require an in-person appointment with certified originals. The residence permit is the long pole: in Berlin, Leipzig, Munich and Frankfurt, slots for the Ausländerbehörde run four to ten weeks ahead, which is why the correct strategy is to book the appointment in week one and supply documents later. Treat the whole of apply for masters in germany step 11 arrival as a two-month project that starts the moment your flight is booked.

What do I physically need to carry with me?

Your passport, your visa or entry sticker, your admission letter, about four passport photographs, roughly EUR 800 to EUR 1,500 in immediately available funds, and the complete set of certified documents for enrolment and immigration, in paper, in hand luggage. Not a photograph of a document on a phone. If your originals sit in checked baggage that goes to the wrong carousel, you have a serious problem. Put the three files in one A4 folder and a digital backup on a cloud account that works without a data connection.

What should I do in the first 72 hours after landing?

Land, get connected, and buy a German SIM in the arrivals hall if you did not. Then resist the temptation to explore. Within a week you should have an Anmeldung appointment booked, health insurance in motion, an enrolment appointment or portal login, and a residence permit appointment in the diary. Cities are full of students who spent their first fortnight sightseeing while their 14-day registration window and their visa clock quietly burned down. Apply for masters in germany step 11 arrival is not a holiday period; it is an administrative sprint with a five-day sightseeing window at most.

Do I need temporary accommodation before I have a permanent room?

Ideally no, and this is the most important decision you can make from Pakistan. Secure either a university dormitory place (a Studentenwohnheim place) or a signed flat contract before you fly, because the Anmeldung requires a landlord's Wohnungsgeberbestätigung confirming that you live there. Landlords in the big cities will not list a room without knowing your name and nationality, and a viewing by video call is normal. If you must arrive with no room, book a hostel for a maximum of two or three nights and treat flat-hunting as a full-time job in week one. Registering from a temporary address is possible in some cities but triggers a second registration when the real address changes, which is wasted time.

What is the very first appointment I should book?

The Anmeldung at the Bürgeramt, Bürgerbüro, Einwohnermeldeamt or Serviceamt for your district, booked the day you land, online, before anything else. Every other task depends on a registered address, so this appointment is the root of the dependency tree. Berlin's portal is the best documented example at https://service.berlin.de. If a slot is weeks out, add neighbouring districts and ask the Meldeamt whether walk-ins are accepted for students.

Should I open a bank account in the first three days?

Not physically, unless you find a branch that accepts a passport plus a visa as address proof. But shortlist two or three banks on day one and apply online the same week. Traditional banks such as the Sparkasse, the local Volksbank, the Commerzbank and the Deutsche Bank accept a passport and an entry visa for identity but normally want a registered address, so they are effectively step two. Mobile-first banks such as N26, DKB and ING work with a German address and a residence permit or electronic identity document, and post a card to your home address.

What if I cannot do everything in week one?

Then do it in this order: Anmeldung, residence permit booking, enrolment, health insurance, bank account, semester contribution, orientation week. The reason is dependency, not importance. Orientation week is the most valuable item for your day-to-day life and the least time-critical, because it happens on fixed dates. The Anmeldung is the least enjoyable item and the most structurally urgent, because nothing legal proceeds without it. Students who rank by enjoyment end up in trouble.

Should I handle this myself or ask the university?

Both, in parallel, with clear divisions. The university international office owns enrolment, the semester contribution, the student ID and exam deadlines. The Bürgeramt owns the Anmeldung. The Ausländerbehörde owns the residence permit. The insurer owns the certificate. The bank owns the account. You own the bookings, the document folder and the follow-up. A single confused email to the wrong office costs a week; a clear email with your name, application reference, nationality, programme and a specific question gets answered in two days.

When must I register my address, and what do I bring?

Within 14 days of moving into the flat. This is a statutory obligation under German registration law for anyone living in a dwelling, and it applies to foreign nationals exactly as it applies to German citizens. The registration is what makes you visible to the state, and it is the document that unlocks a bank account, a health insurance policy, a residence permit application, a library card, a mailing address for official notices, and eventually a tax number. Treat the 14 days as a hard ceiling, not a target. Applying for masters in germany step 11 arrival includes a registration appointment that you book before you land and attend in your first week, which is the only way this becomes stress-free.

What documents does the Bürgeramt need?

A passport or national identity card, plus any previous residence permit. A signed Wohnungsgeberbestätigung from your landlord or the dorm office, confirming the address, the number of people in the household and the landlord's details, which is the document most often forgotten. A signed registration form, often downloadable from the city portal and completable on site. A Meldeschein, though in most cities the confirmation is generated automatically once the registration is recorded. For a jointly rented flat, a confirmation covering all residents. A civil status document if relevant: a marriage certificate, a birth certificate for each child moving with you, a divorce decree or a death certificate for a changed surname. And a fee of up to roughly EUR 25 to EUR 37 in some cities, though registration itself is free in a number of municipalities. Bring originals plus one photocopy each, because some offices photocopy and charge for it.

What if I do not have a Wohnungsgeberbestätigung?

Ask the registration office for a substitute. A landlord is legally obliged to issue the confirmation, and a citizen who cannot obtain one may present an Wohnraumverweis from the housing office confirming where they are entitled to live, or in the last resort give a self-declaration. In practice, the commonest cause of a missing confirmation is a student who arrived with no contract or who is subletting informally. Both are risky: subletting without registration in cities such as Munich or Berlin can be treated as a permit issue. Sorting the paperwork with your landlord before you fly eliminates the entire category.

Do family members register too, and what changes with them?

Everyone who lives in the flat registers, each with their own appointment, and a family arriving together can often be handled in one visit with a marriage certificate or birth certificates. Bringing a spouse on a study visa is a separate immigration matter with its own proof of funding and, in some cases, a language requirement, and it is not automatically granted. Children need birth certificates and, if enrolled in a German Kita, a place. If your family situation changes after registration, report it to both the Bürgeramt and the Ausländerbehörde, since dependants affect the conditions of your permit.

How long does the Anmeldung appointment take?

Thirty to sixty minutes in the office, preceded by a wait of three to twelve weeks for the slot in high-demand cities. The interview is short and routine: the clerk checks identity, records the address, asks whether you have ever been registered in Germany before, and prints a confirmation. That confirmation is what you will show to banks, insurers, the university and the immigration office, so take two or three photocopies on the spot and photograph it the same day.

Do I need a German bank account, and can I open one before I register?

Yes for daily life, and in practice within days of registering. Rent is paid from a German current account by almost every German landlord, the semester contribution goes through a German account, wages and scholarships are paid into German accounts, and the blocked account that funded your visa pays out monthly into whatever account you nominate, which is usually German. A Girokonto is therefore not a convenience, it is infrastructure. Having said that, you are not blocked from receiving money before it exists: an international transfer service or a foreign account nominated on the visa paperwork can receive the payout, and Pakistani banks that support the visa account typically pay out to the account of record or to a nominated international account.

Which bank should a student pick?

Look for a student tariff, a free or cheap account, a free German IBAN, English or German language service, and no minimum balance. The public-sector Sparkasse banks and the Volksbanken Raiffeisenbanken cooperatives are strong on cash machines and student services, and many branches will not open an account without a residence permit or registered address, so they are a week-three task. N26, ING, DKB, the Commerzbank and the Deutsche Bank open accounts from a German address with an electronic identity document or residence permit, sometimes entirely by video, and are the realistic week-two option. The cost difference over a degree is small; the onboarding speed difference is large.

What do I need to open the account?

Passport, a German address, a residence permit or electronic identity document or entry visa plus registration confirmation, a proof of address, and often proof of student status, which is one more reason to complete enrolment in the first week. Online onboarding normally takes fifteen minutes plus a video identity check, and the physical card is posted to your registered address, arriving in about five to eight business days. Until then you can usually still use the account details for transfers, and German banks with a large branch network let you withdraw cash at their machines with a passport, which solves the gap.

How does the blocked account fit in once I am here?

The blocked account was a visa condition, and it stays a visa condition. If you opened it through a Pakistani bank before travel, you will receive the monthly amount in Pakistan or to a nominated account, and your visa officer will want evidence that you have been drawing the correct sum, because the monthly instalment is a condition of the visa and of the later permit. The German migration portal sets out the money proof options, including the blocked account, a scholarship award letter, a secured loan or a signed declaration by a host, at https://www.making-it-in-germany.com. For the wider money picture, see study abroad loans in Pakistan and the study abroad cost and budget guide.

Do I still need the blocked account after I have a residence permit?

Yes, for the duration of the permit in most cases. Funding sufficient for the period is a statutory condition of the study residence permit, and the authority re-checks it at every renewal. A scholarship that replaces the blocked account should be documented and presented at renewal, and a switch mid-degree is a reportable change. One genuine exception is the move from a master's to a doctorate, where funding is assessed on its own terms. If your funding fails or ends, contact the Ausländerbehörde before it fails rather than afterwards, because an unresolved funding gap is the commonest reason a renewal is refused.

How do I enrol at university, and what do I get for it?

Enrolment, called Immatrikulation or Einschreibung, converts your admission into student status. Until it is done you are not a student in the legal sense: you cannot register for examinations, borrow from the library, use a laboratory, receive a transcript, or be covered by the university's framework for a part-time job. Most universities set a deadline, often a few weeks before lectures begin and earlier for programmes with laboratory or clinical placements. In the current setup for apply for masters in germany step 11 arrival, enrolment is the task that converts an offer letter into a life, and it is the task students most often leave until the second week because it requires gathering papers.

What documents does enrolment need?

The admission letter or Zulassungsbescheid, your passport, the Beglaubigter Hochschulabschluss, that is a certified copy of your bachelor's degree certificate, and a certified transcript, with certified German translations where the originals are not in German or English. The APS certificate or the individualised APS confirmation number. Proof of the required language certificate. A certificate of health insurance enrolment as a student. Evidence of financing, such as a blocked account statement. A registration form, a recent passport photograph for the student ID, and a certificate of residence or a lease with landlord confirmation if asked. Some universities require documents in person or by post in hard copy even where the application was digital, and some accept scans for enrolment and then verify originals before the first examination.

What is the semester contribution, and what is the semester ticket?

The Semesterbeitrag is a legally required contribution that varies widely by university and state, commonly somewhere between EUR 100 and EUR 350 for a full-time student, and it bundles the student union fee (Studentenwerksbeitrag), an administration fee, social services, and the transport ticket. The transport ticket, called Semesterticket or Semesterscheibe, covers a defined area for the whole semester, usually for a period longer than the official semester so you can arrive a week early and travel home at the end. Prices range from around EUR 28 in Hamburg to over EUR 80 in Munich, and it frequently replaces a monthly transit subscription, which is a real saving. Non-payment of the semester contribution is one of the commonest reasons universities mark a student as not enrolled, so pay it in week one and keep the confirmation PDF.

What is the student ID, and what does it unlock?

The Studierendenausweis is a plastic card with a chip and a photo, normally free or a few euros, and it is your campus identity. It opens library borrowing, laboratory access, canteen payment, campus Wi-Fi, and discounts on transport or cultural venues. The paper Immatrikulationsbescheinigung, a signed and stamped letter, is the document that appears on almost every administrative form, and it is what you present as proof of your course and workload. Keep both, and keep three or four printed copies of the Immatrikulationsbescheinigung, because each office asks for a fresh one and printers close at 9pm in a university town.

What if the university asks for something I do not have?

Write to the enrolment office the same day, in German if you can manage it or in English with a request to reply in English, stating exactly what is missing, why, and when you will have it. The common gaps are: a certificate still with HEC attestation, a language certificate older than the two-year rule, a missing APS confirmation, or a certified translation that was never commissioned. Where a Pakistani attestation is still in process, universities routinely grant conditional enrolment with the document due before the first assessment. What they do not forgive is silence: a student who never chases a request is deregistered, and a student who chases is nearly always accommodated. What health insurance do I need, and why will nobody enrol me without it?

Almost every student needs health insurance, and in Germany it is not optional for the same reason it is not optional for employees: statutory insurance obligation, enforced as a legal duty, with a penalty for being uninsured that includes a blocking order from your local office. The good news is that student tariffs are cheap, and the certificate you get is accepted by the university, the immigration office and every doctor. Apply for insurance in your first week, because the insurer needs several facts about you, and the certificate is usually issued the same day if you apply online in the evening, or within one to three working days if a paper application is involved.

Public or private insurance?

If you are under 30, you are almost always required to take gesetzliche Krankenversicherung, the statutory system, which includes the large public funds such as Techniker Krankenkasse, AOK, BARMER and the IKK, plus many smaller competing funds. Student contributions in the public system typically run in the region of EUR 40 to EUR 90 per month depending on your age, your income and the fund, billed monthly or in semester instalments. Private student tariffs exist, typically EUR 80 to EUR 200 per month, and they can be a poor deal for young students because age-banded pricing punishes exactly the demographic that has no income. Compare on the official comparison tool rather than on a broker's top-three list, and factor in appointment availability and hospital networks, not only the premium.

What do I need to apply for insurance, and what is the certificate?

A German address, so the Anmeldung comes first, and the following details: your full name, date of birth, nationality, passport or ID number, your university and programme, your start of study, whether you are a first-semester student, and your Versichertennummer if you have one from a previous fund. The insurer then issues an Versicherungsbescheinigung, a stamped letter naming the university and confirming coverage. That letter is what enrolment wants, what the Ausländerbehörde wants, and what a doctor will ask for. Note the practical chicken-and-egg: the insurer asks for your matriculation number or a confirmation of enrolment, and the university asks for the insurance certificate. Break the loop with a printout of your enrolment application or a Bescheinigung über die beabsichtigte Einschreibung, which most universities issue on request specifically for this purpose. Ask the international office for the exact form on day one.

Is dental, optical and mental health included?

Statutory insurance covers medical, dental, hospital, psychiatric, preventive and rehabilitation treatment, with statutory co-payments, and students are exempt from the additional contributions that older members pay. Optical care is not fully covered, and glasses are an out-of-pocket item unless you have very poor vision. Mental health care is covered, and university psychosoziale Beratungsstellen provide free short-term counselling to enrolled students regardless of insurance, which is a genuinely useful first stop and considerably faster than waiting for a statutory therapist. For low-level anxiety, adjustment difficulty, exam stress or homesickness, the university service is the correct and fastest route, and using it early is normal rather than a sign of trouble.

How do I convert my entry visa into a residence permit?

You apply at the Ausländerbehörde, the local foreigners authority, which is sometimes part of the Bürgeramt and sometimes a separate office. A German student visa issued for study is normally valid for about 90 days, and sometimes longer if issued as a national visa; that validity is the entire window you have to obtain the Aufenthaltserlaubnis. The critical rule: you must apply before the visa expires, and you must never plan to arrive on the expiry date. If you apply in time, existing residence is protected in the ordinary way while the authority decides, and you are not illegal while waiting. If the visa expires first, your legal position changes immediately, and repairing it takes months, money and a lawyer. In the sequence of apply for masters in germany step 11 arrival, this is the highest-stakes item after the 14-day registration clock.

How do I book the appointment, and how far ahead?

Online, through your city or Land portal, and as early as possible. Berlin, Munich, Frankfurt, Cologne and Leipzig typically show multi-week waiting times, and popular hours disappear within a day of new slots opening. Check whether your university or the local international office runs a service that books immigration appointments on your behalf, because several do, and student networks often have contacts at the authority. Expect to submit a complete file at the counter, because many offices do not accept partial files or let you book without a file already assembled. Treat the appointment itself as a one-visit event: incomplete files are routinely deferred, and a deferred appointment is a two-month delay.

What do I take to the residence permit appointment?

Your passport and entry visa, the Anmeldung confirmation, the enrolment certificate, the health insurance certificate, evidence of financing such as blocked account statements covering the requested period, your admission letter, previous residence documents from earlier stays in Germany, a completed application form, and proof of the fee payment if the portal asked for an online transfer in advance. Depending on the Land and on your personal circumstances, the authority may additionally ask for a language certificate, a degree-related document, evidence of a room in a dorm if the visa specified one, a photograph for the electronic residence card, and an e-mail or phone number where you can reliably be reached. Add two photocopies of everything to the original, because photocopying on site is possible but slow and not always free.

How long does it take, and how long is the permit?

The permit for study is issued under section 16b of the Residence Act and is normally valid for one year, which means a renewal conversation every twelve months. A second year or a longer period can be granted where the course duration, funding and progress justify it, and doctoral students commonly receive longer periods. The fee is in the region of approximately EUR 100, with typical amounts falling somewhere between EUR 90 and EUR 120 depending on the Land, the length applied for, your age and any express processing fee. Some cities charge express-service supplements for a faster decision, which is legitimate but which you only need if your visa is about to expire. The electronic residence card arrives separately by post, often two to four weeks after the decision, so plan for the window where the decision exists and the card does not.

What do I do while the permit is being processed?

Continue your life as a validly present applicant, and keep every requirement alive. Stay enrolled and pay the semester contribution, keep the health insurance active, keep the blocked account drawing down, attend lectures and register for examinations, and remain in contact. If you change address, change university, change degree subject, start a side job above the permitted hours, or receive a new scholarship, report it. Reporting is cheap and keeps you compliant; failing to report a change discovered later is what turns a routine renewal into an investigation. And check the tracking reference monthly, because offices sometimes need a supplementary document and will wait rather than chase you.

Can I work while the permit is pending, and can I travel?

Yes, within the usual student rules. Enrolled students from non-EU countries may work up to 20 hours per week during the lecture period, and the rules are set out in part-time jobs for international students. Short-term travel within the Schengen area is normally fine, but your original passport is a visa document for some nationalities, and a student residence permit is valid for travel in Germany and the EU. Before planning a long-haul return to Pakistan, check what your specific document and nationality require, because losing legal residence status by being abroad too long during a pending application is a real and avoidable failure. If you intend to leave the EU permanently, look at the post-study options before you go, set out in the post-study work visa comparison.

Should I register for orientation week, and what does it give me?

Yes, and treat it as a task rather than an optional event. It is variously called Ersti-Woche, Einführungswoche, O-Woche or, in English-language programmes, International Student Orientation. It normally runs in the week before term starts or in the first days of term, and it is the fastest way to settle into a German university. On a campus of 30,000 students where nothing is signed and everything is online, orientation is where you find out which system issues your exam registration number, where the photocopier is, how the library's lending periods work, and which professors answer email. A Pakistani student who attends orientation typically saves three to four weeks in their first term; a student who skips it typically spends those weeks discovering what everybody else was told in a plenary hall.

What happens at orientation, and is it in English?

Usually a mix: a session in English or a second common language, sessions in German for specific faculties, department-level introductions where the professor speaks, and a programme of city-orientation tours, formalities desks, and social events. Bigger universities run parallel tracks by faculty so that a law student is not sitting through an engineering safety briefing. Many cities also run a separate city-orientation programme for newcomers with a registration appointment helper, a bank appointment helper, and a guided tour, and these are frequently the most practically useful hour of the trip. Expect to leave with a campus map, a timetable, a named contact person, and a social group.

Should I also visit the international office or a welcome centre?

Yes, and both. The Internationale Hochschülerschaft or the Akademisches Auslandsamt at the university is the authority for enrolment, visas issued by the university, scholarships, and residence permit advice from people who read thousands of files a year. Several cities run dedicated welcome centres for international students, and Berlin, Leipzig and Freiburg are the best-known examples; they exist precisely because municipal and university systems are separate, and they bridge the two. One appointment there can resolve an Anmeldung problem, a bank problem and a residence permit problem that would otherwise take a month of your own emails. Go early in the semester while the staff still have time.

What about a mentor or a buddy?

Ask for one. Almost every university runs a Buddy or Mentor programme pairing a first-semester international student with an upper-year student from the same or a similar background, and in some faculties the pairing is arranged automatically. For a Pakistani student, a mentor who already has an Anmeldung, a Girokonto, a residence permit and a working relationship with the Ausländerbehörde is worth more than most of the official handbooks, because they answer the specific question you are too new to know how to phrase. Expect to do the same for the person behind you next year; that is how the system sustains itself.

What mistakes do arriving students make in month one?

Almost all of them are the same mistakes, made by students who arrived on a Friday and treated the first week as a holiday. Each one below is cheap to avoid and expensive to repair, and collectively they are why some students finish their first semester with no registered address, no bank account, no insurance and a residence permit application that cannot be filed. The good news is that the pattern is so well known that avoiding it requires no cleverness, only a checklist and a willingness to sit in a waiting room.

Do I lose my legal status if my visa expires while I wait for a permit?

That is exactly what happens, and it is the most serious error on this list. Your entry visa and the pending permit are different legal objects, and the visa has a printed expiry date that no pending application changes. Applying in time is what protects you; being in a queue is not a defence. Check the expiry date in your passport the week you land, and if you are landing close to it, tell the university before you fly, because the visa may need to be issued with a shorter or earlier expiry.

Is it a problem if I stay in a hostel for three weeks before finding a room?

Not legally, but practically yes. Some cities will not register a commercial hostel address for a longer period, some do not register it at all without a special document, and the standard Wohnungsgeberbestätigung is simply not available. Meanwhile, the 14-day clock started when you moved in, not when you found somewhere permanent. Book one to two nights from Pakistan, use a friend or a flatmate's address where the law allows it, and start flat-hunting before you fly.

Can I enrol without insurance, or attend lectures without enrolling?

You can submit your enrolment paperwork without a finished certificate where the university allows a pending document, but you are not enrolled until the university says you are, and the semester contribution must be paid. Practically, this means: lectures are often open, but no exam registration, no credit, no transcript, no lab access, no proof of status for a landlord or the tax office, and no coverage under the university's accident and liability arrangements. That last point is not theoretical. Cover documented requirements like these in advance, because discovering them in week six produces a term you cannot complete.

Will a job, a scholarship or a new address break my permit?

Not if you report them. The permit's conditions are your course, your workload, your funding and your housing. A change to any of them should be communicated, and a side job inside the permitted hours usually needs only the standard notification. A new scholarship that replaces the blocked account must be evidenced, because the authority is confirming that you are funded for the permit period, and an undeclared income source is treated as an unverified one. Keep the habit of sending one email per change, with the date, the fact and the attachment, so your file is a clean narrative rather than a series of surprises.

Frequently asked questions

What is the first thing to do after landing in Germany?+

Register your address (Anmeldung) at the local citizens’ office within 14 days of moving in — it is the prerequisite for opening a bank account, health insurance and finally the residence permit.

How do I enroll at my German university?+

After arrival, complete enrollment (Immatrikulation) before the semester starts by uploading or submitting certified documents, showing proof of insurance and payments, and paying the semester contribution that includes the transport semester ticket.

When and how do I get my residence permit?+

Book an appointment at the Ausländerbehörde and bring your passport, Anmeldung, enrollment, insurance and blocked account proof. While valid, the visa covers your stay, so the permit appointment is a priority but not urgent.

Do I need a German bank account for daily life?+

Yes — a Girokonto (current account) is expected for rent, salary, semester fees and the blocked account payouts. Banks such as N26, DKB or Sparkasse allow online opening once you have a German address.
