About this scholarship

University-level awards for non-EU students, now the main route because tuition has been chargeable since 2023/24. No single national fully funded scheme exists for degree students. Masters 2 years; PhD 3 years

Norwegian Government Scholarship for Developing Countries eligibility criteria

  • Citizen of a developing country on the DAC list, for the country-specific awards
  • Admitted to a full-degree Master or PhD programme at a Norwegian university
  • English proficiency (TOEFL 80+ / IELTS 6.0+)
  • Meet the specific admission requirements of the chosen university
  • Non-EU applicants pay tuition and must document the required living funds

Benefits and coverage

  • Partial tuition waivers granted by individual Norwegian universities
  • Some awards include a living stipend, set by the institution
  • Non-EU/EEA students have paid tuition since the 2023/24 academic year
  • Exemptions remain for exchange students, doctoral candidates, Erasmus Mundus and a few named programmes
  • Proof of funds for a study permit was NOK 15,488 per month, or NOK 170,368 a year, for 2026-2027

Required documents

  • Online application through the Norwegian university portal
  • Academic transcripts and degree certificates
  • English proficiency certificate
  • Statement of purpose
  • CV/Resume
  • Two recommendation letters (for PhD)
  • Copy of passport

How to apply for the Norwegian Government Scholarship for Developing Countries

  1. Review the eligibility criteria above and confirm you meet every requirement.
  2. Prepare all required documents early — transcripts, CV, recommendation letters and motivation letter.
  3. Varies by university and closes early for non-EU applicants. UiT closed on 15 November 2026 for autumn 2027; check each institution rather than relying on a single national date. Submit a complete application before the December deadline.
  4. Always confirm the latest details on the official website before applying.
