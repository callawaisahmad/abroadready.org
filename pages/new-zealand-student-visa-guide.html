New Zealand Student Visa: Fees & Requirements

🗓️ Aug 12, 2026⏱️ 12 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

The New Zealand student visa is one of the most affordable and most strategically useful in the developed world. In 2026 the fee-paying student visa costs NZD $850, the living-cost proof is NZD $20,000 per year, and a Level 7+ New Zealand degree plugs directly into the Skilled Migrant and Green List residence systems. That combination of low entry cost and a clear path to residency makes New Zealand a standout choice for students who want to stay after they graduate. This guide covers the fee, the funds you must prove, the documents, the work rights and the timeline.

New Zealand is genuinely small: eight universities, around 5.2 million people, and a tight housing market in Auckland, but the system is engineered to fast-track skilled graduates toward residence.

Key takeaways

  • The fee-paying student visa costs NZD $850 (Pathway Student Visa NZD $750), non-refundable.
  • You must prove NZD $20,000 per year for living costs (about $1,667 a month for stays under a year) plus tuition, return travel and insurance, with funds ideally held for three-plus months.
  • You need an Offer of Place from an NZQA-registered (approved) education provider and mandatory medical and travel insurance.
  • Tertiary students can work 25 hours a week during term and full-time in scheduled holidays; master's-by-research and PhD students can work unlimited hours.
  • Processing takes about 8 weeks, so apply around four months before your course starts (main intakes: February and July).
  • An NZ bachelor's earns 5 of the 6 Skilled Migrant points needed; an NZ master's can earn 5–6 outright, making the degree a direct route toward residence.

Visa fee and costs for 2026

The fee-paying student visa is NZD $850, and the Pathway Student Visa (for study plans with an approved pathway provider) is NZD $750. On top of the visa you must show evidence of funds for: first-year tuition, living costs of NZD $20,000 per year, return travel and health insurance (around NZD $500–855 a year). University tuition for international undergraduates runs roughly NZD 26,000–47,000 a year, while polytechnics start around NZD 9,000, and international PhD students pay domestic-rate fees (about NZD 7,500–9,000).

Documents you need

Start with the Offer of Place from an NZQA-approved provider, since everything flows from it. Then prepare proof of funds with a clear, traceable history, valid passport, a genuine-intention statement (reason for study and return plans), an English test if required (IELTS 6.0–6.5 is the typical university bar; 5.5–6.0 for polytechnics), medical and travel insurance, and, where needed, an x-ray or medical certificate. Biometrics are part of the application for most nationalities.

Work rights while studying

Tertiary students on a student visa can work up to 25 hours a week during term and full-time during scheduled holidays. Students in master's-by-research and PhD programs can work unlimited hours, and secondary students in Years 12–13 can work up to 20 hours a week with permission. The New Zealand minimum wage sits around NZD $23.50–23.95 an hour, so part-time work makes a real dent in living costs.

After graduation: staying in New Zealand

After study you can apply for the Post Study Work Visa (NZD $1,670): Level 7 degrees of 30+ weeks earn 1–3 years, and master's and PhD graduates get three years. A

new 6-month Short-term Graduate Work Visa also launches on 16 November 2026. The migration payoff is the real story: an NZ bachelor's earns 5 of the 6 points in the Skilled Migrant system, an NZ master's can earn 5–6 outright, and NZ qualifications get a +1 point bonus over overseas ones from August 2026. Green List occupations (IT, engineering, health, construction) can go straight to residence. See our immigrate to New Zealand guide for the full points breakdown.

New Zealand Student Visa Application Process Step by Step

The New Zealand student visa application is managed online through Immigration New Zealand website, and the process is designed to be straightforward for applicants who prepare their documents thoroughly before starting. The first step is to secure an offer of place from a New Zealand education provider that is approved to enrol international students. Your education provider will issue you with an offer of place document that includes your programme details, tuition fees and the start date. Y

ou will also need to pay a deposit toward your tuition, typically at least 50 percent of the first year fees, before your visa application can be processed. The education provider will send you a receipt for this payment, which you will include in your visa application.

The visa application itself is submitted online through the Immigration New Zealand portal. You will need to create an account, complete the application form and upload supporting documents, including your passport, offer of place, proof of tuition payment, evidence of financial capacity, health insurance documentation, police clearance certificates and academic transcripts. F

inancial capacity is one of the most important elements: you must demonstrate access to at least NZD 20,000 per year for living costs, plus the full amount of your tuition fees and return airfare. At the time of writing, these figures represent the minimum threshold, and Immigration New Zealand may ask for additional evidence if your financial situation is complex. The police clearance must be obtained from any country where you have lived for twelve months or more since turning seventeen.

Health requirements include a chest X-ray and, depending on your country of residence and the length of your intended stay, a full medical examination. For Pakistani applicants, a medical examination by an Immigration New Zealand panel physician is required, and the results are sent directly to Immigration New Zealand. The health check must be completed within three months of your visa application. Y

ou must also hold valid health insurance for the duration of your stay: your education provider may offer a group health insurance plan, or you can arrange your own cover through a New Zealand-based insurer, as long as it meets the minimum requirements set by Immigration New Zealand. The insurance must cover medical treatment, hospital care and repatriation.

Processing times for student visa applications from Pakistan vary, but at the time of writing, applications are typically processed within four to eight weeks if all documents are complete and no additional verification is required. Incomplete applications or those that require additional information can take significantly longer, so submitting a thorough and well-organised application is the single most effective way to avoid delays. O

nce approved, your visa will be issued as an electronic visa linked to your passport, and you will receive a notification email with your visa details. You should print a copy of this notification for your travel, although airlines can verify your visa status electronically. Plan to arrive in New Zealand no more than twenty-eight days before your programme starts, as your student visa permits early entry only within this window.

Acceptable Evidence of Funds for a New Zealand Student Visa

Demonstrating that you have sufficient funds is one of the most critical elements of a New Zealand student visa application, and Immigration New Zealand applies strict standards to what counts as acceptable evidence. The minimum requirement is NZD 20,000 per year for living costs, in addition to your full tuition fees for the duration of your programme and the cost of return airfare. F

or a two-year programme, this means you need to show access to at least NZD 40,000 for living costs, plus the total tuition amount, plus an estimated return airfare. The funds must be readily available to you, meaning they must be in a bank account, investment or financial instrument that can be accessed without significant restriction.

Acceptable forms of evidence include bank statements showing a consistent balance over a period of at least six months, a student loan approval letter from a recognised financial institution, a scholarship award letter that specifies the amount and duration of funding or a financial undertaking from a sponsor who is financially responsible for your studies. I

f you are relying on a sponsor, the sponsor must provide a statutory declaration or letter of support confirming their relationship to you and their commitment to funding your studies, along with evidence of their own financial capacity. B

ank statements that show a sudden large deposit shortly before the application are likely to attract additional scrutiny, as Immigration New Zealand is looking for evidence that the funds have been available to you for a sustained period rather than borrowed temporarily to meet the visa requirement.

For Pakistani applicants, there are additional practical considerations. Bank statements from Pakistani banks are accepted, but they must be translated into English if they are in Urdu. The format should show the account holder name, account number, date of the statement and the balance in a currency that can be converted to New Zealand dollars. I

f your funds are held in multiple accounts, you can combine statements from different accounts to meet the threshold, but each account must be in your name or in the name of your sponsor. Scholarship letters from Pakistani government bodies, international organisations or universities should clearly state the total amount of funding, the duration and the conditions attached. I

f your scholarship covers tuition but not living costs, you will still need to show separate evidence of NZD 20,000 per year for your living expenses.

Immigration New Zealand may contact your bank or financial institution to verify the authenticity of your documents, so ensure that all information is accurate and that your bank is aware that a verification may come through. Do not provide altered, fabricated or misleading financial documents: this is a ground for visa refusal and can result in a ban on future applications. I

f your financial situation changes between submitting your application and receiving a decision, notify Immigration New Zealand immediately and provide updated evidence. The financial evidence requirement is not a formality: it is one of the most common reasons for visa delays and refusals, and it is worth investing time in getting it right before you submit.

Work Rights and Post-Study Work Visas in New Zealand

New Zealand offers some of the most generous work rights for international students in the developed world, and these rights extend beyond graduation into a post-study work visa that can lead directly to residence. While studying, you can work up to twenty hours per week during term time and full-time during scheduled breaks, such as summer and winter holidays. T

hese work rights apply to students enrolled in programmes at NZQF level 7 or above (bachelor degrees and higher) at an approved education provider, and they are automatically included with your student visa. Part-time work during study is a practical way to reduce your living costs, gain New Zealand work experience and build professional connections that may help after graduation.

New zealand student visa eligibility: who qualifies?

The Post-Study Work Visa (PSWV) is available to international students who complete an eligible qualification in New Zealand. At the time of writing, the PSWV allows graduates to work in New Zealand for a period of one to three years, depending on the level and duration of the qualification they completed. A bachelor degree or higher typically qualifies for a three-year open work visa, meaning you can work for any employer in any occupation. A

postgraduate diploma or certain other qualifications may qualify for a two-year visa, and shorter qualifications may qualify for a one-year visa. The PSWV is an open visa, which means you are not tied to a specific employer, giving you the freedom to change jobs, pursue different career opportunities or start your own business during the visa period.

The transition from a PSWV to residence is one of New Zealand strongest draws. Graduates who work in occupations on the Green List, which includes roles in healthcare, engineering, construction, ICT and agriculture, can apply for residence through the Skilled Migrant Category or through a Green List-specific pathway. The specific requirements vary by occupation and change periodically, so check the Immigration New Zealand website for the current Green List before making career decisions. A

t the time of writing, some Green List roles offer a straight-to-residence pathway after two years of work in the role, while others require a two-year work period before residence can be applied for. Planning your study and career with the Green List in mind from the outset gives you the strongest chance of a smooth transition to residence.

For Pakistani students, the combination of affordable tuition, generous work rights and a clear post-study pathway makes New Zealand a strategically strong choice. The key is selecting a qualification and a field of study that align with both the Green List and your career goals. Before enrolling, research which occupations are on the Green List, which universities and polytechnics offer relevant programmes and what the employment landscape looks like in New Zealand for your field. N

The work rights and post-study visa are valuable, but they are most valuable when they are part of a deliberate career strategy rather than a passive benefit.

Health Insurance Requirements for New Zealand Student Visa Holders

Health insurance is a mandatory requirement for all international students in New Zealand, and your student visa will not be granted without evidence of appropriate cover. Immigration New Zealand requires that your health insurance covers medical treatment, hospital care and repatriation for the full duration of your stay. The policy must be issued by a New Zealand-based insurer or an insurer that is recognised by Immigration New Zealand, and the coverage must meet the minimum standards specified in your visa conditions. At the time of writing, the minimum cover includes medical treatment for illness and accident, hospital accommodation and repatriation to your home country if medically necessary.

Many New Zealand education providers offer group health insurance plans for international students, which are designed to meet Immigration New Zealand requirements and are often the most convenient option. These plans are typically arranged through the university or polytechnic and are added to your tuition fees. The cost varies by provider and programme length, but at the time of writing, group plans for international students typically cost between NZD 500 and NZD 800 per year. G

roup plans have the advantage of being pre-approved by Immigration New Zealand, which eliminates the risk of your insurance not meeting the required standard. If you prefer to arrange your own insurance, you must ensure that the policy meets all Immigration New Zealand requirements and that you can provide a certificate of insurance in English with your visa application.

Understanding what your insurance covers, and what it does not, is important for managing your health and finances while in New Zealand. Most student health insurance plans cover doctor visits, hospital stays, emergency treatment and prescription medication, but they may not cover dental treatment, optical care or pre-existing conditions. Some plans have excess amounts (the amount you pay per claim before insurance coverage begins), and some limit the total amount payable per year. R

ead the policy document carefully before you commit to a plan, and if you have a specific health condition, confirm that it is covered or that you can arrange supplementary cover. New Zealand public healthcare system provides emergency treatment to everyone regardless of visa status, but ongoing treatment for international students is typically handled through private providers, which is why health insurance is essential.

If you hold a scholarship that includes health insurance, confirm with your scholarship provider that the cover meets Immigration New Zealand requirements. Some international scholarships provide health insurance through a global provider that may not be recognised by Immigration New Zealand, in which case you may need to supplement it with a New Zealand-based policy. T

he evidence of health insurance must be submitted with your visa application, and if you change your insurance provider after your visa is granted, you must ensure that the new policy continues to meet the visa conditions. Failure to maintain adequate health insurance throughout your stay can result in visa conditions being breached, which can affect future immigration applications. F

or Pakistani students who may be unfamiliar with New Zealand insurance system, the education provider international student office is a reliable first point of contact for guidance on suitable insurance options.

Dependent Visas and Bringing Family to New Zealand

If you are studying in New Zealand on a student visa and want to bring your partner or dependent children, New Zealand offers a straightforward dependent visa pathway. Your partner can apply for a partner of a student work visa, which allows them to work full-time in New Zealand for any employer for the duration of your student visa. D

ependent children can apply for student or visitor visas, and if they are of school age, they can enrol in New Zealand schools. The dependent visa applications are linked to your student visa, so the validity of your partner and children visas is tied to the duration of your programme. T

his is a significant advantage for Pakistani students with families, as it allows your family to accompany you without the financial strain of separate visa applications and separate work rights for your partner.

To qualify for a dependent visa, you must demonstrate that your relationship with your partner is genuine and stable and that you have the financial capacity to support your family in New Zealand. Evidence of your relationship can include a marriage certificate, joint bank statements, shared accommodation records and photographs. I

f you are not married but are in a de facto relationship, you will need to provide evidence that you have been living together in a relationship similar to marriage for at least twelve months. Financial evidence for dependent visas is assessed in addition to your own financial evidence for the student visa, so you will need to show that you have sufficient funds to cover the living costs of your entire family. The minimum amount increases with each additional family member.

A practical consideration for families is the cost and availability of housing in New Zealand, particularly in Auckland, where the international student population is concentrated. Accommodation costs are a significant part of the family budget, and the New Zealand rental market can be competitive. University-owned student accommodation is typically designed for single students and may not be suitable for families, so most students with families rent privately. L

ooking for accommodation before you arrive, using university accommodation advisory services and connecting with Pakistani community groups in New Zealand can help you find suitable housing more quickly. Schools in New Zealand are generally of high quality, and dependent children of student visa holders can enrol in state schools, although fees may apply for international students at some schools. The education provider international student office can provide specific guidance on school enrolment for your children.

The ability of your partner to work full-time is one of the most valuable aspects of the dependent visa. Your partner income can significantly supplement your family finances while you study, and the work experience they gain in New Zealand contributes to their own professional development and to your family long-term immigration prospects. If your partner secures employment in an occupation on the Green List, this may strengthen your family overall position when you apply for residence after graduation. D

iscuss your family career goals and immigration strategy together before you leave Pakistan, so that both of you can make informed decisions about work, study and housing from the moment you arrive. The dependent visa pathway is designed to support families, but it works best when both partners approach the New Zealand experience as a shared project with coordinated goals.

New Zealand Student Visa Timeline and Pre-Departure Checklist

The New Zealand student visa timeline begins well before you submit your application, and working backwards from your programme start date ensures that you complete each step on time. The sequence is: secure an offer of place from a New Zealand education provider, pay your tuition deposit, gather all required documents (passport, academic transcripts, police clearances, financial evidence, health insurance, medical examination results), submit your visa application online, wait for processing, receive your electronic visa and book your travel. E

ach of these steps takes time, and delays in any one of them can push back your arrival. A realistic timeline begins at least four to five months before your programme start date, with the visa application submitted at least twelve weeks before you intend to travel.

Your pre-departure checklist should include several practical items that affect your experience from day one in New Zealand. Arrange your health insurance before you arrive, as the visa requires evidence of cover and you will need insurance from the moment you land. Research accommodation options and, if possible, arrange temporary housing for your first two weeks, whether through the university residential halls, a homestay or a short-term rental. O

pen a New Zealand bank account if your education provider offers this service before arrival, or plan to open one within your first week. Notify your education provider of your travel dates and arrival time so that they can arrange airport pickup or orientation support. Pack appropriate clothing for New Zealand weather, which varies significantly between the North and South Islands and between summer and winter. B

ring copies of all important documents, including your visa notification, offer of place, insurance certificate and academic transcripts, both in physical and digital form.

Upon arrival in New Zealand, there are several administrative tasks that you must complete within your first weeks. Register with your education provider international student office, attend orientation and collect your student ID card. Register with a local doctor (GP) so that you can access healthcare when needed. If you plan to work while studying, confirm your work rights with your education provider and begin looking for part-time employment through university job boards, local newspapers or online platforms. S

et up a mobile phone plan, as having a New Zealand phone number is important for opening bank accounts, renting accommodation and applying for jobs. Familiarise yourself with New Zealand road rules if you plan to drive, and with public transport options if you will be using buses or trains.

The transition to life in New Zealand is generally smooth for international students, but there are cultural differences worth understanding before you arrive. New Zealand culture values directness, punctuality and personal space, and the pace of life is generally more relaxed than in South Asian countries. The outdoors plays a significant role in New Zealand culture, and many social activities centre on hiking, sports and nature. N

ew Zealand is a bicultural country with both Maori and European heritage, and understanding and respecting this bicultural identity is an important part of integration. The Pakistani community in New Zealand is growing, and Muslim community organisations, Halal food shops and mosques are available in major cities. Building a social network that includes both New Zealanders and fellow international students enriches your experience and helps you feel at home more quickly.

New Zealand has eight universities, numerous polytechnics and institutes of technology, and several private training establishments, and choosing the right provider for your programme is one of the most important decisions you will make. A

ll eight universities are ranked in the top three percent globally, and they each have particular strengths: the University of Auckland is strong in engineering, medicine and business; Victoria University of Wellington is known for law, humanities and public policy; the University of Canterbury excels in engineering and science; and the University of Otago is strong in health sciences and humanities. P

olytechnics and institutes of technology, such as Otago Polytechnic and Wellington Institute of Technology, offer more vocationally oriented programmes with strong industry connections. The right choice depends on your career goals, your budget and the specific programme you want to study.

When evaluating programmes, look beyond the university ranking and examine the specific curriculum, the faculty expertise, the industry partnerships and the graduate outcomes. A programme that includes a work placement, industry project or practicum component provides practical experience that employers value, and it can significantly strengthen your post-study work prospects. Check whether the programme is accredited by the relevant professional body in New Zealand, particularly if you are studying in a regulated field such as engineering, nursing, teaching or accounting. Accreditation ensures that your qualification meets the standards required for professional registration in New Zealand, which is essential if you plan to work in a regulated profession after graduation.

Auckland, as New Zealand largest city, has the highest cost of living but also the widest range of employment opportunities and the largest international student community. Wellington, the capital, offers a vibrant cultural scene and strong government and public sector employment. Before you commit to a programme, verify that the education provider is approved by the New Zealand Qualifications Authority (NZQA) and that the programme is on the list of programmes approved for international student enrolment. Providers that are not NZQA-approved cannot enrol international students on student visas, and programmes that are not approved do not qualify for post-study work visas. T

The Immigration New Zealand website maintains a list of approved providers and programmes, and you should cross-reference your chosen programme against this list before you accept an offer and pay your tuition deposit.

New Zealand Student Visa: Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

The most common mistake in New Zealand student visa applications is submitting incomplete financial evidence. Immigration New Zealand requires specific documentation to prove that you have sufficient funds, and the evidence must meet strict criteria regarding format, time period and source. Bank statements that show a sudden large deposit, statements that are not in English, or evidence that does not clearly demonstrate access to the required amount will result in a request for additional information, which delays your application. T

o avoid this, begin building your financial evidence at least six months before you plan to submit your visa application, ensuring that your bank statements show a consistent and sufficient balance throughout this period.

A second common error is failing to provide adequate health insurance documentation. Immigration New Zealand requires evidence of health insurance that meets specific criteria, and many applications are delayed because the insurance policy provided does not clearly state the coverage details, the duration or the insurer recognition. B

efore you submit your visa application, verify that your health insurance policy explicitly states that it covers medical treatment, hospital care and repatriation for the full duration of your stay, and that the insurer is recognised by Immigration New Zealand. If your education provider offers a group health insurance plan, this is often the simplest option, as it is pre-approved and meets all requirements.

Police clearance certificates are another area where delays occur. You must provide a police clearance from any country where you have lived for twelve months or more since turning seventeen, and obtaining these certificates can take several weeks, particularly if you have lived in multiple countries. B

egin the process of obtaining police clearances as soon as you decide to apply for a New Zealand student visa, as the certificates are typically valid for a limited period and must be current at the time of your visa application. If you have lived in Pakistan for the past several years, the Pakistani police clearance is the primary document you need, and it can be obtained from the regional police office in the city where you reside.

What should you write in your new zealand student visa essays?

Finally, many applicants underestimate the importance of the statement of purpose or study plan that accompanies their visa application. While New Zealand does not require a formal essay, a clear and concise explanation of why you chose New Zealand, why you chose your specific programme and what you plan to do after graduation strengthens your application and demonstrates that you are a genuine student. T

his statement should be one to two pages, written in clear English, and should connect your academic background to your career goals and to the specific programme you have chosen. Taking the time to write a thoughtful statement is a small investment that can make a meaningful difference in the outcome of your application.
