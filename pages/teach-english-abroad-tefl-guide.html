Teach English Abroad: TEFL Guide & Destinations

🗓️ Aug 5, 2026⏱️ 10 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Teaching English abroad is one of the few jobs you can land straight out of university with no prior experience, and demand remains strong across Asia, Europe, the Middle East and Latin America. For American, Canadian, British and Australian graduates, it is a realistic way to live overseas for a year or two, build savings in some destinations, and return with a genuinely international CV, or simply stay longer than you planned.

This guide covers the requirements, the certificates and what they cost, the destinations that actually pay well, and the visa reality behind every teaching job.

Key takeaways

  • You need a bachelor's degree and native-level English; most employers want a TEFL or TESOL certificate too.
  • A 120-hour online TEFL course ($300 to $600) is enough for many jobs; CELTA ($1,500 to $2,500) matters most for career teachers.
  • The JET Programme in Japan and Spain's auxiliar program are the most popular sponsored routes for Western graduates.
  • Salaries range from enough to break even (Spain) to genuine savings (Japan, South Korea, China, parts of Vietnam).
  • Employers must sponsor your work visa; working on a tourist visa is illegal everywhere.
  • Plan six to twelve months ahead, including background checks and authenticated documents.

The basic requirements

The universal baseline is a four-year bachelor's degree and native-level English; employers usually verify this with a passport or an interview rather than a test. Beyond that, a TEFL certificate is expected almost everywhere, and a clean criminal background check is required in most countries. Many governments also require documents such as apostilled degree certificates, so start gathering paperwork early. Age limits, teaching experience and accent preferences vary by destination, so check each program's fine print.

TEFL, TESOL or CELTA: what to choose

The certificate you need depends on where you want to teach. A 120-hour TEFL or TESOL course from a reputable provider, usually $300 to $600 online, satisfies employers across Asia, Latin America and the Middle East. If you want a career in teaching or a position in a competitive European market, a CELTA (or Trinity CertTESOL) is the gold standard: an intensive four-week course costing roughly $1,500 to $2,500 that serious employers and government programs accept and often require. Choose the destination first, because your destination decides your certificate.

Best destinations and what they pay

  • Japan (JET Programme): a monthly salary around 280,000 yen, subsidized housing, and visa sponsorship; applications open in the fall through jetprogramme.org.
  • Spain (auxiliar program): US and Canadian citizens teach 12 to 16 hours a week for a modest monthly stipend, enough to live on in many regions, with excellent travel access.
  • South Korea (EPIK): paid accommodation and strong salaries for new and experienced teachers alike.
  • China: the highest salaries in the industry, though program choice matters more than ever; teach only for accredited, vetted schools.
  • Vietnam and Thailand: lower salaries but very low living costs, great for breaking even while enjoying Southeast Asia.
  • Czech Republic and the UAE: interesting options for certified teachers, though entry requirements are higher.

Visas, contracts and legalities

Legality is non-negotiable: a work visa, not a tourist visa, is required to teach, and schools that offer to "sort it out later" should be avoided. The safest routes are government programs like JET, EPIK and Spain's auxiliares, because they handle sponsorship, health care and contracts properly. For direct jobs, ask for the contract in writing, check the employer's accreditation, and never pay a recruiter before verifying the school. British, Canadian and Australian citizens also have working holiday visas with several countries that make moving abroad much simpler.

How much it costs to get started

Getting ready costs less than most people think: roughly $400 to $700 for an online TEFL, or $1,500 to $2,500 for CELTA, plus around $1,000 to $2,000 for flights and a startup buffer. Most reputable employers provide or help with housing, and government programs cover or subsidize accommodation. Teaching abroad is genuinely a net-positive money move in destinations like Japan, Korea and China, and a break-even adventure in Europe.

Making it count

The best teaching jobs abroad double as career moves: CELTA graduates can move into international school teaching, and a year in the classroom is excellent preparation for fields like education, TESOL graduate study, diplomacy and development. Pair the income with good savings habits, and you come home with more than a paycheck. Frequently asked questions

Do I need a degree to teach English abroad?+

In almost every country, yes, a four-year bachelor's degree is the standard requirement for a legal work visa. The degree subject rarely matters.

What is the difference between TEFL, TESOL and CELTA?+

TEFL and TESOL are general certificates, and a 120-hour online course is the common baseline. CELTA is a more rigorous, widely recognized qualification that serious employers and competitive markets prefer.

Which country pays the most for teaching English?+

China typically offers the highest salaries, with Japan, South Korea and parts of the Middle East close behind. Europe, especially Spain, pays less but offers unmatched travel access.

Do I need a TEFL certificate before applying to JET or EPIK?+

No. Government programs like JET and EPIK do not require TEFL, but having one makes you more competitive for other jobs and for the destinations that do require it.

Is teaching English abroad worth the cost of getting certified?+

Yes. A $400 online TEFL pays for itself quickly, and a $2,000 CELTA opens better-paying, longer-term positions in markets that require recognized qualifications.
