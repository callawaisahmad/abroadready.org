IELTS 7+ in 6 Weeks: A Realistic IELTS Study Plan

🗓️ Apr 1, 2026⏱️ 12 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Can I reach Band 7 in six weeks?

Yes, if you already test at roughly Band 6 to 6.5 and can study consistently. This IELTS study plan gives you a realistic, week-by-week route to the score rather than vague advice to practise more. It assumes roughly 1.5 to 2 hours on weekdays and a longer block at weekends. If your current level is closer to Band 5, use this as a strong foundation and plan on extending the timeline.

What makes this IELTS study plan realistic?

A useful IELTS study plan is not designed to teach English from scratch. Its job is to teach you the test, close specific skill gaps, and build the stamina required to perform for nearly three hours. Band 7 rewards accuracy, range, and control, so fluent conversation alone is not enough.

Key takeaways

What are the most important takeaways?

  • Band 7 in six weeks is realistic if you start around Band 6 to 6.5 and study consistently.
  • Take a full timed diagnostic test before you plan anything, so your IELTS study plan targets real weaknesses.
  • Writing and Speaking are where most people lose marks; they need active feedback, not passive practice.
  • Reading and Listening improve fastest through timed practice plus error analysis.
  • Simulate full test conditions in the final two weeks to build stamina and reduce exam-day surprises.

Understand what Band 7 actually requires

How is my overall IELTS band calculated?

Each of the four sections is scored from 0 to 9, and your overall result is the average, rounded to the nearest half band. To reach an overall 7, you generally need scores of about 6.5 to 7.5 across the board, so one weak section can pull the total down. Many universities also impose a minimum score in every section, making balance more useful than one exceptional result.

What does a Band 7 performance look like?

Band 7 descriptors reward clear, measurable behaviours. In Writing, you need clear organisation, varied sentence structures, and only occasional errors. In Speaking, you need fluency without long pauses, flexible vocabulary, and generally accurate grammar. Once you understand those standards, your IELTS study plan becomes a sequence of targeted improvements rather than guesswork.

Choose Academic or General Training early

Most university and scholarship applicants take the Academic version. Listening and Speaking are the same in both versions, but Reading and Writing differ. Confirm that you are booked for Academic before spending six weeks practising the wrong Task 1 if your goal is study or a scholarship.

Week 0: The diagnostic weekend

Why should I take a diagnostic test first?

Before Week 1, sit one complete official practice test under timed conditions. Do not pause, look up words, or give yourself extra minutes. This is the most valuable hour of the entire IELTS study plan because it shows your real starting point before targets and study time can be assigned.

How should I score and record the diagnostic?

Mark the test honestly. Convert your Reading and Listening raw totals into band scores. For Writing and Speaking, compare your performance with the public band descriptors, or obtain feedback from a teacher or reliable assessment tool. Record four numbers, one for each section, because that baseline will determine what every later session should prioritise.

Weeks 1 to 2: Build accuracy and fix the basics

How should I balance work in the first fortnight?

Turn your diagnosis into action. Alternate Reading and Listening on different days, schedule Writing twice a week, and practise Speaking in short daily bursts. This balanced opening phase corrects basic errors before you add speed, pressure, and more advanced language.

How should I improve Listening?

Complete one Listening section each day and always review your mistakes. Common losses come from spelling, plurals, and losing your place when a speaker paraphrases. Predict answers before the audio begins, and keep listening after an error instead of freezing over the answer you missed.

What is the best Reading approach in Weeks 1 and 2?

Prioritise technique over speed at first. Learn to scan for keywords, recognise paraphrase, and handle True/False/Not Given, which can trip up even strong readers. Give each passage 20 minutes so the clock feels normal from the beginning rather than becoming a crisis later.

How much Writing should I complete?

Write one Task 1 and one Task 2 during the fortnight, but analyse model answers more deeply than you simply produce extra essays. Study the structure of a Band 7 essay: a clear position, two developed body paragraphs, and a conclusion. For Academic Task 1, begin building a reliable bank of phrases for trends, comparisons, and processes.

How can I improve Speaking every day?

Record yourself answering Part 1 questions each day and listen back for hesitation, repetition, and abrupt endings. Even two minutes of self-recording exposes habits that you cannot notice while speaking in the moment. Use the recordings to make your next answer more fluent and more precise.

Weeks 3 to 4: Expand range and add pressure

What should change in Weeks 3 and 4?

By now, the format should feel familiar, so use the middle phase to expand range. Work on more sophisticated vocabulary, more varied grammar, and faster processing. Move Reading and Listening to complete timed sections, and increase the amount of feedback you receive on Writing.

How should I grow vocabulary in context?

Do not memorise isolated word lists. Collect contextual vocabulary around common IELTS themes such as education, environment, technology, health, and urban life. For each theme, record five collocations that you can actually use in an essay or Speaking answer. Range with control is more valuable than rare words used incorrectly.

How should I target Writing Task 2?

Give Task 2 the most attention because it carries more weight than Task 1. Write two fully timed 40-minute essays each week. After each one, ask whether you answered the exact question, whether every paragraph develops one idea, and whether your sentence structures vary. Grammatical range and accuracy accounts for 25 percent of the Writing score, so deliberately combine simple, compound, and complex sentences.

The fastest way to lose a Writing band is to write a memorised essay that does not address the specific question. Examiners are trained to spot this instantly.

How can I build Speaking stamina?

Move to full Part 2 long turns. Use the one-minute preparation period to jot down keywords, then speak for the entire two minutes without stopping. If your ideas run dry, add an example, a contrast, or a short personal story. Fluency includes stamina, and two minutes feels difficult until you have practised it repeatedly.

Weeks 5 to 6: Simulate, refine, and peak

How should I use full mock tests?

Sit two full timed mock tests, ideally at weekends, in one sitting with realistic breaks. The final fortnight should focus on performance rather than new learning. This concentration drill is what separates a genuine Band 7 performance from a candidate who scores well on isolated sections but fades during the real test.

What should I repair between mocks?

Spend the time between mocks repairing only your weakest area. If Listening Part 4 repeatedly costs marks, drill that part. If Writing coherence is weak, rewrite two older essays with better linking. Avoid spending most of your time on comfortable strengths merely because practising them feels more pleasant.

How should I plan my exam-day routine?

Set section-by-section timings in advance. Reading usually needs 20 minutes per passage, and you should never leave a blank answer. For Writing, allow about 20 minutes for Task 1 and 40 for Task 2, then use the final five minutes to check slips. Rehearsing this routine removes difficult decisions from the actual exam.

Section strategies that reliably add marks

What should I do during Listening?

Read the questions before each recording and underline keywords. Watch for distractors in which a speaker mentions a wrong answer before correcting it. Transfer your answers carefully, and never leave a blank because there is no penalty for guessing.

What should I do during Reading?

Do not read every word. Skim for the text structure, then scan for the information that each question specifically requests. Control your pace so one passage cannot take minutes needed by another, and remember that most questions follow the order of information in the text.

What should I do before and during Writing?

Plan for two minutes before you begin. A short plan prevents the mid-answer drift that damages coherence. Use clear paragraphs, natural linking devices, and precise vocabulary, then reserve time to proofread articles, plurals, subject-verb agreement, and verb tenses.

How should I approach Speaking Part 2?

Extend every answer with a reason and an example. Treat the examiner as a curious conversation partner rather than an interrogator. It is acceptable to correct yourself, and a natural pause to think sounds much better than repeated filler noise.

How to know your plan is working

How should I track progress across six weeks?

Record your mock band scores throughout the six weeks. Expect steady but uneven improvement: Reading and Listening often rise first because they respond quickly to deliberate practice, while Writing and Speaking usually move more slowly because they depend on feedback and changed habits. If a score remains flat for two weeks, change the method instead of repeating the same ineffective routine.

Common mistakes that keep people at Band 6.5

Which mistakes prevent candidates from reaching Band 7?

Three habits stall applicants just short of Band 7. First is passive practice: completing test after test without analysing any errors. Second is memorising essays and answers, which examiners penalise. Third is ignoring one weak section until it quietly caps the overall score. A disciplined IELTS study plan addresses all three by combining practice with reflection and balancing effort across every section.

What should I do to avoid staying at Band 6.5?

Stay consistent, review every mistake, and simulate the real test before exam day. Six focused weeks of that disciplined approach will move most candidates who begin around Band 6.5 into Band 7 territory, provided they respond to feedback and concentrate on their weakest areas.

How to structure your daily IELTS study plan sessions

Why does daily session structure matter?

A week-by-week roadmap is important, but your day-to-day habits determine whether six weeks produce real improvement. A structured IELTS study plan session turns effort into measurable progress; unstructured practice may merely make you familiar with the test format without closing any skill gaps.

How should I organise a 90-minute weekday session?

Divide a productive weekday session of ninety minutes to two hours into three parts. Begin with a warm-up focused on your weakest skill, which for many Pakistani students is Writing Task 2 or Speaking Part 3. Use the first twenty-five minutes to write one timed body paragraph or answer two Part 3 questions aloud, record yourself, and listen back.

What are the IELTS study plan requirements?

The middle section should last about fifty minutes and provide skill-building work on a rotating basis. Alternate Reading and Listening on different days, complete one practice-test section under time, and then spend fifteen minutes examining every error. In Reading, identify whether an error came from vocabulary, a misread question, or time pressure. In Listening, replay the relevant audio and write exactly what you missed.

How should I consolidate vocabulary and grammar?

Use the final twenty minutes for vocabulary and grammar consolidation. Keep a notebook divided by IELTS topic, including education, health, environment, and technology. After each session, record three to five useful words or phrases and write one original sentence using each. Review the notebook at the start of every session; spaced repetition builds the range that separates a Band 6 writer from a Band 7 writer.

What should a weekend session include?

Make weekend sessions three to four hours when possible. Take one full timed practice test in the morning and review it thoroughly in the afternoon. Sit at a desk, use only permitted materials, follow every section limit strictly, and reproduce the real test's breaks rather than inventing extra time.

Best free and low-cost IELTS resources for Pakistani students

Do I need an expensive IELTS course?

No. High-quality IELTS preparation does not require an expensive course or premium materials. Free and low-cost resources can provide the practice, models, and feedback needed to move towards Band 7 when you use them actively and consistently throughout your IELTS study plan.

Which official practice resources should I prioritise?

The most valuable free resource is IELTS official sample tests. British Council, IDP, and Cambridge publish practice tests that reflect the real question types and difficulty. Complete at least four full official tests during the six-week plan, ideally one per week in Weeks 2 through 5. Accurate official material is far more useful than a large pile of unrelated third-party questions.

What is IELTS study plan?

For listening practice, BBC Learning English and the BBC World Service podcast archive provide useful material at an appropriate level. Focus on science, education, and social issues, which resemble topics in Listening Sections 3 and 4. Listen actively, take notes, summarise what you heard, and check your understanding against a transcript when one is available.

What should you write in your IELTS study plan essays?

For writing, free online feedback platforms can be useful. The British Council IELTS Prepare website provides model answers assessed against official band descriptors. Compare your work paragraph by paragraph and identify the difference between Band 6 and Band 7 writing: more precise vocabulary, varied cohesion devices, and a clear position maintained throughout.

How can I practise Speaking for free?

For speaking, record yourself answering Part 2 cue cards on your phone. Allow one minute to prepare and two uninterrupted minutes to speak. Listen back honestly and ask whether you maintained fluency, used a range of grammar, developed your ideas, and avoided unnecessary repetition.

Where can I find free Reading practice?

For reading, academic articles from The Guardian, The Economist, and New Scientist make useful practice material. Read one article each day under a twenty-minute timer. Identify the main idea, the supporting arguments, and the author's attitude instead of trying to understand every isolated word.

How much does IELTS study plan cost?

Low-cost paid options include Cambridge IELTS practice-test books, which cost approximately two to three thousand rupees and contain four complete tests each. Buy at least two volumes during your preparation period, use the tests progressively, and review each answer before moving on.

Band 7 scoring demands: what each IELTS section actually requires

Why should I learn the section-by-section Band 7 requirements?

Understanding what Band 7 demands in each IELTS section lets you target preparation precisely. It prevents you from spending every week improving general English and hoping an unpredictable score will follow. Your diagnostic should determine how much time each skill receives within the overall IELTS study plan.

What does Listening Band 7 require?

Listening (band 7 = 30 to 31 correct answers out of 40): You need to catch detailed information in academic discussions, follow multi-stage arguments, and identify a speaker's opinion or attitude. A common problem for Pakistani students is pace rather than vocabulary: the audio moves faster than natural conversation at home. Train by listening to academic podcasts at normal speed and taking notes simultaneously.

What does Reading Band 7 require?

Reading (band 7 = 30 to 31 correct answers out of 40): A Band 7 reader follows complex arguments, understands implicit meaning, and identifies main ideas in dense academic texts. True, False, Not Given is often the decisive weakness because it requires separating information contradicted by the text from information the text simply does not provide.

What does Writing Band 7 require?

Writing (band 7 = scored on four criteria): Examiners assess task response, cohesion and coherence, lexical resource, and grammatical range and accuracy. Band 7 requires full task coverage, a clear position, logical organisation, effective paragraphing, sufficiently precise vocabulary, some correct less-common items, and a controlled mix of simple and complex sentences with only occasional errors.

What does Speaking Band 7 require?

Speaking (band 7 = scored on four criteria): You should speak at length without noticeable effort, use discourse markers and some less-common or idiomatic vocabulary, and handle complex structures with good control. Over-reliance on memorised phrases is a major barrier because it limits development and can be detected immediately.

How should I balance uneven section scores?

Once you know the Band 7 standard, identify your exact weaknesses and allocate preparation time accordingly. For example, a candidate at Band 6 in Listening and Band 7.5 in Reading should devote more of Weeks 1 through 4 to Listening drills. This does not mean ignoring strengths; it means preventing a low score from capping the overall result.

How Pakistani students can leverage local study groups for IELTS preparation

Why should I join a local study group?

Solo study has value, but a local group can accelerate progress in ways isolated preparation cannot easily reproduce. Its strongest value is accountability and realistic Speaking practice, while written peer review can also expose weaknesses before they become exam-day surprises.

How can a study group improve Speaking?

The clearest benefit is regular speaking practice with feedback. IELTS Speaking is a face-to-face conversation, so speaking to a mirror or a recording only goes so far. A group of three to five can meet weekly, rotate examiner and candidate roles, and use official cue cards from recent test cycles. After each two-minute turn, give structured feedback on fluency, vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation.

How do you apply for IELTS study plan?

A group also provides accountability. If you commit to reviewing writing every Saturday morning, you are much more likely to complete the week's homework than if only you know your plan. That social commitment sustains momentum during the difficult middle weeks, when personal motivation often falls.

Which IELTS study plan tips actually work?

For the writing section, peer review is especially useful because different writers approach the same prompt in different ways. Have four people answer one Task 2 question and exchange essays. Use a fixed review order—task response, coherence, vocabulary, then grammar—to mirror the official scoring criteria and make feedback consistent.

How can I find study-group members in Pakistan?

Look through university English departments, coaching centres, and local Facebook groups for IELTS candidates. A group of four or five people from different universities often works well because members bring different perspectives, experience levels, and English-language backgrounds. Choose people who attend consistently and offer honest feedback rather than those who merely want company.

What rules should an IELTS study group set?

Agree at the beginning to meet at a fixed time and place, complete assigned work before each meeting, and give feedback that is honest but constructive. Clear expectations turn an informal gathering into a useful support system. A well-run group can be the difference between repeatedly scoring Band 6.5 and breaking through to Band 7.

A week-by-week practice test schedule for the six-week IELTS 7.0 plan

How should I schedule practice tests?

Practice tests are the backbone of serious IELTS preparation, but random tests waste their diagnostic and training value. A planned schedule gives every test a specific purpose at the correct stage of your six-week IELTS study plan.

What should I do in Week 1?

Week 1 (diagnostic): Complete one full official practice test under strict timed conditions and immediately record an estimated band for each section. Treat this as your baseline, then focus weekday sessions on your two weakest sections.

What should I do in Week 2?

Week 2 (accuracy focus): Do not take another full test. Complete individual sections from a second test, one per day, initially without time pressure so you can focus on accuracy. Record every wrong answer and label its cause: a misread question, vocabulary gap, careless error, or time pressure.

What should I do in Week 3?

Week 3 (timed sections): Complete a full Listening test and a full Reading test under timed conditions. Finish both Writing tasks within the official sixty minutes. Track how long each task takes and note where you begin to run out of time.

What should I do in Week 4?

Week 4 (full test under pressure): Take a complete practice test under strict exam conditions, with no phone, dictionary, or extra breaks. Score it and compare the result with the Week 1 baseline. Aim to improve by at least half a band in your weakest section, even if the overall gain is smaller.

What should I do in Week 5?

Week 5 (refinement): Take another complete test, but spend most of the review on answers that were correct yet uncertain. Eliminating near-misses can move you from Band 6.5 to Band 7. Review Writing and Speaking against the official band descriptors as well.

What should I do in Week 6?

Week 6 (peak simulation): Take your final full practice test in the first half of the week. Use the second half to review all previous error notebooks, concentrating on recurring patterns instead of learning new material. The objective is confidence in the skills you have already established.

Common time traps in the IELTS exam that keep candidates below band 7

What time traps cause avoidable score losses?

Many candidates who understand English well enough for Band 7 lose marks through preventable time-management errors, not a lack of language ability. Once you can name each trap, you can rehearse a specific response before the real test.

How should I manage Reading time?

In IELTS Reading, the most damaging trap is reading every word from beginning to end. The test contains roughly 2,500 words across three passages, but only sixty minutes are available. Strong Band 7 candidates skim the structure first, then use keyword scanning to locate the exact information each question requires.

What IELTS study plan tips should you follow?

In IELTS Listening, the main trap is remaining stuck on a missed question. If the audio continues while you rethink the previous answer, you can lose the next several questions. Write your best guess, mark the item, and move forward. Use the transfer time at the end of a section to revisit marked questions.

How do I avoid the Task 1 time trap?

In IELTS Writing Task 1, candidates can waste time describing individual data points rather than identifying major patterns. Task 1 tests selective summary and comparison, not transcription. Spend three to four minutes finding two or three major trends, then write a structured overview and grouped details in approximately fifteen to eighteen minutes.

How much space should Task 2 receive?

In IELTS Writing Task 2, an overlong introduction and underdeveloped body can leave no room for a proper conclusion or fully developed argument. A Band 7 response usually has a two- or three-sentence introduction, two coherent body paragraphs of six to eight sentences, and a concise two-sentence conclusion. Develop ideas rather than padding sentences.

How should I manage energy in Speaking?

In IELTS Speaking, giving overly long answers to Part 1 can use up mental energy before the more demanding Parts 2 and 3. Keep Part 1 responses to two or three sentences. Save fuller explanations, examples, and analysis for the two-minute long turn and the abstract questions that follow.

How to stay motivated during the six-week IELTS preparation period

How can I stay motivated for six weeks?

Motivation naturally fluctuates during intensive preparation, and most candidates underestimate that challenge. Initial enthusiasm often fades around Week 3, exactly when steady work matters most. Build motivation strategies into the IELTS study plan from the beginning so the mid-cycle dip does not derail your progress.

How does visual progress tracking help?

The first strategy is visual progress tracking. After each practice test, record every estimated section score on a chart or spreadsheet. An upward trend provides concrete evidence that your work is producing results. When motivation is low, reviewing that evidence reminds you why the daily IELTS study plan sessions are worth completing.

How does the IELTS study plan application work?

The second strategy is reward-based milestones. Set a specific weekly goal and attach a modest reward to it. For example, complete all sessions in Week 2 and reach at least Band 6.5 in a practice test before treating yourself to something enjoyable. Achievable rewards make progress tangible and keep the larger Band 7 goal from feeling abstract.

Why does social accountability matter?

The third strategy is social accountability. Tell a friend, relative, or study partner about your plan and ask for occasional check-ins. Knowing that someone is following your progress creates an external commitment that supports motivation. A study group also supplies a useful weekly accountability point without requiring constant self-monitoring.

How can I reinforce my reason for taking IELTS?

The fourth strategy is purpose reinforcement. Remind yourself whether you are working towards a scholarship, admission condition, career goal, or another concrete outcome. Write the reason on a card and keep it where you study. Connecting daily effort to a meaningful long-term goal changes the IELTS study plan from a temporary chore into an investment in your future.

How does variety protect my motivation?

The fifth strategy is variety in practice materials. Repeating the same activity every day quickly becomes monotonous. Alternate official IELTS tests, topics, and skill focuses, and combine practice material with academic articles, podcasts, and suitable online resources. Variation keeps attention active while still testing the same core abilities.

What should I do on a low-motivation day?

Finally, accept that motivation fluctuates. Not wanting to study on some days is normal. Promise yourself only fifteen minutes of light practice, such as reviewing the vocabulary notebook or listening to a short podcast. Starting is often the hardest step, and momentum frequently carries you into a full session. Consistency on difficult days matters more than exceptional intensity on easy ones.

How Pakistani students can use online IELTS communities and forums for preparation

How can online communities support my preparation?

Online communities can supplement an IELTS study plan with free resources, peer encouragement, and current experiences from other candidates. They work best when you use them selectively: gather useful evidence, contribute constructively, and verify advice against official scoring information.

What peer support can I find online?

Platforms such as Reddit, Facebook groups, and dedicated forums offer peer support and shared experiences. Reading about Pakistani students who reached Band 7 in six weeks can provide practical tips and realistic proof that your target is possible. Members may also share study strategies, resource recommendations, and mistakes to avoid.

Can online communities provide materials and feedback?

Yes. Online groups often share free practice materials and feedback, including links to practice tests, model essays, and cue cards. Some run weekly writing submissions with peer comments. This feedback cannot replace professional assessment, but it can reveal clarity, grammar, or structure problems that self-review misses.

How can I find a local study partner online?

Online communities can also connect you with study partners in your city or region. IELTS Facebook groups for Pakistani students often contain requests from learners in Lahore, Karachi, Islamabad, and other cities. A suitable online connection may be more effective than waiting for a partner to appear on a university notice board.

Which online communities should I choose?

Search specifically for IELTS preparation for Pakistani students. Prefer groups with active moderation, regular discussion, questions, and genuinely shared resources. Avoid communities that are mainly promotional or that encourage the distribution of copyrighted practice materials. A smaller active group can be more useful than a huge inactive one.

How should I participate in an online forum?

Contribute as well as consume. Answer another student's question when you can, share your own experience, and give specific, respectful feedback. Explaining a strategy to others exposes gaps in your understanding and builds confidence in what you know. Clear reasoning is more useful than unsupported claims that one method works for everyone.

Should I trust every tip found online?

No, because not all advice in online communities is accurate. IELTS advice varies widely, and some popular tips contradict the official scoring criteria. Verify new strategies against official IELTS resources, assess whether they make logical sense, and test them briefly against your own practice performance before giving them a permanent place in your study routine.

Can online members provide useful test-day information?

Online communities are useful for test-day information. Recent test-takers may describe the centre environment, format updates, arrival procedures, or their experience on the day. This cannot predict actual questions, but it can reduce uncertainty by clarifying logistics and the general conditions you should expect.

What should I do during the final two days?

The last two days are not for new learning. Use them to consolidate familiar material, calm your nerves, and confirm practical arrangements. A busy revision schedule at this stage can increase anxiety without improving your established skills.

What should I do the day before the exam?

On the day before the exam, revise lightly. Read your vocabulary notebook, review the grammar errors identified during six weeks of practice, and skim one or two model essays. Do not take a full test, because a disappointing score this late offers little useful information and may unnecessarily damage your confidence.

What documents do you need for IELTS study plan?

Prepare everything the evening before. Charge your phone if your centre requires a mobile device, and pack your admission ticket, passport or national identity document, water, and permitted stationery. Confirm the exact centre location and travel plan, allowing generous time for traffic and the delays common in busy Pakistani commercial areas.

Why should I organise my test materials early?

Early preparation removes avoidable stress. On test morning, your attention should remain on staying calm and performing, not locating a missing document or worrying that traffic will make you late. A simple checklist and a confirmed route allow you to leave home with confidence.

What should I do on the exam morning?

On the morning of the exam, eat a light meal before leaving home. Arrive at least thirty minutes early to allow for security checks and registration. Trust the preparation you completed, manage each section according to your rehearsed routine, and focus on the question in front of you rather than worrying about earlier answers.

What should I do after the IELTS test?

After the test, take a break before considering your next steps. Results normally arrive within a few days. Whatever the outcome, six weeks of structured preparation will have improved your English and revealed your remaining priorities; if you need a retake, you will be able to build a more focused IELTS study plan from the first attempt.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really improve by a full band in six weeks?+

Moving from around 6 to 6.5 up to a 7 is realistic in six weeks with consistent, focused study and honest error analysis. Jumping two full bands, for example from 5 to 7, usually needs longer because it involves fundamental language growth, not just test technique.

How many hours a day should I study for IELTS?+

Around 1.5 to 2 hours on weekdays plus a longer weekend block works well for most working applicants. Quality matters more than quantity: one hour of reviewing your mistakes is worth more than three hours of untargeted practice.

Which section is hardest to improve quickly?+

Writing and Speaking usually improve the slowest because they depend on feedback and changing ingrained habits. Reading and Listening tend to rise faster because timed practice and error review produce quick, measurable gains.

Should I take the Academic or General Training test?+

Almost all university and scholarship applicants need the Academic version. Confirm the exact requirement with your institution before booking, because the Reading and Writing sections differ between the two versions.

Is it worth memorising sample answers?+

No. Examiners are trained to detect memorised responses, and using one that does not fit the specific question will lower your score. Learn flexible structures and vocabulary instead, so you can adapt to whatever question appears.
