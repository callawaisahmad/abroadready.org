Study in Australia: Complete 2026 Guide (Admissions, Visa, Costs & Work)

🗓️ Aug 17, 2026⏱️ 28 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Australia is one of the most popular study-abroad destinations in the world, welcoming over 750,000 international students in 2025. It combines world-class universities with generous post-study work rights, a high quality of life, and a clear pathway to permanent residence. Whether you are from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, or anywhere else, this guide covers every step of the process — from choosing a university to landing your first job after graduation.

This is the most detailed guide you will find on studying in Australia in 2026. We cover tuition fees with exact figures, the student visa process step by step, the Temporary Graduate visa 485, top university profiles, scholarships, living costs by city, and the full permanent residence pathway. Bookmark this page and refer to it throughout your journey.

1. Why Study in Australia?

Australia consistently ranks among the top five study-abroad destinations globally, and for good reason. The country offers a combination of academic excellence, post-study work rights, and lifestyle that few other nations can match. Here are the key reasons international students choose Australia.

World-class universities in the global top 100. Eight Australian universities — the Group of Eight (Go8) — regularly appear in the QS World University Rankings top 100. The University of Melbourne ranks #1 in Australia and within the top 15 globally. The Australian National University, University of Sydney, UNSW, and others are all research powerhouses. A degree from any Go8 institution is recognised and respected by employers worldwide.

Generous post-study work rights. The Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) allows international graduates to live and work in Australia for 2 to 4 years after completing their degree. If you study in a regional area, you can get an additional 1 to 2 years on top. This is one of the most generous post-study work packages in the world. Read our full comparison at Post-Study Work Visa Comparison 2026.

Strong permanent residence pathway. Australia's skilled migration system actively targets international graduates. After the 485 visa, you can transition to permanent residence through the Subclass 189 (independent), Subclass 190 (state-nominated), or Subclass 491 (regional) visas. Studying in Australia is one of the most reliable routes to PR in any English-speaking country.

Work while you study. International students on a student visa can work up to 48 hours per fortnight during term and unlimited hours during scheduled breaks. With Australia's minimum wage of AUD 24.10 per hour, you can earn a meaningful income alongside your studies.

High quality of life. Australian cities consistently rank in the top 20 most liveable cities in the world. Melbourne, Sydney, Brisbane, Perth, and Adelaide all offer excellent public transport, healthcare, safety, and cultural diversity. The weather is warm and sunny for most of the year, which is a significant draw for students from South Asia and Africa.

Multicultural society. Over 30 percent of Australia's population was born overseas. You will find communities, restaurants, cultural events, and places of worship from virtually every country. This makes the transition much easier for international students who may feel homesick.

Research excellence. Australia produces a disproportionate share of the world's research output relative to its population. If you are interested in a research degree, Australian universities offer cutting-edge facilities and generous funding through programmes like the Research Training Program (RTP).

In short, Australia offers the full package: quality education, work rights, a pathway to residency, and an exceptional lifestyle. The sections below will show you exactly how to make it happen.

2. Australian Education System

Understanding how the Australian education system works is essential before you apply. The system is different from the UK, US, and many other countries, so pay attention to the details below.

43 universities. Australia has 43 universities in total. Of these, 8 are members of the Group of Eight (Go8), which is the equivalent of the Russell Group in the UK or the Ivy League in the US. The remaining 35 universities range from large multi-faculty institutions to specialised research universities.

CRICOS registration. Every course offered to international students must be registered on the Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students (CRICOS). This is a legal requirement. Always verify that your course and institution are CRICOS-registered before you apply. You can search the CRICOS database online at the Australian Government's Department of Education website.

TAFE (vocational education). In addition to universities, Australia has a robust TAFE (Technical and Further Education) system. TAFE institutions offer diplomas, advanced diplomas, and certificate courses in areas like hospitality, engineering technology, healthcare, and business. TAFE is more affordable than university and can be a pathway to a university degree through credit transfers.

Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF). All qualifications in Australia are structured under the AQF, which has 10 levels. The levels most relevant to international students are:

  • Level 5-6: Diploma and Advanced Diploma (TAFE)
  • Level 7: Bachelor's Degree (3-4 years)
  • Level 8: Graduate Certificate and Graduate Diploma (6 months-1 year)
  • Level 9: Master's Degree (1-2 years coursework, 2 years research)
  • Level 10: Doctoral Degree (PhD, 3-4 years)

Semester structure. Australian universities typically run two main semesters:

  • Semester 1: Late February to late May (main intake)
  • Semester 2: Late July to late November (secondary intake)

Some universities also offer a Trimester system with three intake periods per year. This gives you more flexibility in when you start your degree.

Coursework vs research degrees. Australia offers both coursework and research degrees at the postgraduate level. A coursework master's involves lectures, assignments, and exams — similar to an undergraduate structure. A research master's or PhD involves original research under the supervision of a faculty member. Research degrees often come with scholarships through the Research Training Program.

University vs TAFE pathway. Many students start with a TAFE diploma, complete it in 1-2 years, and then receive credit towards a bachelor's degree at a university. This pathway is cheaper and can be easier to get into, especially if your high school results are not strong enough for direct university entry.

3. Tuition Fees — Detailed Breakdown

Tuition fees are one of the biggest factors in your decision. Below is a comprehensive breakdown of what you can expect to pay as an international student in Australia in 2026. All figures are in Australian dollars (AUD) per year.

Undergraduate Degrees

International undergraduate tuition typically ranges from AUD 25,000 to AUD 45,000 per year. The variation depends on the university and the field of study:

  • Arts, humanities, social sciences: AUD 25,000-35,000/year
  • Business, commerce, economics: AUD 28,000-40,000/year
  • Engineering, computer science, IT: AUD 30,000-42,000/year
  • Science (biology, chemistry, physics): AUD 30,000-40,000/year
  • Law: AUD 35,000-45,000/year
  • Medicine: AUD 60,000-80,000/year

A standard bachelor's degree in Australia takes 3 years (4 years for engineering, 4-5 years for some science and health programmes). Medicine is typically 5-7 years.

Postgraduate Coursework Degrees

Postgraduate coursework tuition ranges from AUD 28,000 to AUD 50,000 per year. Key fields:

  • Master of Arts / Master of Science: AUD 28,000-38,000/year
  • Master of Business Administration (MBA): AUD 40,000-80,000/year (Go8 and top business schools charge the highest fees)
  • Master of Engineering: AUD 32,000-45,000/year
  • Master of IT / Data Science: AUD 30,000-42,000/year
  • Master of Teaching: AUD 28,000-36,000/year

Most coursework master's degrees are 1.5 to 2 years in duration. Some universities offer 1-year master's for students with relevant backgrounds.

Postgraduate Research Degrees

Research master's and doctoral tuition ranges from AUD 28,000 to AUD 42,000 per year. However, many research students receive fee waivers through the Research Training Program (RTP). If you secure an RTP scholarship, your tuition is fully covered and you receive a living stipend of approximately AUD 32,000 per year.

MBA Programmes

MBA fees vary widely. At Go8 universities, expect to pay AUD 50,000 to AUD 80,000 per year. At other institutions, MBA fees range from AUD 35,000 to AUD 55,000 per year. Executive MBAs can cost even more. The duration is typically 1.5 to 2 years.

Medicine and Health Sciences

Medical degrees are the most expensive programmes in Australia. International medical student fees range from AUD 60,000 to AUD 80,000 per year. Dentistry, veterinary science, and pharmacy are similarly priced. These programmes are 4-7 years in duration.

Living Costs by City

The Australian government requires international students to demonstrate they have at least AUD 24,505 per year for living costs as part of the visa application. This is a minimum — actual costs depend heavily on where you live:

  • Sydney: AUD 27,000-30,000/year (highest due to rent)
  • Melbourne: AUD 24,000-27,000/year
  • Brisbane: AUD 22,000-26,000/year
  • Perth: AUD 22,000-25,000/year
  • Adelaide: AUD 20,000-24,000/year
  • Regional cities (Geelong, Wollongong, Townsville): AUD 18,000-22,000/year

Beyond tuition and living costs, budget for OSHC health insurance (AUD 600-700/year), visa fees (AUD 710), and airfare (AUD 800-2,000 depending on your country). For a full financial planning guide, see our Study in Australia overview.

4. Top Australian Universities — Detailed Profiles

Choosing the right university is one of the most important decisions you will make. Below are detailed profiles of Australia's best universities, organised by ranking and reputation.

Group of Eight (Go8) — Australia's Elite

The Group of Eight is Australia's equivalent of the Ivy League or Russell Group. These eight universities dominate research funding, graduate employment, and global rankings. Employers in Australia and worldwide specifically value Go8 degrees.

1. University of Melbourne — Ranked #1 in Australia and #13 in the world (QS 2026). Melbourne is the most prestigious university in Australia across virtually every subject. It excels in medicine, law, engineering, arts, and business. The university has a strong research focus and offers generous scholarships for international students. Campus life is vibrant, and Melbourne itself is consistently ranked one of the world's most liveable cities.

2. Australian National University (ANU) — Located in Canberra, the national capital. ANU is ranked #1 in Australia for many research fields and is particularly strong in political science, international relations, philosophy, astronomy, and physics. Being in Canberra means proximity to government agencies and policy think tanks, making it ideal for students interested in public policy. ANU offers the prestigious Chancellor's International Scholarship covering full tuition.

3. University of Sydney — Australia's oldest university, founded in 1850. Sydney is ranked in the top 20 globally and is especially strong in medicine, law, humanities, and architecture. The university's sandstone campus is iconic. Sydney's location offers access to Australia's largest economy and job market. The university offers the Sydney Scholars Award for high-achieving international students.

4. University of Queensland (UQ) — Based in Brisbane, UQ is a leader in science, agriculture, environmental science, and mining engineering. It is ranked in the top 50 globally and has one of the largest campuses in Australia. Brisbane offers a lower cost of living than Sydney or Melbourne, and the city is growing rapidly. UQ offers generous research scholarships and has strong industry connections.

5. University of New South Wales (UNSW Sydney) — UNSW is Australia's leading university for engineering, computer science, and business. It is ranked in the top 20 globally for engineering and technology. The university has strong ties to the tech and startup ecosystem in Sydney. UNSW offers the International Scholar Award reducing tuition by up to AUD 5,000 per year.

6. Monash University — Located in Melbourne with campuses in Malaysia and South Africa. Monash is ranked in the top 40 globally and is particularly strong in pharmacy, engineering, business, and medicine. It has a large international student community and offers flexible study options including evening classes.

7. University of Western Australia (UWA) — Based in Perth, UWA is ranked in the top 80 globally. It excels in mining engineering, marine science, agriculture, and astronomy. Perth offers a relaxed lifestyle with lower living costs. UWA offers the Global Excellence Scholarship worth up to AUD 48,000 over four years for undergraduate students.

8. University of Adelaide — Located in South Australia, Adelaide is ranked in the top 100 globally. It is known for wine science, defence technology, agriculture, and engineering. Adelaide is one of the most affordable major cities in Australia, and South Australia actively sponsors international graduates for permanent residence through state nomination.

Other Top Universities

University of Technology Sydney (UTS) — A technology-focused university in central Sydney. Strong in IT, engineering, business, and design. Known for industry-connected learning and employability.

RMIT University — Melbourne-based with a focus on design, architecture, engineering, and business. RMIT has strong vocational connections and offers practical, career-focused programmes.

Deakin University — A large multi-campus university in Victoria with campuses in Melbourne, Geelong, and Wollongong. Known for online learning, education, nursing, and sports science. Deakin is considered a regional campus in some locations, which can give you additional post-study work benefits.

Flinders University — Based in Adelaide, Flinders is a popular choice for international students due to lower fees and Adelaide's affordable living costs. South Australia's strong state nomination programme makes Flinders an excellent choice for students targeting PR.

Charles Darwin University — Located in the Northern Territory, this is a genuinely regional university. Studying here can earn you additional years on your 485 visa and bonus points for skilled migration. The Northern Territory also has dedicated skilled occupation lists.

5. Student Visa — Subclass 500 (Complete Guide)

The Student visa (subclass 500) is the visa you need to study full-time in Australia. This section covers every requirement in detail.

Eligibility and Requirements

To apply for a Student visa (subclass 500), you must meet the following requirements:

1. Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE). You must have a CoE from a CRICOS-registered institution. The CoE is issued after you accept your offer and pay your deposit. You cannot apply for a student visa without a CoE.

2. Financial capacity. You must prove you have enough money to cover:

  • Living costs: AUD 24,505 per year
  • Tuition fees for the first year of your course
  • Travel costs: approximately AUD 2,000 for a return ticket
  • School costs for dependents: AUD 8,000 per child per year

You can demonstrate this through bank statements, loan approvals, scholarship letters, or a combination. Funds must have been held for at least 3 months in many cases.

3. Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC). You must purchase OSHC for the entire duration of your visa. The main providers are Allianz, Bupa, Medibank, OSHC Worldcare, and NIB. A 12-month single policy costs approximately AUD 600 to AUD 700. You must show proof of OSHC when you apply.

4. English language evidence. The minimum requirements are:

  • IELTS: 5.5 overall (with package course) or 6.0 overall (direct entry)
  • PTE Academic: 42 (package) or 50 (direct entry)
  • TOEFL iBT: 46 (package) or 64 (direct entry)
  • Cambridge C1 Advanced: 162 (package) or 169 (direct entry)

Some universities set higher requirements. Medicine, law, and teaching typically require IELTS 7.0 or higher. If you want to study without IELTS, see our guide on studying abroad without IELTS.

5. Genuine Student requirement. In 2024, Australia replaced the Genuine Temporary Entrant (GTE) requirement with the Genuine Student (GS) test. You must demonstrate that you genuinely intend to stay in Australia temporarily for study, and that your chosen course aligns with your career goals and academic history. This is assessed through a written statement and your overall application.

6. Health check. You may be required to undergo a medical examination by a panel physician. This depends on your country of residence, the length of your stay, and your chosen course. Health checks are typically required for courses longer than 6 months or for students from certain countries. Pakistan and India are commonly flagged for health checks.

7. Police clearance. You must provide police clearance certificates from any country where you have lived for 12 months or more in the past 10 years. This is to satisfy the character requirement.

8. Valid passport. Your passport must be valid for at least the duration of your intended stay in Australia.

Visa Cost and Processing Time

The current visa application fee is AUD 710 (as of 2026). This is non-refundable. Additional costs include health checks (AUD 300-500) and police clearances (varies by country).

Processing times vary based on your country and application complexity:

  • 75% of applications: processed within 42 days
  • 90% of applications: processed within 90 days

Apply at least 6 to 8 weeks before your course starts. Peak processing periods are January to March (Semester 1) and June to July (Semester 2).

Where to Apply

All student visa applications are submitted online through ImmiAccount on the Department of Home Affairs website. You do not need to visit an embassy in most cases, though you may be asked to attend a biometrics appointment.

Country-specific notes:

  • Pakistan: Applications are processed through the Australian High Commission in Islamabad. Health checks are typically required. Processing times can be longer — apply early.
  • India: Applications can be submitted through the Australian High Commission in New Delhi or consulates in Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, or Bangalore. India has a streamlined process for many applicants.
  • Bangladesh: Processed through the Australian High Commission in Dhaka. Health checks and police clearances are usually required.
  • Nigeria: Processed through the Australian High Commission in Abuja. Additional documentation may be requested.

6. Application Process — Step by Step

The application process has several stages. Follow these steps in order to avoid delays or mistakes.

Step 1: Choose a CRICOS-registered institution and course. Research universities and programmes on the CRICOS website. Consider ranking, location, fees, entry requirements, and career outcomes. Use our Study in Australia page to compare options.

Step 2: Check entry requirements. Every course has specific academic and English language requirements. For undergraduate programmes, you typically need completion of Year 12 or equivalent with specific subject prerequisites. For postgraduate programmes, you need a relevant bachelor's degree with a minimum GPA (usually 50-65 percent depending on the university).

Step 3: Apply directly or through an agent. Most Australian universities accept direct applications through their website. Some students prefer to apply through a registered education agent, which can be free (universities pay commissions to agents). If you use an agent, make sure they are registered on the Australian Government's Register of Education Agents.

Step 4: Receive your offer letter. If your application is successful, you will receive either a conditional offer (if you still need to provide documents like final transcripts or English test results) or an unconditional offer.

Step 5: Accept your offer and pay the deposit. Accept the offer in writing and pay the required deposit, which is typically one semester's tuition (AUD 12,000-25,000 depending on the university). Some universities require a larger upfront payment.

Step 6: Receive your Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE). After your deposit is processed, the university issues your CoE. This is the document you need for your visa application.

Step 7: Purchase OSHC. Buy Overseas Student Health Cover from an approved provider. You need coverage for the full duration of your stay.

Step 8: Prepare financial evidence. Gather bank statements, scholarship letters, loan documents, or sponsor letters to prove you can cover tuition and living costs.

Step 9: Apply for your student visa online. Create an ImmiAccount on the Department of Home Affairs website. Upload all documents: CoE, OSHC, financial evidence, English test results, passport, health check results (if applicable), and police clearances.

Step 10: Complete health check (if required). If you are asked to do a health examination, book an appointment with a Bupa Medical Visa Services panel physician in your country.

Step 11: Wait for a decision. Processing takes 42-90 days. You may be asked for additional documents. Check your ImmiAccount regularly for updates.

Step 12: Travel to Australia. Once your visa is granted, you can enter Australia up to 90 days before your course starts. Arrange accommodation, airport pickup, and orientation in advance.

Key Deadlines

  • Semester 1 (February/March start): Apply by October-November the prior year
  • Semester 2 (July/August start): Apply by April-May the same year
  • Trimester intakes: Varies by university — check individual websites

Start the process at least 6 months before your intended start date. Late applications are possible but risky — visa processing can be slow, and you may not get accommodation or orientation support.

7. Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485)

The Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) is one of the biggest advantages of studying in Australia. It allows international graduates to live, work, and study in Australia temporarily after completing their qualification. This visa is the bridge between your studies and permanent residence.

Post-Study Work Stream

The Post-Study Work stream is the most common pathway. The length of stay depends on your qualification:

  • Bachelor's degree (including honours): 2 years
  • Master's by coursework: 2 years
  • Master's by research: 3 years
  • Doctoral degree (PhD): 4 years

Regional study extension. If you study in a designated regional area, you can receive an additional 1 to 2 years:

  • Major cities (Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane): standard duration only
  • Classified regional cities (Perth, Adelaide, Gold Coast, Sunshine Coast, Hobart, Canberra, Wollongong, Newcastle): +1 year
  • Other regional areas: +2 years

This means a bachelor's graduate studying in a truly regional area could get up to 4 years on the 485 visa, and a PhD graduate could get up to 6 years.

Graduate Work Stream

The Graduate Work stream is for graduates whose qualification is related to an occupation on Australia's Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL). This stream grants 18 months of stay. You must have a skills assessment for your nominated occupation from the relevant assessing authority.

485 Visa Requirements

  • You must have completed a qualification at a CRICOS-registered institution
  • You must be under 50 years of age
  • You must hold a valid student visa or have held one within the last 6 months
  • You must have adequate health insurance (OSHC or equivalent)
  • You must have no substantial compliance issues with visa conditions
  • You must meet English language requirements: IELTS 6.0 overall with minimum 5.0 in each band (or equivalent)

The 485 visa fee is AUD 1,895. You can include family members in your application for additional fees.

What You Can Do on a 485 Visa

  • Work full-time for any employer
  • Change employers freely
  • Study further
  • Travel in and out of Australia
  • Build work experience for a skilled migration visa application

The 485 visa is a crucial stepping stone. During the 2-4 years it gives you, you can gain Australian work experience, improve your English, and build the points you need for a permanent residence application.

8. Scholarships in Australia

Australia offers a wide range of scholarships for international students. Some are fully funded by the Australian Government, others by universities, and some by external organisations.

Australia Awards (DFAT)

The Australia Awards are fully funded scholarships provided by the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT). They cover:

  • Full tuition fees
  • Return airfare
  • Living allowance (approximately AUD 3,000 per month)
  • Overseas Student Health Cover
  • Introductory academic programme
  • Fieldwork allowance

Australia Awards are available to students from specific countries (including Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nepal, and many African and Pacific nations). Applications typically open in April-May and close in August-September for the following year's intake. These scholarships are competitive but life-changing. Read our full Australia Awards and RTP guide.

Research Training Program (RTP)

The RTP is the Australian Government's main research scholarship programme. It provides:

  • Full tuition fee offset
  • Stipend of approximately AUD 32,000 per year (tax-free)
  • Health insurance
  • Up to 3 years for a research master's, up to 4 years for a PhD

RTP scholarships are allocated by universities, not directly by the government. You apply for RTP when you apply for a research degree. Selection is based on academic merit and research potential.

Destination Australia Programme

The Destination Australia programme provides scholarships of up to AUD 15,000 per year for international students studying at universities in regional Australia. This programme aims to attract students to regional areas, which benefit from international student spending and cultural diversity.

International Postgraduate Research Scholarship (IPRS)

The IPRS is a prestigious research scholarship for international postgraduate students. It covers tuition fees and is often combined with a university-funded living stipend. The IPRS is allocated through universities and is highly competitive.

University-Specific Scholarships

Most Australian universities offer their own scholarships for international students:

  • Melbourne Graduate Scholarship: up to AUD 50,000 for master's students
  • ANU Chancellor's International Scholarship: 25-100 percent tuition reduction
  • Sydney Scholars Award: AUD 10,000 per year for undergraduate students
  • UNSW International Student Award: AUD 5,000-10,000 per year
  • UWA Global Excellence Scholarship: up to AUD 48,000 over 4 years
  • Deakin Vice-Chancellor's International Scholarship: 50-100 percent tuition reduction

Many universities also offer automatic merit scholarships — you are considered for them when you apply, and no separate application is needed. Always check the scholarships page of each university you apply to.

9. Living in Australia — What to Expect

Moving to a new country is a big adjustment. Here is a practical guide to living in Australia as an international student.

Accommodation

Accommodation is your biggest living expense. Your options include:

  • On-campus residential colleges: AUD 250-500/week. Includes meals, social events, and study support. Great for making friends in your first semester. Competitive to get into — apply early.
  • Shared rental (flat-share): AUD 200-400/week. The most common option for students. You share a house or apartment with other students. Use Flatmates.com.au, Gumtree, or university housing groups on Facebook to find housemates.
  • Private rental (studio or 1-bedroom): AUD 300-600/week depending on city. More expensive but gives you privacy. Sydney and Melbourne are the most expensive.
  • Homestay (living with an Australian family): AUD 250-400/week including meals. Good for improving English and experiencing Australian culture. Can feel restrictive for some students.

Start looking for accommodation as soon as you accept your offer. On-campus housing fills up quickly, especially for Semester 1.

Food and Groceries

Expect to spend AUD 80-150 per week on food. Cooking at home is the most affordable option. Major supermarket chains are Woolworths, Coles, and Aldi. Aldi is generally the cheapest. Asian and Indian grocery stores are widely available in major cities and offer familiar ingredients at lower prices.

Eating out costs AUD 15-30 per meal at a casual restaurant. Fast food is AUD 10-15. Most universities have affordable on-campus food courts.

Transport

Public transport is excellent in major cities. Each city has a contactless smart card system:

  • Sydney: Opal card
  • Melbourne: Myki card
  • Brisbane: Go card
  • Perth: SmartRider
  • Adelaide: Metrocard

Students typically receive discounted fares. Weekly transport costs are AUD 20-40 depending on the city. Cycling is also popular, and many universities provide bike storage.

Healthcare

OSHC is mandatory and covers doctor visits, hospital treatment, ambulance services, and some prescription medications. It does not cover dental, optical, or cosmetic procedures. You can purchase additional cover from private insurers if needed.

If you need to see a doctor, most have bulk billing (free for the patient) through OSHC, but some charge a gap fee. Hospital treatment is covered for emergencies.

Banking

Open an Australian bank account as soon as you arrive. The major banks are:

  • Commonwealth Bank (CBA)
  • ANZ
  • Westpac
  • National Australia Bank (NAB)

You can open an account online before you arrive using your passport and CoE. All major banks have student accounts with no monthly fees. You will need your bank account for receiving wages, paying rent, and managing day-to-day expenses.

Phone and Internet

Budget AUD 30-50 per month for a mobile phone plan. Major providers are Telstra, Optus, Vodafone, and MVNOs (Amaysim, Boost, Aldi Mobile). Prepaid plans are popular because they do not require a contract. Most include unlimited calls and text plus 10-50 GB of data. Internet at home is AUD 60-80 per month for unlimited broadband.

Culture and Lifestyle

Australians are generally friendly and informal. The culture values egalitarianism, work-life balance, and outdoor activities. Barbecues, beach days, and sports are central to Australian social life. Major sports include cricket (summer), Australian rules football and rugby (winter), and soccer (year-round).

Australia is ethnically diverse. You will find Indian, Pakistani, Chinese, Vietnamese, Lebanese, Greek, and many other cultural communities. Major cities have suburbs known for specific cuisines — for example, Footscray in Melbourne for Vietnamese food, or Harris Park in Sydney for Indian restaurants.

10. Working in Australia as a Student

Working while studying is one of the best ways to offset living costs and gain Australian work experience. Here is what you need to know.

Work Rights on a Student Visa

Since July 2023, international students on a Student visa (subclass 500) can work:

  • Up to 48 hours per fortnight during teaching periods
  • Unlimited hours during scheduled course breaks (e.g., summer holidays, winter break)

There is no minimum wage exploitation in Australia — the national minimum wage is AUD 24.10 per hour (as of July 2025). Most student jobs pay AUD 25-35 per hour.

Tax File Number (TFN)

You must have a Tax File Number (TFN) to work legally in Australia. Apply for a TFN through the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) website. It takes about 28 days to receive your TFN by post. Without a TFN, your employer will deduct the highest tax rate from your wages.

Popular Student Jobs

  • Hospitality: Cafes, restaurants, bars, and catering. Flexible hours, good tips.
  • Retail: Supermarkets, clothing stores, electronics shops. Weekend and evening shifts available.
  • On-campus work: Library assistants, research assistants, administrative roles. Convenient and understanding of your study schedule.
  • Tutoring: If you are strong in a subject, tutor other students. Good pay (AUD 30-60/hour).
  • Delivery and rideshare: Food delivery (Uber Eats, DoorDash) or rideshare (Uber). Flexible but requires a vehicle.
  • Internships: Some degree programmes include mandatory or paid internships. Business, IT, and engineering students have the most opportunities.

Your student visa also allows you to work in any job — there are no restrictions on the type of work you can do during your permitted hours.

Balancing Work and Study

Australian universities expect you to spend approximately 40-45 hours per week on study-related activities (classes, assignments, reading). Working 20-24 hours per week (the 48-hour fortnight cap) is manageable for most students, but be careful not to overcommit. Your academic performance affects your future scholarship and visa applications.

11. Permanent Residence Pathway

One of the biggest draws of studying in Australia is the pathway to permanent residence. Here is how it works.

The Typical Pathway

The most common route is: Student visa (500) → Temporary Graduate visa (485) → Skilled migration visa (189/190/491).

  1. Complete your degree on a Student visa (subclass 500)
  2. Apply for the 485 visa to gain 2-4 years of post-study work experience
  3. Use that time to gain work experience, improve English, and accumulate points
  4. Apply for a permanent skilled migration visa

Skilled Migration Visas

Subclass 189 (Skilled Independent). A points-based visa with no state nomination required. You must score at least 65 points on the points test. Competitive scores are typically 85-95 points. Points are awarded for: age, English language ability, educational qualifications, Australian work experience, overseas work experience, partner skills, and other factors.

Subclass 190 (Skilled Nominated). Requires nomination by an Australian state or territory government. You get +5 bonus points for state nomination. Each state has its own nomination criteria and occupation lists. Some states specifically target international graduates from their universities. South Australia, Tasmania, and the Northern Territory are particularly generous.

Subclass 491 (Skilled Work Regional — Provisional). A provisional (temporary) visa for regional areas. You get +15 bonus points for regional nomination. After 3 years on this visa, you can apply for the Subclass 191 permanent visa. This is the easiest pathway for students who studied in regional areas.

Points Breakdown

  • Age: 25-32 years = 30 points (maximum)
  • English: Superior English (IELTS 8.0+) = 20 points
  • Education: Australian PhD = 20 points; Australian Master's/ Bachelor's = 15 points
  • Australian study requirement: 2+ years of study in Australia = 5 points
  • Regional study: Studying in a regional area = 5 points
  • Partner skills: Partner with skills assessment = 5-10 points
  • State nomination (190): +5 points
  • Regional nomination (491): +15 points

Which Occupations Are in Demand?

The Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL) changes regularly. As of 2026, high-demand occupations include software engineers, data scientists, nurses, civil engineers, electricians, carpenters, early childhood teachers, secondary school teachers, mechanical engineers, and accountants. Check the current Skilled Occupation List (SOL) on the Department of Home Affairs website for the latest information.

Studying a degree that leads to an occupation on the skills list significantly improves your chances of permanent residence. For more details, see our Post-Study Work Visa Comparison.

12. Application Timeline

Below are detailed timelines for both Semester 1 and Semester 2 intakes. Follow these to stay on track.

Semester 1 Entry (February/March)

  • June-August (12-8 months before): Research universities and courses. Take your IELTS, PTE, or TOEFL test. Start preparing financial documents.
  • September-October (5-4 months before): Submit applications to your chosen universities. Apply to 3-5 universities to maximise your chances.
  • November-December (3-2 months before): Receive and accept offer letters. Pay the required deposit. Receive your CoE.
  • December-January (2-1 months before): Purchase OSHC. Prepare financial evidence. Book health check if required. Gather police clearances.
  • January-February (1 month before): Submit your student visa application through ImmiAccount. Complete health check. Wait for visa decision.
  • February-March: Receive visa. Book flights. Arrange accommodation. Attend orientation week. Start classes.

Semester 2 Entry (July/August)

  • January-March: Research universities and courses. Take English language tests.
  • April-May: Submit university applications. Receive and accept offers.
  • May-June: Receive CoE. Purchase OSHC. Prepare financial documents. Submit visa application.
  • June-July: Wait for visa decision. Book flights and accommodation.
  • July-August: Travel to Australia. Attend orientation. Start classes.

Important Dates to Remember

  • August-September: Australia Awards applications open
  • October-November: University application deadlines for Semester 1
  • December-January: Visa processing peak period
  • February: Semester 1 orientation and classes begin
  • April-May: University application deadlines for Semester 2
  • July: Semester 2 orientation and classes begin
  • November: Semester 2 exams end

Remember: apply early. The earlier you submit your application, the more time you have to resolve any issues that arise. Late applications are possible but risk delays in visa processing and accommodation booking.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to study in Australia?+

International undergraduate tuition ranges from AUD 25,000 to 45,000 per year. Postgraduate tuition ranges from AUD 28,000 to 50,000 per year. Living costs are AUD 24,505 per year (the minimum required for visa purposes, though actual costs are higher in Sydney and Melbourne).

What is the Australian Student visa (subclass 500)?+

The Student visa (subclass 500) allows international students to study full-time in Australia. You need a Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) from a CRICOS-registered institution, proof of financial capacity (AUD 24,505/year plus tuition and travel), OSHC health insurance, and English language evidence. The visa fee is AUD 710.

Can I work while studying in Australia?+

Yes. Student visa holders can work up to 48 hours per fortnight during term and unlimited hours during scheduled breaks. From July 2023, the cap was increased from 40 to 48 hours per fortnight. You need a Tax File Number (TFN) to work legally.

What is the Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485)?+

The 485 visa allows international graduates to live, work and study in Australia temporarily. The Post-Study Work stream gives 2 to 4 years depending on qualification level. Regional graduates can get an additional 1 to 2 years. The Graduate Work stream gives 18 months for skills occupation list qualifications.

What is the Group of Eight (Go8)?+

The Group of Eight is Australia's coalition of eight leading research-intensive universities: University of Melbourne, Australian National University, University of Sydney, University of Queensland, University of New South Wales, Monash University, University of Western Australia, and University of Adelaide. They account for most of Australia's university research output.

Do I need IELTS for Australian universities?+

Most Australian universities require IELTS 6.0 to 6.5 for undergraduate and postgraduate programmes. Some programmes (medicine, law, teaching) require 7.0 or higher. Australia also accepts PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, Cambridge C1 Advanced, and OET (for health professions). For the student visa, the minimum is IELTS 5.5 (with package) or 6.0 (direct entry).

How long does the Australian student visa take?+

Processing times vary: 75% of visas are processed within 42 days, 90% within 90 days. Apply at least 6 to 8 weeks before your course starts. Some applicants may need additional health checks or police clearances which can extend processing.

What scholarships are available in Australia?+

Major scholarships include Australia Awards (fully funded by DFAT), Research Training Program (RTP for postgraduate research), Destination Australia (regional), university-specific scholarships (Melbourne, ANU, Sydney), and IPRS (International Postgraduate Research Scholarship). Many universities offer automatic merit scholarships.

Can I get permanent residence after studying in Australia?+

Yes. After studying, the 485 visa is a stepping stone to permanent residence via skilled migration visas: Subclass 189 (independent points-based), Subclass 190 (state-nominated), or Subclass 491 (regional provisional). Regional study gives bonus points and additional pathways. State nomination streams specifically target international graduates.

What is OSHC and do I need it?+

Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) is mandatory health insurance for all international students in Australia. It covers doctor visits, hospital treatment, ambulance services, and some pharmaceuticals. Typical cost: AUD 600 to 700 for a 12-month single policy. You must maintain OSHC for the entire duration of your visa.
