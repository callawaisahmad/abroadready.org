Immigrate to Spain: The 2026 Visa & Settlement Guide

Spain's three practical routes for people without a local job offer: the Non-Lucrative Visa (live on savings/passive income, €28,800/yr — no work at all), the Digital Nomad Visa (work remotely for foreign employers, €2,849/month), and self-employment (cuenta propia). With the Golden Visa abolished, these plus employer sponsorship are the way in — and settlement runs 5 years to PR, 10 to citizenship.

The Spanish System in One Paragraph

Spain has no skilled-points migration; it has visa categories. If you can prove income, the Non-Lucrative Visa lets you live there without working (or you switch to a work permit after a year). If you earn remotely, the Digital Nomad Visa fits. If you have a Spanish employer, they file for a work permit. Residence permits last 1+2+2 years, permanent residence arrives at 5 years, and citizenship at 10 — or 2 for Ibero-American nationals.

Golden Visa is gone: the real-estate investor route was abolished on 3 April 2025 (Organic Law 1/2025). If you're investing, it no longer buys residence. The NLV, DNV and autónomo routes are now the main doors for non-workers.

Self-Employment (Cuenta Propia) & Work Permits

🧑‍💼 Autónomo visa

Viable freelancing/business plan; living funds 100% IPREM (~€600/month) + business start-up funds (typically €10,000–15,000 assessed). 1 year, renewable 4+5; PR at 5 years.

🏢 Employer work permit

A Spanish employer sponsors your work permit (cuenta ajena); after a year NLV holders can modify to this. Social security: register as autónomo within 30 days; reduced ~€80/month for 24 months.

🏠 Golden Visa

Abolished 3 April 2025. Transitional protection only for applications submitted before that date and existing permits.

Tourist & Short-Stay — the Schengen C Visa

For up to 90 days in any 180-day period across the whole Schengen Area — holiday, family visits and short business trips. Short-stay status, not a residence permit.

Employer Sponsorship: HQP, EU Blue Card & ICT

Beyond the standard cuenta ajena permit, Spain has dedicated routes for highly qualified hires and intra-company transfers.

🏢 Highly Qualified Professionals (HQP) National

National route (Ley 14/2013) for roles requiring a university degree or higher VET. No fixed salary floor — pay just needs to match the collective agreement. Processed by the UGE in ~20 working days; tied to the employer.

🟦 EU Blue Card EU-wide

Salary ≥ ~€41,400/yr (1.4× the INE average, updated May 2026); reduced ~€33,100 for shortage occupations and recent graduates. Needs a 3+ year degree or 5 years' experience. EU mobility after 18 months; 20 working days at the UGE.

🔄 ICT permit Transfer

Intra-corporate transferees (Directive 2014/66/EU) — managers, specialists and trainees. Requires 3 months' prior employment with the group; no labour-market test. Up to 3 years (managers/specialists) or 1 year (trainees). Fees ~€73 + €16 for the card.

📋 Social security regime Autónomo

All workers (employees and self-employed) must register with Social Security. Self-employed (autónomo) pay a reduced flat rate of ~€80/month for the first 24 months. Employment-based permits include social security coverage automatically.

Beckham Law: newly arrived workers can opt into the special inpatriate regime — a flat 24% tax on employment income up to €600K/yr for up to 6 years (available to Blue Card and other qualifying hires who haven't been Spanish tax residents in the last 5 years).

Study → Post-Study → Work

Study time in Spain counts toward settlement. Full guidance lives on the Study in Spain page.

🎓 Student visa

For a place at a Spanish university or accredited centre. Non-EU students may work up to 30 hrs/week if compatible with study, and full-time in holidays. Student time in Spain counts toward the 3-year residence clock for arraigo.

💼 Switch to a work permit

After studies, an employer can file for a cuenta ajena work permit (initial one-year authorisation). Students who complete higher education can transition with a lighter "first work authorisation" process.

🔁 Arraigo pathway

With 3 years' legal residence in Spain (student time counts), you can apply for work via arraigo social or laboral without leaving — the standard route for graduates who stay to work informally first.

🛂 Settlement clock

Permanent residence needs 5 years of residence permits; citizenship needs 10 (or 2 for Ibero-American nationals). Student years help arraigo but not the formal PR clock.

Investor & Entrepreneur Routes

🏠 Golden Visa Abolished

The real-estate investor route (€500K property) was abolished on 3 April 2025 (Organic Law 1/2025). Transitional protection applies only to applications lodged before that date and existing permits — new investment no longer buys residence.

🚀 Entrepreneur visa Start-ups

Ley de Emprendedores route for founders whose business plan is judged of "general economic interest" by the authorities. No employer needed; funding must match the business plan (typically tens of thousands of euros depending on the sector). 1-year initial permit, renewable.

🧑‍💻 Digital Nomad Visa Remote

The closest thing Spain has to an investor/remote-worker route — 200% SMI (€2,849/month), up to 3 years renewable +2. See the full section above.

💶 Non-Lucrative Visa Passive income

For people living on savings/passive income (€28,800/yr) — the non-working alternative to the abolished investor route. See the full section above.

Special & Regularisation Routes

🔁 Arraigo social Regularisation

For people already in Spain (even irregularly): after 2 years' residence (reduced from 3 by the 2025 reform) with a work contract of 20+ hrs/wk or family ties. Under RD 1155/2024 permits now run 4 years.

🔁 Arraigo laboral Worked

2 years' residence in Spain plus at least 6 months of worked (even undeclared) employment — regularises people who are already contributing. Same 4-year permits under the 2025 reform.

🔁 Arraigo familiar Family

For parents of Spanish or EU citizens, and children of Spanish or EU citizens — regularisation through family ties without a waiting period.

🇪🇺 EU family member registration EU/EEA

Family members of EU/EEA/Swiss citizens don't need a visa — they register with the authorities (Tarjeta de Familiar de Ciudadano de la Unión) within 3 months of arrival. Card valid 5 years; permanent after 5. No income test or work permit.

Immigration FAQs

Which Spanish visa is easiest to get in 2026?+

The Non-Lucrative Visa is the most straightforward if you have €28,800/yr of passive income and don't need to work — refusals mainly happen when funds are too close to the minimum. The Digital Nomad Visa suits remote employees earning €2,849+/month from abroad. Work permits need a Spanish employer.

Can I work remotely in Spain without a job there?+

Yes — that's exactly what the Digital Nomad Visa is for: ≥80% of income from outside Spain, 3+ months of remote-work history, and a degree or 3 years of experience. Note that NLV holders can't work remotely at all and can't switch to the DNV (they can switch to a standard work permit after year one).

How long until Spanish permanent residence and citizenship?+

Permanent residence after 5 years (min 183 days/year). Citizenship after 10 years — or 2 for Ibero-American nationals — plus the CCSE exam and DELE A2. Spain allows dual citizenship only for the countries on its reciprocal list.

Is Spain's Golden Visa really gone?+

Yes — abolished by Organic Law 1/2025, effective 3 April 2025. Applications lodged before that date and already-issued permits remain protected under transitional rules, but no new real-estate-investment residence visas are available.

Can I get Spanish citizenship faster as an English speaker?+

No — Spain requires DELE A2 Spanish for non-Spanish-speaking nationals regardless. English speakers from the US/UK follow the 10-year residence rule with no reduced timeline (that privilege is reserved mainly for Ibero-American countries).

Can I switch from a tourist Schengen visa to Spanish residence?+

Generally no — a tourist stay is short-stay and cannot be converted into a residence permit. You must leave and apply for a visa at the Spanish consulate. The main in-Spain routes are the arraigo regularisation pathways (after 2–3 years of residence) and registration as an EU family member.

Can I attend business meetings in Spain without a work permit?+

Yes — short business trips (meetings, conferences, negotiations) are covered by the Schengen C visa or visa-free entry for waiver nationals. You may not be employed or paid by a Spanish employer. Any actual employment needs a work permit, HQP authorisation or EU Blue Card arranged before arrival.

What exactly is "arraigo" in Spain?+

Arraigo is a regularisation route for people already in Spain, including those whose stay became irregular. The 2025 reform (RD 1155/2024) cut the residence requirement to 2 years for arraigo social, kept arraigo laboral (2 years + 6 months worked) and arraigo familiar (family of Spanish/EU citizens), and extended the resulting permits to 4 years with automatic work rights.
