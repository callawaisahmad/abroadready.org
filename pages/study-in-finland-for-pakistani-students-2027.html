Study in Finland for Pakistani Students: 2027

🗓️ Oct 5, 2026⏱️ 5 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

To study in Finland for Pakistani students in 2027, apply in the joint application window on Studyinfo from 7 to 21 January 2027, pay a EUR 100 application fee, and handle funding through university fee waivers, because Finland offers no state scholarship to non-EU students.

Finland is the simplest Nordic system to apply to and the least generous to fund. One window, one portal, one fee, and then it is between you and the institution's own waiver policy.

Study in Finland for Pakistani Students: The January Window

The joint application for autumn 2027 runs on the Studyinfo portal from 7 to 21 January 2027. Degree programmes do not admit on a rolling basis, so if you miss those two weeks you wait a year. Non-EU/EEA applicants pay a EUR 100 application fee, introduced from 1 January 2025, and it applies per application round rather than per programme.

That timing sits awkwardly with Pakistan, because January is also when you should be finishing document attestation and language tests. Prepare in December: uploads and payment problems, not eligibility, are what cause missed Finnish deadlines.

How Finnish Fee Waivers Work

There is no Finnish state scholarship for non-EU degree students. Funding is set institution by institution, and the pattern is usually a waiver for committing early rather than a merit award handed out on grades alone.

Abo Akademi publishes the clearest example: a full fee of EUR 12,000 a year, a EUR 4,000 early-commitment discount that brings it to EUR 8,000, and a second-year waiver of EUR 4,000. That structure matters, because a first-year discount that ends is not the same as a three-year award, and budgeting on year one alone leaves you short in year two.

Ask each institution three questions in writing: what the waiver covers, whether it renews, and whether it is applied for automatically with admission or needs a separate form. Answers vary widely between universities.

Building a Finnish Application from Pakistan

Entry requirements are programme-specific and published on each programme page rather than nationally. Read the required degree and English test there before you pay the EUR 100 fee, because the fee is not refundable if you turn out to be ineligible.

What the Finnish Application Requires

Applications are submitted online on Studyinfo, and each programme publishes its own entry requirements. The common thread is a relevant bachelor's degree and a recognised English test, but some programmes add subject prerequisites, a portfolio, or a motivation task completed before selection.

Selection varies by degree. Some programmes rank purely on academic record, others use a written assignment or an online interview, and a few combine the two. Because the window closes on 21 January, any interview or assignment lands in February and March, which means you should keep your calendar clear through early spring rather than assuming the result arrives immediately.

Uploads are where Pakistani applicants lose time. Degree certificates and transcripts issued in Urdu need certified translations, and getting that done in December is far easier than in the second week of January. Prepare the document set before the window opens, including your passport scan, so that submission is a fifteen-minute task.

Living Costs and Working While Studying

Tuition is the number people compare, but living costs decide whether the plan survives. Finnish cities differ sharply: Helsinki and Espoo are the most expensive, while smaller student cities are noticeably cheaper on rent, which is usually the largest single line in a student budget.

Non-EU students may work alongside their studies, subject to the hours permitted on a residence permit for study. Work is not a substitute for funding, particularly in the first months before you speak any Finnish, but it can cover groceries and rent once you are settled.

Health insurance is a condition of residence rather than an optional extra, and the required cover differs depending on how long your programme runs. Confirm the rule for your own permit length, because under-covering is a residence issue, not a budgeting one. After You Accept a Place

Once an offer arrives you confirm through Studyinfo and then move to the residence permit application, which is filed online before you travel. The permit requires proof that you can fund your stay and, for non-EU students, appropriate health insurance for the length of the permit.

Allow more time than feels necessary for the permit. Finnish decisions are not instant, and a January acceptance still has to clear immigration before an August start. Start the file the week the offer arrives rather than waiting for the final transcript, because supplementary documents can always be added later.

Arrival tasks are straightforward but sequential: register your address, get your Finnish identity number, open a bank account, and confirm insurance cover. The identity number unlocks most of what follows, so registering residence is the first errand after you land.

Frequently asked questions

When do Pakistani students apply to Finnish universities?+

The joint application for autumn 2027 runs on Studyinfo from 7 to 21 January 2027. There is no rolling intake for degree programmes, so the three-week window is the only one.

How much does it cost to study in Finland as a Pakistani student?+

Tuition is set by each institution. Published examples are EUR 9,500 a year for a Seinajoki UAS bachelor and EUR 11,500 for a master, and EUR 12,000 a year at Abo Akademi before discounts.

Are there scholarships for Pakistani students in Finland?+

Yes, but they are university-run rather than state awards. Finnish fee waivers are commonly tied to early commitment or second-year continuation, and applied for alongside or after admission.
