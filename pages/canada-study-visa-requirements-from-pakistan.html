Canada Study Visa Requirements from Pakistan (2026): SDS, GIC & Full Process

📅 Sep 3, 2026 ⏱ 14 min read ✏️ AbroadReady Team

Canada remains one of the most popular study destinations for Pakistani students, and for good reason. The Post-Graduation Work Permit, a realistic Express Entry path to permanent residence, high-quality universities and a large, well-established Pakistani community all make the country feel like a natural fit. But the application itself has its own logic — the SDS stream, the GIC, biometrics at VFS, a medical exam and a police clearance — and getting any of it wrong can cost you time, money and a year of your life.

This guide walks through the Canada study visa requirements from Pakistan in 2026, written specifically for students in Lahore, Karachi, Islamabad and the rest of the country. It covers the SDS route you should be aiming for, the GIC step by step, the full document list, processing times, the common refusal reasons and what happens after you land. If you've been searching for one clear resource to plan your Canada student visa from Pakistan 2026, this is it.

Key takeaways

  • The Student Direct Stream (SDS) is the fastest and most reliable route for Pakistani students, with target processing of about 20 calendar days. Pakistan is an SDS-eligible country.
  • The Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) requirement is CAD 20,635 for 2026 — roughly PKR 40 lakh at current rates, bought through CIBC, Scotiabank, ICICI, SBI Canada or RBC.
  • SDS requires IELTS 6.0 overall with no band below 6.0, proof of first-year tuition paid, an acceptance letter from a Designated Learning Institution (DLI), a medical exam and a police clearance from Pakistan.
  • Visas and fees add up: the study permit application fee is CAD 150 plus biometrics of CAD 85, and tuition at most colleges runs CAD 12,000–20,000 per year for international students.
  • Biometrics are collected at the VFS Canada centres in Islamabad, Lahore and Karachi — book early, because slots fill fast in peak season.
  • With a study permit you can work 24 hours per week during term and full-time in holidays, then use the PGWP for up to three years of post-study work.
  • Refusals for Pakistani applicants almost always come down to insufficient funds, a weak statement of purpose or a failure to convince the officer you will return to Pakistan.

Why Canada for Pakistani students

Canada's appeal for students from Pakistan rests on a few structural advantages rather than hype. First is the Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP). Finish a programme of eight months or longer at a DLI and you can get an open work permit for up to three years — no employer sponsorship, no job offer needed in advance. That open work permit is what most students base their whole plan on: study, work, then use Express Entry to apply for permanent residence.

Second, that Express Entry pathway. Canada is one of the few major destinations where studying in the country gives you a genuinely realistic route to a permanent status. Your Canadian credential, work experience and English test scores all translate directly into Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points, and provincial nomination programs add more. It is not automatic, but the door is open in a way it simply isn't in most other countries.

Third is quality of life and community. Canadian cities consistently rank among the world's most liveable, healthcare is publicly funded (though international students need private insurance until they qualify for provincial coverage), and the safety is a real draw for families back in Pakistan. On top of that, cities like Toronto, Mississauga, Vancouver and Calgary have strong Pakistani and South Asian communities — halal food, mosques, community centres and networks of people who have already gone through the exact process you're facing. Study permit vs visitor visa: what you actually need

It's worth being precise about the terminology, because the two documents are often confused. A visitor visa (TRV) lets you stay in Canada temporarily as a tourist, usually for six months or less, and does not permit study or work. A study permit is the document that lets you study at a DLI for longer than six months and — depending on the conditions printed on it — work part-time.

You need a study permit if you are studying at a DLI for more than six months, which covers virtually every degree, diploma and post-secondary certificate programme international students take. Short courses of under six months don't require a permit but are generally not what Pakistani students fly to Canada for. Note that having a study permit does not automatically give you a TRV — many students need both documents because the study permit is a status, while the visa is the document that lets you actually enter Canada at the border. In practice, when you're approved for a study permit from Pakistan you'll usually receive the permit along with a letter of introduction, and the visa/entry documents are handled as part of the same process. SDS — the Student Direct Stream for Pakistan

The Student Direct Stream (SDS) is a streamlined processing channel for study permit applications from a specific list of countries — and Pakistan is on that list. The whole point is speed and predictability: instead of the 8–15 weeks a regular application can take, IRCC targets processing in about 20 calendar days for complete SDS applications.

To use SDS from Pakistan, you must meet these conditions:

  • An acceptance letter from a Designated Learning Institution (DLI) — check your school is on the official DLI list before applying.
  • Proof that your first year's tuition fees are paid in full to the institution.
  • Proof that you have purchased a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) of CAD 20,635 from an eligible Canadian financial institution.
  • An IELTS score of 6.0 overall with no band below 6.0 in the Academic or General Training module (for 2026 this remains the standard; some four-band French tests are also accepted).
  • A medical exam completed with an IRCC-approved panel physician, relevant to Canada.
  • A police clearance certificate from Pakistan (and any other country where you've lived for six months or more since age 18).

The benefits of SDS are not just speed. Because the requirements are fixed and transparent, applications that qualify for SDS are much less likely to be refused on the grounds of weak proof of funds — the GIC and the paid tuition are already in place, which answers the "can you afford this" question before the officer even asks it. For most Pakistani students with a confirmed offer, SDS is the clear first choice.

There is an important caveat: not every course qualifies for SDS. The programme must be at a DLI and must lead to a diploma, certificate or degree. If your course doesn't qualify, or if you can't meet the IELTS threshold or the full GIC, you'll fall back to the regular route below.

The regular study permit route

If you can't use SDS — perhaps your IELTS score falls short of the 6.0 floor, your course isn't at a DLI, or you simply prefer to avoid the upfront GIC — the regular study permit route still works. It's the same application (IRCC's online portal), just without the SDS speed guarantee and without the strictly defined financial template.

On the regular route, instead of a GIC you must show that you have enough money to cover tuition plus living costs for your first year, and you need to demonstrate that your funding is genuine, stable and from a credible source. IRCC's official guidance suggests roughly CAD 20,635 minimum living costs plus tuition, but in practice the officer looks at the whole picture: your family income, bank statements covering several months, property or business evidence, and whether the amounts match your stated sponsor's finances. This is where many Pakistani applications stumble — irregular bank deposits, funds that appear all at once just before filing, or a sponsor whose declared income can't plausibly support the amounts shown.

Processing on the regular route from Pakistan currently takes roughly 8 to 15 weeks, and it can stretch longer in peak season. Refusal risk is higher than SDS because the officer has more discretion on financial credibility. If you can possibly qualify for SDS, do; if not, prepare the financial file meticulously. GIC — Guaranteed Investment Certificate, step by step

The Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) is the financial cornerstone of the SDS route, and the single biggest number you'll deal with. For 2026 the required amount is CAD 20,635. At an exchange rate around PKR 194 per CAD, that's about PKR 40 lakh (4 million rupees) — a serious figure that most families start planning for well in advance.

A GIC is essentially a fixed-term savings product with a Canadian bank. You deposit the money, the bank confirms you've done so, and that confirmation is one of the key documents in your SDS application. The money is not lost — once you reach Canada and activate your study permit, the GIC is released back to you in instalments to cover your living costs, typically about CAD 2,000 per month over the first year.

Here's how to buy a GIC from Pakistan, step by step:

  1. Choose an eligible bank. The main options for Pakistani applicants are CIBC, Scotiabank, RBC, SBI Canada and ICICI Bank. ICICI and SBI have a presence in Pakistan and are common choices because the money transfer is smoother.
  2. Open the GIC application. Each bank has a GIC-for-students page. You'll fill in your personal details and provide your passport copy, acceptance letter from the DLI and a completed application form.
  3. Receive your GIC bank account number. The bank opens an account for you and gives you the account details plus their Canadian beneficiary information so you can wire the money.
  4. Transfer the funds. You or your sponsor transfer the CAD 20,635 plus transfer fees from a Pakistani bank (via remittance through a bank or an authorised money transfer service) to the GIC account. Keep every receipt — the proof of transfer is important for your file.
  5. Receive your GIC confirmation letter / Certificate of Investment. Once the money clears, the Canadian bank sends you a GIC confirmation document. This is what you upload with your SDS application.
  6. Keep the details safe. When you land in Canada and meet the bank's office, you'll set up the instalment plan to receive your living-cost payments.

Transfers from Pakistan for this kind of amount go through the formal banking channel and can take a few working days, so don't leave it to the last minute. Documents needed for a Canada study permit

Whether you use SDS or the regular route, your file needs a consistent set of documents. Missing a single item is one of the most common reasons applications are delayed or returned, so build your checklist early. Here's the complete list:

  • Letter of acceptance from a Designated Learning Institution (DLI) — confirms your place and your programme.
  • GIC certificate (SDS route) — the CAD 20,635 investment confirmation.
  • Proof of first-year tuition paid — receipt or letter from the DLI confirming the payment.
  • IELTS (or accepted English test) results — for SDS, 6.0 overall with no band below 6.0.
  • Valid passport with at least one blank page and validity well beyond your intended stay.
  • Passport-sized photographs meeting IRCC photo specifications (white background, recent).
  • Medical exam with an IRCC-approved panel physician — the result is uploaded by the doctor, and you must complete it before applying.
  • Police clearance certificate from Pakistan — obtained from the relevant authorities (often via NADRA or the local police), failing which you may be asked to provide one.
  • Statement of Purpose (SOP) — a persuasive, honest letter explaining why Canada, why this programme, and crucially, what you plan to do after.
  • Financial supporting documents (regular route) — bank statements, income proof, property papers, sponsor's documents.

Your statement of purpose deserves real thought. Officers read hundreds of these, and a generic "I want a world-class education" opening does almost nothing. Instead, tie the programme to your specific background, previous studies and a concrete plan for after graduation — whether that's returning to Pakistan to use the qualification or (honestly) building a career in Canada through the PGWP and Express Entry. Consistency between your SOP, your academic history and your finances is what convinces an officer your application is genuine.

Biometrics and the VFS appointment in Pakistan

Biometrics — your fingerprints and a digital photograph — are part of every Canada study permit application from Pakistan. You book your appointment through the VFS Global Canada application centre, and centres operate in Islamabad, Lahore and Karachi. For students in cities like Multan, Faisalabad or Peshawar, Lahore or Islamabad are usually the most practical options.

Here's the sequence:

  1. Pay your biometrics fee of CAD 85 (part of the total fees) when you submit your online application.
  2. Book a biometrics appointment on the VFS Global Canada website, choosing your nearest centre in Islamabad, Lahore or Karachi.
  3. Attend with your valid passport, the biometrics instruction letter from IRCC, and the appointment confirmation.
  4. Have your fingerprints and photo captured. This usually takes under 30 minutes.
  5. Decide whether to collect your passport/travel document in person later or pay for courier delivery after the decision.

Slots at the VFS centres are limited, and in peak application season (roughly April to August, ahead of September intakes) they fill up quickly. Book the moment your application is in — don't wait for a "convenient" date. Late biometrics is one of the most avoidable sources of delay.

Processing time: SDS vs regular

How long a Canada study permit from Pakistan takes depends heavily on which route you use and the current workload at IRCC.

  • SDS route: target of about 20 calendar days from the date IRCC receives a complete application. Some applications come back faster, some slower, but the 20-day target is the reason SDS exists.
  • Regular route: roughly 8 to 15 weeks from Pakistan as of 2026, and it can climb higher during peaks. IRCC publishes its official estimates online each month, so check the current figure when you apply.

The practical advice is the same for both: apply at least 3 to 4 months before your programme start date. The 20-day SDS window is a target, not a guarantee, and anything missing from your file resets the clock. If different Indian/Pakistani case types get held up for additional review, there's nothing you can do except wait — which is exactly why a comfortable buffer matters. A September intake means having your IELTS, GIC, tuition, medical and police clearance wrapped up by roughly April or May.

Interview preparation

Not every Canada study permit applicant is interviewed, but IRCC can ask for one, and for some applicants — often those on the regular route or cases that raise questions — an interview with a visa officer can be part of the assessment. The good news is that the questions are predictable, and preparation fixes most of the risk.

Common questions and how to answer them:

  • "Why Canada and not somewhere else?" Answer with specifics about the programme, the university or college, the city and the reputation of Canadian credentials — not generic glamour. Mentioning the PGWP and path to permanent residence is honest and expected.
  • "Why this particular programme?" Connect the programme to what you studied before and what job you're aiming at. A clear, logical through-line from your background to this course is what officers look for.
  • "How will you fund your studies?" Know your numbers cold: the GIC amount (CAD 20,635), the tuition, your living costs, and where every rupee is coming from. Confusion here is a red flag.
  • "What will you do after graduation?" Have a coherent answer — whether that's working in Canada on the PGWP and pursuing Express Entry, or returning to Pakistan to apply the qualification. Being vague or evasive looks like weak intent.
  • "Why did you choose this DLI over others?" A specific answer about the institution's reputation, a particular professor or lab, or the programme structure shows genuine research rather than a shortcut application.

Stay calm, answer directly and don't volunteer information that wasn't asked. Officers are trained to spot rehearsed, contradicting or implausible answers, so the best preparation is simply knowing your own application inside out. Cost breakdown — what it really costs in PKR

Let's put realistic numbers on the table. Using an exchange rate of about PKR 194 per CAD:

  • GIC: CAD 20,635 ≈ PKR 40 lakh (released back to you in instalments once you arrive).
  • Tuition: CAD 12,000–20,000 per year at most colleges, CAD 20,000–45,000+ at universities for degrees like engineering, business or medicine. In PKR, that's roughly PKR 23–87 lakh per year.
  • Study permit fee: CAD 150 ≈ PKR 29,000.
  • Biometrics fee: CAD 85 ≈ PKR 16,500.
  • Medical exam: about CAD 150–225 ≈ PKR 29,000–44,000 with an approved panel physician in Pakistan.
  • Police clearance: a small fee, usually under PKR 1,000–3,000, depending on the issuing authority.
  • Living costs (first year): roughly CAD 12,000–15,000 ≈ PKR 23–29 lakh for rent, food, transport and incidentals, though the GIC covers much of this.

Add it up for a year at a college: CAD 20,635 GIC + CAD 15,000 tuition + CAD 150 + CAD 85 + medical ≈ CAD 36,000, which is about PKR 70 lakh as an upfront figure — remembering that most of the GIC comes back to you in instalments. That GIC is not spent; it's your living-expense money travelling with you. The cheapest colleges for Pakistani students

If budget is your main constraint, your starting point should be colleges rather than universities, and provinces with lower cost of living. Public colleges in Ontario, British Columbia and Alberta offer strong diploma and degree programmes at a fraction of university tuition, and their graduates are in demand for the PGWP-to-Express Entry path.

Some of the more affordable options Pakistani students commonly target include Seneca Polytechnic, Durham College, Conestoga College and Georgian College in Ontario, Douglas College and KPU (Kwantlen) in British Columbia, and Bow Valley College in Alberta. Diplomas and advanced diplomas from these colleges typically run CAD 12,000–16,000 per year. It's worth noting that the cheapest colleges in Canada for Pakistani students are usually outside the big urban centres, where both tuition and rent are lower — but always confirm your chosen institution is a DLI, because only DLIs qualify their students for a study permit and a PGWP.

Common refusal reasons for Pakistani students

A refusal is demoralising, but it is rarely random. For Pakistani applicants, refusals cluster around a handful of predictable causes, and understanding them is your best defence.

  • Insufficient or unconvincing proof of funds. This is the number one reason. On the regular route, bank statements that show sudden large deposits, an unexplained source, or amounts that don't match your sponsor's declared income are common triggers. On SDS, a missing or incomplete GIC or unpaid tuition is an automatic problem.
  • A weak or generic statement of purpose. A vague SOP that reads like it was copied from a template does not convince anyone you're a genuine student with a plan. The SOP is your chance to connect your past, your chosen programme and your future — missing that connection is a classic refusal cause.
  • Not convincing the officer of your intent to return to Pakistan. Canadian officers assess "ties" to your home country. If your application shows no clear reason to return — no family, no property, no job prospects or course of study that connects back to Pakistan — the officer may conclude you intend to overstay. This is why the SOP and your supporting evidence of family and career links matter so much.
  • Missing or inconsistent documents. A name spelled differently across documents, an expiring passport, unverified bank letters, or a medical that wasn't completed correctly all create delays or refusals.
  • Choosing a DLI or course inconsistent with your profile. A computer science graduate applying for an unrelated diploma with no explanation can raise genuine-student doubts.

The officer's test is really one question: are you a genuine student who will follow the rules and leave when your permit expires? Everything in your file either answers that question well or poorly. After you arrive in Canada

Getting approved is the hard part, but landing is where the real life starts. Here's what to do in your first weeks:

  • Get a Social Insurance Number (SIN). You can apply at a Service Canada office (or online in many cases) once you have your study permit. You need the SIN to work and to be paid legally under your Canadian identity.
  • Activate your GIC. Visit your GIC bank's office in Canada with your passport, study permit and the letter from the bank's international office to set up your monthly instalment payments.
  • Sort out your work rights. With a study permit you can work 24 hours per week during regular sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks. Full-time enrolment is required to keep your status. Our part-time work guide covers finding a job and the rules that apply.
  • Arrange health insurance. International students are usually not covered by provincial health plans for their first months, so buy private insurance (many DLIs offer a plan) until you qualify for provincial coverage, which varies by province.
  • Open a Canadian bank account. Take your passport, study permit and SIN to a bank (often discounted or free student accounts) and set up a local account for your GIC instalments, part-time earnings and everyday spending.
  • Register for classes and find accommodation. Whether on-campus residence or a rented apartment, sort this early — housing near colleges fills fast in September.

And look ahead to graduation. The PGWP gives you up to three years of open work time, which pairs directly with the Express Entry system for permanent residence. Different provincial nominee programs reward particular skills, so research the one where you study. The bottom line

To meet the Canada study visa requirements from Pakistan in 2026, the winning formula is: get a confirmed offer from a DLI, hit IELTS 6.0 with no band below 6.0, put up the CAD 20,635 GIC, pay your first year's tuition, finish the medical and police clearance, and file a clean, complete SDS application through IRCC with biometrics at VFS. Do that and you're looking at a decision in about 20 days instead of months. Budget roughly PKR 70 lakh as an upfront commitment (most of the GIC returns to you in instalments), apply 3–4 months ahead, and treat your statement of purpose as the single most persuasive document in your file. Canada's PGWP and Express Entry make it one of the strongest study-to-pr-come-to-Canada pathways available — and with a solid application, that path is genuinely open to you.

Frequently asked questions

What is the SDS route and do Pakistani students qualify?+

SDS (Student Direct Stream) is a faster Canada study permit processing stream. To qualify you need an acceptance letter from a Designated Learning Institution (DLI), a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) of CAD 20,635, proof of first-year tuition paid, an IELTS score of 6.0 overall with no band below 6.0 (academic or general training), a medical exam, and a police clearance certificate from Pakistan. As of 2026 Pakistan remains an SDS-eligible country, so Pakistani students can use the SDS route to get decisions in about 20 calendar days.

How much is the GIC for Canada from Pakistan?+

The Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) required for the SDS route is CAD 20,635 for 2026. At an exchange rate of roughly PKR 194 per CAD, that works out to about PKR 40 lakh (4 million rupees). You pay this amount into a GIC account through an approved Canadian bank or partner such as CIBC, Scotiabank, ICICI Bank, SBI Canada or RBC. The bank transfers the money to Canada, and you receive living-cost instalments once you arrive.

How long does the Canada study visa take from Pakistan?+

On the SDS route, Canada aims to process study permit applications in about 20 calendar days from the date IRCC receives a complete application. Regular (non-SDS) study permit applications from Pakistan currently take roughly 8 to 15 weeks, and processing times vary month to month. To keep things on track, apply 3 to 4 months before your course start date, book biometrics early, and make sure every document is complete.

Can I work while studying in Canada on a study permit?+

Yes. If your study permit includes a condition allowing work, you can work up to 24 hours per week during regular academic sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks such as the winter and summer holidays. Full-time study is required to keep your status. After you graduate, you can apply for a Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP), which lets you work in Canada for up to three years depending on the length of your programme.

What IELTS score do I need for a Canada study visa?+

For the SDS route you need an IELTS score of 6.0 overall with no band below 6.0 (Academic or General Training). If you apply through the regular route instead of SDS, your university will set its own English language requirement, which is often IELTS 6.0 to 6.5 overall. Individual programmes and universities may set higher minimums, so check your DLI's exact requirement before you book the exam.
