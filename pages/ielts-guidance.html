What is IELTS and Who Needs It?

The International English Language Testing System (IELTS) is the world's most widely accepted English proficiency test, trusted by more than 11,500 organisations in over 140 countries — including almost every university, immigration authority and professional body in the English-speaking world.

You typically need an IELTS result when you want to:

  • Study abroad — most English-taught degree programmes in the USA, UK, Canada, Australia, Europe and beyond ask for a minimum band score.
  • Apply for a work or skilled-worker visa — the UK, Australia, New Zealand and Canada assess English for work routes and points.
  • Immigrate / get permanent residence — Canada's Express Entry, Australia's skilled migration and New Zealand's Skilled Migrant Category award points for your IELTS bands.
  • Register with professional bodies — nursing, medicine, engineering, accounting and teaching councils often demand a specific band.

Your result is scored from band 1 (non-user) to band 9 (expert user), and is valid for 2 years. The score you need depends entirely on the country, the visa type and the institution — that's exactly what this guide breaks down.

The Four IELTS Modules

Every IELTS test (Academic and General Training) has four modules. Your overall band score is the average of all four, rounded to the nearest half band.

🎧 Listening

30 minutes + 10 to transfer answers · 40 questions · band 1–9

Four recorded sections with increasingly academic content — a social conversation, a monologue, a training discussion and a lecture. One mark per question, no negative marking. The accent can be British, American, Australian or Canadian, so train your ear across all of them.

📖 Reading

60 minutes · 40 questions · band 1–9

Academic: three long texts from journals and books with tasks like matching headings and true/false/not-given. General Training: five shorter everyday texts (adverts, notices, workplace documents). Time management is the real test — practise finishing on schedule.

✍️ Writing

60 minutes · 2 tasks · band 1–9

Task 1 — Academic: describe a chart, graph or diagram (150 words). General Training: write a letter (150 words). Task 2 — both: an argumentative essay (250 words). You are marked on task response, coherence, vocabulary and grammar. Leave 40 minutes for Task 2 — it carries two-thirds of the marks.

🗣️ Speaking

11–14 minutes · face to face with an examiner · 3 parts

Part 1: questions about yourself. Part 2: a 2-minute talk on a cue card. Part 3: a follow-up discussion on abstract ideas. Same format for Academic and General Training. The examiner assesses fluency, coherence, pronunciation and range of language — speaking naturally matters more than perfect grammar.

Results, Validity & Retakes

  • Results: computer-delivered in 3–5 days, paper-based in 13 days.
  • Validity: your Test Report Form is valid for 2 years for visas and university applications.
  • Retakes: with IELTS One Skill Retake you can resit a single module within 60 days of your original test and keep your other scores. One retake per completed test.
  • No pass/fail: you receive bands from 1 to 9; the "required" score depends on your target university or visa route.
  • Not happy with your centre? You can ask for a formal review (enquiry on results) within six weeks, for a fee.

IELTS FAQs

What IELTS score do I need for a student visa?+

Most universities and student visa routes require IELTS 6.0 to 6.5 overall. The UK Student route accepts 5.5 in each band (B2), Australia requires 6.0 overall for a student visa, and Canadian SDS applicants need 6.0 overall with no band below 5.5. Always confirm with the university and the official immigration authority.

Is IELTS required for permanent residence (PR)?+

In points-based systems — Canada's Express Entry, Australia's skilled migration and New Zealand's Skilled Migrant Category — yes, and your score earns points. Higher bands mean more points: Canada maps IELTS to CLB (6.0 each band = CLB 7), and CLB 9 gives maximum points. Most European PR routes do not ask for IELTS.

What is the difference between IELTS Academic and General Training?+

Academic is for university study and professional registration; General Training is for work, immigration and PR. Listening and Speaking are identical; Reading and Writing differ. Academic uses academic texts and a data-description writing task; General Training uses everyday texts and a letter-writing task.

How much does the IELTS test cost?+

Typical ranges: Pakistan PKR 53,000–62,000, India INR 17,000–18,500, UK £215–235, USA US$235–270, Canada CAD 319–340, Australia AUD 485–525. Computer tests sometimes cost a little more. Confirm the exact fee at your chosen centre at booking.

How long is an IELTS score valid?+

Two years from the test date for universities and visa authorities. Some employers accept older scores, but visa applications almost always require a result issued within the last two years.

What are the four IELTS modules?+

Listening (30 min, 40 questions), Reading (60 min, 40 questions), Writing (60 min, 2 tasks) and Speaking (11–14 min, face to face). Each is scored 1–9 and your overall band is the average, rounded to the nearest half band.
