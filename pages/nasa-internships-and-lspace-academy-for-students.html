NASA Internships and L'SPACE Academy: Eligibility, Facts, and Alternatives

🗓️ Aug 5, 2026⏱️ 9 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

NASA internships are the dream of many STEM students, and NASA's flagship student internship programme offers some of the most sought-after placements in the world. But there is an uncomfortable fact that most articles skip: NASA internships are generally limited to US citizens, and the popular L'SPACE Academy also requires US citizenship or permanent residency. If you are an international student, knowing the real rules before you apply will save you time and point you toward the routes that are actually open to you.

This guide explains how NASA internships work, what L'SPACE Academy is, and what international students should do instead if they want to break into the space industry.

Key takeaways

  • NASA's central internship programme is open to US citizens, with international access only through rare formal arrangements.
  • L'SPACE Academy is a free, online training programme for US citizens and permanent residents at US colleges and universities.
  • L'SPACE is not an internship; it is project-based training that strengthens applications for real internships.
  • International students can build space careers through national agencies, IAESTE and funded research programmes.
  • Always check citizenship requirements on every posting before investing time in an application.

How NASA internships actually work

NASA's student internship programme, run through the STEM Gateway portal at stemgateway.nasa.gov, places students at NASA centers across the United States, from Goddard to JPL. Interns work with NASA mentors on real projects, are paid, and can return for multiple terms. Applications open three times a year for fall, spring and summer sessions, and summer is by far the most competitive.

The eligibility rule is the part that matters most for international students. NASA internships require US citizenship for almost all positions. A small number of arrangements exist for international students at certain centers or through formal partnerships, but they are rare and usually channelled through universities or national agencies rather than open applications.

What is L'SPACE Academy?

L'SPACE, the NASA Lucy Student Pipeline Accelerator and Competency Enabler, is a free, fully online programme run by Arizona State University as the official student collaboration program for NASA's Lucy mission. It was created to train the next generation of the space workforce by teaching students how NASA actually plans, designs and reviews missions.

Eligibility is strict: you must be a US citizen or permanent resident, enrolled in a US college or university as an undergraduate or graduate student. International students studying in the US on a student visa are not eligible.

The two academies

L'SPACE has two academies. The Mission Concept Academy (MCA) takes undergraduates through a full mission design cycle, from requirements to a preliminary design review. The NASA Proposal Writing and Evaluation Experience (NPWEE) teaches how real NASA proposals are written and evaluated. Students typically complete one academy per semester, and each runs for about twelve to fifteen weeks with a significant time commitment.

Why L'SPACE matters even though it is unpaid

L'SPACE is not an internship and pays no stipend, but alumni consistently report that it changed their career trajectory. You learn mission procedures from people who have worked on real missions, you collaborate with students from across the US, and you finish with portfolio-ready deliverables such as mission concept documents and proposal reviews. Many alumni go on to internships at NASA, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman and other space employers.

For students who are eligible, L'SPACE is one of the highest-value free investments you can make in a space career. For international students, the programme itself is closed, but the skills it teaches are not, and you can learn the same fundamentals through free NASA resources, open coursework and project work.

What international students should do instead

If you cannot access NASA directly, you still have several genuine routes into space science and engineering.

Use your own national space ecosystem

Most space-faring countries have an agency or institute that runs student internships: ESA in Europe, JAXA in Japan, the Canadian Space Agency, and national agencies across Africa, Asia and Latin America. Many of these hire international students or students from partner countries, and some fund the placement.

Use funded international programmes

Programmes like IAESTE and DAAD RISE Germany place STEM undergraduates in research groups and companies abroad, and space-related placements appear in their offer lists. These are genuinely open to international students and are often fully or partly funded.

Build a space profile online

NASA publishes enormous amounts of free material, from open data and citizen science projects to course-like resources and virtual challenges. Working on an open-source space project, entering a space hackathon, or analysing NASA open data gives you similar portfolio material to L'SPACE alumni, without the citizenship requirement.

How to apply: next steps

If you are a US citizen or permanent resident, create a profile on stemgateway.nasa.gov for NASA internships and apply through lspace.asu.edu for L'SPACE. If you are an international student, skip the NASA portal and instead register with your national space agency, check IAESTE and DAAD RISE offers, and start a portfolio project that demonstrates your skills.

Frequently asked questions

Are NASA internships open to international students?+

Almost always no. NASA's central internship programme requires US citizenship, and international access exists only through rare formal arrangements.

What is the L'SPACE Academy?+

L'SPACE is a free, online training programme run by Arizona State University for the NASA Lucy mission, with two academies, MCA and NPWEE. It is not an internship.

Who can apply to L'SPACE Academy?+

US citizens and permanent residents enrolled at US colleges or universities as undergraduate or graduate students; international students on student visas are not eligible.

Is L'SPACE paid?+

No. L'SPACE is unpaid training, but alumni use the project work to strengthen applications for NASA and space-industry internships.

What should international students apply to instead?+

Options include their national space agency, IAESTE, DAAD RISE Germany and open-source space projects, all of which are open to international students.
