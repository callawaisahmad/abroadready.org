UK Student Visa from Pakistan 2026: Documents, Fees & Timeline

🗓️ Aug 31, 2026⏱️ 28 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Every year, thousands of Pakistani students begin their journey to study at a UK university. The United Kingdom remains one of the most popular destinations for Pakistani learners, thanks to world-ranked institutions, one-year master's degrees, the post-study Graduate Route, and deep academic ties between the two countries. But before you can pack your bags, you need a visa — and the UK Student visa (the route that replaced the old Tier 4 General student visa) has specific requirements that trip up even well-qualified applicants. In 2026, those requirements are stricter than ever: the application fee rose on 8 April 2026, maintenance fund thresholds increased significantly, and the credibility assessment is tougher for Pakistani nationals. This guide covers everything you need to know, with 2026 figures, practical tips, and the kind of detail that prevents avoidable refusals.

Key takeaways

  • The UK Student visa application fee is £558 (increased from £524 on 8 April 2026), plus the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) of £776 per year of your visa, paid upfront.
  • You need a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) from a licensed UK university before you can apply.
  • You must show first-year tuition fees (as on your CAS, minus any deposit paid) plus living costs of £1,529 per month (London) or £1,171 per month (outside London) for up to 9 months.
  • Funds must sit in your account (or a parent's account) for 28 consecutive days ending within 31 days of your application date.
  • A tuberculosis (TB) test from a UKVI-approved clinic is mandatory for all Pakistani applicants.
  • Apply up to 6 months before your course starts; standard processing takes about 3 weeks from biometrics.
  • Pakistani applications face extra scrutiny — ensure every document is genuine, every detail matches your CAS, and your personal statement demonstrates genuine study intent.

What is the UK Student visa?

The Student visa is the UK's immigration route for international students aged 16 and above who want to study a full-time course at a licensed UK education provider. It replaced the former Tier 4 (General) student visa in October 2020, though the underlying rules remain very similar. If you see references to "Tier 4" in older guides, know that they refer to the same route.

The Student visa falls under the UK's points-based immigration system. You earn points for having a CAS, meeting the financial requirement, demonstrating English language ability, and satisfying the genuine student test. If you score enough points, your visa is granted. In practice, this means you need four things: an unconditional offer confirmed by a CAS from a licensed sponsor, enough money to pay your fees and support yourself, proof of English language proficiency, and a convincing case that you are a genuine student who intends to study and then return home.

The visa is valid for the duration of your course, plus a short additional period before and after (typically a few weeks on each end). For most Pakistani students, this means the visa covers your full study period — one year for a master's, three years for a bachelor's, and three to four years for a PhD.

Who needs a Student visa?

You need a Student visa if you are a Pakistani national (or a national of any other country that does not have right of abode in the UK) and you want to study:

  • A full-time course at Regulated Qualifications Framework (RQF) Level 4 or above — this includes foundation degrees, bachelor's degrees, master's degrees, and PhDs.
  • A course lasting more than 6 months. Short courses of 6 months or less may be studied on a Standard Visitor visa instead, though the work rights and flexibility are far more limited on a visitor visa.
  • A part-time course at RQF Level 7 or above, if you are a senior professional or academic.

You must be 16 years of age or older when you apply. If you are 16 or 17, you will need written consent from both parents (or the person with parental responsibility) confirming that they consent to your travel, your living arrangements in the UK, and the visa application itself. You will need to provide your birth certificate as evidence of the parent-child relationship.

If you are under 16 and want to study at an independent school in the UK, you would apply for a Child Student visa instead. This guide focuses on the standard Student visa route for adult learners.

The Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)

The CAS is the single most important document in your application. It is an electronic record — not a physical letter — that your UK university assigns to you on the UKVI system once you have accepted an unconditional offer and met all conditions, including paying any required deposit. The university then sends you a CAS statement containing your unique CAS reference number (a 14-character code), along with details of your course, tuition fees, and personal information.

What your CAS confirms

Your CAS statement will contain:

  • Your full name, date of birth, and passport number (these must match your application exactly)
  • The course title, start date, and end date
  • The total tuition fee for the first academic year and any amount already paid
  • The name and sponsor licence number of your university
  • Which documents the university used to assess your application (your qualifications, English test scores, etc.)
  • Confirmation that you have the right to study in the UK

How to get your CAS

  1. Apply to UK universities and receive an unconditional offer. If you receive a conditional offer, meet the conditions (submit final transcripts, English test scores, deposit payment).
  2. Accept the offer and pay the required deposit (usually £1,000–£5,000, depending on the university).
  3. Request your CAS. Most universities issue a CAS within 1–4 weeks of receiving your acceptance and deposit. Some require you to complete a pre-CAS questionnaire.
  4. Check every detail. Your name spelling, passport number, course title, start date, end date, and tuition fees on the CAS must match your application precisely. Even a single-letter discrepancy can trigger a refusal.

Your CAS is valid for 6 months from the date it is assigned. You must submit your visa application within this window. You can only use a CAS once — if your application is refused, you need a new CAS from the university to reapply.

Universities charge a small administrative fee for issuing a CAS, typically around £25. This is separate from your tuition deposit.

A crucial point for Pakistani students: your CAS also indicates whether the university assessed your English language ability itself (in which case you may not need a separate SELT) or whether you need to provide an approved English test result. Check this carefully — it affects what documents you need to prepare.

On 8 April 2026, the UK Home Office increased the Student visa application fee from £524 to £558. This applies to all applications submitted from that date onwards, regardless of when the decision is made. The fee is the same whether you are applying from outside the UK, extending from inside the UK, or switching from another visa category.

Dependant fees

If you are bringing a spouse, civil partner, or dependent child, each person must pay their own visa application fee (£558 each) and their own IHS at the same student rate (£776 per year). Only students studying at PhD level or certain postgraduate research programmes can typically bring dependants — see the dependants section below.

English language requirement

To obtain a Student visa, you must demonstrate English language ability at CEFR Level B2 (approximately IELTS 5.5–6.5 depending on the skill) for degree-level study. Your university specifies the exact level it requires on your CAS.

How to prove your English

There are several ways to satisfy the requirement:

  • Secure English Language Test (SELT): Take an approved test such as IELTS for UKVI (Academic), PTE Academic UKVI, TOEFL iBT (home edition not accepted for visa purposes), or Trinity College London ISE II. The test must be taken at a UKVI-approved test centre. Most Pakistani universities require an overall score of 6.0–6.5 with no individual band below 5.5–6.0.
  • University-assessed English: Your university can assess your English ability itself — through a recognised test result, a Medium of Instruction (MOI) letter from your previous institution, or its own internal assessment — and confirm this on your CAS. If your CAS confirms that the university assessed your English, you do not need a separate SELT for the visa application.
  • Degree taught in English: If you hold a bachelor's degree or higher that was taught entirely in English, this may satisfy the requirement. This is more commonly accepted at master's level and above.
  • Nationality exemption: Citizens of majority English-speaking countries (e.g., USA, Canada, Australia, Ireland) are exempt. Pakistani nationals are not exempt.

Which test should Pakistani students take?

IELTS for UKVI (Academic) is the most widely accepted and the safest option. It is offered by both the British Council and IDP Pakistan at centres across the country. PTE Academic UKVI is a growing alternative — it is computer-delivered and results are typically available within 2–5 days. TOEFL iBT is accepted by many universities but confirm that the home edition is not valid for UKVI purposes — you must take it at an official test centre.

Book your test early. Slots in Islamabad, Lahore, and Karachi fill up quickly, especially in the months leading up to the September intake (May–July). Budget approximately PKR 50,000–80,000 for the test fee, depending on the provider.

Financial requirement — the most critical section

Financial evidence is the number one reason for UK student visa refusals from Pakistan, accounting for roughly 40% of all rejections. The rules are strict, specific, and unforgiving. You must prove you can cover both your tuition fees and your living costs, and you must do so in a very specific way.

How much money do you need to show?

You must demonstrate that you have access to:

  • First-year tuition fees: The exact amount stated on your CAS, minus any deposit you have already paid and which is recorded on the CAS.
  • Living costs (maintenance funds): Calculated based on where you will study:
Study LocationMonthly RateMaximum (9 months)
London (City of London + 32 boroughs)£1,529/month£13,761
Outside London (rest of England, Wales, Scotland, NI)£1,171/month£10,539

These rates were updated in January 2025 and remain in force through 2026. They represent a significant increase from the previous rates (£1,334/£1,023 per month). Older guides may quote the old figures — always verify on gov.uk/student-visa/money.

Worked example

Suppose you have been accepted for a 1-year MSc at a university in Manchester (outside London). Your CAS states a tuition fee of £16,000, and you have already paid a £3,000 deposit. Your financial requirement is:

  • Outstanding tuition: £16,000 − £3,000 = £13,000
  • Maintenance funds: £1,171 × 9 months = £10,539
  • Total required: £23,539

You must show at least £23,539 in your bank account (or a combination of your own and a sponsor's account). UKVI converts your funds to GBP using the OANDA exchange rate on the date of your application, not the date of your bank statement. If the Pakistani rupee weakens between your statement date and your application date, your balance might fall short. Add a 5–10% buffer above the minimum to protect against currency fluctuation.

The 28-day rule

This is the rule that catches the most applicants. Your funds must have been held in an eligible account for 28 consecutive days, with the last day of the 28-day period falling within 31 days of the date you submit your visa application.

In practice, this means:

  • Your bank statement must cover at least 28 days.
  • On every single day of those 28 days, the balance must be at or above the required amount. Not just the opening and closing balance — every day.
  • If the balance dips below the required amount on even one day — even briefly, even due to a fee deduction — the clock resets. You must start the 28-day period again.
  • The closing date of the 28-day period on your statement must be no more than 31 days before you submit your application.

Common financial evidence mistakes

  • Funds parking: Transferring a large sum into your account just weeks before the 28-day window looks suspicious. UKVI treats sudden large deposits as borrowed money. Move funds into the account 45–60 days before the 28-day window starts, so your statement shows a month of stable balance before the required period begins.
  • Wrong account type: Funds must be in a cash account — a savings account, current account, or accessible fixed deposit at a regulated financial institution. Shares, bonds, pensions, mutual funds, Sahulat accounts, National Savings certificates, cryptocurrency, and credit card limits do not count.
  • Unstamped statements: Printouts from online banking without an official bank stamp and letterhead are routinely refused. Get your statements directly from your bank branch, on bank letterhead, signed and stamped.
  • Multiple accounts: Splitting funds across different accounts creates complexity and increases the risk of errors. The cleanest approach is a single account with the full required amount.
  • No source-of-funds evidence: If a large deposit appears in your account, UKVI may question where it came from. Be prepared to show salary slips, business income records, property sale documents, or other evidence explaining the source.

Using a parent's or sponsor's bank account

If the funds are in your parent's or legal guardian's account rather than your own, you can still use them — but you must provide:

  • A signed consent letter from your parent/guardian confirming they are funding your studies and consent to the visa application.
  • A birth certificate or NADRA Family Registration Certificate (FRC) proving your relationship to the sponsor.
  • The sponsor's CNIC copy (Computerised National Identity Card).
  • The sponsor's 28-day bank statement meeting all the same requirements above.
  • Evidence of the sponsor's income or wealth — salary slips, business registration, property documents, tax returns — to demonstrate that the funds are genuine and not borrowed.

The bank statement must be in the sponsor's name, and the consent letter must explicitly reference your name, your passport number, the university name, and the course. A generic letter without these specifics is not enough.

You can also use a partner's account (spouse or civil partner) if they are applying with you or already hold valid UK permission, or an official sponsor's funds (e.g., a government scholarship, HEC, British Council, or university scholarship). Student loan letters from government or government-sponsored loan companies are also accepted.

Official sponsorship and scholarships

If your studies are fully funded by a recognised scholarship body — such as the HEC Commonwealth Scholarship, Chevening, or a university scholarship — the scholarship letter must confirm the amount of tuition and living costs covered, the duration, and the name of the sponsoring body. If the scholarship covers all your costs, you may not need to provide personal bank statements. However, UKVI may still request financial evidence after submission, so keep your documents ready regardless.

Exemptions from financial evidence

You do not need to provide financial evidence if:

  • You have been in the UK on a valid visa for at least 12 months on the date of your application (in-country applications only).
  • You are a Student Union Sabbatical Officer.
  • You are a doctor or dentist in postgraduate training.

As a Pakistani applicant applying from outside the UK, you almost certainly do need to provide financial evidence. There is no exemption for Pakistani nationals.

Age requirement and parental consent

You must be 16 or over when you apply. If you are 16 or 17 years old, you need:

  • Written consent from both parents (or the person with sole or joint parental responsibility).
  • The consent letter must confirm that both parents consent to the visa application, the travel to the UK, and the living arrangements in the UK.
  • A copy of your birth certificate showing the names of both parents.

If you are 18 or over, parental consent is not required, regardless of whether your parents are funding your studies.

Complete document checklist for 2026

This is the full list of documents you should prepare. Not every document will apply to every applicant, but this covers all common scenarios for Pakistani students:

  1. Valid Pakistani passport — must be valid for the full duration of your intended stay, with at least one blank page for the visa stamp. Include all previous/old passports showing your travel history.
  2. CAS reference number — from your university. You will enter this in your online application; you do not need to print the CAS statement, but keep a copy for your records.
  3. Bank statements — meeting the 28-day rule, on bank letterhead, stamped and signed, showing the required amount. Or a loan letter / scholarship letter / official sponsorship letter as applicable.
  4. Academic documents — all qualifications listed on your CAS: degree certificates, transcripts, HSSC / A-Level certificates, SSC / O-Level certificates. Originals or certified copies.
  5. English language test result — IELTS UKVI, PTE Academic UKVI, or equivalent. Only if required (check your CAS — if the university assessed your English, this may not be needed separately).
  6. Tuberculosis (TB) test certificate — from a UKVI-approved clinic in Pakistan. Mandatory for all Pakistani applicants. Valid for 6 months from the date of the test.
  7. ATAS certificate — only if your course requires it. The Academic Technology Approval Scheme certificate is needed for certain postgraduate courses in science, engineering, and technology. Your university will tell you if you need one. It must be obtained before you apply for the visa.
  8. Passport-size photographs — meeting UK biometric photo requirements (white background, specific dimensions). Some VFS centres take your photo at the appointment.
  9. Parental consent and birth certificate — if you are under 18.
  10. Sponsor's documents — if a parent is funding you: their CNIC, bank statements (28-day compliant), consent letter, birth certificate / FRC, and income evidence (salary slips, business documents, tax returns).
  11. Personal statement / Statement of Purpose — explaining why you chose this course, this university, and how it connects to your career plans. While not always a formal visa document requirement, it is critical for the genuine student assessment and may be requested.
  12. CV / resume — particularly useful for postgraduate applicants to show academic and work history.
  13. Work experience letters — if relevant to your course (e.g., MBA, professional master's).
  14. Family Registration Certificate (FRC) — from NADRA, if your family is sponsoring you. Useful for demonstrating family ties to Pakistan.
  15. Scholarship letter — if applicable, from the scholarship body.
  16. Certified English translations — for any document not in English. The translation must include the translator's name, credentials, contact details, date, and a declaration of accuracy.

The genuine student requirement

Under paragraph ST 5.1 of Appendix Student, the UKVI caseworker must be satisfied that you are a genuine student. This is not just a formality — it is an active assessment. The caseworker evaluates:

  • Whether your course choice makes sense given your academic background and career goals.
  • Whether your personal statement demonstrates genuine motivation for studying this course at this university.
  • Whether you can explain your study plan in a credibility interview (if called).
  • Whether your financial circumstances are consistent with the study plan.
  • Whether you have ties to Pakistan that suggest you intend to return after your studies.

This is where many well-qualified Pakistani applicants fail. A computer science graduate applying for a master's in data science has a clear, logical path. A business graduate applying for a nursing course with no explanation of the pivot raises questions. Your personal statement is the key document here — see the tips section below.

How to apply online — step by step

The entire Student visa application is completed online. Here is the exact process for Pakistani applicants in 2026:

Step 1: Gather your documents

Before starting the application, have all your documents ready: CAS number, passport details, bank statements, TB test certificate, English test score, academic certificates, and sponsor documents if applicable. You will need to reference these during the application.

Step 2: Complete the online application on GOV.UK

Go to gov.uk/student-visa/apply and select "Apply from outside the UK." You will create a UKVI account (if you don't already have one) and fill in the application form. The form asks for:

  • Your personal details (name, date of birth, passport number — must match your CAS exactly)
  • Your CAS reference number
  • Your course details
  • Financial information (how you will fund your studies)
  • Travel history
  • Criminal record declarations
  • Whether you have been refused a visa before (from any country)

You can save and return to the application later, but your application date is the date you submit and pay — not the date you start filling it in.

Step 3: Pay the visa application fee

The £558 application fee is paid online by debit or credit card as part of the application process. The fee is non-refundable, even if your application is refused.

Step 4: Pay the Immigration Health Surcharge

After paying the application fee, you will be directed to the IHS payment portal. The system calculates the amount automatically based on your visa duration. For a 1-year master's with a 16-month visa, the IHS would be £776 × 2 = £1,552. Pay by card and keep your IHS reference number — you will need it.

Step 5: Book your biometric appointment

After payment, you will be directed to the VFS Global website to book an appointment at a Visa Application Centre in Pakistan. You must attend in person to provide your biometric data (fingerprints and a digital photograph). VFS Global operates centres in three Pakistani cities:

  • Islamabad — Park Road, Chatta Bakhtawar, Chak Shahzad
  • Lahore — Ground Floor, MCT Madina Corporate Tower, 8 Queens Road
  • Karachi — 43/1/D, Razi Road, PECHS, Shahrah-e-Faisal

All three centres are open Monday to Friday, 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM, with submission hours typically 8:00 AM to 3:00 PM. Book your appointment as soon as possible after submitting your online application — slots fill up fast, especially in Lahore and Karachi during peak season.

Step 6: Attend the biometric appointment and submit documents

On your appointment day, bring:

  • Your current passport (and old passports if applicable)
  • A printed copy of your application summary
  • All supporting documents — bank statements, academic certificates, TB test certificate, sponsor documents, English test result, etc.
  • The appointment confirmation from VFS Global

At the centre, your fingerprints and photo will be taken, and your documents will be submitted (either uploaded digitally or handed in, depending on the centre's process). Some VFS centres offer optional document upload assistance for an additional fee.

You may also be asked to pay a VFS service fee at the centre. Some centres include this in the appointment booking; others charge separately. Check the VFS Global Pakistan website for current details.

Step 7: Wait for the decision

UKVI will process your application and make a decision. Standard processing from Pakistan takes approximately 3 weeks (15 working days) from the date your biometrics are submitted. During peak season (June–September), processing can take 4–6 weeks.

Priority and super-priority processing

If you need a faster decision, UKVI offers optional priority services:

  • Priority service: Decision within approximately 5 working days. Available for an extra fee of approximately £500. Availability depends on demand at your VFS centre.
  • Super-priority service: Decision within approximately 1 working day. Available for an extra fee of approximately £1,000. Very limited availability in Pakistan — this may not be offered at all centres.

These services are optional. Most Pakistani applicants use the standard service. If your course starts soon and you are cutting it close on timing, check whether priority is available at your chosen VFS centre when you book your appointment.

After approval — what happens next

eVisa and travel

From January 2025, the UK transitioned to a digital immigration system. When your Student visa is approved, you will not receive a physical visa sticker (vignette) in your passport in most cases. Instead, your immigration status is stored digitally as an eVisa. You will need to:

  • Create or access your UKVI account (the same account you used for the application).
  • Link your passport to your UKVI account using the "View and prove" service on GOV.UK.
  • Use the UK Immigration: ID Check app to verify your identity.

Your eVisa confirms your immigration status, work rights, and any conditions attached to your visa. You can share your immigration status with employers, landlords, and universities using a share code generated through your UKVI account.

Entering the UK

When you arrive at a UK port of entry, present your passport and explain that you are coming to study. The Border Force officer may ask to see your CAS, proof of funds, or your university enrolment details. Carry a printout of your CAS and your university offer letter, even though these are stored electronically.

You can enter the UK up to 1 month before your course starts if your visa is for more than 6 months. Do not arrive more than 1 month early — you may be refused entry.

Collecting your BRP (if applicable)

In some cases, you may still be asked to collect a Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) card from a designated Post Office within 10 days of arrival. Check your visa decision letter for instructions. However, the UK is phasing out physical BRPs in favour of eVisas, so this requirement may not apply to all 2026 applicants. Follow the instructions in your decision letter.

Working rights on a Student visa

One of the biggest advantages of the Student visa is the ability to work while studying. Your work rights are:

  • During term time: Up to 20 hours per week (typically defined as Monday to Sunday).
  • During vacation periods: Full-time (no hourly limit). This includes the summer break, Christmas break, and Easter break as defined by your university's academic calendar.
  • After graduation: You may be eligible for the Graduate Route visa (2 years for master's/bachelor's graduates, 3 years for PhD graduates), which allows you to work full-time without restrictions.

You cannot:

  • Be self-employed or engage in business activity.
  • Fill a permanent full-time vacancy.
  • Work as a professional sportsperson or sports coach.
  • Work as an entertainer.
  • Work as a doctor or dentist in training (unless specifically permitted).

Your right to work is verified by employers using your share code from the UKVI system. Most university campus jobs and part-time retail/hospitality roles are perfectly fine within the 20-hour limit.

Extending and switching your visa

You can apply to extend your Student visa from inside the UK if:

  • You are already in the UK on a Student visa (or the old Tier 4 visa).
  • Your new course begins within 28 days of your current visa expiring.
  • You have a new CAS for the extended course.

You can also switch to a Student visa from inside the UK if you are on certain other visa types (e.g., a Graduate visa, a Standard Visitor visa in some circumstances, or a Short-term Study visa). The application fee for switching or extending is also £558.

After completing your studies, you may be eligible to switch to the Graduate Route, a Skilled Worker visa, or another immigration category, depending on your circumstances.

Common refusal reasons and how to avoid them

Pakistani students face one of the highest refusal rates among UK student visa applicants globally — approximately 32% in the 2024–25 cycle, compared to a global average of around 12%. UKVI operates a risk-scoring system, and Pakistan is classified as a higher-risk origin country based on historical data. The good news is that almost every refusal is caused by avoidable mistakes. Here are the top reasons:

1. Financial evidence failures (~40% of refusals)

  • The 28-day rule broken: Even a brief dip below the required amount resets the clock. Always maintain a buffer.
  • Unexplained large deposits: Sudden lump sums look like borrowed money. Move funds early and explain the source.
  • Wrong account type: Savings certificates, mutual funds, and credit card limits do not count. Use a standard savings or current account.
  • Unstamped bank statements: Always get official, stamped statements on bank letterhead.
  • Currency conversion shortfall: UKVI uses the exchange rate on the date of application. If the PKR weakens, your balance may fall short. Add a 5–10% buffer.

2. Credibility (genuine student) refusals (~25%)

  • Course choice does not match background: If your master's is in a completely different field from your bachelor's, explain the transition clearly.
  • Weak personal statement: Generic, template-style statements are easy to spot. Write specifically about why this course, why this university, and what you will do after returning to Pakistan.
  • Failed credibility interview: If called for an interview, know your course modules, tuition fees, university name, and career plan. Contradicting your application is fatal.
  • Phrases that signal immigration intent: Avoid phrases like "explore opportunities in the UK" or "see what the future holds." Focus on returning to Pakistan and applying your education there.

3. CAS problems (~3%)

  • An expired CAS (more than 6 months old).
  • A mismatched CAS — name, dates, or fees that do not match your application.
  • A withdrawn or spent CAS — each CAS supports only one application.

4. Missing documents (~2%)

  • No TB test certificate, or an expired one (must be valid within 6 months).
  • No ATAS certificate when one is required.
  • Untranslated documents (anything not in English needs a certified translation).
  • Missing parental consent for under-18 applicants.

5. Immigration history issues (~10%)

  • Failure to declare a previous visa refusal from any country. UKVI will find it. Non-disclosure is treated as deception.
  • Previous overstays or immigration violations.

6. Deception (the most serious ground)

If UKVI concludes that you knowingly used false documents or information, you face a mandatory refusal and a 10-year ban on entry clearance applications. This includes fake bank statements, forged employment letters, and fabricated academic records. This outcome is the single worst result in the process — never risk it.

Pakistani-specific tips

These tips address issues that are particularly relevant to applicants from Pakistan:

CNIC and identification

Carry your CNIC (Computerised National Identity Card) at all times during the application process. Your name on the CNIC must match your passport. If you have changed your name (e.g., after marriage), ensure all documents reflect the current name, and carry supporting evidence (marriage certificate, gazette notification).

Bank letter from Pakistani banks

Many Pakistani bank statements do not meet UKVI requirements by default. Request an official bank letter that includes: the account holder's name, account number, account type, currency, opening balance, closing balance, and the balance for each day of the statement period. The letter should be on bank letterhead, signed and stamped by a bank officer. Banks such as HBL, MCB, UBL, Alfalah, and Bank Alfalah can provide these — request them at least 2 weeks before you need them.

HEC attestation

While UKVI does not always require HEC (Higher Education Commission) attestation of academic documents, some UK universities may request it as part of the CAS process. If your university requires attested documents, get them attested by HEC before sending them. HEC attestation confirms the authenticity of your degree and transcripts.

NADRA Family Registration Certificate (FRC)

If your family is sponsoring you, a NADRA FRC is a powerful supporting document. It officially establishes your family relationship and is recognised by UKVI. You can apply for an FRC online through the NADRA website or visit a NADRA centre. Processing typically takes 5–10 working days.

Choosing the right university

UKVI gives more scrutiny to applications from students going to lower-ranked or less well-known institutions. If possible, choose a Russell Group university or an institution with a strong international reputation. Your visa application is stronger when the university is well-known to the caseworker. You can check whether your university is a licensed student sponsor on the GOV.UK Register of Licensed Sponsors.

Managing the PKR exchange rate

The Pakistani rupee has been volatile against the pound. A bank balance that was sufficient at the start of your 28-day window may fall short by the time you apply if the PKR weakens. Always maintain a buffer of at least 10% above the minimum required amount. Monitor the exchange rate and, if possible, hold funds in GBP or maintain a balance well above the threshold.

Booking TB tests early

TB tests at UKVI-approved clinics in Pakistan are mandatory for all Student visa applicants staying more than 6 months. Approved clinics include IOM (International Organisation for Migration) clinics in Islamabad, Lahore, and Karachi, and other designated facilities. Appointments fill up quickly, especially in the months before the September intake. Book at least 3–4 weeks before you need the certificate. The certificate is valid for 6 months — ensure it remains valid when you submit your visa application.

Credibility interview preparation

In 2026, credibility interviews are more common for Pakistani applicants. If called for one (by phone or in person), be prepared to discuss:

  • Why you chose this specific course and university
  • What modules you will study
  • How the course connects to your career goals in Pakistan
  • How you will fund your studies
  • What your plans are after graduation (return to Pakistan)

Do not memorise scripted answers. Speak naturally and honestly. If your answers contradict your personal statement, that is a red flag.

Scams to avoid

The UK student visa process attracts scams targeting hopeful students and their families. Be aware of:

Fake agents and unlicensed consultants

Many so-called "education consultants" in Pakistan are not licensed or regulated. They may:

  • Promise a guaranteed visa — no one can guarantee a visa. Only UKVI makes that decision.
  • Charge excessive fees for services that you can do yourself (filling out the online application, booking a VFS appointment).
  • Submit fabricated documents (fake bank statements, fake employment letters) — this leads to refusal and a 10-year ban.
  • Tell you to hide previous refusals — UKVI will find them, and hiding them is treated as deception.

If you want professional help, use an adviser registered with the OISC (Office of the Immigration Services Commissioner) in the UK, or a solicitor regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA). You can verify registration on the OISC or SRA websites. For Pakistani-based consultants, ask for their track record, ask to see reviews, and never hand over original documents.

Fake bank statements and documents

Some agents offer to "arrange" bank statements or employment letters. UKVI verifies financial evidence directly with banks, especially for Pakistani and Nigerian applicants. A fake statement will be detected, leading to refusal and potentially a 10-year ban for deception. Never use fabricated documents.

Phishing and email scams

Be wary of emails claiming to be from UKVI, VFS Global, or a UK university asking for payment or personal information. Official communications come through the GOV.UK portal or your university's official email. Never share your UKVI account password or payment details in response to an unsolicited email.

Visa "slot" selling

Some people claim to sell or reserve VFS Global appointment slots. VFS appointments are booked through the official VFS Global website after you have submitted your online application and paid the fees. There is no legitimate secondary market for appointment slots. Book directly through the official portal.

Applying with dependants

Under rules tightened in January 2024, most Student visa holders can no longer bring dependants (spouse or children) unless:

  • You are studying a PhD or other doctoral research programme, or
  • You are a government-sponsored student studying for a course longer than 6 months.

If you do qualify to bring dependants, each dependant must:

  • Pay their own visa application fee (£558).
  • Pay their own IHS at the student rate (£776 per year).
  • Show maintenance funds of £845 per month (London) or £680 per month (outside London) for up to 9 months, in addition to your own maintenance funds.
  • Provide evidence of the relationship (marriage certificate, birth certificate for children).

If you are unsure whether you qualify to bring dependants, check the latest rules on gov.uk/student-visa/family-members.

The Graduate Route — what happens after you study

After completing your degree, you may be eligible for the Graduate Route visa, which allows you to stay in the UK and work (or look for work) for:

  • 2 years — if you completed a bachelor's or master's degree.
  • 3 years — if you completed a PhD or other doctoral qualification.

The Graduate Route is a one-time, non-extendable visa. The application fee is £937 (as of 2026), plus the standard IHS rate of £1,035 per year (the non-student rate). You must apply from within the UK before your Student visa expires.

During the Graduate Route, you can work full-time in any job, switch employers, and be self-employed. After the Graduate Route expires, you would need to switch to another visa category (e.g., Skilled Worker visa) to remain in the UK.

Troubleshooting and additional scenarios

What if my visa is refused?

You have two options:

  • Administrative review: You can request an administrative review of the decision for a fee of £80. This is a paper review — no new evidence can be submitted. It is only worthwhile if you believe UKVI made a clear error in applying the rules. Administrative review takes approximately 28 days.
  • Reapply: In most cases, the better option is to fix the problem and reapply with a new CAS. Address the specific reason for refusal (e.g., correct the financial evidence, obtain a new TB test, rewrite your personal statement), get a fresh CAS from the university, and submit a new application with the £558 fee again.

If your refusal was on the ground of deception, take professional legal advice immediately. A 10-year ban may be in play.

What if my course gets deferred?

If your university defers your course to a later intake, your CAS will be cancelled and a new one will be issued for the new start date. You must wait for the new CAS before applying for your visa. The 28-day financial window must align with the new application date.

What if I change my university?

If you switch to a different UK university after receiving a CAS, your original CAS is no longer valid. You need a new CAS from the new university. Inform your original university so they can cancel the CAS on the UKVI system.

Can I apply if I have a previous UK visa refusal?

Yes, but you must declare the refusal on your application. A previous refusal does not automatically bar you from obtaining a Student visa — but you need to address the reason for the previous refusal and explain why the circumstances have changed. Non-disclosure of a previous refusal is treated as deception and carries far heavier consequences than the original refusal.

Summary: your 2026 UK Student visa action plan

Here is a condensed checklist you can print and tick off as you go:

  1. ☐ Receive unconditional offer from a UK licensed sponsor university.
  2. ☐ Accept offer, pay deposit, request CAS.
  3. ☐ Verify all CAS details match your passport and course information.
  4. ☐ Take IELTS UKVI (or equivalent SELT) if required.
  5. ☐ Get TB test from a UKVI-approved clinic in Pakistan.
  6. ☐ Obtain ATAS certificate if your course requires one.
  7. ☐ Ensure funds are in eligible account for at least 28 consecutive days.
  8. ☐ Get official stamped bank statements (or sponsor's statements + consent letter).
  9. ☐ Prepare all supporting documents (academic, personal, sponsor).
  10. ☐ Write a strong, specific personal statement.
  11. ☐ Complete the online application at gov.uk/student-visa/apply.
  12. ☐ Pay visa fee (£558) and IHS (£776/year) online.
  13. ☐ Book biometrics appointment at VFS Global (Islamabad, Lahore, or Karachi).
  14. ☐ Attend appointment, submit biometrics and documents.
  15. ☐ Wait for decision (~3 weeks standard).
  16. ☐ If approved: link passport to UKVI account, prepare for travel.
  17. ☐ Enter the UK up to 1 month before your course starts.
  18. ☐ Enrol at your university and begin your studies.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a UK student visa cost from Pakistan in 2026?

As of 8 April 2026, the Student visa application fee is £558 per applicant. On top of that you pay the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) at £776 per year of your visa, plus approximately PKR 10,000–17,000 for the TB test and roughly PKR 50,000–80,000 for your IELTS UKVI test. Budget for all of these before you begin. For a 1-year master's, total visa-related costs (excluding tuition) are approximately £1,334–£2,110 in visa fees and IHS alone.

How much bank balance is required for a UK student visa from Pakistan?

You must show your first-year tuition fees (as stated on your CAS, minus any deposit already paid) plus living costs of £1,529 per month if studying in London or £1,171 per month if outside London, for up to 9 months. That means a maximum of £13,761 for London or £10,539 for outside London, on top of any unpaid tuition. Funds must sit in your account (or a parent's account) for 28 consecutive days ending within 31 days of your application. Always add a 5–10% buffer for currency fluctuation.

How long does the UK student visa take to process from Pakistan?

Standard processing is approximately 3 weeks (15 working days) from the date UKVI receives your biometrics. During peak season (June–September) it can take 4–6 weeks. Priority processing (around 5 working days) is available for an extra fee of approximately £500 if offered at your VFS centre. Super-priority (next working day) may be available for about £1,000. Always apply early — at least 8–10 weeks before your course starts.

Can I work in the UK as a student?

Yes. If you hold a Student visa, you can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official vacation periods (including the summer break). You cannot be self-employed, fill a permanent full-time vacancy, or work as a professional sportsperson or sports coach. Your right to work is verified by employers using your UKVI share code. After graduation, the Graduate Route allows you to work full-time for 2 years (3 years for PhD graduates).

What is the CAS number and do I need it?

CAS stands for Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies. It is an electronic record issued by your UK university once you accept an unconditional offer and meet all conditions (including paying any required deposit). It contains a unique 14-character reference number that you must enter in your visa application. Without a valid CAS, you cannot apply for a Student visa. Your CAS is valid for 6 months from the date it is assigned, and it can only be used once.

Final thoughts

Applying for a UK Student visa from Pakistan in 2026 is a multi-step process that demands careful planning, meticulous documentation, and genuine engagement with your study plans. The fee increase to £558, the higher maintenance thresholds of £1,529/£1,171 per month, and the intensified credibility assessment all mean that Pakistani applicants cannot afford to cut corners. But the process is straightforward when you approach it methodically: get your CAS early, maintain your funds with a buffer, prepare every document to UKVI standards, write a personal statement that is authentically yours, and submit your application well ahead of your course start date.

The UK remains one of the best places in the world to study, with institutions that are globally recognised, a post-study work route that gives you real career flexibility, and a multicultural society that includes one of the largest Pakistani diasporas in the world. Thousands of Pakistani students successfully obtain their visas every year. With proper preparation, you can be one of them.
