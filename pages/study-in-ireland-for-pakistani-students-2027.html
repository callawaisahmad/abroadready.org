Study in Ireland for Pakistani Students: 2027 Guide

🗓️ Oct 10, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Study in Ireland for Pakistani students in 2027 comes down to one competitive national scholarship, a strong set of university awards, and a two-year post-study stay-back that makes the cost easier to justify. Ireland is one of the fastest-rising destinations in Europe, and for a Pakistani applicant it now sits beside Germany and the Netherlands as a realistic plan rather than a long shot.

The direct answer is that most Pakistani students fund a one-year master’s themselves and then use the Government of Ireland scholarship and university awards to cut the bill. Plan for roughly EUR 12,000 to EUR 25,000 in tuition and about EUR 1,200 to EUR 1,800 a month in Dublin, with Cork, Galway and Limerick running cheaper. Read the whole guide before you commit, because the funding rules and the visa paperwork decide whether the plan survives.

Study in Ireland for Pakistani Students: What 2027 Looks Like

Ireland is not a free-tuition country. What it offers is quality, English as the language of instruction, and a work route after graduation. That combination is why it keeps appearing in the top ten destinations for international students across 2026 surveys, even as some traditional destinations tighten their rules.

The country runs two main intakes. September is the full intake with every programme on offer, while January suits a smaller set of taught master’s and is useful if you miss the autumn deadlines. Irish degrees use the National Framework of Qualifications, and most postgraduate courses sit at level 9, which matters because the scholarship and the stay-back permit both key off that level.

One habit pays off immediately: email the international office before you apply and ask two questions. What automatic scholarships apply to my programme, and what is the exact net tuition after those awards. The answer often changes which university is cheapest.

Funding: The Government of Ireland Scholarship and University Awards

The Government of Ireland International Education Scholarship, usually shortened to GOI-IES, is the flagship award. It supports one year of full-time study at NFQ level 9 (master’s or postgraduate diploma) or level 10 (PhD), and each award covers a full tuition fee waiver plus a EUR 10,000 stipend for living costs.

About 60 scholarships are funded each year across all Irish higher education institutions, and the competition is open to applicants domiciled outside the EU, EEA, Switzerland and the United Kingdom. Pakistan is therefore eligible, but you must already hold an offer, conditional or final, from an eligible institution when you apply for the scholarship.

There is a EUR 50 application fee to the institution and it cannot be waived, even for successful GOI-IES recipients. Because the award only covers one year, students on two-year master’s should plan how they will fund the second year separately and should not assume the stipend repeats.

Separate from the national scheme, most universities run their own merit awards: global excellence scholarships, early-payment discounts, and faculty bursaries that cut tuition by 10 to 50 percent. Some are automatic on admission, others need a short separate form. Always ask the international office which awards apply to your programme, because many are granted without a second application and are easy to miss.

Tuition, Living Costs and the Two-Year Stay-Back

Tuition is the biggest line item. A taught master’s at a non-EU rate generally lands between EUR 12,000 and EUR 25,000 for the year, with engineering, computing and business at the upper end. Research master’s and PhD places are often funded by the supervisor, so they can be cheaper than a taught degree, and PhD funding usually comes with a stipend.

Living costs decide whether the total is bearable. Dublin accounts for most of the national average, while Cork, Galway, Limerick and Waterford let you live on noticeably less. Rent is the variable that matters; on-campus and purpose-built student accommodation is the cheapest reliable option and should be booked early, because the private rental market in Dublin is tight and expensive.

The post-study rule is what makes Ireland competitive. Under the Third Level Graduate Programme, a master’s or PhD graduate can stay for up to 24 months to look for work and then move into employment, while an honours bachelor’s graduate gets 12 months. That window is why families often compare Ireland with Canada, where post-study rules have tightened.

Before you rely on any figure, confirm the current visa financial requirement on the official Irish immigration site and check programme fees directly with the university. Budget with our cost and budget guide so that you are comparing net cost, not headline tuition.

Deadlines and How to Apply from Pakistan

Ireland runs its application calendar in a way that rewards people who move early. Applications usually open in late autumn and many competitive taught master’s courses close once they are full, so applying by early 2027 gives you the best choice of programmes and the best chance at a scholarship.

The GOI-IES call typically opens in late January and closes in mid-March, with results released around June. Because the award requires an admission offer, the practical order is: shortlist programmes, apply for admission, secure your offer, then submit the scholarship application before the deadline. Working backwards from the intake is the only reliable way to avoid a rushed submission.

For the visa, prepare a clear financial trail. Funds must be genuinely available, not locked in deposits that mature after your start date, and the account history should show where the money came from. Our proof of funds guide for Pakistani applicants explains the bank formats that are accepted.

Your supporting documents matter as much as your grades. A tight, specific statement of purpose is often what separates two similar candidates, and our guide on how to write a winning statement of purpose covers the structure examiners reward. Confirm every date and figure on the official HEA GOI-IES page and on Education in Ireland before you apply.

Entry Requirements for Pakistani Applicants

Requirements vary by programme, but the pattern is consistent. You need a completed bachelor’s degree, usually a four-year degree or a two-year master’s following a two-year bachelor’s, with a strong second division or better. Competitive master’s programmes look for a first division and relevant coursework.

English is the other gate. Most universities want IELTS 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0, or an equivalent TOEFL or PTE score, though some accept a recognised English-medium instruction letter from your previous institution. Programmes in medicine and law often set higher thresholds.

Some courses add a statement of purpose, two academic references, a CV and occasionally an interview. Meet those extras properly rather than treating them as paperwork, because scholarships are often decided on the same documents. Verify the exact entry criteria on your university’s own page, since the national minimum is only a floor.

Frequently asked questions

Do Pakistani students need a visa to study in Ireland?+

Yes. Pakistan is a visa-required country, so you apply for a long-stay study visa after you hold an offer and pay the deposit. You will need proof of funds, health insurance and an accepted English test.

Is the Government of Ireland scholarship open to Pakistani students?+

Yes. GOI-IES is open to applicants domiciled outside the EU, EEA, Switzerland and the UK, which includes Pakistan. It is competitive, with about 60 awards a year and a full fee waiver plus a EUR 10,000 stipend.

How long can I stay in Ireland after graduating?+

A master’s or PhD graduate can stay up to 24 months under the Third Level Graduate Programme, while an honours bachelor’s graduate gets 12 months. The permit is granted to look for work and take up employment.
