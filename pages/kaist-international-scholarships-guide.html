KAIST International Scholarships Guide

🗓️ Aug 5, 2026⏱️ 9 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

The KAIST International Scholarships are the main funding route for international students at the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology, South Korea's leading research university in science and technology. KAIST offers full-tuition scholarships, monthly stipends, and health insurance to international students across its undergraduate and graduate programs. Because the scholarships are awarded automatically through the admission application, many students do not realise how accessible the funding is.

This guide explains how KAIST International Scholarships work, what they cover, who is eligible, and how the application process fits together. If you want a fully funded education in engineering, science, or technology in Asia, KAIST is one of the strongest options.

Key takeaways

  • The KAIST International Scholarship covers full tuition for international students plus a monthly living stipend.
  • It also provides national health insurance to international students during their studies.
  • The scholarship is automatic: you are considered when you apply for admission to a KAIST degree program, with no separate form.
  • Graduate students in many programs can combine the scholarship with research assistantship pay from their lab.
  • Deadlines vary by admissions track, so plan around your program's specific application window.

What is the KAIST International Scholarship?

KAIST, based in Daejeon, South Korea, is a research university founded in 1971 to train scientists and engineers. It is consistently ranked among the best universities in Asia and the world for engineering and technology. To attract top international talent, KAIST funds incoming international students through the KAIST International Scholarship, which is granted automatically based on admission merit.

This design is unusual and valuable: instead of requiring a separate scholarship essay and timeline, the scholarship rides on your admission application. If you are admitted as an international student, you typically receive the funding as part of your offer.

What does the scholarship cover?

The KAIST International Scholarship is a comprehensive package. It typically covers:

  • Full tuition. The entire cost of tuition for your degree program is waived.
  • A monthly stipend. You receive a monthly allowance for living expenses; for graduate students this is around KRW 350,000 per month, and for undergraduates it varies by level.
  • Health insurance. KAIST supports international students with national health insurance coverage.

Graduate students who join research labs also earn research assistantship salaries from their professors, which can add significantly to the base stipend. Confirm the exact current figures on the official admission website, admission.kaist.ac.kr, before you apply.

Eligibility

You are eligible for a KAIST International Scholarship if you are an international applicant applying to a KAIST degree program, whether bachelor's, master's, or doctoral. You must not be receiving another scholarship that already covers your tuition and fees. Students on government scholarships such as the Global Korea Scholarship may not be able to combine the two, so check the terms of each funding source.

There is no separate scholarship application. Admission to KAIST as an international student is what triggers the award, though the exact level of funding can depend on the program and admissions track.

How to apply step by step

Choose your program and track

KAIST runs its admissions through the Graduate Admissions or Undergraduate Admissions system, with multiple tracks per year. Confirm which track is open for your program and read its requirements.

Prepare your application documents

You will need transcripts, degree certificates, a statement of purpose, recommendation letters, proof of English proficiency, and your passport details. Requirements vary by program.

Submit the online application

Complete the online application by your track's deadline. The scholarship consideration is included automatically, so there is no additional scholarship step.

Await the admission decision

Admission results are announced several weeks after the deadline. If you are admitted, your offer letter will state the scholarship you have been awarded, including the stipend level.

Secure your visa and arrive

Accepted students arrange a D-2 student visa through the Korean embassy or consulate and complete any required documents before arrival.

Key dates

KAIST International Scholarships follow the admission calendar, not a separate scholarship calendar. Graduate admission tracks typically open in the autumn and close in early winter or spring depending on the program, with a second track in some years. Undergraduate admission runs similar cycles. Check admission.kaist.ac.kr for the deadlines that apply to your program and track, and begin document preparation weeks ahead.

KAIST International Scholarships: Admission Process and Automatic Funding

One of the most important things to understand about KAIST International Scholarships is that the scholarship application is integrated into the admission process: you do not apply for the scholarship separately. When you submit your application for admission to KAIST, you are automatically considered for the available scholarships, which include full tuition coverage, a monthly stipend and health insurance. T

his means that the strongest thing you can do to secure a KAIST scholarship is to submit the strongest possible admission application. The scholarship is awarded based on academic excellence, research potential and the overall competitiveness of your application, and there is no additional form or separate deadline for scholarship consideration.

The admission application itself requires several components: academic transcripts, a statement of purpose, letters of recommendation, English proficiency test scores and, for some programmes, standardised test scores such as the GRE. The statement of purpose is your opportunity to explain your academic background, your research interests and your reasons for choosing KAIST. The committee evaluates this document for clarity of thought, specificity of purpose and alignment with KAIST research strengths. R

ather than writing a general statement about wanting to pursue a master or doctoral degree, identify specific research groups, faculty members or projects at KAIST that interest you, and explain how your background prepares you to contribute to that work. This specificity demonstrates that you have researched KAIST thoroughly and that your application is targeted rather than generic.

rovide your referees with a copy of your statement of purpose, your CV and a summary of the research you want to pursue at KAIST, so that they can tailor their letters to the programme. Follow up with your referees at least two weeks before the deadline to confirm that the letters have been submitted, and again forty-eight hours before the closing date as a final reminder. KAIST Scholarships for International Students: Language and Cultural Adjustment

KAIST conducts its programmes primarily in English, and English proficiency test scores are a requirement for international applicants. At the time of writing, the minimum requirements are typically IELTS 6.5 or TOEFL iBT 83, though some departments set higher thresholds. If your scores are close to but not quite at the required level, retaking the test with focused preparation is the most direct path to meeting the requirement. K

AIST does not typically accept medium-of-instruction letters as a substitute for formal English test scores, so plan to take the IELTS or TOEFL well before the application deadline. Scores are valid for two years, so ensure that your test date falls within the validity window at the time of application.

Korean language ability is not required for admission or for the scholarship, but learning Korean before or during your time at KAIST significantly improves your daily life and your integration into Korean society. KAIST offers Korean language courses for international students, and many students take these courses during their first semester. Reaching a basic level of Korean (conversational ability for daily interactions) within the first six months is a realistic and valuable goal. W

hile Daejeon, where KAIST main campus is located, has a smaller international community than Seoul, the university itself has a vibrant international student population and Korean students are generally welcoming to international peers. The cultural adjustment to life in Korea is a significant consideration for international students, and KAIST provides support services to help with the transition. The International Student Services office assists with visa matters, housing, health insurance and other practical issues. KAIST also organises orientation programmes for new international students, which cover Korean cultural norms, academic expectations and campus resources. K

orean academic culture is different from Western or South Asian norms: the relationship between professors and students is more formal, group work is emphasised and the pace of the academic calendar is intensive. Housing is one of the first practical challenges you will face. KAIST provides on-campus housing for international students, and the residences are affordable and conveniently located near academic buildings. On-campus housing is typically the first choice for new international students, as it provides a built-in social community and eliminates the need to navigate the Korean rental market. I

f on-campus housing is unavailable or if you prefer to live off-campus, the Daejeon rental market is significantly more affordable than Seoul, but understanding the Korean rental system, which often requires a large deposit (jeonse) or a monthly rent plus a key money deposit, is important before you sign a lease. The international student office can provide guidance on housing options and help you understand the terms of Korean rental contracts.

KAIST vs Other Korean and Asian Scholarships: A Comparison

KAIST International Scholarships exist alongside several other major scholarship programmes in South Korea, and understanding how KAIST compares helps you make an informed decision about where to apply. The Global Korea Scholarship (GKS), formerly the Korean Government Scholarship Program, is the largest government-funded scholarship in Korea and supports both undergraduate and graduate students. GKS covers tuition, a monthly stipend, airfare, health insurance and a year of Korean language training. T

he key difference between KAIST scholarships and GKS is that KAIST scholarships are institution-specific and automatic, while GKS is a national programme that requires a separate application and is managed by the Korean embassy in your country. If you are applying to KAIST specifically, the KAIST scholarship is the more direct route, as it does not require a separate application and is integrated into the admissions process.

When comparing KAIST to other top Korean universities, the scholarship offerings are broadly similar in terms of what they cover: full tuition, a monthly stipend and health insurance. However, KAIST particular strength in science and technology means that the research facilities, faculty expertise and industry connections in STEM fields are among the best in Korea. I

f your field is in engineering, computer science, physics, chemistry or a related discipline, KAIST research environment is difficult to match at other Korean institutions. For students in business, social sciences or humanities, other universities such as Seoul National University, Yonsei University or Korea University may offer stronger programmes and more relevant faculty. The decision should be driven by your specific field and research interests, not by the university name alone.

Compared to scholarships in other Asian countries, KAIST scholarships are competitive in both generosity and prestige. The China Scholarship Council (CSC) provides full funding for international students at Chinese universities, and the MEXT scholarship in Japan covers tuition, a monthly stipend and airfare. Both are well-established programmes with large numbers of recipients. KAIST scholarships offer a comparable level of financial support, with the added advantage of being automatic and institution-specific, which simplifies the application process. F

or students who are deciding between Korea, China and Japan, the choice often comes down to the specific research fit, the language you want to learn and the career opportunities in each country after graduation. For Pakistani students, the practical considerations include the availability of a Pakistani community in each country, the cost of living, the language barrier and the post-graduation work prospects. The cost of living in Daejeon, where KAIST is located, is moderate by international standards, and the monthly stipend is generally sufficient. T

KAIST Application Timeline and Key Deadlines for International Students

Understanding the KAIST application timeline is essential for planning your scholarship strategy, as the deadlines for admission and financial support are intertwined. KAIST typically operates two admission cycles per year: one for the spring semester (starting in March) and one for the autumn semester (starting in September). The autumn intake is the main admission cycle for international students, and it is when the majority of scholarship places are available. A

pplications for the autumn intake typically open in September or October and close in November or December, with admission decisions announced in the following February or March. The spring intake is smaller and has fewer scholarship places, with applications typically closing in May or June.

kaist international scholarships deadlines and timeline

The application itself is submitted through the KAIST online admissions portal, and it requires several documents that take time to prepare. Academic transcripts must be obtained from your university, and if they are not in English or Korean, they must be translated and sometimes notarised. English proficiency test scores must be sent directly from the testing agency to KAIST, which can take several weeks. L

etters of recommendation must be submitted by your referees through the portal, and you should give your referees at least four weeks to complete their letters. The statement of purpose is the document that requires the most time and thought: plan to spend at least two to three weeks drafting, revising and refining it before submission. Creating a backwards timeline from the application deadline, with specific dates for each document, ensures that nothing is left to the last minute.

After submission, the admissions committee reviews all applications and makes decisions within two to three months. Successful applicants receive an admission offer and a scholarship award letter that specifies the terms of the funding. If you receive an offer, you must confirm your acceptance within the specified period, usually two to four weeks, and begin the visa application process. T

he Korean student visa (D-2) requires a certificate of admission from KAIST, proof of financial capacity (which is simplified if you hold a KAIST scholarship), a valid passport and health insurance. The visa application is submitted to the Korean consulate in your country, and processing typically takes two to four weeks. Plan to arrive in Korea at least two weeks before the semester begins to complete administrative tasks such as residence registration, bank account setup and orientation.

For Pakistani applicants, the visa process has specific requirements that deserve attention. You will need to obtain a police clearance certificate from the Pakistani authorities, which can take several weeks to process. The health examination must be conducted by a panel physician approved by the Korean consulate, and the results must be in the format required by Korean immigration. F

inancial evidence for the visa is simplified if you hold a KAIST scholarship, as the scholarship award letter serves as evidence of financial support, but you should still prepare supplementary bank statements showing access to funds for the initial period before your first stipend payment. Begin gathering these documents as soon as you receive your admission offer, as delays in document preparation are one of the most common reasons for late arrivals and missed orientation sessions. The KAIST International Student Services office can provide guidance on the specific documents required for Pakistani nationals.
