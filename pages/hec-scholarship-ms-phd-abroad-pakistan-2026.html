HEC Overseas Scholarship: MS & PhD Guide 2026

🗓️ Sep 14, 2026⏱️ 31 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Few government programmes change a Pakistani family's trajectory like the HEC overseas scholarship. For MS and PhD students, this is the fully funded passport to a top-ranked foreign university — tuition, stipend, airfare and allowances — and after a decade of engineering-only batches, the 2026 cycle (Phase-III, Batch-IV) reopened the programme to social sciences, business, governance and other fields. If you are a Pakistani or AJK national with a strong 16-year or 18-year degree, the question is no longer "can I study abroad?" but "how do I win one of these HEC overseas scholarships for MS, MPhil and PhD from Pakistan?" This guide answers that question end to end.

This guide covers the Phase-III overseas scholarship in full — what it funds, who qualifies, how the HAT and interview work, which fields are eligible, how to apply through the HEC e-Services portal — and then the parallel HEC schemes (US-Pakistan Knowledge Corridor PhD, the Commonwealth nomination, Faculty Development and ASDIS) so you can see the whole funding landscape at once. It ends with the mistakes that got otherwise-qualified applicants rejected, and the exact timeline that wins.

What is the HEC overseas scholarship programme?

The Higher Education Commission's Overseas Scholarships programme is Pakistan's flagship instrument for sending its brightest students to the best universities in the world. Under the current structure (Overseas Scholarships for MS/M.Phil and PhD Studies, Phase-III), selected candidates study at universities ranked in the top 200 of the QS or THE world rankings, in a fixed set of "thematic areas" that Pakistan has decided matter most to its development. The award is fully funded — tuition, a monthly stipend, return airfare and the allowances you need to live and study — and in the Batch-IV cycle announced in 2026, the thematic list expanded to include Social Sciences, Business & Governance alongside the classic engineering, energy, agriculture, health, and natural-science fields.

Batch IV's significance is easy to miss if you read only the eligibility checklist. For the first time in roughly ten years, HEC opened the MS/PhD gate to disciplines outside engineering and technology, which means an economics graduate, a business graduate or a political-science graduate from a Pakistani university now has a real shot at the funding that previously belonged to engineers alone. The competition will be fierce precisely because the door is newly open, so the application discipline in this guide matters more than ever.

The official references you should bookmark

Before you read another word, save these official pages: the HEC programme page for Overseas Scholarships (linked later in the external references section), the HEC e-Services Scholarship Portal where applications actually open, and the HAT test calendar page. Two fine prints deserve emphasis. First, "at most one second division throughout your academic career" is strict: a single second division in your BS is tolerated, but a second division in both your intermediate and your BS is the kind of formal disqualification HEC's document-screening stage catches automatically. The HAT: your strongest single lever

The Higher Education Aptitude Test is the filter that decides national-pool ranking. HEC selection is merit-based on HAT score plus interview, with the provincial quotas of Pakistan applied across the slate, and in practice the HAT score is the number that separates shortlist from reject. The test is administered regularly through HEC's test calendar, your score remains valid for two years, and the Commonwealth program in the HEC family demands at least 60 out of 100 — treat 60 as a floor and 70+ as the realistic target.

HAT preparation is a three-month, not a three-week, project. The test covers verbal, quantitative and analytical reasoning in proportions that reward consistent practice with past HAT papers, and the quality of preparation is the single strongest signal your application can control once your university shortlist is fixed. Register early on the HAT portal, because test seats fill, and the calendar moves — the practical sequencing is laid out in the timeline section at the end of this guide.

When you can skip the HAT

Candidates holding a confirmed unconditional admission from a top 200 QS/THE university in an eligible thematic area for the upcoming semester are exempted from the scholarship test and interview, subject to satisfying every other requirement. That exemption is the fastest lane in the programme: it means you compete on the strength of the admission itself rather than on the HAT, and it makes the humble act of applying directly to universities months ahead proportionately powerful. The general guidance in our complete fall-intake scholarship timeline shows exactly how to build that admission a year before you sit a test.

What the scholarship actually covers

Successful candidates sign a scholarship agreement that lays out a fully funded award: tuition fees at the host university up to the agreed amount, a monthly stipend for living expenses, return airfare, the visa and related allowances, and the health/insurance arrangements required by the host country. The agreement also carries the obligations: scholars are expected to return to Pakistan on completion and serve in the manner the agreement sets out, and those who previously availed HEC MS/MPhil funding must complete a residency period in Pakistan before taking the PhD award (documented with an NOC). The message to read beneath the fine print is simple: the HEC overseas scholarship is a national-investment contract, not a lottery win, and the strongest applicants are those who understand the service expectation on day one.

How to apply: the e-Services portal walkthrough

The application happens entirely online through the HEC e-Services Scholarship portal, with an application fee of Rs. 3,000 paid via 1LINK. The step-by-step sequence that produced successful Pakistan applications in the 2026 cycle is below.

  1. Create your e-Services account with accurate personal details; your CNIC, birth date and qualifications must match your documents exactly, because mismatches are a classic rejection reason.
  2. Register for and sit the HAT through the HEC test calendar before the application window closes, and target 70+.
  3. Compile your documents: educational certificates and transcripts, equivalence (where needed), CNIC/B-Form, passport, a professional CV, and the declaration that you are not availing any other scholarship.
  4. Select your universities: the portal lets you indicate preferred universities and countries, and you must verify each one against the top-200 QS/THE list, because a non-eligible university is an immediate disqualification at screening.
  5. Submit and pay: complete the online form before the deadline (the August 28, 2026 deadline for Batch-IV applied at 4:00 PM), pay the Rs. 3,000 fee, and keep the payment receipt.
  6. Prepare for the interview: shortlisted applicants face a panel that scrutinises motivation, the research plan, and the alignment of your proposed study with the thematic area.

The full document and nomination mechanics, including how HEC's nomination flows to the sponsoring partners, are the kind of detail our competitive scholarship profile guide turns into a routine. Where Batch-IV stands out is the interview: because the social-science cohort is new, panels are unusually alert to applicants who cannot articulate how their research serves Pakistan — prepare that answer now.

Beyond Phase-III: the wider HEC foreign-scholarship family

Phase-III is not the only HEC route abroad, and the mistake of fixating on a single deadline costs Pakistani students real chances each year. The US-Pakistan Knowledge Corridor PhD Scholarship targets top-100 QS US universities for Pakistani PhD applicants (with admissions often required upfront, GRE/HAT thresholds, and 2027 cycles that opened in 2026). The Commonwealth Scholarship partnership has HEC nominate Pakistani candidates for UK master's and PhD funding, with the 2027-28 application cycle opening from Pakistan (HAT 60+ required). The Faculty Development Programme (FDP) funds faculty members, and the Advanced Skills Development through International Scholarships (ASDIS) programme targets MS and PhD in computer science, software, IT, computer engineering, electrical and avionics fields. And the bilaterial scholarship with China for study abroad worlds runs through HEC nomination as part of the general foreign-scholarship umbrella.

The strategic point: HEC runs multiple doors that open at different times, and the applicant who treats "the HEC scholarship" as one event is playing a single-ingredient game against students working a whole portfolio. The how to find scholarships you are actually eligible for guide frames that portfolio thinking, and the Commonwealth scholarship guide shows why the UK chair is one of the best complementary applications a Pakistani candidate can make. Each of these schemes has its own eligibility, tests and deadlines, and winning any one of them changes the financial story of your entire postgraduate career.

The anatomy of a winning HEC application

A winning HEC overseas application is not a perfect transcript attached to a CV; it is a small dossier that tells one coherent story: a Pakistani achiever whose research plan serves a listed thematic area and who is ready to study at a top-200 university. Around that story the five parts are: the academic record (first division/3.0 CGPA, no lurking second divisions); the documents (clean names, attested copies, equivalence in order); the test (HAT above the floor, ideally 70+); the motivation (a research plan that the panel can read in two minutes and immediately grasp why it matters for Pakistan); and the logistics (universities on the top-200 list, a working contact with a potential supervisor, and a window that matches the thematic area). Candidates who treat these as five separate chores produce the flat files that get screened out; candidates who build one story produce the ones that interview panels remember.

There is a quieter but decisive detail buried in the selection mechanics: HEC applies Pakistan's approved provincial quotas and the reserved AJK places, so your merit is computed both absolutely and against your own provincial pool. That fact cuts both ways for your preparation. It means your strongest comparison is with applicants of your own province, and it means you should never assume a "national" average decides your chances. Polish the personal story, sit the HAT early, and treat the provincial pool as the level at which your file must win.

The document checklist that survives scrutiny

Design your document folder as if a clerk in a busy government office will read it on a bad day. That means: attested educational certificates and transcripts for every degree (IBCC or HEC equivalence where your qualification is foreign); the CNIC/B-Form and passport that match your online entry exactly; the equivalence certificate where your degree needs one; the professional CV in HEC's format; the declaration confirming you are not availing any other scholarship; and every fee receipt. The three words that cause rejections are mismatched names, unattested copies, and missed windows — and no interview panel ever meets a candidate who was rejected for those reasons. If your transcript is in percentage marks, convert and document it with the same care the CGPA conversion guide describes, because the 3.0 CGPA line is a hard gate.

HEC versus CSC versus Chevening: which fits you

Pakistani candidates routinely confuse themselves comparing the flagship schemes, so here is the honest grid. The HEC overseas scholarship (this guide's subject) is Government-of-Pakistan funding, Pakistani-managed, tied to the thematic areas, restricted to top-200 QS/THE universities, and its great virtues are that it covers anything from AI to social sciences and that its scholars serve Pakistan on return. The Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC) is China-funded, university or council managed, tied to Chinese universities and to the fields those universities offer, and its virtues are the sheer breadth of universities and the generous stipend; the application mechanics are our CSC guide. Chevening is UK-government funded for a one-year master's at any UK university, leadership-focused, and administered by the UK; it is covered in depth in our Chevening guide. Commonwealth (via HEC nomination) funds UK master's and PhD through the Commonwealth route.

The strategic reading: there is no "best" scheme, only the one that fits your profile. An engineer targeting top US universities should overlap the HEC overseas route with the US-Pakistan Knowledge Corridor. A social scientist whose chance, after a decade, has reopened should treat Phase-III as the primary bet and Chevening as a complementary one-year play. A student targeting China specifically overlaps HEC's bilateral window with CSC and provincial applications. The people who fund-study abroad with only one application in the system are gambling; the ones who run the whole eligible portfolio are working a pipeline, and the pipeline, not the single shot, is what produces the acceptance letter.

The interview: what the panel is really looking for

A shortlisted HEC applicant sits before a selection panel that has read the file and formed a view. The panel evaluates three things, and preparing exactly these three wins the seat. First, research clarity: can the applicant state the research question, the proposed university and supervisor, and the plausible method in ninety seconds? Second, commitment: does the applicant understand the service-to-Pakistan obligation and articulate concretely how the degree will be used (teaching post in a university, research institute, industry pipeline)? Third, polish: is the applicant composed, honest about gaps, and realistic about the plan? Candidates who rehearse their story out loud and who can switch between the big vision and the concrete detail are the ones who interview panels remember. Candidates who recite a memorised CV get the polite version of the same question twice and no more.

The other interview reality is that panels dislike two failure patterns above all: the "degree-shopping" answer (say yes to every country regardless of fit) and the unprepared research switchero (announce a topic that appears in the file only that morning). Neither is recoverable in a fifteen-minute slot, and both are prevented in the same way — by the year-long preparation this guide's timeline pushes.

Life as an HEC scholar: stipend, coursework and the return

Once awarded, the HEC overseas scholarship runs as a managed relationship with the host university and HEC. The monthly stipend, disbursed through the agreed banking channels, is calibrated to the host country's living costs and should be budgeted with the same discipline the study-abroad budget guide teaches — scholars who run out in month ten know exactly why planning matters. Tuition is paid to the university against the agreement, and progress reports are expected; the annual report discipline is not an administrative afterthought, it is the mechanism by which HEC ensures its national investment is on track. Scholars planning to bring dependents, extend the programme, or change research direction must seek approval through the scholarship channel first, because the agreement, not local convenience, governs these changes.

The return obligation deserves a paragraph of its own. HEC-funded scholars are expected to return to Pakistan after completion and serve the country in the manner the agreement sets out, and the Commonwealth-adjacent and Knowledge-Corridor agreements carry similar service expectations. That is the covenant underneath the funding: the state finances your top-200 education so the knowledge lands back in Pakistan's classrooms, labs, ministries and industries. The scholars who plan this return from day one — negotiating a host-university teaching position, a Pakistani research-intersection project, or the industry placement they will occupy — are the ones whose careers accelerate. The scholarship is not a one-way ticket; it is a round trip.

Mistakes that sink otherwise-strong applications

The rejection list for HEC overseas scholarships is shorter than applicants fear and more predictable than they hope. The five mistakes that sink strong files are: applying to a university outside the top-200 QS/THE list; mismatching the declared thematic area with the actual degree; submitting documents with inconsistent names or unattested copies; treating the HAT as a minor hurdle and entering with an uncompetitive score; and leaving the application to the final hours of the e-portal window when the system slows. Every one of these is preventable with the discipline this article demands — which is precisely why the winners are predictable, and why your file should aim to be predictably excellent rather than spontaneously lucky.

Publications, supervisors and the research advantage

The single most under-appreciated advantage in a HEC file is a research footprint. A Pakistani applicant who owns one published paper, or who brings a concrete internship with a supervisor's explicit invitation to study with them, converts a competitive file into a memorable one. For PhD candidates the supervisor contact matters disproportionately: a professor at a top-200 university who has agreed to supervise your research is a near-guarantee that the school's admission letter (and the HEC exemption that follows) materialises. The equivalent for MS candidates is a strong statement of purpose that names the programme, the courses and the research group rather than "your excellent university." Academic publications, conference posters and funded research internships — all documented in the funded summer research internship guide's sweep — are the raw material that separates the profile from the crowd, and they are built in the years before the application, not the weeks of it.

Putting it all together

The HEC overseas scholarship, like every government credit, rewards the prepared, not the hopeful. Its power belongs to the engineer who secured the top-200 admission while the batch window sat open, to the social scientist who wrote the thematic-area research plan in April, to the GK worker who kept the HAT score fresh through the two-year validity, and to the family that converted "we want our child abroad" into a concrete, calendar-driven dossier. The funding is real, the doors are open, and the thematic areas of 2026 have widened. What is missing from every single story that ends in an acceptance is luck; what is present is a year of deliberate, documented, boring preparation. Preparing beside your job: the slow-burn strategy

Not every HEC applicant is a full-time student. A large share are working professionals, faculty members and public-sector employees who must prepare while holding down their jobs, and the strategy that wins for them is entirely different from the undergraduate's sprint. The slow-burn plan is a calendar, not a mood: six months of HAT preparation at an hour a day (the score is ratio-driven, and hour-a-day compounds), one evening a week on the research plan, and one month of daily practice in the six weeks before the test. The documents — transcripts, equivalence, CV, references — are collected in the quiet months between announcements. The single most dangerous feeling in a working professional's scholarship attempt is guilt about the time already lost; it feeds procrastination. Trade it for progress: one small finished item a week beats a great intention a month, and the HEC file, like every deliverable, rewards the person who just ships the pieces.

Public-sector employees also carry a structural complication the file must solve cleanly: the NOC and the service undertaking. A government officer studying abroad under HEC funding typically requires department clearance and commits to a service return on specific terms, and the paperwork between the department, HEC and the university is exactly the kind of cross-agency chain that takes months. Start those signatures the day you decide to apply, not the day you are shortlisted — Apply the same readiness discipline the competitive profile guide applies to transcripts, this applies just as hard to the memo trail.

GAT, GRE, HAT and IELTS: which test for which HEC scheme

The acronym soup defeats more applicants than the subject matter, so here is the map. The HAT (Higher Education Aptitude Test) is HEC's own general aptitude test, valid two years, and the filter for the Phase-III overseas scholarship and the Commonwealth nomination (60+ threshold). The GAT (Graduate Assessment Test) is the HEC test for admission to Pakistani universities' MS programmes and reappears in some HEC-administered schemes; it is not the passport to the overseas programme, and the two are often confused. The GRE (General) is the American-standard test whose scores dominate US university admissions, and the US-Pakistan Knowledge Corridor explicitly accepts GRE (300+) or the HAT at 60%. The IELTS/TOEFL is the English-proficiency gate required by the host university for virtually every English-medium programme, regardless of HEC. The sequencing that works: sit the language test first (universities want it for the admission you will use to seek exemption), then the HAT (for the national pool), and treat the GRE as optional unless your target university or the Corridor demands it. The score thresholds relevant to every country are in our IELTS requirements by country guide.

The AJK and provincial quota nuance

Because HEC applies the provincial quotas of Pakistan and the reserved AJK share to its scholarship slate, the applicant should understand what that means for the file. A candidate's merit is ranked within the provincial pool, and the pools are unequal sizes, which changes what "a strong score" means in your own province. The practical consequences are three: do not compare yourself against a national panic figure, because your competition is your province's; do not assume your province is a handicap, since every province sends scholars and the quotas exist precisely to broaden the national pipeline; and treat the AJK reserved places as their own door, which many AJK-national candidates under-use. The deeper advice is to stop optimising the thing you cannot control — the pool — and optimise the thing you can: a clean file, an early HAT, a stronger research plan.

From scholarship to visa to landing: the award-service sequence

Winning the award is not the finish; the service sequence between the acceptance letter and the first lecture is a chain of its own. The HEC scholars' agreement is signed after selection, the university admission and the financial schedules are reconciled, and the visa is applied for on the strength of the scholarship documents — the same documentation discipline our post-award visa guide describes for every funded programme. The practical rhythm that serves every scholar: obtain the official scholarship notification and agreement before the embassy stage; coordinate the tuition billing with the university's international office so the fee schedule matches the award's coverage; open the banking arrangements for the stipend early; and book the travel so the arrival lands inside the university's orientation window. The fine print matters here: the agreement usually binds you to a start term, and drifting past that term costs approval, not just time. Treat the post-award months as a tenth of the project — meticulous and calendar-bound.

When the HEC overseas scholarship is not the right fit

For completeness, some strong candidates should not chase the Phase-III award at all. If your dream university lies outside the top-200 QS/THE list but still serves your career, the thematic-area restriction and the university-eligibility rule make the HEC slot a poor match — direct admission with other funding is the better route. If your subject does not sit inside the seven thematic areas (creative arts, design, law in some layouts, certain humanities), the quota cannot bend to include it, and your energy is better spent on the fully funded PhD roundup and the discipline-specific lists this site maintains. If you are unwilling to commit to the return-service obligation, the honest position is that the HEC award is a national-investment contract and another funder suits you better. None of these disqualify a talented applicant; they simply route that talent to the correct door. The respect comes from choosing the right door, not from fitting the wrong one.

Second applications, appeals and getting stronger after rejection

Finally, the prospect this guide owes its readers: what to do if the first application fails. HEC rejection is not a verdict on your worth; in the Phase-III world it is usually a file that lost the provincial ranking, or a document that tripped a formal screen, or a thematic-area mismatch. The rebuild plan after rejection has four parts: obtain and honestly read the feedback (where available) or diagnose the file yourself against this guide's checklist; strengthen the two highest-leverage inputs, typically the HAT score (resit at 70+) and the admission file (a strong unconditional admission to a top-200 university converts the whole fight into the exemption lane); refresh the references and the research plan with the current thematic wording; and reapply in the next batch with the discipline of the previous one already done. The winners of top scholarships are overwhelmingly reapplicants — the best funded masters and doctoral lists on this site repeat that truth for HEC, for CSC and for Chevening alike — and the second file, built on the first one's lessons, is usually the one that lands.

Women in the HEC pipeline: opportunities and the courage to apply

The HEC overseas scholarship is quietly one of Pakistan's most important vehicles for women's access to top-ranked education abroad, and the female applicant pool has grown every cycle. The eligibility rules are gender-neutral and the provincial quotas do not disadvantage women; the real barriers are the same ones the whole system faces — self-selection, under-prepared files and the family struggle over the return-service commitment — plus, for many, the added pressure of the distance-from-home question. The honest read for a female applicant is encouraging: strong female candidates are routinely over-represented among awardees precisely because the strongest files in many thematic areas are now built by women, and the international research networks a funded PhD builds are exactly the leverage that shifts a career permanently. The practical suggestions: apply early in your career so the age caps never bind; build the publication and supervisor edge early; and speak the research plan for the panel the way this guide's interview section prepares every applicant. The higher-education pipeline in Pakistan belongs to those who prepare and apply, and that pipeline has no gender dial.

From smaller, less-resourced universities: a note of encouragement

Applicants from the smaller and lesser-resourced universities of Pakistan too often convince themselves that HEC overseas scholarships belong to the graduates of the elite institutions. The evidence of the selection notes says otherwise: the ranking is built on the scholarship's own criteria — first division, CGPA 3.0, clean career record, HAT score, thematic fit — not on the brand of the alma mater. What the applicant from a regional university must compensate for, honestly, is the thinner research environment: fewer publication channels, less sophisticated lab culture, weaker supervision networks. The counterweight is the same one this guide pushes for everyone: a concrete research asset. A funded summer internship (the list is in our funded summer research guide), one published paper, or a supervisor who has spent a year pulling a publication out of you converts a "modest university" file into a story the panel reads as resilience and appetite. Apply, build the asset, and let the file speak for the work, not the campus.

The social-sciences lane reopened: a window worth taking

The 2026 widening of Phase-III into Social Sciences, Business & Governance matters more than most readers realise, because the scholarly pipeline in those fields has been starved of exactly this funding for years. The practical consequences for the applicant are three: the competition will be thinner than in the classic engineering lanes (many Pakistani candidates simply have not processed that the door reopened); the research plans in the social sciences must be built with the same rigour as an engineering proposal — data, method, question, Pakistan-context — because the panel is newly sensitive to waffle; and the alumni networks that follow the funding will value your subject as much as your university. An economics, development, governance or business applicant who reads this guide in September 2026 and builds the full preparation calendar before the next batch is positioning exactly where the early movers always want to be: in the first cohort after a door opens.

Final-week submission rituals: the last things that win

The last week before an HEC deadline is where careless applicants score own-goals, so here is the final-week checklist. Print the e-portal form and read it against your CNIC and transcripts for name/date mismatches; confirm the thematic area you selected matches the degree title on the university's website; verify every university on the list sits in the top-200 QS/THE ranking for this year; re-attest the one document a clerk might reject; keep the Rs. 3,000 fee receipt beside the application reference; and submit a full day before the deadline hour, never in the final hour, because the portal's last hours historically crawl. Then forward yourself the submission confirmation, save the reference number, and start preparing the interview narrative — the winner's file is always the one that felt finished three days early. That is the discipline, end to end, that turns a strong application into an HEC overseas scholarship worth lakhs in tuition and a career's worth of international access.

Building the file while working a full-time job

Employed applicants carry a hidden advantage and a hidden risk: the advantage is income, stability and the years of experience that strengthen the research plan; the risk is that the file slides down the priority list behind work, family and fatigue. The professional's calendar is brutally simple — six targeted months, one deliberate hour a day on HAT practice and a protected Sunday of document work each week. The transcript-equivalence, the CV with your job impact quantified, the references from a line manager and an academic, and the research plan's three pages of draft are all calendar items, not aspirations. Sit the HAT early enough to resit once if the score lands under the target, because the work-and-study calendar has no second chances built in. And above all, do not announce the application publicly until the file is safe; the pipeline to the department's NOC has its own patience, and the surprise of a complete file is the strongest negotiating position for the signatures that follow.

The file as a national investment: a closing word

The HEC overseas scholarship is technically a scholarship programme and strategically a national investment, and the honest closing word belongs to that duality. When HEC funds your fee, your stipend and your development year abroad, it is buying your expertise at the frontier of your field with the explicit contract that the expertise returns to Pakistan's universities, industries and laboratories. The winners honour that contract not because the agreement says so, but because the whole architecture — the thematic areas, the provincial quotas, the HAT, the award-and-return mechanism — is designed to convert individual ambition into national capacity. For the applicant reading this deep into the guide, the practical translation is: build the file that will make the investment obvious to the panel. Craft the research plan that solves a Pakistani problem with world-class method, present the transcript that proves the discipline, and let the return-service commitment sound in your voice as a mission rather than a clause. That is the complete HEC overseas scholarship story — and the applicants who read it this carefully are the ones who win the award.

Frequently asked questions

What are the eligibility criteria for the HEC overseas scholarship for MS/MPhil?+

For MS/MPhil you need 16 years of education with a minimum CGPA of 3.0 out of 4.0 (or its annual-system equivalent), at most one second division across your career, Pakistani or AJK nationality, and an age limit of 30 years for general applicants in 2026.

Do I need to take the HAT for the HEC overseas scholarship?+

Yes. Selection is based on your Higher Education Aptitude Test (HAT) score and an interview, with provincial quotas applied. Candidates holding an unconditional admission from a top 200 QS/THE university within a thematic area may be exempted from the test and interview.

Which universities can I choose for the HEC overseas scholarship?+

The programme restricts placement to universities ranked in the top 200 of the QS or Times Higher Education world rankings, and HEC reallocates universities and countries at its discretion for shortlisted candidates.

Does the HEC scholarship cover full tuition?+

The HEC overseas scholarship is a fully funded award covering tuition fees, a monthly stipend, return airfare, and related allowances, subject to the terms of the scholarship agreement you sign.

What other HEC scholarships exist for study abroad?+

Alongside the Phase-III overseas scholarship, HEC runs the US-Pakistan Knowledge Corridor PhD programme for top 100 US universities, the Commonwealth Scholarship nomination for the UK, the Faculty Development Programme, and Advanced Skills Development through International Scholarships (ASDIS) for computer and electrical fields.
