IELTS vs PTE vs TOEFL vs Duolingo: Which Test Should You Take in 2026?

🗓️ Aug 17, 2026⏱️ 28 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Why Choosing the Right English Test Matters

Choosing the wrong English proficiency test can cost you months of delay and hundreds of dollars. Every year, thousands of students either take a test that their target university does not accept, or they choose a test that does not align with their strengths. The result is wasted money, missed deadlines, and unnecessary stress.

In 2026, there are four major English proficiency tests that universities and immigration authorities around the world accept: IELTS, PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, and the Duolingo English Test. Each one has a different format, different scoring system, different cost, and different level of acceptance depending on the country you are applying to.

The test that works perfectly for a student applying to universities in Australia may be completely wrong for someone targeting the UK. The cheapest test may not be accepted for a Canadian visa. The fastest test may not be accepted by your dream university in Germany.

This is why choosing the right test is not just about which one is easiest or cheapest. It is about which one is the best fit for your specific situation — your target country, your target university, your budget, your timeline, and your personal strengths as an English speaker.

In this comprehensive guide, we compare all four tests across every dimension that matters: format, duration, cost, scoring, results speed, country-by-country acceptance, and which test is best for different types of students. By the end of this article, you will know exactly which test to take and how to prepare for it.

If you are also exploring countries that do not require English test scores at all, read our guide on studying abroad without IELTS.

Test Format Comparison: How Each Test Works

Understanding the format of each test is critical. The format determines how you prepare, how long the test takes, and what skills you need to demonstrate. Here is a detailed breakdown of each test's format.

IELTS Format

The International English Language Testing System (IELTS) has four sections:

  • Listening: 30 minutes. Four recordings of conversations and monologues. You answer 40 questions. Topics range from everyday social situations to academic lectures.
  • Reading: 60 minutes. Three passages from books, journals, magazines, and newspapers. 40 questions total. Academic version uses more complex texts.
  • Writing: 60 minutes. Two tasks. Task 1 requires you to describe a chart, graph, or diagram (150+ words). Task 2 is an essay (250+ words).
  • Speaking: 11–14 minutes. Face-to-face interview with an examiner. Three parts: introduction, long turn (2-minute speech on a topic), and discussion.

IELTS offers two test formats: IELTS Academic (for university admission) and IELTS General Training (for work visas and immigration). You can take the test on paper or on a computer at a test centre.

Key distinction: IELTS is the only major test where Speaking is conducted face-to-face with a human examiner. Many students find this more natural than speaking into a microphone, while others find it more stressful.

PTE Academic Format

The Pearson Test of English (PTE) Academic is entirely computer-based and uses AI scoring. The test has three sections:

  • Speaking and Writing: 54–67 minutes. This combined section includes reading aloud, repeating sentences, describing images, essay writing, and summarizing texts. It is the longest section and tests multiple skills simultaneously.
  • Reading: 29–30 minutes. Multiple-choice questions, re-order paragraphs, fill in the blanks, and reading comprehension. Includes integrated tasks that combine reading with other skills.
  • Listening: 30–43 minutes. Summarize spoken text, fill in the blanks, multiple-choice, and write from dictation. Audio clips are played once only.

PTE is known for its fast pace. Questions appear quickly, and you need to respond immediately. There is no time to reconsider or go back to previous answers. The test uses a wide variety of question types — over 20 different formats — which keeps you on your toes throughout.

All scoring is done by AI algorithms, which means no human bias in grading. Results are typically available within 2–5 days, making PTE one of the fastest tests for receiving scores.

See our full PTE Academic guide for 2026 for preparation tips and strategies.

TOEFL iBT Format

The Test of English as a Foreign Language (TOEFL) Internet-Based Test is the most academically oriented of the four tests. It has four sections:

  • Reading: 20 minutes. Two passages from academic texts with 10 questions each. Passages are on university-level topics like history, science, and social sciences.
  • Listening: 28 minutes. Lectures and conversations in academic settings. You take notes during audio clips and answer questions afterward.
  • Speaking: 16 minutes. Four tasks that combine reading, listening, and speaking. You speak into a microphone and responses are scored by AI and human raters.
  • Writing: 29 minutes. Two tasks: an integrated writing task (read a passage, listen to a lecture, write a summary) and an Academic Discussion task (contribute to a class discussion online).

TOEFL is heavily focused on academic English. The test simulates real university scenarios — attending lectures, reading textbooks, participating in discussions. If you are comfortable in academic environments, this test plays to your strengths.

Note-taking is essential for TOEFL. You will need to listen to lectures and take detailed notes before answering questions. Strong note-taking skills can significantly improve your TOEFL score.

TOEFL can be taken at a test centre or from home via TOEFL iBT Home Edition, which is proctored online. Check our TOEFL iBT complete guide for 2026 for more details.

Duolingo English Test Format

The Duolingo English Test is the newest and most unconventional of the four tests. It has three components:

  • Adaptive Test: 45 minutes. The test adjusts difficulty based on your answers. Questions include reading aloud, listening and typing, writing responses, and speaking into the camera. The test covers listening, reading, writing, and speaking in an integrated format.
  • Video Interview: Unscored. You record spoken responses to open-ended questions. These recordings are sent to universities along with your score.
  • Writing Sample: Unscored. You write a short response to a prompt. This is also sent to universities for their review.

The biggest advantage of Duolingo is convenience. You take the entire test at home on your own computer with a webcam, microphone, and stable internet connection. There is no need to travel to a test centre.

The adaptive format means the test gets harder as you answer correctly and easier as you answer incorrectly. This allows the test to pinpoint your exact proficiency level in a shorter time than traditional tests.

The video interview and writing sample are not scored but are sent directly to universities. Many admissions officers use these to get a fuller picture of the applicant beyond just the numerical score.

Scoring Comparison: Understanding the Numbers

Each test uses a different scoring system, which makes direct comparison confusing. Below, we explain each scoring system and provide a conversion table so you can compare scores across tests.

IELTS Scoring

IELTS uses a band scale from 0 to 9, with half-band increments (e.g., 6.0, 6.5, 7.0). You receive separate scores for Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking, plus an Overall Band Score which is the average of the four section scores.

Common IELTS bands and what they mean:

  • Band 9: Expert user — full command of the language
  • Band 8: Very good user — occasional inaccuracies
  • Band 7: Good user — operational command, some inaccuracies
  • Band 6: Competent user — generally effective, some errors
  • Band 5: Modest user — partial command, frequent limitations
  • Band 4: Limited user — basic competence only

Most universities require an overall band of 6.0 to 7.0 for undergraduate and postgraduate admission. Some competitive programs require 7.0 or higher. For visa purposes, requirements vary by country (see our IELTS score required for visa by country guide).

PTE Academic Scoring

PTE uses a scale from 10 to 90. You receive scores for four communicative skills: Listening, Reading, Speaking, and Writing. You also receive two enabling scores: Oral Fluency and Grammar. The Overall Score is based on the communicative skills.

PTE scores are entirely generated by AI algorithms. The system evaluates pronunciation, fluency, grammar, vocabulary, spelling, and discourse structure. This eliminates human bias but can sometimes penalize non-standard accents or speech patterns.

Key PTE score thresholds:

  • 85–90: Expert (equivalent to IELTS 9)
  • 76–84: Very good (equivalent to IELTS 8)
  • 65–75: Good (equivalent to IELTS 7)
  • 50–64: Competent (equivalent to IELTS 6–6.5)
  • 30–42: Modest (equivalent to IELTS 5)

TOEFL iBT Scoring

TOEFL uses a scale from 0 to 120. Each of the four sections (Reading, Listening, Speaking, Writing) is scored from 0 to 30. Your total score is the sum of all four section scores.

TOEFL scoring is more granular than IELTS, which means small differences in ability can result in different scores. The scoring combines AI scoring (for Speaking and Writing) with human review for quality assurance.

Key TOEFL score thresholds:

  • 100–120: Excellent (equivalent to IELTS 7.5–9)
  • 80–99: Good (equivalent to IELTS 6.5–7.5)
  • 60–79: Fair (equivalent to IELTS 5.5–6.5)
  • 40–59: Limited (equivalent to IELTS 4.5–5.5)

Many top US universities require a total TOEFL score of 80 to 100, with some requiring 100+ for competitive programs.

Duolingo English Test Scoring

Duolingo uses a scale from 10 to 160, with 5-point increments. The score is a single composite number that measures overall proficiency. Subscores are also provided for Literacy, Comprehension, Conversation, and Production.

The scoring is entirely AI-based and uses the adaptive nature of the test to calculate your proficiency level. Because the test adjusts to your level in real-time, the scoring is considered precise even within the short test duration.

Key Duolingo score thresholds:

  • 140–160: Advanced (equivalent to IELTS 7.5–9)
  • 120–135: Upper-intermediate (equivalent to IELTS 6.5–7.5)
  • 100–115: Intermediate (equivalent to IELTS 5.5–6.5)
  • 80–95: Lower-intermediate (equivalent to IELTS 4.5–5.5)

Value for Money Analysis

Duolingo at $65 is by far the cheapest option — roughly one-third the cost of IELTS. However, cost should not be your primary decision factor. If your target institution or visa authority does not accept Duolingo, paying $65 is wasted money.

PTE offers good value because of its free score reports. If you are applying to multiple universities, you save significantly compared to IELTS and TOEFL, which charge per report. Over five applications, PTE can save you $75–$100 in report fees alone.

IELTS is the most expensive, but the One Skill Retake option can save money if you only need to improve one section. Instead of paying for a full retake, you can retake just the section you scored lowest in.

Acceptance by Country: Where Is Each Test Valid?

This is the most critical section of this guide. The best test in the world is useless if your target country or university does not accept it. Here is the country-by-country breakdown.

United Kingdom

The UK has the strictest English test requirements of any country. For student visa purposes, the UK government only accepts:

  • IELTS for UKVI (Secure English Language Test — SELT)
  • PTE Academic UKVI (taken at a UKVI-approved test centre)
  • TOEFL iBT (accepted for visa purposes since 2024)
  • LanguageCert and Trinity College London exams

Duolingo is NOT accepted for UK student visas. This is one of the biggest limitations of Duolingo. Even if a UK university accepts Duolingo for admission, you still need an approved test for the visa application.

The UKVI version of IELTS and PTE are specific tests taken at approved centres. You cannot use a regular IELTS or PTE score for a UK visa — it must be the UKVI version.

For university admission (not visa), many UK universities also accept regular IELTS, TOEFL, PTE, and in some cases Duolingo. But the visa requirement is non-negotiable.

Check our detailed IELTS score requirements by country for UK-specific requirements.

United States

The US is the most flexible country for English test acceptance. All four tests are widely accepted:

  • IELTS: Accepted by virtually all US universities
  • PTE: Accepted by most US universities (growing acceptance)
  • TOEFL: Strongest acceptance — preferred by many US universities, especially Ivy League and top-ranked schools
  • Duolingo: Accepted by 3,000+ US institutions, including many top universities

TOEFL has the strongest brand recognition in the US because it was developed by ETS, an American organization. Some US universities still list TOEFL as their preferred test, though most now accept all four equally.

For US visa purposes (F-1 student visa), you need to demonstrate English proficiency. Most US embassies accept IELTS, TOEFL, and PTE. Duolingo may be accepted by some consulates but is less established for visa purposes.

Canada

Canada has specific requirements depending on the visa pathway:

  • Student Direct Stream (SDS): Requires IELTS 6.0 in each band OR PTE 60+ overall. Duolingo and TOEFL are NOT accepted for SDS.
  • Regular Study Permit: Most Designated Learning Institutions (DLIs) accept IELTS, TOEFL, PTE, and increasingly Duolingo for admission. The visa officer has discretion on which test scores to accept.
  • Express Entry (PR): IELTS General Training orCELPIP are the standard tests. PTE is also accepted. TOEFL and Duolingo are not accepted for immigration.

If you are planning to use the SDS fast-track route to Canada (which processes visas in 20 days), you must take IELTS or PTE. This is one of the most important test selection factors for Canadian immigration.

For more details, see our complete guide to studying in Canada.

Australia

Australia has strong acceptance for IELTS and PTE:

  • IELTS: The most widely accepted test. Required for student visas, skilled migration, and professional registration.
  • PTE Academic: Very strong acceptance. PTE was actually developed in Australia and is particularly popular there. Accepted for all visa categories.
  • TOEFL: Accepted by most universities for admission, but less commonly used for visa purposes.
  • Duolingo: Limited acceptance. Some universities accept it for admission, but it is NOT accepted for student visa purposes by the Australian government.

For Australian student visas, the Department of Home Affairs accepts IELTS, PTE, TOEFL, Cambridge C1 Advanced, and OET. Duolingo is not on this list.

If Australia is your target, PTE is an excellent choice — it is fast, widely accepted, and often preferred by Australian institutions.

Germany

Germany's requirements vary by university:

  • IELTS: Universally accepted by all German universities
  • TOEFL: Widely accepted, especially by technical universities
  • PTE: Growing acceptance, accepted by many universities
  • Duolingo: Accepted by some universities but not all. Check individual university requirements.

For German student visas, the embassy generally accepts IELTS, TOEFL, and PTE. Some German embassies also accept Duolingo for visa purposes, but this varies by region.

Note that many German universities offer programs in English, but some require German language proficiency (TestDaF or DSH) in addition to English proficiency.

New Zealand

  • IELTS: The primary test — accepted for all visa types
  • PTE Academic: Widely accepted for visas and university admission
  • TOEFL: Accepted by universities, accepted for some visa categories
  • Duolingo: Limited acceptance, not accepted for visa purposes

New Zealand immigration primarily uses IELTS and PTE as their benchmark tests. If you are targeting New Zealand, these two tests give you the most options.

Decision Guide: Which Test Should You Take?

Now that you understand the format, scoring, cost, and acceptance of each test, here is a practical decision guide. Choose the scenario that best matches your situation.

Choose IELTS if…

  • You are applying to universities in the UK, Australia, Canada, or New Zealand
  • You need a test that is accepted for both university admission AND visa purposes
  • You prefer face-to-face speaking with a human examiner
  • You want the most universally accepted test worldwide
  • You are applying to multiple countries simultaneously
  • You are comfortable with paper-based or computer-based testing
  • You want the option of One Skill Retake to improve a single section

IELTS is the safest choice for students who want maximum flexibility. It is the test most accepted by immigration authorities worldwide, and it works for university admission in virtually every country.

Choose PTE if…

  • You want fast results (2–5 days)
  • You are comfortable with computer-based testing and speaking into a microphone
  • You are applying to Australia (where PTE is extremely popular)
  • You want free score reports sent to unlimited institutions
  • You prefer AI scoring over human judgment
  • You want a test that is shorter than IELTS (2 hours vs 2h45m)
  • You are applying for Canadian SDS (PTE 60+ qualifies)

PTE is the best choice for students who want speed and convenience. The AI scoring removes the variability of human examiners, and the fast results mean you can meet tight application deadlines.

Choose TOEFL if…

  • You are applying to universities in the USA (especially top-ranked)
  • You are comfortable with academic English and university-level content
  • You are a strong note-taker who can process lectures effectively
  • You prefer a test that closely simulates real university experiences
  • You want to take the test at home (TOEFL Home Edition available)
  • Your target university specifically prefers or requires TOEFL

TOEFL is the test of choice for serious academic candidates targeting US universities. Its focus on academic skills means your TOEFL score demonstrates your readiness for university-level coursework.

Choose Duolingo if…

  • Budget is tight — at $65, it is the most affordable option
  • You need results urgently (48 hours)
  • You want to take the test at home without traveling to a test centre
  • You are applying to US universities that accept Duolingo
  • You want to take a practice test first (free on the Duolingo website)
  • You are applying to universities that accept it for admission but not for visa

Duolingo is the best starting point for students on a budget or with tight timelines. However, always verify acceptance with your specific target institutions and visa authorities before committing to Duolingo.

Pros and Cons of Each Test

Here is a balanced look at the strengths and weaknesses of each test to help you make an informed decision.

IELTS — Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • Most widely accepted test globally (140+ countries)
  • Accepted for student visas, work visas, PR, and immigration in most countries
  • Face-to-face speaking feels more natural for many students
  • Paper or computer test options available
  • One Skill Retake option saves time and money
  • British Council and IDP have extensive test centre networks

Cons:

  • Most expensive test ($235–270)
  • Paper test results take 13 days (slow)
  • Human scoring can be subjective
  • Speaking test scheduling can be inconvenient (may be on a different day)
  • Limited availability in some remote areas
  • UKVI versions cost more and must be taken at specific centres

PTE Academic — Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • Fast results (2–5 days)
  • AI scoring eliminates human bias
  • Free unlimited score reports
  • Shorter test duration (2 hours)
  • Computer-based format suits tech-savvy students
  • Very popular in Australia with strong institutional support

Cons:

  • Not accepted for UK visas unless taking UKVI version
  • Speaking into microphone can feel unnatural
  • Fast pace can be overwhelming for slow processors
  • AI scoring may penalize non-standard accents
  • Fewer test centre locations than IELTS
  • Less accepted in North America compared to IELTS and TOEFL

TOEFL iBT — Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • Strongest acceptance at US universities
  • Home Edition available for remote test-takers
  • Academic content closely mirrors real university work
  • Detailed score breakdown by skill (0–30 per section)
  • Accepted for US visa purposes
  • Widely accepted in 160+ countries for university admission

Cons:

  • Academic focus can be challenging for non-academic backgrounds
  • Speaking section requires strong note-taking and multitasking
  • Not always accepted for visa purposes in all countries
  • More expensive than PTE and Duolingo
  • Results take 4–8 days (slower than PTE and Duolingo)
  • Complex integrated tasks can be confusing for first-time test-takers

Duolingo English Test — Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • Cheapest test at $65
  • Fastest results (48 hours)
  • Take at home — no need to travel to a test centre
  • Free practice test available on the website
  • Adaptive format accurately measures proficiency
  • Unlimited free score reports

Cons:

  • NOT accepted for UK student visas
  • NOT accepted for Canadian SDS
  • NOT accepted for Australian student visas
  • Limited acceptance in Europe and Asia compared to IELTS/TOEFL
  • Accepted by only 5,000+ institutions (far fewer than IELTS or TOEFL)
  • Video interview and writing sample are unscored but sent to universities
  • Requires stable internet, webcam, and quiet room at home
  • Less established track record than the other three tests

Key Differences That Matter Most

While all four tests measure English proficiency, there are critical differences that can determine which test is right for you. Here are the factors that matter most in 2026.

Test Centre vs Home Testing

IELTS, PTE, and TOEFL all require you to visit a physical test centre (TOEFL also offers a home edition). This means you need to:

  • Travel to the centre on test day
  • Follow strict rules about what you can bring
  • Perform under surveillance with other test-takers nearby
  • Deal with potential distractions from other candidates

Duolingo and TOEFL Home Edition let you take the test from home. This eliminates travel stress and allows you to test in a familiar environment. However, you need a quiet room, a stable internet connection, a webcam, and a microphone.

If you perform better in formal, controlled environments, test centre-based tests may suit you better. If you prefer the comfort of your own space, home-based testing is a significant advantage.

Human vs AI Scoring

The scoring method significantly affects your experience:

  • IELTS: Human-scored for Speaking and Writing. This means your score can be influenced by the examiner's judgment, accent familiarity, and subjective evaluation. Some students find this unfair; others find it more accurate.
  • PTE: 100% AI-scored. No human judgment involved. This eliminates bias but can feel rigid — especially if your accent is non-standard or your speaking style differs from what the AI expects.
  • TOEFL: Mixed scoring. AI scores Speaking and Writing, with human raters providing quality checks. This combines the consistency of AI with the nuance of human evaluation.
  • Duolingo: 100% AI-scored. Similar to PTE in its reliance on algorithms. The adaptive format means the AI is constantly adjusting to your level.

If you have a strong accent or non-standard speaking patterns, human examiners (IELTS) may be more forgiving than AI systems (PTE, Duolingo). Conversely, if you are a clear, standard speaker, AI scoring may give you more consistent results.

Score Report Flexibility

How you send your scores to institutions varies significantly:

  • IELTS: You get 5 free score reports. Additional reports cost $15–20 each. You must request reports before or at the test.
  • PTE: Unlimited free score reports. You can send your scores to as many institutions as you want at no extra cost. This is a major advantage for students applying to multiple universities.
  • TOEFL: You can send scores to 4 institutions for free. Additional reports cost $20 each.
  • Duolingo: Unlimited free score reports. Like PTE, you can share your scores with as many institutions as needed.

If you are applying to many universities, the cost of score reports can add up. PTE and Duolingo offer the most flexibility with free unlimited reports.

Special Situations: Test Selection for Specific Scenarios

Here are recommendations for specific situations that many students face.

If You Are Applying to Multiple Countries

Take IELTS. It is the only test accepted everywhere for both university admission and visa purposes. Taking IELTS gives you maximum flexibility to apply to the UK, USA, Canada, Australia, Germany, New Zealand, and other countries without worrying about acceptance issues.

If You Need a Visa for the UK

You must take IELTS for UKVI or PTE Academic UKVI or TOEFL iBT. Regular IELTS and PTE scores are NOT accepted. Plan early, as UKVI test centres may have limited availability. Budget extra time and money for the UKVI-specific test.

If You Are on a Tight Budget

Start with Duolingo at $65. Many US and some international universities accept it for admission. If you later need a visa-specific test, you can take IELTS or PTE. This way, you spend the least amount possible upfront while keeping your options open.

If You Need Results in Under a Week

Take Duolingo (48 hours) or PTE (2–5 days). Both deliver results faster than IELTS or TOEFL. If your application deadline is approaching and you do not have test scores yet, these two tests are your best options.

If You Are Applying to Top US Universities

While all four tests are accepted, TOEFL is still the strongest choice for elite US institutions. Many Ivy League universities have a long history with TOEFL and list it as their preferred test. A strong TOEFL score (100+) signals that you are well-prepared for American academic life.

If You Are Targeting Australia or Canada

For Australia: PTE or IELTS are your best options. Both are accepted for student visas and university admission. PTE is often faster and more convenient.

For Canada SDS: You must take IELTS (6.0 each band) or PTE (60+ overall). No other tests qualify for the SDS fast-track route.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Here are the most common mistakes students make when choosing and taking English proficiency tests.

  1. Choosing based on price alone. Duolingo is cheap but not accepted everywhere. If your target university or visa requires IELTS or PTE, paying $65 on Duolingo is wasted money.
  2. Not checking visa requirements. A university may accept Duolingo for admission, but the visa authority may not. Always check both the university AND the immigration authority requirements.
  3. Taking the test without preparation. All four tests require preparation. Even if you are fluent in English, the test format, timing, and question types require practice.
  4. Ignoring the UKVI requirement. For UK visas, you MUST take the UKVI version of IELTS or PTE. Regular versions are not accepted. This catches many students off guard.
  5. Waiting too long to take the test. Book your test early, especially during peak application seasons (September–January). Test centre slots fill up quickly.
  6. Not using the free practice tests. All four tests offer free practice materials. Use them to assess your current level before deciding which test to take.
  7. Retaking the test without changing preparation strategy. If you scored below your target, analyze what went wrong before retaking. Take a different approach to preparation rather than just hoping for a better score.

Timeline: When to Take Your Test

Timing your test correctly is crucial. Here is a recommended timeline for students planning to start university in September 2027 (the most common intake).

  • 12 months before (September 2026): Research your target universities and their English test requirements. Take a free practice test for IELTS, PTE, TOEFL, and Duolingo to see which one you score highest on.
  • 10 months before (November 2026): Choose your test and begin focused preparation. Use official materials and practice tests.
  • 8 months before (January 2027): Take your first test attempt. If you reach your target score, you are done. If not, you have time to retake.
  • 6 months before (March 2027): Retake if needed. Most tests have no waiting period between attempts.
  • 5 months before (April 2027): Send your scores to universities. Deadlines for September intake are typically between April and June.
  • 4 months before (May 2027): Use your scores in your visa application. Remember that scores are valid for 2 years, so your test from January 2027 will still be valid.

This timeline gives you multiple attempts without the pressure of last-minute testing. The worst scenario is taking the test one week before your application deadline with no time for a retake.

Final Verdict: Our Recommendation

After comparing all four tests across every dimension, here is our straightforward recommendation:

For maximum flexibility and safety, take IELTS. It works everywhere, for every purpose, in every country. Yes, it is the most expensive and the longest test, but it is also the most reliable choice. You will never face a situation where your IELTS score is not accepted.

For speed and convenience, take PTE. Fast results, AI scoring, free score reports, and strong acceptance in Australia, Canada, and growing acceptance worldwide. If you are comfortable with computer-based testing, PTE is an excellent all-around choice.

For US university applications, take TOEFL. It is the gold standard for American higher education. A strong TOEFL score demonstrates your readiness for the academic rigor of US universities.

For budget-conscious students, start with Duolingo. At $65, it is low-risk and fast. Use it for initial university applications, then take IELTS or PTE later if needed for visa purposes. Think of Duolingo as your first step, not your only step.

Ultimately, the "best" test is the one that your target institutions accept, that fits your budget, that you can prepare for effectively, and that you can take with confidence. Choose wisely, prepare thoroughly, and you will achieve the score you need.

Frequently asked questions

Which English test is the easiest?+

It depends on your strengths. PTE suits students comfortable with computers and faster-paced tasks. IELTS suits students who prefer face-to-face speaking and paper-based writing. TOEFL suits strong note-takers who prefer academic English. Duolingo is the shortest and most convenient but accepted by fewer institutions.

Which English test is the cheapest?+

Duolingo is the cheapest at USD 65 globally. PTE ranges from USD 180-220. TOEFL ranges from USD 200-250. IELTS is the most expensive at USD 235-270. However, price should not be the only factor — acceptance by your target institutions matters more.

Which English test gives results fastest?+

Duolingo gives results within 48 hours. PTE gives results in 2-5 days. TOEFL gives results in 4-8 days. IELTS computer test gives results in 3-5 days, paper test in 13 days. If you need results urgently, Duolingo or PTE are the fastest options.

Which English test is accepted by the most countries?+

IELTS is the most widely accepted globally — accepted for student visas, work visas, PR and immigration in 140+ countries. PTE is accepted in 70+ countries. TOEFL is accepted in 160+ countries for university admission but not always for visas. Duolingo is accepted by 5,000+ institutions but not for UK visas.

Can I use PTE for a UK student visa?+

Yes, but only PTE Academic UKVI (taken at a UKVI-approved centre). Regular PTE Academic is not accepted for UK visas. You need PTE Academic UKVI with a minimum score equivalent to IELTS 5.5 per band (B2) for direct entry.

Can I use Duolingo for a Canada student visa?+

Duolingo is accepted by many Canadian universities for admission, but it is NOT accepted for the SDS (Student Direct Stream) route. For SDS, you need IELTS 6.0 or PTE 60+. For regular study permits, some institutions accept Duolingo depending on the DLI.

Which test should I take if applying to multiple countries?+

IELTS is the safest choice if applying to multiple countries, as it is accepted everywhere for both university admission and visa purposes. PTE is the second most versatile. If you are only applying to the USA, TOEFL is strong. If budget is tight, start with Duolingo and take IELTS/PTE if needed for visa.

What is the score conversion between IELTS, PTE, TOEFL and Duolingo?+

IELTS 6.0 ≈ PTE 50-57 ≈ TOEFL 79-93 ≈ Duolingo 95-105. IELTS 6.5 ≈ PTE 58-64 ≈ TOEFL 94-101 ≈ Duolingo 105-115. IELTS 7.0 ≈ PTE 65-72 ≈ TOEFL 102-110 ≈ Duolingo 115-125. These are approximate — always check the specific institution's requirements.

How long are all English test scores valid?+

IELTS, PTE, TOEFL and Duolingo scores are all valid for 2 years from the test date. This is a universal standard across all major English proficiency tests. Most institutions and visa authorities require results within the last 2 years.

Can I retake any of these tests?+

Yes. All four tests can be retaken as many times as needed. IELTS: no waiting period, plus One Skill Retake available. PTE: no waiting period. TOEFL: no waiting period. Duolingo: must wait for results (48 hours) before retaking. There is no limit on the number of attempts for any test.
