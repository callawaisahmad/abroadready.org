Study in UK from Pakistan Without IELTS 2026: Complete Guide

🗓️ Aug 17, 2026⏱️ 14 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Every year, thousands of Pakistani students begin the process of applying to UK universities only to stall at the IELTS requirement. The test is expensive, the preparation takes months, and a single band score below the threshold can derail an otherwise strong application. The good news is that IELTS is no longer the only path. More than 150 UK universities now accept PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, Duolingo English Test, and Cambridge C1 Advanced for admission. Some even waive the English requirement entirely if your previous degree was taught in English.

There is one important distinction to understand from the start. University admission and the UK student visa have separate English language requirements. Universities set their own rules and accept a wide range of tests. The UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) authority, however, requires an approved Secure English Language Test (SELT) for the visa itself. This guide covers both, showing you exactly which tests work at each stage and how to plan around them.

Key takeaways

  • More than 150 UK universities accept PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, or Duolingo for admission alongside IELTS.
  • For the UK student visa, the UKVI-approved tests are IELTS for UKVI, PTE Academic UKVI, TOEFL iBT Home Edition, and Cambridge C1 Advanced.
  • Typical alternative scores: PTE 50–65, TOEFL 72–100, Duolingo 100–120 for undergraduate and postgraduate programmes.
  • Chevening and Commonwealth Scholarships fully fund Pakistani students for master's study in the UK.
  • Total first-year budget ranges from GBP 28,000 to 55,000 depending on city and programme.
  • Apply by October for September intake; scholarship deadlines are often earlier.

UK universities that accept PTE Academic

The PTE Academic test is the most widely accepted IELTS alternative in the UK. It is computer-scored, results arrive within two to five working days, and most UK visa offices accept the UKVI version. Below are major UK universities and their typical PTE requirements:

  • University of Glasgow — PTE 60–65 overall
  • University of Bristol — PTE 67 overall with no less than 60 in each component
  • University of Leeds — PTE 60–64 overall
  • University of Sheffield — PTE 61–67 overall depending on programme
  • Queen Mary University of London — PTE 62–67 overall
  • University of Nottingham — PTE 65 overall
  • Newcastle University — PTE 62–70 overall
  • University of Birmingham — PTE 59–76 overall depending on programme
  • University of Warwick — PTE 59–76 overall depending on programme

These are Russell Group and internationally ranked institutions. A PTE score in the 60–67 range opens the door to a significant number of competitive programmes. For more detail on English test comparisons, see our IELTS guidance page.

Universities that accept Duolingo English Test

The Duolingo English Test is cheaper than both IELTS and PTE, can be taken from home, and delivers results within 48 hours. It is increasingly accepted by UK universities, particularly for postgraduate programmes:

  • University of Aberdeen — Duolingo 105–110
  • De Montfort University — Duolingo 105–115
  • University of East London — Duolingo 100–110
  • London South Bank University — Duolingo 105–115
  • University of Greenwich — Duolingo 100–110
  • University of West London — Duolingo 100–110

Duolingo is a strong option for university admission, but remember it is not on the UKVI SELT list. You will still need an approved SELT for the visa stage.

Universities that accept TOEFL iBT

TOEFL iBT is accepted by virtually every UK university. If you have already taken TOEFL or prefer its format, you will have no difficulty finding programmes that accept it:

  • UCL — TOEFL 92–100 overall with minimum component scores
  • Imperial College London — TOEFL 92 overall
  • University of Edinburgh — TOEFL 92 overall
  • King's College London — TOEFL 92–100 overall
  • University of Manchester — TOEFL 88–100 overall

TOEFL is accepted for both admission and, in its UKVI-approved format, for the student visa. This makes it a practical choice if you want one test that covers both stages.

UK student visa process: step by step

The English language test is one piece of a multi-step process. Here is the full sequence for a Tier 4 (Student) visa application from Pakistan:

Step 1: Get an unconditional offer. Apply to your chosen UK university through UCAS (for undergraduate) or directly (for postgraduate). You need an unconditional acceptance before you can proceed with the visa.

Step 2: Prepare financial evidence. You must demonstrate that you can cover tuition fees plus living costs of GBP 1,334 per month for London or GBP 1,023 per month outside London, held for at least 28 consecutive days. The funds must be in your name or a parent's name.

Step 3: Take an approved English test. For the visa, you need a UKVI-approved SELT. The accepted options are IELTS for UKVI, PTE Academic UKVI, TOEFL iBT Home Edition, and Cambridge C1 Advanced. Book this test well in advance, as slots fill quickly in Pakistan.

Step 4: Apply online and pay the IHS. Complete the student visa application on the UK government website. Pay the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) of GBP 776 per year, which gives you access to the NHS.

Step 5: Biometrics and decision. Attend a biometrics appointment at a UK visa application centre in Pakistan (Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad). Standard processing takes approximately three to six weeks. Priority services may be available for faster decisions.

Scholarships for Pakistani students in the UK

Funding is the second major concern after English requirements. Several prestigious schemes are open to Pakistani applicants:

Chevening Scholarships are the UK government's flagship awards for future leaders. They cover full tuition within an annual cap, a monthly living stipend, return economy flights, and visa fees. The programme requires around two years of work experience and centres on four leadership essays. Applications typically open in August and close in early November. See our complete Chevening guide for details.

Commonwealth Scholarships are fully funded awards for students from Commonwealth countries, including Pakistan. They are available for master's and doctoral study and cover tuition, living costs, flights, and thesis grants. Applications are made through the Higher Education Commission (HEC) of Pakistan.

GREAT Scholarships are jointly funded by the British Council and UK universities. They offer GBP 10,000 toward tuition fees and are available for students from Pakistan studying at specific UK institutions. Each university sets its own eligibility criteria.

University-specific awards are also worth investigating. The Clarendon Fund at Oxford, Gates Cambridge Scholarship, and university merit awards of GBP 2,000 to 5,000 at institutions like Glasgow, Leeds, and Sheffield can significantly reduce costs. Many universities automatically consider you for merit scholarships when you apply, so no separate application is needed.

Cost breakdown: studying in the UK from Pakistan

Understanding the total cost is essential for planning. Here is a realistic breakdown:

  • Tuition fees: GBP 11,000 to 38,000 per year depending on programme and university. Humanities and social sciences tend to be on the lower end; engineering, business, and medical programmes are higher.
  • Living costs (London): approximately GBP 15,480 per year, covering accommodation, food, transport and other essentials.
  • Living costs (outside London): approximately GBP 12,168 per year. Cities like Glasgow, Leeds, Sheffield and Birmingham offer significantly lower living costs than London.
  • Immigration Health Surcharge: GBP 776 per year, payable upfront for the duration of your visa.
  • Visa application fee: GBP 490.
  • Total first-year budget: GBP 28,000 to 55,000 depending on city, university, and lifestyle.

Pakistani students should also budget for the English test (GBP 150–200 for PTE, GBP 180–250 for IELTS), airfare, and initial settling-in costs. For a comprehensive budgeting guide, see our cost and budget guide.

Application timeline for September 2027 intake

Planning ahead is the single most important thing you can do. Here is a month-by-month timeline:

  • August–September 2026: Research programmes and universities. Book and take your English test (PTE, TOEFL, or Duolingo for admission; IELTS for UKVI or PTE Academic UKVI for the visa).
  • October–November 2026: Submit applications through UCAS (undergraduate deadline is usually late January) or directly to universities (postgraduate). Begin preparing Chevening or Commonwealth Scholarship applications.
  • December 2026–January 2027: Receive conditional or unconditional offers. Follow up on any missing documents.
  • February–April 2027: Apply for scholarships (Chevening closes in November, Commonwealth typically in December, GREAT scholarships vary by university). Accept your firm university offer.
  • May–July 2027: Prepare financial evidence. Apply for the student visa. Book accommodation.
  • August 2027: Receive visa. Book flights. Arrive in the UK and attend orientation.

Can you work while studying in the UK?

Yes. International students on a Tier 4 visa can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during holidays. This is a significant advantage for Pakistani students, as part-time work can help offset living costs. After graduation, the Graduate Route visa allows you to stay and work in the UK for two years (three years for doctoral graduates) without needing a sponsor.

Final thoughts

The IELTS barrier is real but not insurmountable. With PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, and Duolingo now accepted by the majority of UK universities, Pakistani students have more pathways than ever. The key is understanding which test works for your university admission and which one you need for the visa, then planning accordingly. Start early, take the right test, and the UK becomes genuinely accessible. For more on studying in the UK, visit our study in the UK overview page.

Frequently asked questions

Can I study in the UK from Pakistan without IELTS?+

Yes. Many UK universities accept PTE Academic, Duolingo English Test, TOEFL iBT, and Cambridge C1 Advanced instead of IELTS. Some universities also accept a medium-of-instruction letter from your previous degree institution, or may waive English requirements entirely if your prior education was in English.

What PTE score do UK universities accept?+

Most UK universities accept PTE Academic scores between 50 and 65 overall for undergraduate and postgraduate programmes. Russell Group universities typically require 62 to 70. Some universities accept PTE for the UKVI (SELT) version for visa purposes.

Is Duolingo accepted for UK student visa?+

Duolingo is accepted by over 150 UK universities for admission, but it is not currently on the UKVI SELT list. This means it works for university admission but not for the student visa itself. For the visa you still need IELTS for UKVI, PTE Academic UKVI, or another approved SELT.

How much does it cost to study in the UK from Pakistan?+

Tuition ranges from approximately GBP 11,000 to 38,000 per year depending on programme and university. Living costs are GBP 12,000 to 15,000 per year in London or GBP 9,000 to 12,000 outside London. Total first-year budget including visa and IHS is typically GBP 28,000 to 55,000.

What scholarships are available for Pakistani students in the UK?+

Chevening Scholarships (fully funded, leadership-focused), Commonwealth Scholarships, GREAT Scholarships, and university-specific awards like Clarendon (Oxford) and Gates Cambridge. Many UK universities also offer automatic merit scholarships of GBP 2,000 to 5,000 for strong applicants.
