KAIST International Scholarships Guide

🗓️ Aug 5, 2026⏱️ 9 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

What are the KAIST International Scholarships? They are the main funding route for international students at the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology, South Korea's leading research university in science and technology. KAIST offers full-tuition scholarships, monthly stipends, and health insurance to international students across its undergraduate and graduate programs. Because the scholarships are awarded automatically through the admission application, many students do not realise how accessible the funding is.

How do the KAIST International Scholarships work? They are awarded automatically to international students who are admitted to a KAIST degree program, so there is no separate scholarship essay, no separate form, and no separate deadline to track. The funding is attached to the admission outcome rather than sitting in a separate competition. That single design decision makes KAIST one of the most accessible fully funded options in Asia for students who want an engineering, science, or technology degree.

This guide explains how KAIST International Scholarships work, what they cover, who is eligible, how the application process fits together, and what life looks like before and after graduation. Every section below is written as a direct question so you can jump straight to the answer you need. If you want a fully funded education in engineering, science, or technology in Asia, KAIST is one of the strongest options available.

Key takeaways

What do the KAIST International Scholarships actually give me?

  • The KAIST International Scholarship covers full tuition for international students plus a monthly living stipend.
  • It also provides national health insurance to international students during their studies.
  • The scholarship is automatic: you are considered when you apply for admission to a KAIST degree program, with no separate form.
  • Graduate students in many programs can combine the scholarship with research assistantship pay from their lab.
  • Deadlines vary by admissions track, so plan around your program's specific application window.

Do I need to apply for the scholarship separately?

No. The single most useful fact about the KAIST International Scholarship is that it is not a separate application. You are considered for it at the moment you submit your admission application to a KAIST degree program, and the award travels with your admission offer. There is no extra essay, no extra form, no extra deadline, and no separate committee. Everything you do to strengthen your admission application is also the thing that strengthens your chances of the funding.

Can I combine the scholarship with other income?

Yes, in most cases. Graduate students who join a research lab frequently receive research assistantship pay on top of the base stipend, and a university or lab-level arrangement can supplement the standard package. What you usually cannot do is hold the KAIST funding at the same time as another award that already covers your tuition and fees, so read the terms of any scholarship you are offered before accepting two that overlap.

What is the one thing I must not get wrong?

The dates. Because the funding follows the admission calendar, there is no separate scholarship deadline to save you. If you miss your program's application window, the funding opportunity disappears with it. Plan backwards from your specific track's closing date, not forwards from whenever you feel ready.

What is the KAIST International Scholarship?

What is KAIST, and why does that matter for funding?

KAIST, based in Daejeon, South Korea, is a research university founded in 1971 to train scientists and engineers. It is consistently ranked among the best universities in Asia and the world for engineering and technology. To attract top international talent, KAIST funds incoming international students through the KAIST International Scholarship, which is granted automatically based on admission merit. The university's research focus is the reason the funding is generous: a place at KAIST is cheap for the institution to justify when the student will go on to produce research, publications, and industry talent.

How does an automatic scholarship award actually work?

This design is unusual and valuable: instead of requiring a separate scholarship essay and timeline, the scholarship rides on your admission application. If you are admitted as an international student, you typically receive the funding as part of your offer. The admissions committee assesses academic excellence, research potential, and the overall competitiveness of your application, and the funding decision follows from that assessment rather than from a separate funding panel with separate criteria.

Why do so few applicants know this exists?

Because most scholarship searches return national programmes with visible application portals, and an automatic university award does not show up in that kind of search. Students accustomed to writing scholarship essays, collecting recommendation letters for a funding committee, and tracking funding deadlines often assume KAIST works the same way. It does not. Recognising the automatic model early is often the difference between applying to KAIST as a fully funded option and applying to KAIST as an expensive one you cannot afford.

What does the scholarship cover?

Is the KAIST scholarship a comprehensive package? Yes. The KAIST International Scholarship is a comprehensive package. It typically covers:

  • Full tuition. The entire cost of tuition for your degree program is waived.
  • A monthly stipend. You receive a monthly allowance for living expenses; for graduate students this is around KRW 350,000 per month, and for undergraduates it varies by level.
  • Health insurance. KAIST supports international students with national health insurance coverage.

Does the scholarship really cover the full tuition fee?

Yes, tuition is waived in full for the awardees. That is the single largest financial item for an international student in Korea, and removing it is what turns a KAIST offer into a genuinely funded one. It also removes a hidden cost: international tuition rates at Korean universities are substantially higher than domestic rates, so an award that covers only part of the fee would leave a gap you have to fund yourself.

How large is the monthly stipend, and is it enough to live on?

For graduate students, the stipend is around KRW 350,000 per month; for undergraduates it varies by level. The amount is designed to cover living costs in Daejeon, where the main campus is located. Daejeon is significantly more affordable than Seoul, and the stipend is generally sufficient for a comfortable standard of living, though personal spending habits and lifestyle choices will affect how far it stretches. If you are used to Seoul rents or eating out every night, recalibrate your expectations before you arrive.

Is health insurance part of the package?

Yes. KAIST supports international students with national health insurance coverage, and the support office helps international students register for it. This matters more than it sounds: without insurance, an illness or injury in a foreign country becomes a serious financial event, and national coverage removes that risk for the duration of your studies.

Can graduate students earn more than the base stipend?

Often, yes. Graduate students who join research labs also earn research assistantship salaries from their professors, which can add significantly to the base stipend. This is one of the most attractive features of the graduate funding model: the scholarship covers your tuition and your baseline living costs, and the lab covers your research work. Students who join a lab early in their programme usually find that their effective income is comfortably above the headline stipend figure. Confirm the exact current figures on the official admission website, admission.kaist.ac.kr, before you apply, since amounts and conditions can change between admission cycles.

Eligibility

Who counts as an eligible international applicant?

You are eligible for a KAIST International Scholarship if you are an international applicant applying to a KAIST degree program, whether bachelor's, master's, or doctoral. The scholarship is designed for students who do not hold Korean citizenship, which means your nationality and your visa status as an international student are what matter, not your age or the number of years you have studied.

Can I hold the KAIST scholarship alongside other funding?

You must not be receiving another scholarship that already covers your tuition and fees. Students on government scholarships such as the Global Korea Scholarship may not be able to combine the two, so check the terms of each funding source carefully. This is a genuine conflict: two tuition-waiving awards normally cannot be held at once, so if you are shortlisted for GKS, decide which route you are taking before you sign anything. Stipends and research assistantship pay are usually a different matter from the tuition waiver, but confirm rather than assume.

Is there a separate scholarship application?

No. There is no separate scholarship application. Admission to KAIST as an international student is what triggers the award, though the exact level of funding can depend on the program and admissions track. In practice this means your application checklist has no scholarship documents on it: transcripts, degree certificates, a statement of purpose, recommendation letters, proof of English proficiency, and passport details do all the work.

Does everyone admitted receive the same amount?

No. The exact level of funding can depend on the program and the admissions track you applied through, which is why the award letter matters so much. Read the stipend level and duration of coverage stated in your offer rather than assuming you have the standard package. If the figures matter to your decision, ask the admissions office before you commit.

How to apply step by step

How do I choose the right program and track?

KAIST runs its admissions through the Graduate Admissions or Undergraduate Admissions system, with multiple tracks per year. Confirm which track is open for your program and read its requirements. The track you choose determines your deadline, your document list, and the intake you will join, so this decision comes first in the process. Applying to the wrong track is one of the most common and most avoidable ways to lose a funding year.

What documents do I need to prepare?

You will need transcripts, degree certificates, a statement of purpose, recommendation letters, proof of English proficiency, and your passport details. Requirements vary by program, so treat the official list for your specific track as the definitive version. Several of these items are slow to obtain — transcripts must be requested from your university, notarised translations can take time, and English scores must travel directly from the testing agency — which is why you should start before you feel ready to apply.

How do I submit the online application?

Complete the online application by your track's deadline through the KAIST online admissions portal. The scholarship consideration is included automatically, so there is no additional scholarship step and nothing extra to attach. Before you hit submit, check that every required field and every uploaded document has gone through, because incomplete applications are typically not considered at all.

What happens after I submit?

Admission results are announced several weeks after the deadline, and for the main international intake the full cycle usually takes a few months. If you are admitted, your offer letter will state the scholarship you have been awarded, including the stipend level. Keep that letter safe: it becomes your financial evidence for the visa application.

How do I secure my visa and arrive?

Accepted students arrange a D-2 student visa through the Korean embassy or consulate and complete any required documents before arrival. The visa step is where preparation pays off, because a certificate of admission and a scholarship award letter substantially simplify the proof of financial capacity you have to show. Plan to arrive in Korea at least two weeks before the semester begins so you can finish residence registration, bank account setup, and orientation without rushing.

Key dates

When do KAIST admission tracks open and close?

Graduate admission tracks typically open in the autumn and close in early winter or spring depending on the program, with a second track in some years. Undergraduate admission runs similar cycles. Because the cycles are program-specific rather than uniform, the only reliable answer is the published schedule for your own track.

Is there a separate scholarship calendar?

No. KAIST International Scholarships follow the admission calendar, not a separate scholarship calendar. This is the practical reason to treat the admission deadline as a funding deadline. Any scholarship planning you do should be built on top of the admissions schedule, not alongside it, because there is nothing else to track.

How early should I start preparing documents?

Begin document preparation weeks ahead. Transcripts, notarised translations, referee letters, and English test scores all sit outside your control once requested, and each of them can quietly consume a month if you leave it late. Working backwards from the deadline with a date assigned to every document is the most reliable method of arriving on time with a complete application.

KAIST International Scholarships: Admission Process and Automatic Funding

Do I apply for the scholarship separately from admission?

No, and this is the point most applicants miss. One of the most important things to understand about KAIST International Scholarships is that the scholarship application is integrated into the admission process: you do not apply for the scholarship separately. When you submit your application for admission to KAIST, you are automatically considered for the available scholarships, which include full tuition coverage, a monthly stipend and health insurance.

What actually wins the funding?

This means that the strongest thing you can do to secure a KAIST scholarship is to submit the strongest possible admission application. The scholarship is awarded based on academic excellence, research potential and the overall competitiveness of your application, and there is no additional form or separate deadline for scholarship consideration. Everything you invest in the admission package is simultaneously an investment in the funding decision.

How do you apply for KAIST International Scholarships?

You apply through the admission portal, and the application itself requires several components: academic transcripts, a statement of purpose, letters of recommendation, English proficiency test scores and, for some programmes, standardised test scores such as the GRE. Work through them in that order of difficulty, because transcripts and test scores have external lead times while your statement of purpose is entirely under your control.

What does the statement of purpose have to achieve?

The statement of purpose is your opportunity to explain your academic background, your research interests and your reasons for choosing KAIST. The committee evaluates this document for clarity of thought, specificity of purpose and alignment with KAIST research strengths. Rather than writing a general statement about wanting to pursue a master or doctoral degree, identify specific research groups, faculty members or projects at KAIST that interest you, and explain how your background prepares you to contribute to that work. This specificity demonstrates that you have researched KAIST thoroughly and that your application is targeted rather than generic.

Who should write my recommendation letters?

Letters of recommendation are typically required from two or three academic referees who can speak to your research potential, your academic performance and your personal qualities. Choose referees who know your work well and can provide specific examples of your abilities, rather than senior figures who can only offer general praise. Provide your referees with a copy of your statement of purpose, your CV and a summary of the research you want to pursue at KAIST, so that they can tailor their letters to the programme.

What if my referees are slow?

Follow up with your referees at least two weeks before the deadline to confirm that the letters have been submitted, and again forty-eight hours before the closing date as a final reminder. Incomplete applications without all required letters are typically not considered, so this administrative step is critical. Build a reminder schedule into your backwards timeline rather than relying on goodwill, and always keep a backup referee on standby.

When will I hear back, and what will the letter say?

The scholarship award decision is made by the admissions committee after all applications have been reviewed. Successful scholarship recipients are notified along with their admission decision, which typically arrives several months after the application deadline. The scholarship terms, including the monthly stipend amount and the duration of coverage, are specified in the award letter, so read those details carefully rather than assuming you have received the headline package.

Will the funding actually cover my living costs in Daejeon?

At the time of writing, KAIST provides full tuition coverage and a monthly stipend that is intended to cover living costs in Daejeon, where the main campus is located. Daejeon is significantly more affordable than Seoul, and the stipend is generally sufficient for a comfortable standard of living, though personal spending habits and lifestyle choices will affect how far it stretches. Confirm the current scholarship terms on the KAIST admissions website before you apply, as amounts and conditions may change.

KAIST Research Areas and Faculty Matching for Strong Applications

Which subjects are KAIST strongest in?

KAIST's strength lies in its focused research environment, and the strongest scholarship applications demonstrate a clear understanding of the specific research areas and faculty members that align with the applicant's interests. KAIST is organised into schools and departments that span science, engineering, business and technology, and each department has faculty members with active research programmes.

How do I match faculty members to my interests?

Before you apply, browse the faculty profiles in your target department, read recent publications from the research groups that interest you and identify two or three faculty members whose work genuinely aligns with your goals. The ability to name specific research topics and explain why they attract you is one of the most effective ways to strengthen your application, because it converts a generic application into a targeted one.

Which research areas are most active at KAIST?

KAIST research strengths are particularly pronounced in electrical engineering, computer science, mechanical engineering, physics, chemistry, biological sciences and business administration. Within these broad areas, specific research groups are working on cutting-edge topics such as artificial intelligence, robotics, semiconductor design, quantum computing, biotechnology and energy systems. If your academic background and research interests align with these areas, your application will be naturally competitive.

What if my field is not a KAIST strength?

You can still be competitive. If your interests are in a less prominent area at KAIST, demonstrate how your work connects to KAIST's broader mission of science and technology innovation and by identifying faculty members whose work intersects with your own interests in creative ways. Cross-disciplinary framings are common in strong applications, and an applicant who can show what their topic contributes to a bigger KAIST project is easier to place than one who simply lists a subject label.

Should I contact potential supervisors before I apply?

Yes. Contacting potential supervisors before you apply is a strategy that can significantly strengthen your application, and KAIST academic culture supports this approach. A brief, professional email to a faculty member introducing yourself, explaining your research interests and asking whether they are accepting new students can open a dialogue that improves both your application and your understanding of the research group.

What should my email to a supervisor include?

In your email, include a one-page summary of your academic background, your research experience and your specific interests, and reference one or two recent publications from the group that you have read and found relevant. Do not send a mass email to multiple faculty members in the same department: choose your top one or two choices and invest time in making each email specific and genuine. Depth on a small number of targets always outperforms a broadcast.

Does a positive reply from a professor guarantee admission?

No. A positive response from a faculty member does not guarantee admission, but it indicates that your profile is of interest and that your application will receive careful attention. Treat it as a signal about fit rather than a promise about the funding decision, and never write your application as though an informal email has already settled the matter.

KAIST Scholarships for International Students: Language and Cultural Adjustment

What English proficiency do I need?

KAIST conducts its programmes primarily in English, and English proficiency test scores are a requirement for international applicants. At the time of writing, the minimum requirements are typically IELTS 6.5 or TOEFL iBT 83, though some departments set higher thresholds. If your scores are close to but not quite at the required level, retaking the test with focused preparation is the most direct path to meeting the requirement.

Are medium-of-instruction letters accepted instead?

No. KAIST does not typically accept medium-of-instruction letters as a substitute for formal English test scores, so plan to take the IELTS or TOEFL well before the application deadline. Scores are valid for two years, so ensure that your test date falls within the validity window at the time of application. Booking the test early also leaves room for a retake without endangering your deadline.

Do I need Korean to get the scholarship?

No. Korean language ability is not required for admission or for the scholarship, so a lack of Korean will not affect your eligibility for the KAIST International Scholarship. What it will affect is your daily life and your integration into Korean society, which is a different problem with a different solution.

Is it worth learning Korean anyway?

Learning Korean before or during your time at KAIST significantly improves your daily life and your integration into Korean society. KAIST offers Korean language courses for international students, and many students take these courses during their first semester. Reaching a basic level of Korean — conversational ability for daily interactions — within the first six months is a realistic and valuable goal, and it opens doors to social activities, cultural experiences and part-time work opportunities that would be difficult to access without the language.

How does Korean academic culture differ from what I know?

Korean academic culture is different from Western or South Asian norms: the relationship between professors and students is more formal, group work is emphasised and the pace of the academic calendar is intensive. Understanding these differences before you arrive helps you adapt more quickly and avoid misunderstandings. Adjusting to formality, group dynamics and a compressed calendar takes intention, not just time.

What support does KAIST give new international students?

KAIST provides support services to help with the transition. The International Student Services office assists with visa matters, housing, health insurance and other practical issues. KAIST also organises orientation programmes for new international students, which cover Korean cultural norms, academic expectations and campus resources. Daejeon has a smaller international community than Seoul, but the university itself has a vibrant international student population, and Korean students are generally welcoming to international peers. The international student community includes students from across Asia, Europe and the Americas, and this community provides both social support and a network of peers who are navigating the same adjustment.

Where should I live in Daejeon?

On-campus housing is the first practical choice. KAIST provides on-campus housing for international students, and the residences are affordable and conveniently located near academic buildings. Because on-campus housing provides a built-in social community and eliminates the need to navigate the Korean rental market, it is typically the first choice for new international students. If on-campus housing is unavailable or if you prefer to live off-campus, the Daejeon rental market is significantly more affordable than Seoul, but understanding the Korean rental system — which often requires a large deposit (jeonse) or a monthly rent plus a key money deposit — is important before you sign a lease. The international student office can provide guidance on housing options and help you understand the terms of Korean rental contracts.

KAIST vs Other Korean and Asian Scholarships: A Comparison

How does KAIST compare with the Global Korea Scholarship?

KAIST International Scholarships exist alongside several other major scholarship programmes in South Korea, and understanding how KAIST compares helps you make an informed decision about where to apply. The Global Korea Scholarship (GKS), formerly the Korean Government Scholarship Program, is the largest government-funded scholarship in Korea and supports both undergraduate and graduate students. GKS covers tuition, a monthly stipend, airfare, health insurance and a year of Korean language training.

Which route is more direct for me?

The key difference between KAIST scholarships and GKS is that KAIST scholarships are institution-specific and automatic, while GKS is a national programme that requires a separate application and is managed by the Korean embassy in your country. If you are applying to KAIST specifically, the KAIST scholarship is the more direct route, as it does not require a separate application and is integrated into the admissions process. Choose the route that matches the university you actually want to attend rather than the one with the longer application form.

How does KAIST compare with other Korean universities?

When comparing KAIST to other top Korean universities, the scholarship offerings are broadly similar in terms of what they cover: full tuition, a monthly stipend and health insurance. However, KAIST's particular strength in science and technology means that the research facilities, faculty expertise and industry connections in STEM fields are among the best in Korea. If your field is in engineering, computer science, physics, chemistry or a related discipline, KAIST's research environment is difficult to match at other Korean institutions. For students in business, social sciences or humanities, other universities such as Seoul National University, Yonsei University or Korea University may offer stronger programmes and more relevant faculty. The decision should be driven by your specific field and research interests, not by the university name alone.

How does KAIST compare with CSC and MEXT?

Compared to scholarships in other Asian countries, KAIST scholarships are competitive in both generosity and prestige. The China Scholarship Council (CSC) provides full funding for international students at Chinese universities, and the MEXT scholarship in Japan covers tuition, a monthly stipend and airfare. Both are well-established programmes with large numbers of recipients. KAIST scholarships offer a comparable level of financial support, with the added advantage of being automatic and institution-specific, which simplifies the application process. For students who are deciding between Korea, China and Japan, the choice often comes down to the specific research fit, the language you want to learn and the career opportunities in each country after graduation. Korea's technology sector, China's manufacturing and innovation ecosystem and Japan's research and engineering tradition each offer distinct advantages, and your decision should be informed by your long-term career goals as much as by the scholarship itself.

What should Pakistani students weigh up specifically?

For Pakistani students, the practical considerations include the availability of a Pakistani community in each country, the cost of living, the language barrier and the post-graduation work prospects. Korea has a growing Pakistani community, particularly in Seoul and Daejeon, and Halal food is increasingly available in major cities. The cost of living in Daejeon, where KAIST is located, is moderate by international standards, and the monthly stipend is generally sufficient.

Is the language barrier a real problem after graduation?

The Korean language is a significant barrier for many international students, but the availability of language courses and the increasing use of English in academic settings mitigate this challenge. After graduation, Korea offers a job-seeker visa that allows international graduates to remain in the country while looking for employment, and the technology sector actively recruits international talent. Comparing these factors across your target countries gives you a realistic basis for choosing where to apply, rather than a decision made purely on the size of the award.

KAIST Application Timeline and Key Deadlines for International Students

How many admission cycles does KAIST run?

Understanding the KAIST application timeline is essential for planning your scholarship strategy, as the deadlines for admission and financial support are intertwined. KAIST typically operates two admission cycles per year: one for the spring semester (starting in March) and one for the autumn semester (starting in September). The autumn intake is the main admission cycle for international students, and it is when the majority of scholarship places are available.

When does the autumn intake open and close?

Applications for the autumn intake typically open in September or October and close in November or December, with admission decisions announced in the following February or March. The spring intake is smaller and has fewer scholarship places, with applications typically closing in May or June. If your goal is the widest selection of funded places, plan around the autumn cycle.

Which document takes the longest to prepare?

The application itself is submitted through the KAIST online admissions portal, and it requires several documents that take time to prepare. Academic transcripts must be obtained from your university, and if they are not in English or Korean, they must be translated and sometimes notarised. English proficiency test scores must be sent directly from the testing agency to KAIST, which can take several weeks. Letters of recommendation must be submitted by your referees through the portal, and you should give your referees at least four weeks to complete their letters. The statement of purpose is the document that requires the most time and thought: plan to spend at least two to three weeks drafting, revising and refining it before submission. Creating a backwards timeline from the application deadline, with specific dates for each document, ensures that nothing is left to the last minute.

How long does the review take after I submit?

After submission, the admissions committee reviews all applications and makes decisions within two to three months. Successful applicants receive an admission offer and a scholarship award letter that specifies the terms of the funding. If you receive an offer, you must confirm your acceptance within the specified period, usually two to four weeks, and begin the visa application process immediately, because the visa stage has its own lead time.

What does the D-2 student visa require?

The Korean student visa (D-2) requires a certificate of admission from KAIST, proof of financial capacity (which is simplified if you hold a KAIST scholarship), a valid passport and health insurance. The visa application is submitted to the Korean consulate in your country, and processing typically takes two to four weeks. Plan to arrive in Korea at least two weeks before the semester begins to complete administrative tasks such as residence registration, bank account setup and orientation.

What extra steps do Pakistani applicants face?

For Pakistani applicants, the visa process has specific requirements that deserve attention. You will need to obtain a police clearance certificate from the Pakistani authorities, which can take several weeks to process. The health examination must be conducted by a panel physician approved by the Korean consulate, and the results must be in the format required by Korean immigration. Financial evidence for the visa is simplified if you hold a KAIST scholarship, as the scholarship award letter serves as evidence of financial support, but you should still prepare supplementary bank statements showing access to funds for the initial period before your first stipend payment. Begin gathering these documents as soon as you receive your admission offer, as delays in document preparation are one of the most common reasons for late arrivals and missed orientation sessions. The KAIST International Student Services office can provide guidance on the specific documents required for Pakistani nationals.

KAIST Campus Life and Student Support Services for International Students

What is the KAIST campus like?

KAIST campus life is shaped by the university identity as a research-intensive institution focused on science and technology, and international students are an integral part of this community. The main campus in Daejeon is a self-contained academic environment with lecture halls, research laboratories, libraries, student residences, cafeterias and recreational facilities all located within walking distance. The campus atmosphere is intense and focused, reflecting the demanding academic culture, but it also includes a range of social and cultural activities organised by student clubs, the international student office and the university administration. For Pakistani students, the campus provides a supportive environment where you can concentrate on your studies while building a network of peers from Korea and around the world.

What does the International Student Services office do?

The International Student Services office is your primary point of contact for administrative and support needs. This office assists with visa registration, residence permit renewals, health insurance, bank account setup and other practical matters that arise when you move to a new country. The office also organises orientation sessions for new international students, which cover Korean cultural norms, academic expectations, campus resources and practical tips for daily life. These orientation sessions are valuable for understanding the social dynamics of Korean academic culture, which can differ significantly from what you are accustomed to.

What academic support is available to me?

Academic support services at KAIST include the university library, which holds extensive collections in science and engineering, writing support centres that help students with academic English and research communication and tutoring services for students who need additional help with coursework. The library provides access to major academic databases, and its digital resources are available to students off-campus as well. The writing support centre is particularly valuable for international students who are writing research papers, thesis chapters or conference presentations in English. Taking advantage of these services from the beginning of your programme helps you maintain high academic standards and develop the writing and communication skills that are essential for a successful academic career.

How do I get involved beyond my research group?

Participate in the international student community, which is active and well-organised, and in events and clubs that provide both social support and practical advice from people navigating the same transition. Social life at KAIST is enriched by the variety of student clubs and organisations on campus: academic clubs focused on specific research areas, cultural clubs that celebrate international diversity, sports clubs, music and arts groups and community service organisations all provide opportunities to meet people outside your immediate academic circle. For Pakistani students, joining both Korean and international student clubs creates a balanced social life that includes both cultural immersion and a sense of community with fellow international students.

What is there to do outside the campus?

Daejeon itself is a mid-sized city with parks, museums, restaurants and cultural venues, and exploring the city and the surrounding region is an important part of the KAIST experience. The combination of rigorous academics, a supportive international community and the cultural richness of Korea makes KAIST a distinctive and rewarding place to study. Budgeting some of your stipend on travel and local activities is part of making the most of the programme.

Post-KAIST Career Prospects for International Graduates

Who hires KAIST graduates in Korea?

Graduating from KAIST with a fully funded degree opens doors to career opportunities both in Korea and internationally, and understanding the post-graduation landscape helps you plan your career strategy from the beginning of your programme. In South Korea, the technology sector is the largest employer of KAIST graduates, with major companies like Samsung, LG, SK, Hyundai and Naver actively recruiting KAIST talent.

What does the job-seeker visa give me?

The Korean government also offers a job-seeker visa that allows international graduates to remain in Korea for up to two years after graduation while looking for employment, which provides a valuable transition period. For Pakistani graduates, the combination of a KAIST degree and Korean language proficiency makes you a competitive candidate for positions in Korean companies that are expanding internationally or that need professionals who understand both the Korean and South Asian markets.

What can I do with a KAIST degree outside Korea?

International career opportunities for KAIST graduates are strong, particularly in fields where KAIST's research reputation is well-established. KAIST alumni are found in leading technology companies, research institutions and universities around the world, and the KAIST brand carries significant weight in international hiring. For Pakistani graduates who want to return home, a KAIST degree positions you for senior roles in Pakistan's technology sector, academic positions at Pakistani universities and research roles at international organisations operating in Pakistan. The Korean government and KAIST itself maintain alumni networks that can facilitate international job searches, and the connections you build during your studies with Korean and international peers provide a professional network that spans multiple countries and industries.

Does KAIST support an academic career path?

Yes. KAIST provides strong preparation for doctoral programmes and research positions. The research experience you gain during your master or doctoral programme at KAIST, combined with the publication opportunities and the international collaboration that KAIST's research environment fosters, positions you competitively for PhD programmes at top universities worldwide or for research positions at leading institutions. KAIST faculty members often have international collaborations and can provide introductions and recommendations that support your next career step. For Pakistani graduates considering an academic career, the combination of KAIST research training and a strong publication record provides a foundation for competitive applications to academic positions in Korea, Pakistan and internationally.

How should I prepare for jobs while I am still studying?

The practical steps you take during your studies at KAIST to prepare for your post-graduation career are as important as the degree itself. Attend career fairs, connect with company recruiters, complete internships with Korean or international companies and build a professional network through academic conferences, industry events and alumni connections. Develop your Korean language skills to at least an intermediate level, as this significantly expands your employment options in Korea and demonstrates cultural adaptability to international employers. Maintain a strong academic record and publish research where possible, as these are assets for both academic and industry positions. The most successful KAIST graduates are those who treat their career preparation as a parallel project to their studies, investing time and effort throughout their programme rather than waiting until graduation to begin thinking about their next steps.

KAIST Scholarships: Tips for Writing a Winning Statement of Purpose

How important is the statement of purpose?

The statement of purpose is the most important document in your KAIST scholarship application, and it is where you make the case for why you are the right candidate for the programme and the scholarship. Since the funding decision follows the admission decision, a weak statement of purpose does not merely cost you admission — it costs you the KAIST International Scholarship as well.

How long should it be, and what three things must it do?

A strong statement of purpose does three things: it explains your academic background and how it prepares you for the specific programme you are applying to, it describes your research interests and how they align with KAIST faculty and research groups, and it outlines your career goals and how the KAIST degree will enable you to achieve them. The statement should be one to two pages, written in clear and concise English, and structured around these three themes. Those three themes are also a good revision checklist.

What should the opening section do?

Begin your statement by describing your academic journey: what you studied, what sparked your interest in your field and what specific experiences or projects shaped your decision to pursue graduate study. Avoid general statements about wanting to study at a top university or about being passionate about science: instead, describe the specific research questions or problems that fascinate you and the experiences that led you to these interests. For Pakistani applicants, describing the specific challenges or opportunities in your field in Pakistan, and how your research will address them, provides a compelling motivation for your graduate study and demonstrates that you have thought about the practical impact of your work.

What belongs in the middle section?

The middle section of your statement should focus on your research interests and how they align with KAIST's specific strengths. Identify two or three faculty members whose work interests you, reference specific publications or projects from their research groups and explain how your background prepares you to contribute to their work. If you have contacted any of these faculty members and received a positive response, mention this in your statement, as it demonstrates initiative and indicates that your profile is of interest to the department. Be specific about the research methods, tools and approaches you want to use, and explain how KAIST facilities and resources will support your research. This specificity demonstrates that you have researched the programme thoroughly and that your application is targeted rather than generic.

How should I close the statement?

The final section of your statement should address your career goals after graduation. Explain how the KAIST degree fits into your longer-term professional trajectory, whether you plan to pursue a doctoral programme, enter industry or return to Pakistan to contribute to your field. For Pakistani applicants, explaining how your KAIST education will enable you to contribute to Pakistan's technology sector, academic system or research community provides a concrete and compelling motivation that the admissions committee values. Close by summarising your key qualifications, your research fit with KAIST and your commitment to making the most of the opportunity. Avoid generic conclusions that could apply to any programme: instead, end with a specific statement about what you will do with the KAIST experience and how it will shape your career.

Frequently asked questions

Is the KAIST International Scholarship fully funded?+

It is a full-tuition award and more: it covers the full cost of tuition, provides a monthly stipend for living expenses, and offers national health insurance for international students.

Who is eligible for KAIST scholarships?+

Newly admitted international students applying for KAIST degree programs, including bachelor's, master's, and doctoral programs, who are not already benefiting from other government or KAIST scholarships.

How much is the KAIST monthly stipend?+

The monthly stipend for graduate students is around KRW 350,000 per month, plus the full tuition waiver. Exact figures are set each year and confirmed in your admission offer.

Do I need to apply for a KAIST scholarship separately?+

No. International applicants to KAIST degree programs are automatically considered for the KAIST International Scholarship through their admission application. It is not a separate form.

What are the KAIST application deadlines?+

KAIST runs multiple admissions tracks with deadlines that vary by program, typically in the autumn and spring. Check the official admission site for the track and deadline that applies to you.
