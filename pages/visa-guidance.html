Student Visa Guidance: Every Requirement, Fee & Timeline

The student visa is where most applications fall apart. This hub breaks down the exact visa type, fee, funds requirement, documents and processing timeline for every major study destination — so your visa gets approved first time.

Why Visas Get Rejected

Over 60% of study-abroad visa applications get refused the first time — almost always for fixable reasons: insufficient or poorly documented funds, weak ties to home, mistakes in the application form, or applying too late. The good news? Every one of these is preventable with a checklist.

Golden rule: never pay tuition, book a flight or resign your job until your visa is approved. Universities refund deposits if a visa is refused — but only if you apply within the window. See our guide to visas after winning a scholarship.

The Universal 10-Step Visa Checklist

Every country follows the same skeleton. Master these 10 steps and you can apply to any destination confidently.

1️⃣ Confirm your CAS / I-20 / offer

Your university issues the document that unlocks your visa: I-20 (USA), CAS (UK), admission letter (Canada/Australia/Europe), or official confirmation from Campus France / Universitaly / Uni-Assist.

2️⃣ Pay the tuition deposit

Most visas require proof you've paid tuition (UK, Australia, Ireland) or at least the first semester. Keep the receipt — it's a required document.

3️⃣ Book biometrics early

Biometrics appointments fill up weeks ahead, especially in summer. Book the moment you decide on your university. For the USA, this is the interview slot itself.

4️⃣ Prepare funds proof

Bank statements (usually 3–6 months), blocked account, or sponsor documents. Funds must be in your name or a sponsor's, with proof of relationship. Never borrow money days before — it's a red flag.

5️⃣ Get the right health insurance

Mandatory in Australia (OSHC), Germany, the Netherlands and Ireland. The UK charges the IHS fee with your visa. Confirm your policy meets the exact coverage rules.

6️⃣ Book your medical (if required)

Australia and New Zealand require a panel-doctor medical for longer courses. Check whether your course length triggers a medical — book early to avoid delays.

7️⃣ Fill the form carefully

Use your passport exactly as written. No nicknames, no estimates. A single mismatch (birth date, spelling of your university) is a common grounds for refusal.

8️⃣ Attend the interview or app

The USA requires an in-person interview. Others are mostly paperless. For interviews: be honest, know your course, know how you'll pay, and show you'll return or progress after study.

9️⃣ Track and chase

Processing times are averages, not guarantees. If yours runs long, your university can send a status inquiry (UK, Canada). Keep a calendar with your intended travel date at the front of mind.

🔟 Arrive & register

On arrival: pick up your BRP/ARC/TIE, register with the immigration authority (Ireland's GNIB, Spain's police station, German Ausländerbehörde), and never overstay your entry dates.

Funding Your Visa Without Cash Flow Problems

The biggest visa killer is money — either not enough, or badly documented. Solutions that actually work:

  • Blocked accounts (Germany, Sweden, France): deposit the year's amount in a regulated account and receive a certificate for your visa.
  • Sponsorship: parents, relatives or a government sponsor can provide funds — but you need a signed affidavit and proof of the relationship (birth certificate, family register).
  • Scholarships as proof: a fully funded scholarship letter is the strongest possible funds proof. If you've won one, show the official award letter and how stipends are paid.
  • Education loans: recognised in the UK, Australia, Canada and Ireland when from an approved lender. Keep the sanction letter and disbursement proof.

Visa FAQs

When should I apply for my student visa?+

Most countries allow applications up to 6 months before your course start. Apply 2–4 months ahead as a safe zone. The USA allows F-1 applications 365 days before start; the UK 6 months; Australia usually 8–10 weeks ahead is realistic. Never book flights before the visa is stamped.

Can I work on a student visa?+

Generally yes, within limits: 20 hrs/week term-time in the UK and Italy, 24 hrs in Canada, 48 hrs/fortnight in Australia, 20 hrs on-campus in the USA (off-campus needs special approval), 964 hrs/year in France. The Netherlands allows 16 hrs/week or full-time in summer. Each country guide has the exact rules.

What happens if my visa is refused?+

You get a refusal letter stating the reason. You can either re-apply after fixing the issue (get a new CAS/I-20 if yours expires), appeal where allowed (UK has an administrative review), or seek fresh admission for the next intake. Universities typically refund deposits on proof of refusal. Don't fabricate documents to "fix" it — that creates a ban.

Does my scholarship change my visa application?+

Yes — a funded scholarship simplifies funds proof dramatically and reduces refusal risk. Still apply for the correct visa (you may need a specific visa category, e.g. USA J-1 for exchange vs F-1). Read our scholarship-visa guide for the details.

Which country has the fastest student visa processing?+

Roughly: UK (~3 weeks, or days with priority), Italy (2–4 weeks), France (2–4 weeks), Turkey (2–4 weeks), USA (2–6 weeks plus interview wait), Australia (4–10 weeks), Canada (4–16 weeks and it depends heavily on country of application). Germany and Sweden can take 1–4 months in peak season. Apply early regardless.
