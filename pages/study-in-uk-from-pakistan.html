Study in the UK from Pakistan: Complete 2026 Guide (Visa, Costs, Process)

📅 Sep 3, 2026 ⏱ 14 min read ✏️ AbroadReady Team

Every year thousands of Pakistani students pack their bags for the UK, drawn by one-year master's degrees, world-class universities and a post-study work visa that lets you stay and build a career after graduation. Getting there, though, requires navigating UKVI rules, the CAS process, proof of funds, the Immigration Health Surcharge and a visa interview at the British High Commission — and the details matter more than most applicants expect. A wrong bank balance, a late CAS or a poorly prepared interview can sink months of effort.

This guide is written specifically for students in Lahore, Karachi, Islamabad and everywhere else in Pakistan who want to study in UK from Pakistan in 2026. It covers costs in pounds and rupees, the exact visa process step by step, scholarships that actually exist, work rights, and the mistakes that cause refusals. If you've been searching for a single resource that lays out the whole thing without fluff, this is it.

Key takeaways

  • The UK student visa application fee is £490, and you must also pay the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) of £776 per year (or £1,035 if applying from inside the UK).
  • Tuition for Pakistani students ranges from £11,000–£18,000 for undergrad and £12,000–£35,000+ for postgrad, depending on the university and programme.
  • Living costs are set at £1,334 per month in London and £1,023 per month outside London — you need this amount (for 9 months) in your bank for 28 consecutive days.
  • You need a CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) from your university before applying for the visa — start the process at least 3–4 months before your intended start date.
  • Pakistani students can work 20 hours per week during term and full-time during holidays; the Graduate Route visa allows 2 years of post-study work (3 for PhD).
  • Scholarships like Chevening, Commonwealth and GREAT are fully or partially funded and open to Pakistani nationals — apply early, not late.
  • The UK study visa success rate Pakistan is generally high when applications are prepared properly; refusals almost always come from weak financials or missing documents.

Why the UK makes sense for Pakistani students

The UK has three structural advantages that make it especially attractive for students from Pakistan. First, the one-year taught master's degree. While most countries run two-year master's programmes, the UK crams the same credit load into 12 months. That means you graduate a year earlier, start earning sooner and spend less on living costs — a significant financial advantage when your family is funding the trip from PKR.

Second, the Russell Group. These 24 research-intensive universities — Manchester, Birmingham, Leeds, Glasgow, Bristol and others — are the UK's equivalent of America's Ivy League. They appear high in global rankings, they have strong graduate employment rates, and Pakistani employers recognise them. Getting into a Russell Group university isn't easy, but the doors they open for your career are real.

Third, the Graduate Route. After you finish your degree, you can switch to a Graduate visa that lets you stay in the UK for two years to work any job, change employers, or start a business — no sponsorship needed. For Pakistani students worried about making the most of their investment, this is the safety net that makes the UK more appealing than countries where you have to leave immediately after graduating.

Best courses and fields for Pakistani students

Choosing the right field matters because it affects your tuition, your visa interview story and your career prospects after graduation. Here are the fields that consistently draw Pakistani students to the UK and offer strong post-graduation outcomes.

Computer Science and IT. The UK tech sector is booming, with companies across London, Manchester and Edinburgh hiring aggressively. Courses in artificial intelligence, data science and software engineering have direct pathways to Graduate Route visas and Skilled Worker sponsorship. Tuition typically runs £16,000–£28,000 for a one-year master's.

Engineering. Civil, mechanical, electrical and chemical engineering programmes are offered across the Russell Group and have high employability. The IET and IMechE accredit most UK engineering degrees, which matters for professional registration. Fees range from £14,000–£30,000 per year.

Business, MBA and Finance. Business schools in the UK are globally ranked, and an MBA from a top school opens doors that are hard to access through other routes. MBA programmes can cost £25,000–£60,000, but post-MBA salaries in the UK can exceed £50,000–£80,000, making the investment worthwhile for the right candidate.

Public Health and Global Health. The NHS and universities like London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine, Edinburgh and Glasgow run world-class public health programmes. These are popular with Pakistani students because they combine practical training with globally transferable qualifications. Fees run £15,000–£28,000.

Law (LLM). A UK LLM is highly regarded, though it's important to note that it doesn't automatically qualify you to practise law in Pakistan or the UK — you'll need additional steps in both countries. Still, the qualification carries prestige and the analytical training is valued across sectors.

Top UK universities for Pakistani students

Russell Group universities dominate the list, but not all of them have the same entry flexibility or international student support. Here are universities that actively recruit Pakistani students and have strong track records for visa success.

  • University of Manchester — consistently top 30 globally, excellent for engineering, CS, business and health sciences. Large Pakistani student community. Tuition for international undergrad: £21,000–£30,000.
  • University of Birmingham — Russell Group, strong for business, computer science and engineering. Located in a city with a large Pakistani community and affordable living. Undergrad fees: £19,000–£28,000.
  • University of Leeds — well-known for business, media studies and engineering. Good international student support and competitive fees. Undergrad fees: £19,000–£27,000.
  • University of Glasgow — Russell Group with strong programs in medicine, engineering and education. Scotland's main research university. Undergrad fees: £19,000–£28,000.
  • University of Bristol — known for engineering, law and sciences. Consistently ranked in the UK's top 10. Undergrad fees: £21,000–£30,000.

Beyond the Russell Group, universities like University of Huddersfield, Leeds Beckett, Coventry University and De Montfort University offer lower tuition (often £12,000–£18,000 per year) and are known for being accessible to Pakistani students with a wider range of academic profiles. If budget is the primary constraint, these institutions deliver solid education without the Russell Group price tag. They are often cited as among the cheapest UK universities for Pakistani students looking for quality without overspending.

Tuition fees: what you'll actually pay

UK universities charge international students significantly more than domestic students. Here's a realistic breakdown of tuition fees for Pakistani students across different levels and fields.

Undergraduate programmes (3 years):

  • Humanities and Social Sciences: £11,000–£18,000 per year
  • Engineering and Computer Science: £16,000–£28,000 per year
  • Business: £15,000–£26,000 per year
  • Medicine: £30,000–£45,000 per year

Postgraduate taught (1 year):

  • Humanities and Social Sciences: £12,000–£20,000
  • Engineering and Computer Science: £14,000–£30,000
  • Business and MBA: £18,000–£60,000
  • Public Health: £15,000–£28,000

Postgraduate research (PhD, 3–4 years): Usually £18,000–£35,000 per year, though many funded PhD positions come with a tuition waiver and a living stipend.

Remember: UK tuition fees are per year (or per programme for one-year master's), and you'll be paying in pounds. When you convert to PKR, even a "cheap" programme can feel expensive — but the one-year structure means your total outlay is often less than a two-year master's in Germany or Australia. As of September 2026, £1 is approximately PKR 370–380, so a £15,000 tuition works out to roughly PKR 55–57 lakh.

Living costs: London vs everywhere else

The UK government sets a minimum living-cost requirement for visa purposes, and that number differs between London and the rest of the UK. Here's how it breaks down.

Outside London (Manchester, Birmingham, Leeds, Glasgow, Bristol):

  • Monthly minimum: £1,023 (~PKR 39,000)
  • Nine-month total: £7,707 (~PKR 29.3 lakh)
  • Realistic monthly budget: £800–£1,200 (rent £400–£650, food £150–£250, transport £50–£100, everything else £100–£200)

London:

  • Monthly minimum: £1,334 (~PKR 50,700)
  • Nine-month total: £10,224 (~PKR 38.8 lakh)
  • Realistic monthly budget: £1,200–£1,800 (rent £700–£1,100, food £200–£350, transport £100–£180, everything else £150–£300)

London is more expensive, but the Graduate Route and Skilled Worker sponsorship opportunities are also more concentrated there. If your priority is getting a job after graduation and you can afford the upfront cost, London makes sense. If budget is tight, northern cities like Manchester, Leeds and Glasgow offer a much better quality of life per pound spent — and large Pakistani communities you can tap into for housing and social support.

For context, your total cost of studying in UK for Pakistani students for a one-year master's outside London looks roughly like this: £15,000 tuition + £7,707 living + £490 visa fee + £776 IHS = approximately £24,000, which is around PKR 90–92 lakh. Add a 10% buffer for unexpected costs and you're looking at roughly PKR 1 crore total, which is the realistic figure most families should budget for.

UK student visa from Pakistan: step by step

The UK student visa process (formerly the Tier 4 General Student visa) follows a clear sequence, but the order of operations matters and mistakes at any stage can delay you by months. Here's the exact process.

Step 1: Get your university offer

Apply to UK universities through UCAS (for undergrad) or directly (for postgrad). Once you receive a conditional or unconditional offer, accept it and pay any deposit required — usually £1,000–£3,000, which is credited toward your first term's tuition.

Step 2: Meet your conditions

Conditional offers typically require you to meet academic grade requirements and provide an English language test score. For IELTS UKVI, the minimum is usually UKVI IELTS 5.5–6.5 depending on the programme. Some universities accept TOEFL, PTE Academic or Duolingo. If you haven't taken IELTS yet, our guide on study in UK without IELTS covers the alternatives that some universities accept.

Step 3: Receive your CAS

After meeting all conditions and confirming your place, the university issues a CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies). This is an electronic record — you won't receive a physical letter. The CAS number is what you use in your visa application. Processing takes 1–3 weeks after the university confirms you've met all conditions. The CAS is valid for six months from the date of issue.

Step 4: Get your finances in order

You must show:

  • Enough money to pay your first year's tuition fees (or first year if paying in installments), plus
  • Enough money to cover living costs: £7,707 outside London or £10,224 in London (for 9 months)

The funds must be in a bank account (yours or a parent's/sponsor's) and held for 28 consecutive days before the date of your visa application. The bank statement must be dated no more than 31 days before your application date. This is non-negotiable — the UKVI checks this strictly, and "my father will deposit the money next week" is not acceptable. Read our proof of funds requirements guide for the detailed rules.

Step 5: Pay the visa fee and IHS

UK visa application fee: £490. Immigration Health Surcharge: £776 per year of your course (or £576 for a six-month course). For a one-year master's, your IHS is £776. For a three-year undergrad degree, it's £2,328. You pay both online during the application.

Step 6: Complete the online application

Apply on the gov.uk website. The application takes about 30–45 minutes. You'll need your CAS number, passport details, financial evidence, English test results and your ATAS certificate if applicable (some science and technology programmes require Academic Technology Approval Scheme clearance).

Step 7: Book your biometrics appointment

After submitting the application, you'll be directed to the VFS Global or TLScontact website (depending on the city) to book an appointment at the UK Visa Application Centre in Islamabad, Karachi, Lahore or other cities. You'll submit your fingerprints and a digital photograph. Book this as soon as possible — peak season slots in Karachi and Islamabad fill up fast.

Step 8: Attend your appointment and submit documents

Bring your passport, printed application summary, financial evidence, CAS printout, English test certificate, passport-sized photos and any supporting documents. In some cases you may need to attend an interview — the UKVI may ask about your study plans, why you chose the UK, how you'll fund your studies and what you plan to do after graduation.

Step 9: Wait for the decision

Standard processing time from Pakistan is 3 weeks (15 working days). Priority processing is available for an additional fee and can reduce this to 5 working days in some locations. Apply early — don't wait until the last possible moment before your course starts.

Scholarships for Pakistani students

Scholarships are the single most impactful way to reduce the cost of UK study. Here are the main ones open to Pakistani nationals.

Chevening Scholarships. Fully funded by the UK government, covering tuition fees, living expenses, travel costs and visa fees. You need at least two years of work experience and must return to Pakistan for at least two years after your studies. The application opens in August/September each year and closes in early November. See our full Chevening Scholarship guide for the application strategy.

Commonwealth Scholarships. For students from Commonwealth countries including Pakistan, funded by the UK's Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office. Covers tuition, living and travel costs for master's and PhD programmes. Typically requires you to demonstrate how your studies will benefit Pakistan. Applications go through the Higher Education Commission (HEC) of Pakistan.

GREAT Scholarships. Jointly funded by the British Council and UK universities, these offer £10,000 towards tuition fees for one-year master's programmes. Each university has a limited number of awards and specific eligible countries — check with your chosen university directly. These are competitive but not as daunting as Chevening.

University-specific scholarships. Most UK universities offer their own scholarships for international students. Examples include the University of Manchester's Global Futures Scholarship, the University of Birmingham's International Achievement Scholarship and the University of Leeds International Postgraduate Scholarship. These range from £2,000–£10,000 off tuition and are applied for through the university, not centrally.

Work rights while studying

UK student visa holders can work, and the rules are straightforward:

  • During term time: up to 20 hours per week
  • During university holidays: full-time (no hour limit)
  • After graduation: switch to the Graduate Route — 2 years of unrestricted work (3 years for PhD)

You cannot work as a professional sportsperson or sports coach, and you cannot be self-employed. Most Pakistani students find part-time work in retail, hospitality, campus roles or tutoring. Earning £10–£15 per hour for 20 hours a week gives you roughly £800–£1,200 per month — enough to cover most of your living costs outside London if you're disciplined with budgeting.

The Graduate Route is the real prize. For two years after graduation you can work any job, switch employers, and even change immigration categories. There's no sponsorship required, no minimum salary, and no cap on the number of graduates who can use it. Many Pakistani students use this window to build experience and then switch to the Skilled Worker visa if their employer is willing to sponsor them long-term. Common refusal reasons for Pakistani students

The UK study visa success rate for Pakistani applicants is generally good when the application is prepared properly — the UKVI doesn't have a systemic bias against Pakistan. But refusals do happen, and they almost always come down to a few predictable issues.

  • Insufficient or improperly presented financial evidence. This is the number one refusal reason. The bank statement must show funds held for exactly 28 consecutive days, with the balance at or above the required amount every single day. A deposit made on day 15, a withdrawal on day 22, or an account that dipped below the threshold for one day can disqualify you.
  • CAS issues. Applying with an expired CAS, or a CAS that doesn't match the course or university you're applying to, leads to an automatic refusal.
  • English language evidence. Submitting a non-UKVI IELTS score when UKVI IELTS is required, or submitting a test score below the programme requirement.
  • Weak credibility. If the visa officer isn't convinced that you're a genuine student — perhaps because your study plan doesn't connect to your previous education or career, or because you couldn't explain why you chose the UK over alternatives — you may get a refusal.
  • Missing documents. Every document listed on the UKVI checklist is mandatory. Don't assume "they probably don't need that." They do.

Application timeline: when to do what

Timing is where most Pakistani students either get ahead or fall behind. UK universities and the visa system have fixed deadlines, and missing them can delay your entry by a full year. Here's a month-by-month guide for a September 2026 start.

  • September–November 2025: Research universities and programmes. Prepare and take IELTS UKVI if you haven't already. Write your personal statement. Apply to universities (UCAS deadline for undergrad is usually late January; postgrad applications are rolling but apply early).
  • December 2025–January 2026: Receive offers. Accept your firm choice and pay any deposit. Apply for scholarships — Chevening closes in early November, Commonwealth usually closes by December, university scholarships vary but many close in early 2026.
  • February–March 2026: Meet any remaining conditions on your offer. Your university will start preparing your CAS. Start gathering financial documents.
  • April–May 2026: Receive your CAS. Ensure your bank account has the required balance and has been holding it for at least 28 days. Book your VFS/TLScontact biometrics appointment.
  • June 2026: Submit your visa application online and attend your biometrics appointment. Priority processing recommended at this point to avoid delays.
  • July 2026: Receive your visa decision. If approved, collect your passport with the vignette from the application centre. Book your flights and start arranging accommodation.
  • August 2026: Arrive in the UK. Some universities have orientation events in late August. Register with your university, open a UK bank account, collect your Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) from the designated Post Office, and register with a local GP for NHS healthcare.
  • September 2026: Classes begin.

This timeline works for most students. If you're applying for a January start (many UK universities offer January entry for postgrad), shift everything forward by roughly three months. Don't compress the timeline — the 28-day bank balance rule alone means you need your money in place well before you apply.

The bottom line

To study in UK from Pakistan in 2026, you need three things done well: a university offer with a valid CAS, finances that meet the UKVI threshold for 28 consecutive days, and a visa application that tells a clear, consistent story about why the UK, why this programme, and what you'll do afterwards. Budget roughly PKR 80–100 lakh for a one-year master's outside London (tuition, living, visa and IHS), or apply for a scholarship that covers most of it. The Graduate Route gives you a genuine pathway to build a career after graduation, and the UK's one-year master's structure means you'll be working and earning a year ahead of students on two-year programmes. Start your research now, get your IELTS done early, and apply for scholarships before the deadlines pass. The process has a lot of steps, but none of them are mysterious — and with proper preparation, the UK study visa success rate Pakistan is firmly in your favour.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost for a Pakistani student to study in the UK?+

Tuition fees for Pakistani students in the UK typically range from £11,000–£18,000 per year for undergraduate programmes and £12,000–£35,000+ per year for postgraduate programmes, depending on the university and field. Medicine and MBA programmes can exceed £40,000. Living costs are around £1,000–£1,300 per month outside London and £1,300–£1,800 per month in London. The total cost of studying in UK for Pakistani students for a one-year master's programme usually falls between £25,000 and £50,000 including tuition, living expenses, visa fees and the Immigration Health Surcharge.

What is the CAS letter and how do I get it?+

The Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) is an electronic record issued by your UK university after you accept a firm offer and meet all conditions. The university assigns you a CAS number, which you use in your visa application. To get it, you must accept your offer, pay any required deposit (typically £1,000–£3,000), provide your passport details, and meet academic and English language conditions. Processing usually takes 1–3 weeks. The CAS is valid for six months from the issue date and you must apply for your visa before it expires.

How much bank balance do I need for a UK student visa from Pakistan?+

For a student visa, you must show you can cover your first year's tuition fees plus living costs. The UK government sets living costs at £1,334 per month for London (£10,224 for nine months) or £1,023 per month outside London (£7,707 for nine months). So if your tuition is £15,000 and you're studying outside London, you need at least £22,707 in your bank account. The funds must be held for 28 consecutive days before your visa application date, and the statement must be no older than 31 days when you submit.

Can Pakistani students work in the UK while studying?+

Yes. Pakistani students on a UK Student visa (formerly Tier 4) can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official university holidays, provided your course is at degree level at a Higher Education Provider with a track record of compliance. You cannot work as a professional sportsperson or sports coach, and you cannot be self-employed. After completing your studies, the Graduate Route visa allows you to stay and work (or look for work) for two years (three years for PhD graduates) with no sponsorship requirement.

Which UK universities are easiest for Pakistani students to get into?+

Several Russell Group universities have strong tracks of admitting Pakistani students, including the University of Manchester, University of Birmingham, University of Leeds, University of Glasgow and University of Bristol. Beyond the Russell Group, universities like the University of Huddersfield, Leeds Beckett, Coventry and De Montfort are known for being accessible with lower entry requirements and competitive tuition fees. The easiest route depends on your academic profile, IELTS score and budget. Many Pakistani students also target postgraduate taught (one-year master's) programmes, which have more flexible entry criteria than undergraduate degrees.
