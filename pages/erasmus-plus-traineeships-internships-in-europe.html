Erasmus+ Traineeships: Paid Internships in Europe

🗓️ Aug 5, 2026⏱️ 9 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

What are Erasmus+ traineeships, and why are they so misunderstood?

Erasmus+ traineeships are one of the most accessible funded internship routes in Europe, yet they are widely misunderstood. Erasmus+ is the European Union's flagship programme for education, training and mobility, and its traineeship strand funds higher education students to complete an internship in another participating country. Because the money flows through universities rather than through employers, an Erasmus+ traineeship is usually easier to arrange and more affordable than a conventional internship abroad.

That single design decision explains almost everything people find confusing about the scheme. Nobody applies to Brussels for an Erasmus+ traineeship. The grant is administered by a home university that already holds an Erasmus+ charter, and once that is understood, the process stops looking mysterious. The question shifts from "is this too good to be true?" to "how do I actually apply, and how much money arrives?"

This guide explains who can take part, what the grant covers, how to find a host organisation, and how to apply through your own university. It also covers the eligibility rules that catch applicants out, the credit recognition process, how to write a motivation letter, and how Erasmus+ traineeships compare with alternatives such as IAESTE and direct corporate internships, so you can decide whether this route fits your degree, your budget and your career plans.

Key takeaways

Before the detail, here are the answers most applicants need on a single screen. If you only remember five things about Erasmus+ traineeships, remember these.

  • Erasmus+ traineeships fund internships in another programme country for students at participating universities.
  • Eligibility is tied to your university, not your nationality, so many international students can take part.
  • You apply through your home university's international office, which manages the funding allocation.
  • The grant is a monthly contribution based on the host country's living costs, sometimes topped up for travel.
  • Host organisations can be companies, research institutes, NGOs or universities.

Two further points sit behind those bullets. First, there is no central Erasmus+ traineeship portal you apply to directly; every application runs through a university, so your first action is always to find your international or Erasmus+ office. Second, the grant is a living-cost contribution rather than a salary, which is why so many readers ask how much an Erasmus+ traineeship really costs and how the monthly amount is calculated. Both questions are answered in detail further down this page.

What is an Erasmus+ traineeship?

How is an Erasmus+ traineeship defined?

An Erasmus+ traineeship is a period of practical work abroad, typically two to twelve months, completed by a higher education student as part of their degree. The student finds, or is helped to find, a host organisation in another participating country, and their home university supports the placement with an Erasmus+ grant funded through the European Commission.

When do Erasmus+ traineeships take place?

The arrangement is flexible by design. Traineeships can happen during term time where the curriculum allows, but for most students they happen in the summer. That flexibility is one reason Erasmus+ traineeships suit degrees with a compulsory placement year, a semester of industry work, or a final-year project that benefits from real workplace data.

What kinds of organisations can host an Erasmus+ trainee?

You can work in a company, a research group, a hospital, an NGO or a public institution, as long as the placement is related to your field of study and counts toward your learning goals. The host can even be a small business with no internship budget of its own, because the Erasmus+ grant already covers your living costs while you are there. What the host must provide is real work, supervision and a commitment to sign the Learning Agreement.

Is an Erasmus+ traineeship a degree scholarship?

No, and the distinction matters when you are budgeting. A degree scholarship funds an entire programme of study, whereas an Erasmus+ traineeship is a funded placement inside a degree you are already taking. Think of it as a short, grant-supported work experience abroad rather than a qualification in itself, even though it is formally recognised through your university's credit system and by the European Commission.

Who can take part?

Who is eligible for an Erasmus+ traineeship?

Eligibility depends on where you study, not where you come from. You must be enrolled in a higher education institution that holds an Erasmus+ charter, which covers essentially all universities in the EU member states and the other programme countries: Iceland, Liechtenstein, North Macedonia, Norway, Serbia and Türkiye.

Can international students take part?

Yes, in most cases. International students enrolled at a participating European university can usually join Erasmus+ traineeships on the same terms as local students, provided they hold the right visa or residence status for the host country. The university's international office can confirm whether your specific situation qualifies, so always ask before planning.

Visa and residence status is the detail that catches people out. A student on a residence permit in one programme country is usually treated as a home-country student for mobility purposes, but they still need the correct entry or residence status for the host country. If you hold a third-country national visa tied to your current university, ask your international office in writing before you commit to dates, because the answer decides whether your application is valid at all.

How is a traineeship different from Erasmus Mundus?

Note that Erasmus+ traineeships are different from Erasmus Mundus joint master's programmes, which are full degree scholarships. Traineeships are short funding windows within your existing degree. The comparison matters because applicants sometimes assume an Erasmus+ traineeship requires a separate scholarship application to the European Commission. It does not, and your university remains the only point of contact.

What does the grant cover?

How is the Erasmus+ grant calculated?

The Erasmus+ grant is a monthly contribution toward the cost of living abroad, and the amount depends on the host country, with higher rates for more expensive destinations. Students with fewer opportunities, such as those from disadvantaged backgrounds or with disabilities, can sometimes receive a top-up, and a small travel contribution may also be available.

In practice the rate is published as a country-by-country table, usually a monthly national contribution plus an additional top-up for a smaller number of destinations. Your university applies those national rates rather than inventing its own, which is why two students on the same traineeship programme can receive different monthly amounts. Ask for the figure in writing, and ask which month it applies to, since rates are periodically revised.

Is the Erasmus+ grant a salary?

No. The grant is not a salary and is not designed to replace wages; it typically covers a significant share of rent and living costs. Some host organisations also pay the intern a wage on top, which can make the placement genuinely comfortable. Always confirm the exact grant amount for your destination and the rules of your university's allocation.

This is the most common misunderstanding among applicants. Employers are not expected to pay you, and an organisation that pays nothing beyond the Erasmus+ grant is entirely normal. What matters is that the grant is treated as a contribution to living costs, and that the host gives you meaningful work, supervision and a signed Learning Agreement rather than unpaid coffee-making.

How to find a host organisation

What are the two routes to a host organisation?

You have two main paths. Many universities maintain lists of partner organisations that already accept Erasmus+ trainees, which simplifies paperwork. Alternatively, you can approach an employer or research group yourself and ask whether they would host an Erasmus+ trainee; because the grant covers your living costs, many organisations are willing to take on an intern even without an internal budget.

What should a cold email to a host organisation actually say?

A cold email that explains the Erasmus+ funding model and your relevant skills works well here. Frame it as a proposal that costs the host little, and attach a focused CV. Keep it to a short screen of text: who you are, what you have studied, what you can do, why their work interests you, and a clear statement that your living costs are covered by the Erasmus+ grant, so the placement is low risk for them. Mentioning the grant early removes the most frequent objection, which is that the organisation simply cannot afford an intern.

How to apply step by step

The process is managed by your university, so start with its international office. There is no Erasmus+ traineeship application you submit to Brussels; your university runs the internal selection, signs the paperwork and pays the grant.

Step 1: Confirm eligibility and quotas

Ask your international office whether you are eligible, how many traineeship grants are available this year, and what deadlines apply. Grants are often allocated in rounds, and competition exists for the most popular destinations.

Step 2: Find your placement

Secure a host organisation and agree the dates, tasks and expected learning outcomes. A written acceptance from the host is usually required. Get that acceptance in writing, even if it is only an email, because your coordinator will normally need evidence that the placement is real before the application can move forward.

Step 3: Complete the paperwork

You sign a learning agreement that sets out the internship plan, and your university confirms the grant amount and insurance arrangements. The European Commission also provides an online language course to help you prepare for working in the host country's language.

How long does the whole Erasmus+ traineeship application take?

Allow roughly two to three months from your first email to a signed Learning Agreement, and a further two months before departure for visa, insurance and accommodation. Universities that allocate grants in competitive rounds may close applications months before the placement begins, so the calendar you should work to is your university's, not the host's.

Practical tips

How early should I start planning?

Start planning at least a semester ahead, because grants are limited and paperwork takes time. Choose a placement that genuinely fits your studies, since the learning agreement ties the internship to your degree, and keep your receipts, because the grant may be paid in instalments linked to your progress reports.

What if the Erasmus+ grant is not enough on its own?

If an Erasmus+ traineeship alone is not enough to fund your year abroad, you can combine it with other support. Our guide to funding your studies abroad without a full scholarship explains how to layer funding sources safely and legally. Check any funding combination against your university's rules before you accept it, because some institutions restrict stacking external awards alongside an Erasmus+ top-up.

Eligibility rules for Erasmus+ traineeships: who qualifies and who does not

What are the core eligibility criteria?

Understanding the eligibility rules for Erasmus+ traineeships is essential before you invest time in the application process, because the programme has specific requirements that exclude some candidates. The core eligibility criterion is that you must be a higher education student enrolled at a participating institution in an eligible country. This means you must be officially registered at a university or college that holds an Erasmus+ charter and has signed an inter-institutional agreement with a partner institution or has direct funding for traineeships.

What funding and benefits does erasmus+ traineeships provide?

The programme is primarily available to students in EU member states and programme countries, which include Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, North Macedonia, Serbia and Turkey. Students in partner countries, which include many nations outside Europe, can participate through certain international dimension actions, but the routes are more limited and depend on specific funded projects. If you are studying in Pakistan, for example, you would not typically access an Erasmus+ traineeship through the standard student mobility route unless your university has a specific Erasmus+ partnership that includes your country.

That last point is worth stressing for anyone applying from outside Europe. The route is not impossible, but it is not a standard open call either. Ask your university whether it runs any Erasmus+ international credit mobility projects, because those funded projects are usually the mechanism that brings students from partner countries into the scheme.

What are the erasmus+ traineeships requirements?

There are minimum and maximum duration requirements. A traineeship must last a minimum of two months and a maximum of twelve months. The traineeship must be relevant to your degree programme and must take place in a country other than your country of study and residence. You cannot use the grant for a traineeship in your home country or in the country where you are currently studying.

The cross-border rule is absolute, and it rules out the most obvious workaround. A placement in your own country will not be funded under Erasmus+ traineeship rules, however relevant it might be to your degree. If the experience you want is a domestic placement, you need a completely different funding route.

Erasmus+ traineeships eligibility: who qualifies?

Recent graduates can also participate, but there is an important restriction: you must apply for the traineeship while still enrolled as a student, and the traineeship must begin within twelve months of your graduation. This means you cannot graduate and then decide to do an Erasmus+ traineeship months later. The application must be made during your student status, even if the placement itself takes place after graduation. Your university's Erasmus+ coordinator is the key contact for eligibility questions and can confirm whether your institution participates and what funding is available.

Who does not qualify?

You do not qualify if you are not enrolled at a higher education institution holding an Erasmus+ charter, if your placement is shorter than two months or longer than twelve, if the placement is in your country of residence, or if you have already graduated and are outside the twelve-month window. Staff members, graduates applying after their window has closed, and applicants whose proposed work has no connection to their degree are also excluded.

How much Erasmus+ traineeship funding covers and what you still need to pay

Is the Erasmus+ grant designed to fund the whole experience?

The Erasmus+ traineeship grant is designed to offset the costs of living and working abroad rather than to fully fund the experience. Understanding what the grant covers and what you must pay yourself helps you plan realistically and avoid financial surprises.

How much does erasmus+ traineeships cost?

At the time of writing, the grant is calculated on a monthly basis and varies depending on the country where you undertake the traineeship. Countries with higher costs of living, such as Denmark, the Netherlands and Luxembourg, attract higher monthly grants, while countries with lower costs of living, such as Poland, Portugal and Hungary, attract lower grants. The monthly amount typically ranges from roughly 250 to 700 euros per month depending on the destination country, though exact figures are updated by the European Commission and your university can confirm current rates.

Because the amount tracks national living costs, two Erasmus+ traineeships in different countries can differ by more than a factor of two. A student who compares applications purely on grant size can end up with a better standard of living in a cheaper destination, so compare destinations on net budget rather than on headline funding.

What does erasmus+ traineeships cover?

The grant is paid as a contribution to your living expenses, accommodation, food, transport and other daily costs. It does not separately cover your traineeship salary, if you receive one from the host organisation. Some host organisations pay interns a stipend or salary in addition to the Erasmus+ grant, while others do not. Check this clearly before accepting a placement, as the total financial package matters for your budget.

How do you apply for erasmus+ traineeships?

In addition to the monthly grant, Erasmus+ typically provides a travel grant to help cover the cost of travelling to and from the host country. The amount varies depending on the distance between your home institution and the traineeship location. The travel contribution normally follows straight-line distance bands rather than actual ticket prices, so the figure you receive may be well below the fare you pay. Budget for that difference rather than assuming the travel grant funds the whole journey.

Erasmus+ traineeships visa process

What the grant does not cover includes your tuition fees at your home university (which you continue to pay), health insurance (which you must arrange yourself), visa costs if applicable, and any personal expenses beyond basic living costs. Budget carefully for the months before and after the traineeship as well, since there may be a gap between receiving your grant and incurring your initial expenses abroad. Students who plan their finances thoroughly, including a contingency buffer for unexpected costs, have a much more positive Erasmus+ experience than those who rely on the grant alone.

Visa timing is the part of the process most likely to derail an otherwise well-planned placement. Applications can take longer than expected, appointments can be delayed, and your grant does not stretch to cover a second attempt if something goes wrong. Start the visa process the moment your placement is confirmed, and build a buffer into your start date in case approval arrives after the date you originally agreed with the host.

What alternatives to Erasmus+ traineeships exist?

Erasmus+ traineeships are one of several funded internship routes available to students in Europe, and understanding how they compare helps you choose the right programme for your situation. Each route has different eligibility rules, funding levels and application processes.

Which erasmus+ traineeships tips actually work?

The IAESTE programme, for example, offers paid technical traineeships in over 80 countries and is open to STEM students regardless of whether their university has Erasmus+ partnerships. IAESTE placements are arranged through a centralised exchange system and may offer higher stipends in some destinations than Erasmus+ grants. However, IAESTE does not cover tuition fees or provide the same institutional support that Erasmus+ offers through your university.

Are direct internships with big companies an alternative?

Google, Microsoft, Siemens and other large multinational companies offer their own internship programmes with competitive pay, structured training and global mobility. These programmes are typically more competitive than Erasmus+ traineeships and require specific technical or professional skills, but they offer higher compensation and stronger employer brand recognition on your CV.

Why do so many students choose Erasmus+ first?

The key advantage of Erasmus+ traineeships is accessibility and institutional support. Your university handles much of the administrative work, the application process is straightforward and the funding is available to a wide range of students across many disciplines. For students who want a funded international internship without navigating complex individual applications, Erasmus+ is often the most practical starting point. It also provides a recognised European credential and access to the Erasmus+ alumni network, which can be valuable for future career opportunities across Europe.

So which route should I choose?

The decision between Erasmus+ and other routes ultimately depends on your field, your goals and your circumstances. If you are a STEM student looking for a research-focused placement, IAESTE may be more relevant. If you are targeting a specific employer, a direct application may be more efficient. If you want broad international exposure with strong institutional support and European mobility, Erasmus+ traineeships are hard to beat.

Practical steps to arrange your Erasmus+ traineeship placement

Why does the placement process involve two organisations?

Finding and securing an Erasmus+ traineeship placement involves both your home university and the host organisation, and understanding the process step by step prevents confusion and delays. Neither organisation can do the job alone: the university funds and academically approves the placement, and the host provides the work, the supervision and the workplace.

Step one: contact your Erasmus+ coordinator

Step one is to contact your university's Erasmus+ coordinator. This person manages your institution's Erasmus+ charter, knows which funding is available and can guide you through the internal application process. They will tell you about application deadlines, required documents and any institutional requirements beyond the standard Erasmus+ criteria. Many universities have their own internal selection process for Erasmus+ traineeships, and places may be limited.

What is Erasmus+ traineeships 2026?

Step two is to identify a host organisation. Some universities have pre-existing partnerships with companies and organisations that regularly host Erasmus+ trainees, and your coordinator may be able to suggest placements. If you are searching independently, target organisations in your field that have a track record of hosting interns. Academic institutions, research centres, NGOs, SMEs and large corporations all participate.

Step three: contact potential hosts directly

Step three is to contact potential host organisations directly. Write a professional email or letter introducing yourself, explaining the Erasmus+ traineeship programme and asking whether they would be willing to host you. Include a brief description of your skills, your academic background and the type of work you are interested in. Many organisations are familiar with Erasmus+ and welcome the opportunity to host international trainees.

What documents do you need for erasmus+ traineeships?

Step four is to formalise the placement. Once a host organisation agrees, your university will draw up a Learning Agreement, which is a formal document that defines the tasks you will perform, the skills you will develop, the duration of the placement and the assessment criteria. Both you, your home university and the host organisation sign this agreement before the placement begins. The Learning Agreement is your protection: it ensures that the placement meets academic standards and that you receive credit or recognition for your work.

Step five: arrange the practical details

Step five is to arrange the practical details: visa if required, health insurance, accommodation and travel. Start these arrangements at least two months before your placement start date. Your university's Erasmus+ office can advise on visa requirements and insurance, and the host organisation may assist with accommodation recommendations.

Can recent graduates still do an Erasmus+ traineeship?

One of the lesser-known aspects of Erasmus+ traineeships is that recent graduates are eligible, but the rules are specific and timing-sensitive. Understanding these rules before you graduate ensures you do not miss the window. A surprising number of students discover this rule only after graduation, by which point the application window has closed permanently.

What is the exact rule for graduates?

The key requirement is that you must apply for the Erasmus+ traineeship while you are still enrolled as a student at your participating institution. You cannot wait until after graduation to begin the application. The traineeship itself can take place after graduation, but it must begin within twelve months of your graduation date. This means you need to plan the application during your final year of study, have the placement confirmed before you graduate, and then undertake the traineeship in the period after graduation.

When should a graduating student apply?

Practically, this means that if you are graduating in June, you should be applying for Erasmus+ traineeships in the autumn or spring of your final year. Contact your Erasmus+ coordinator early in your final year to discuss this route and understand your institution's specific procedures for graduate traineeships.

Do graduates receive the same funding?

The funding rules for graduate traineeships are the same as for current students: you receive the monthly grant and travel contribution calculated for your destination country. Your university continues to administer the grant even though you have graduated, as long as the application was submitted while you were enrolled.

How should I choose a destination and use the placement well?

Plan your placement location and duration carefully. Since you will be funding living costs partly from the grant and partly from savings or other resources, choose a destination where the cost of living is manageable relative to the grant amount. Make the most of the post-graduation timing by using the traineeship as a bridge between university and employment. Many graduates use Erasmus+ traineeships to gain international experience, build a professional network and test a career direction before committing to a permanent role.

How to write a strong Erasmus+ traineeship motivation letter

Why does the motivation letter matter?

Many Erasmus+ host organisations and universities require a motivation letter as part of the application, and writing a strong one significantly improves your chances of placement. The motivation letter is your opportunity to explain why you want the placement, what you will bring to the host organisation and how the experience fits your academic and career plans.

How should I structure the first paragraph?

Structure your letter in three parts. In the first paragraph, introduce yourself, your field of study and the specific placement you are applying for. Explain briefly why this particular organisation and role interest you. Be specific: mention the organisation's work, a particular project or research area, or a company value that resonates with your goals.

What should the second paragraph prove?

In the second paragraph, explain what skills and experience you bring. Highlight relevant coursework, technical skills, previous internships or projects, and any cross-cultural experience. If you have specific competencies that match the host organisation needs, such as programming languages, laboratory techniques or language skills, mention them explicitly. This is where you demonstrate that you are not just interested but qualified.

What should the third paragraph explain?

In the third paragraph, describe what you hope to gain from the placement and how it fits your broader academic and career plans. Connect the placement to your degree programme and explain how it will contribute to your professional development. If you are a recent graduate, explain how the placement serves as a bridge between your studies and your career goals.

How long should the motivation letter be?

Keep the letter concise, ideally one page. Avoid generic language and focus on specific, personal content. Have someone review the letter for clarity and grammar before submitting it. A well-written motivation letter demonstrates the professionalism and communication skills that host organisations value in an intern.

Practical tips for succeeding during your Erasmus+ traineeship abroad

What does succeeding during a traineeship look like?

Once you arrive at your Erasmus+ traineeship host organisation, making the most of the experience requires a combination of professional diligence, cultural openness and proactive networking. The students who benefit most from Erasmus+ traineeships are those who treat the placement as both a learning opportunity and a professional engagement from the first day.

How should I handle the local workplace culture?

Start by understanding the workplace culture. European work cultures vary significantly from country to country and even from organisation to organisation. In some countries, the working day begins and ends at fixed times with a long lunch break. In others, the hours are more flexible but the expectations for output are high. Ask your supervisor about the typical daily schedule, communication preferences and any unwritten norms that are important to observe. Observe how your colleagues interact with each other and adapt your behaviour accordingly.

Why should I set goals early?

Set clear goals for your placement early on. Discuss with your supervisor what you hope to learn, what skills you want to develop and what deliverables you are expected to produce by the end of the placement. Having clear objectives gives your work direction and makes it easier to evaluate your progress. It also demonstrates initiative and professionalism, which leaves a positive impression on your supervisor and colleagues.

How should I build a network while on placement?

Network actively within the organisation and beyond. Attend internal meetings, seminars and social events. Introduce yourself to colleagues in other departments and ask about their work. Join professional associations or student groups in your host city. The connections you build during your traineeship can become valuable career resources, and the broader your network, the more opportunities you will discover.

What should I document while I am there?

Document your work and your learning throughout the placement. Keep a record of the projects you contributed to, the skills you developed and any feedback you received. This documentation is valuable when you return home and need to describe the experience on your CV or in future applications. Take photos, collect any certificates or completion documents and write a brief summary of your placement before you leave.

Why does the experience matter beyond the work itself?

Finally, enjoy the experience. Living and working in a European city for several months is a rare opportunity to immerse yourself in a different culture, try new foods, learn a new language and see parts of the world you might never visit otherwise. The Erasmus+ traineeship is not just a professional development opportunity: it is a life experience that broadens your perspective and enriches your personal growth. Make the most of every day.

Understanding the Erasmus+ traineeship Learning Agreement

What is the Learning Agreement and who signs it?

The Learning Agreement is the formal document that underpins every Erasmus+ traineeship, and understanding it thoroughly protects both you and your host organisation. The agreement defines the tasks you will perform, the skills you will develop, the duration of the placement and the assessment criteria. All three parties, you, your home university and the host organisation, must sign it before the placement begins.

What three functions does the agreement serve?

The Learning Agreement serves several important functions. First, it ensures academic recognition: your home university agrees in advance to recognise the traineeship as part of your degree, and the agreement specifies how many credits or what academic value the placement carries. Second, it defines expectations: both you and the host organisation know what is expected, which prevents misunderstandings later. Third, it provides a framework for evaluation: the assessment criteria are agreed in advance, so there are no surprises when the placement ends.

Can the Learning Agreement be changed mid-placement?

If you need to change the Learning Agreement after arriving at your host organisation, which sometimes happens if the initial tasks prove unsuitable or the scope of the placement shifts, you can request a modification through your university Erasmus+ office. Both parties must agree to any changes, and the modification should be documented and signed before the change takes effect. Do not assume that informal changes to your tasks or schedule are acceptable without updating the agreement.

How specific should the agreement be?

When negotiating your Learning Agreement, be specific about the tasks you want to undertake and the skills you want to develop. If there are particular technologies, methodologies or professional competencies you want to gain experience with, include them in the agreement. This ensures that your host organisation makes a deliberate effort to provide the learning experience you need, rather than leaving your development to chance.

How is the placement closed and assessed?

After the placement, your host organisation completes an evaluation of your performance and the Learning Agreement is closed with a final report. This evaluation is part of your academic record and may be used for credit recognition or future applications. Take the evaluation seriously and respond to any feedback constructively, as it provides valuable insight into your professional development and areas for improvement.

How Erasmus+ traineeship credit recognition works at your home university

Why does credit recognition matter so much?

Credit recognition is one of the most important practical aspects of an Erasmus+ traineeship, and understanding how it works at your home university ensures that the placement counts toward your degree. The Learning Agreement defines the academic value of the traineeship in advance, and both your home university and the host organisation agree to it before the placement begins.

How many ECTS credits does a traineeship carry?

At most universities, an Erasmus+ traineeship carries a specific number of ECTS credits, typically ranging from 3 to 12 credits depending on the duration and intensity of the placement. A two-month full-time traineeship might carry 6 ECTS credits, while a six-month placement might carry 12 or more. The exact credit value is determined by your home university based on the workload, learning outcomes and assessment criteria specified in the Learning Agreement.

What is Erasmus+ traineeships?

The assessment of your traineeship is usually conducted by your home university based on a report you submit at the end of the placement. The report typically includes a description of the tasks you performed, the skills you developed, a reflection on what you learned and any evidence of your work. Your host organisation may also complete an evaluation form that is sent to your university.

How do I make sure the credits actually count?

To ensure smooth credit recognition, confirm with your academic advisor before the placement that the traineeship credits will count toward your degree requirements. Some programmes specify which types of placements or activities can carry credit, and not all traineeships may qualify. If the traineeship is not directly related to your major, it may still count as elective credit or as part of a professional development requirement, depending on your university policies.

What paperwork must I keep for credit recognition?

Keep all documentation from your Erasmus+ traineeship, including the signed Learning Agreement, your final report, any evaluation forms from the host organisation and any certificates or letters of completion. These documents are your proof of completion and may be required if there are any questions about credit recognition after you return home. Submit them to your university Erasmus+ office promptly to avoid delays in processing your credits.

How does IAESTE compare for students outside Europe?

Erasmus+ traineeships are one of several funded internship routes available to students in Europe, and understanding how they compare helps you choose the right programme for your situation. The IAESTE programme offers paid technical traineeships across more than 80 countries and is open to STEM students regardless of whether their university holds an Erasmus+ charter. IAESTE placements are arranged through a centralised exchange system and may offer higher stipends in some destinations, but they do not provide the same institutional support that Erasmus+ delivers through your own university.

When is a direct corporate internship the better choice?

Large multinational companies such as Google, Microsoft and Siemens run their own internship programmes with competitive pay, structured training and global mobility. These are typically more competitive than Erasmus+ traineeships and often require specific technical or professional skills, but they offer higher compensation and stronger employer brand recognition. If you have the skills and want the compensation, direct corporate internships are the logical choice.

What should the final decision be based on?

The key advantage of Erasmus+ traineeships is accessibility and institutional support. Your university handles a large share of the administrative work, the application process is straightforward and funding is available to students across many disciplines, not just STEM. Erasmus+ also provides a recognised European credential and access to the Erasmus+ alumni network, which can be valuable for future career opportunities across Europe. The final choice depends on your field, your goals, your budget and how much institutional support you need to make the experience successful.

Frequently asked questions

What is an Erasmus+ traineeship?+

It is a funded internship of two to twelve months in another programme country, completed by a higher education student as part of their degree and supported by an Erasmus+ grant.

Who is eligible for Erasmus+ traineeships?+

Students enrolled at a higher education institution in an Erasmus+ programme country; international students can usually take part if their visa or residence status allows.

Does the Erasmus+ traineeship grant cover all costs?+

The grant is a monthly contribution based on host-country living costs and is not a salary, so it typically covers a significant share of rent and living costs rather than everything.

How do I apply for an Erasmus+ traineeship?+

Through your university's international office, which manages grants, deadlines and the learning agreement you sign with your host organisation.

Do I need to find my own host organisation?+

Many universities have partner organisations, but you can also approach employers yourself and offer an Erasmus+ funded placement, which many organisations welcome.
