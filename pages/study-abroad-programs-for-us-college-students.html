Study Abroad Programs for US College Students

🗓️ Aug 5, 2026⏱️ 11 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

More than a quarter of a million US undergraduates now study abroad every year, according to IIE's Open Doors data, and nearly every American university runs a study abroad office of its own. That breadth is both the good news and the challenge: "study abroad" covers several very different kinds of programs, each with its own cost, credit model, and application process.

This complete guide maps the main study abroad programs for US students, explains how federal financial aid and scholarships apply overseas, and gives you a realistic timeline so you can plan without panic.

Key takeaways

  • Four main routes exist: university exchange, third-party providers, faculty-led courses, and direct enrollment.
  • Exchange programs usually let you pay your home tuition, making them the most affordable path.
  • Federal Pell Grants and Stafford loans can travel abroad if you use an approved, credit-bearing program.
  • The Benjamin A. Gilman Scholarship adds up to $5,000 for Pell-eligible students, more for critical languages or STEM.
  • Providers like CIEE, SIT, API Abroad and IES Abroad charge more but handle almost everything for you.
  • Start working with your study abroad office 8 to 12 months before you want to leave.

The four main types of study abroad programs

Every route below starts at your university's study abroad office, which approves programs, manages credit transfer, and connects you with funding. Which option fits depends on your budget, your language level, and how much structure you want.

University exchange programs

In an exchange, your home school sends you to a partner university abroad and you keep paying your normal home tuition, usually with financial aid applying as if you were still on campus. National networks such as ISEP (isep.org) extend this model through a fee-based membership, giving you hundreds of partner institutions around the world. This is typically the cheapest route, and it protects scholarships that are tied to your home institution.

Third-party provider programs

Providers such as CIEE (ciee.org), SIT Study Abroad (sit.edu), API Abroad (apiabroad.com) and IES Abroad (iesabroad.org) build complete packages: courses, housing, excursions, and full-time on-site staff. You pay their program fee rather than home or host tuition. They cost more than an exchange, but they open up destinations your university may not have partnerships with, and many offer their own grants and early-booking discounts.

Faculty-led and short-term programs

Many departments run 2-to-8-week trips, usually in summer or a January term, taught by your own professors. Credit transfers automatically, logistics are handled by your university, and the price is a fraction of a semester. For many students a short faculty-led trip is the perfect first taste of studying abroad.

Direct enrollment

With direct enrollment you apply to a foreign university as a visiting student, pay its fees, and bring the credit home. In low-tuition countries like Germany this can be dramatically cheaper, as our guide to studying in Germany for free explains. The trade-off is that you arrange visas, housing, and registration yourself.

How US federal financial aid works abroad

Your Pell Grant and Stafford loans generally follow you abroad as long as you stay enrolled at least half-time in a program your home university approves for credit. That approval is the key: study abroad through a program your school does not recognize, and your aid stops. Confirm everything with your financial aid office before you sign anything.

If you receive the Pell Grant, the Benjamin A. Gilman International Scholarship (gilmanscholarship.org) is your first stop, with awards up to $5,000 and up to $8,000 for students studying a critical language or a STEM subject. Gilman is a federal program with a spring and a fall application cycle every year.

Scholarships beyond federal aid

Dedicated funding for US students exists outside Washington too. The Fund for Education Abroad (fundforeducationabroad.org) awards up to $5,000 with a strong focus on students from underrepresented backgrounds. Boren Awards (borenawards.org) fund longer stays for students studying critical languages, in exchange for a year of federal service. Provider programs and most universities also run their own grant rounds, and our scholarships hub lists the main cycles and deadlines.

How to choose the right program

Judge every program on four tests: does the credit count toward your degree, where will you live, how much on-site support is there, and what is the real out-of-pocket cost after aid? If language improvement is a goal, choose a program that guarantees a language course and a homestay. If clean, efficient credit transfer matters most, English-taught exchange destinations like Ireland are hard to beat.

Your application timeline

For a fall semester, go to your study abroad office's info sessions the previous September and October, shortlist programs by December, and apply between January and March. Summer programs move faster, with many deadlines in late winter. Providers often admit on a rolling basis, but the best scholarships close early, so apply for funding first and programs second.

Finally, check your passport's expiry and start visa paperwork at least four months out, since some destinations take that long. With a clear route chosen and funding lined up, the process stops feeling overwhelming and starts feeling like a plan.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to go through my home university to study abroad?+

Not always, but it is almost always the best idea. University-approved programs protect your federal financial aid, make credit transfer straightforward, and are the only route through which your Pell Grant and federal loans can apply.

Can I use my Pell Grant when studying abroad?+

Yes, if you stay enrolled at least half-time in a program your home university approves for credit. As a Pell recipient you are also then eligible for the Benjamin A. Gilman Scholarship, worth up to $5,000 or more.

How much does a semester abroad cost?+

All-in costs typically range from about $8,000 to $20,000. Exchanges where you pay home tuition sit at the low end, while full-service provider programs in expensive cities sit at the top.

When should I start applying for study abroad?+

Start 8 to 12 months ahead. For a fall semester, attend info sessions in the previous September and October, apply to programs between January and March, and file scholarship applications even earlier.

What is the difference between an exchange and a provider program?+

An exchange sends you to a partner university while you keep paying your home tuition. A provider program like CIEE or SIT charges its own program fee but includes housing, excursions, and full-time on-site support.
