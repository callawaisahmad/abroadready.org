Study in Switzerland: The Complete 2026 Guide

Switzerland has two of the world's top engineering universities — ETH Zurich (#7) and EPFL (#22) — and a reputation for paying for it. The famous low tuition just changed: from autumn 2025 ETH and EPFL tripled fees for foreign students to CHF 4,380/year. You'll also need CHF 21,000 in a Swiss bank account and a budget of CHF 2,000+/month. Worth it? For top-10 engineering, often yes.

Why Study in Switzerland?

For engineering, technology and the hard sciences, few places compete with ETH and EPFL — both routinely in the global top 25. Add the University of Zurich (#100), Geneva (#155) and Basel (#158), and you have a compact, elite system. Master's programs at ETH and EPFL are ~90–100% English-taught, and Swiss salaries mean post-study work pays exceptionally well.

Reality check: Switzerland is the most expensive country in Europe to live in. Budget CHF 2,000–3,500/month in Zurich or Geneva. And for bachelor's, ETH teaches in German (C1) and EPFL in French (B2–C1) — the English is at master's level.

Affordable & Easy-Entry Universities

Switzerland's public tuition is genuinely low — but the honest trade-off is that admission is more competitive and living costs are Europe's highest. The universities of applied sciences (FH/HES) are the easier route: English-taught bachelor's programs, entrance-test admission instead of top-grade requirements, and fees around CHF 2,000–3,100/yr. Budget CHF 1,800–2,300/month to live.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it true ETH Zurich tripled tuition for foreigners?+

Yes. From autumn 2025, ETH Zurich and EPFL charge foreign students who move to Switzerland CHF 2,190 per semester (CHF 4,380/year), up from ~CHF 730/semester. Swiss and some EU/EFTA residents still pay the lower rate. Cantonal universities have their own (often lower) scales.

How much money do I need for a Swiss student visa?+

You must prove CHF 21,000 in a bank account held in your name with a Swiss-domiciled bank (this is the ETH-recommended figure, widely used across Switzerland). Parental sponsorship declarations are accepted only from Swiss C- or B-permit holders.

Can I work while studying in Switzerland?+

Yes, up to 15 hrs/week with a permit. For the first 6 months you're limited to the employer named on your permit. After study, Swiss employers can sponsor a B permit — but admission for third-country nationals is quota-controlled (see Immigrate to Switzerland).

Do I need to speak German or French to study in Switzerland?+

For bachelor's, yes — ETH teaches in German (C1), EPFL in French (B2–C1). For master's, no: ~90% of ETH and effectively all EPFL master's programs are in English (IELTS 7.0 / TOEFL 100+). English is also widely spoken in daily life, especially in Zurich.

Can I settle in Switzerland after studying?+

Possible but tough: third-country nationals need an employer to secure a B permit under quota (8,500 total in 2026), then the C permit (permanent) after 10 years (5 for EU/EFTA and some US/Canadian nationals). Studying in Switzerland helps, but doesn't guarantee a permit.
