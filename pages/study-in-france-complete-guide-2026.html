Study in France: Complete 2026 Guide (Admissions, Campus France, Visa & Costs)

🗓️ Aug 17, 2026⏱️ 28 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

France is one of the most popular study-abroad destinations in the world. More than 400,000 international students choose France every year, making it the fourth most popular destination globally after the US, UK, and Australia. And the reasons are compelling: extremely low tuition at public universities, a prestigious higher-education system, over 1,500 English-taught programmes, generous scholarships, and the chance to live in one of the richest cultures on earth.

But navigating the French system — Campus France applications, the VLS-TS visa, CROUS housing, the Eiffel Scholarship — can feel overwhelming if you do not know where to start. This guide is designed to be the only page you need. Whether you are a high-school graduate looking at a Licence, a master's applicant eyeing the Grandes Écoles, or a PhD candidate chasing the Eiffel, everything is here.

We will cover tuition fees with real numbers, the top universities with their strengths, the Campus France step-by-step process, admission requirements at every level, scholarships, the student visa, daily life costs, work rights, and a month-by-month application timeline. Bookmark this page and use it as your checklist.

Key Takeaways

  • Public university tuition in France is government-regulated and very low: EUR 170–380/year for Licence, EUR 243–380 for Master, EUR 380 for Doctorat.
  • Some universities charge a higher rate for non-EU international students: EUR 2,770/year (Licence) and EUR 3,770/year (Master).
  • The Eiffel Excellence Scholarship provides EUR 1,181/month (Master) or EUR 1,700/month (PhD) plus travel and insurance.
  • Students from many countries must apply through Campus France Études en France — a mandatory online platform.
  • The VLS-TS student visa (long-stay visa valid as a residence permit) is the standard route for programmes over 90 days.
  • International students can work 964 hours per year (about 20 hours/week) with no separate work permit.
  • After graduation, a one-year temporary residence permit lets you look for work or start a business.
  • Living costs range from EUR 500/month in smaller cities to EUR 1,500/month in Paris.

1. Why Study in France?

Every year, hundreds of thousands of international students choose France over the US, UK, Canada, and Australia. Here is why France consistently ranks as a top study-abroad destination.

Very Low Public University Tuition

France heavily subsidises higher education for all students, including international ones. At public universities, annual tuition for a Licence (bachelor's) is just EUR 170, and for a Master it is EUR 243. Even with the increased "frais differenciés" for non-EU students, you are looking at EUR 2,770 to 3,770 per year — a fraction of what you would pay in the US, UK, or Australia. Compare that with USD 30,000–60,000 at American universities or GBP 9,000–38,000 at UK institutions.

World-Class Higher Education

France has a long tradition of academic excellence. The Sorbonne was founded in 1257. The Grandes Écoles — including HEC Paris, École Polytechnique, and ENS Paris — are among the most selective and prestigious institutions in the world. French universities consistently appear in the global top 100 across QS, THE, and ARWU rankings. France is particularly strong in mathematics, philosophy, political science, engineering, business, medicine, and art.

Over 1,500 English-Taught Programmes

You do not need to speak French to study in France. As of 2026, there are more than 1,500 programmes taught entirely in English at both public universities and Grandes Écoles. These span every level — Licence, Master, and Doctorat — and cover fields from computer science and business to international law and public health.

The Grandes Écoles System

France's Grandes Écoles are elite, highly selective institutions that produce a disproportionate share of the country's leaders, CEOs, and Nobel laureates. HEC Paris is the number-one business school in Europe. École Polytechnique is a world-leading engineering school. Sciences Po is the training ground for diplomats and politicians. Admission is intensely competitive, but the payoff is extraordinary in terms of career prospects and network.

Central Location in Europe

France sits at the crossroads of Europe. From Paris, you can reach London in two hours by train, Barcelona in six hours, and Rome in under four hours by air. A French student visa also gives you access to the rest of the Schengen Area — 26 countries — for travel and exploration. Weekend trips to the Alps, the Mediterranean, Amsterdam, or Prague are genuinely practical.

Rich Culture, Language, and Lifestyle

France is the birthplace of Enlightenment philosophy, Impressionist art, haute cuisine, and fashion. Living in France means experiencing world-class museums, Michelin-starred restaurants at student-friendly prices, and a culture that values quality of life. Learning French — one of the most widely spoken languages on earth — opens doors across five continents and international organisations like the UN, UNESCO, the Red Cross, and the International Court of Justice.

Post-Study Work Rights

After completing your degree, France allows you to stay for one full year on an autorisation provisoire de séjour (APS) to search for employment or launch a business. Once employed, you can switch to a work permit. France also offers the passeport talent (talent passport) for highly skilled graduates, and after five years of legal residence you can apply for permanent residency.

2. The French Education System

Before applying, you need to understand how the French system is structured. It differs from the American, British, and Australian models in important ways.

Public Universities (Universités)

France has around 70 public universities offering programmes across all disciplines. They are state-funded, which is why tuition is so low. Public universities follow the national curriculum set by the Ministry of Higher Education. Admission to Licence (bachelor's) is generally open to anyone with a baccalauréat or equivalent. Master's programmes are selective and require a bachelor's degree with a minimum GPA. Popular public universities include Sorbonne University, University of Paris, University of Strasbourg, and University of Montpellier.

Grandes Écoles

These are the elite, highly selective institutions outside the public university system. They specialise in engineering, business, political science, and public administration. Admission typically requires completing two years of intensive preparatory classes (Classes Préparatoires aux Grandes Écoles, or CPGE), followed by a competitive entrance exam. Alternatively, some Grandes Écoles admit students through the DUT (Diplôme Universitaire de Technologie) or BTS pathways. The most famous Grandes Écoles include:

  • HEC Paris — Europe's top business school
  • École Polytechnique — leading engineering school
  • ENS Paris — elite school for philosophy and sciences
  • Sciences Po — political science and international relations
  • Mines ParisTech — engineering and applied sciences
  • ESSEC — business and management
  • ESCP — the world's oldest business school (founded 1819)

IUT (Institut Universitaire de Technologie)

IUTs offer two-year vocational degrees (DUT/BUT) in fields like computer science, business, communication, mechanical engineering, and tourism. They are a popular alternative to the traditional university pathway and provide strong links to industry. Many IUT graduates go on to complete a Licence Pro (professional bachelor's) or transfer to a university Master's programme.

The LMD System

France follows the LMD system (Licence–Master–Doctorat), which is harmonised across Europe through the Bologna Process:

  • Licence — 3 years (180 ECTS credits), equivalent to a bachelor's degree
  • Master — 2 years (120 ECTS credits), equivalent to a master's degree (split into Recherche and Professionnelle tracks)
  • Doctorat — 3 years (minimum), equivalent to a PhD

All programmes use the ECTS credit system (European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System), making your credits transferable across Europe. One academic year equals 60 ECTS. A full Licence is 180 ECTS, a Master is 120 ECTS beyond that, and a Doctorat is typically 3 years of research plus a defended thesis.

Campus France

Campus France is the French government agency responsible for promoting French higher education worldwide. It manages the Études en France online platform through which most non-EU students must apply. Campus France is not a university — it is the gateway to the system. Think of it as a combination of a university application portal and a visa processing body.

Academic Year

The French academic year runs from September to June. Most programmes start in September or October. Some master's and doctoral programmes have January intake, but this is less common. Semester 1 runs September to January; Semester 2 runs January to June. Exams typically occur in January and June, with a resit period in September.

3. Tuition Fees — Real Numbers

Understanding tuition fees in France means distinguishing between public universities, Grandes Écoles, and private institutions. Here are the actual numbers for 2026.

Public Universities (Government-Set Rates)

Tuition at French public universities is set by the government and is the same across all public institutions. For 2026:

  • Licence (bachelor's): EUR 170/year for EU/EEA students; EUR 2,770/year for non-EU international students (unless exempted by a bilateral agreement with France)
  • Master: EUR 243/year for EU/EEA students; EUR 3,770/year for non-EU international students
  • Doctorat: EUR 380/year for all students regardless of nationality

Important: Many non-EU students are exempt from the higher "frais differenciés" if they come from a country with a bilateral agreement with France (e.g., most African countries, several South American and Asian nations). Always check your country's status on the Campus France website before budgeting.

Grandes Écoles and Business Schools

These institutions set their own fees, which are significantly higher:

  • HEC Paris — Grande École Programme: EUR 25,000–45,000/year; MBA: approximately EUR 78,000 total
  • ESSEC — Grande École Programme: EUR 15,000–25,000/year
  • ESCP — Grande École Programme: EUR 12,000–20,000/year
  • École Polytechnique — Engineering programme: EUR 15,000–20,000/year for international students
  • Sciences Po — Bachelor's: EUR 13,000/year; Master's: EUR 16,000/year (with generous financial aid covering up to 100% of tuition for admitted students)

Engineering Schools

Public engineering schools (génie civil, informatique, mécanique) charge much less:

  • Public engineering schools: EUR 600–1,500/year for EU students; EUR 2,500–4,000 for non-EU students
  • Private engineering schools: EUR 5,000–12,000/year

Living Costs

Tuition is only part of the equation. Here is what you should budget monthly for living expenses:

  • Paris: EUR 1,000–1,500/month (housing alone can be EUR 600–1,200)
  • Lyon, Bordeaux, Toulouse, Nantes: EUR 700–1,000/month
  • Smaller cities (Lille, Strasbourg, Montpellier, Rennes): EUR 500–800/month

The French government sets a minimum financial requirement of EUR 615/month for student visa applications (approximately EUR 7,380/year). In practice, most students need more, especially in Paris. France requires a minimum of EUR 7,380/year (EUR 615 × 12) in a bank account or a sponsorship letter for visa purposes.

Hidden Costs to Budget For

  • Campus France fee: EUR 50 (one-time, non-refundable)
  • Visa fee: EUR 99
  • Health insurance: EUR 200–500/year (mutuelle) plus EUR 100/year social security contribution
  • Campus France interview: Sometimes included in the EUR 50 fee; in some countries it is separate
  • Registration fees at university: EUR 100–400 (includes CVEC — Contribution Vie Étudiante et de Campus)
  • French language courses: EUR 200–600 if needed

4. Top Universities — Detailed Profiles

France has hundreds of higher-education institutions. Here are the most notable ones for international students, with their key strengths, English programmes, and approximate costs.

1. Université PSL (Paris Sciences et Lettres)

Ranking: QS 2026 — 24th globally, 1st in France.
Location: Paris.
Strengths: Mathematics, physics, philosophy, economics, art history.
What it is: PSL is a federation of some of France's most elite institutions, including ENS Paris, Mines ParisTech, ESPCI Paris, and Conservatoire National Supérieur de Musique. It brings together 14,000 students, 2,900 researchers, and has produced over 30 Nobel laureates. PSL offers English-taught Master's programmes in data science, cognitive science, and innovation. Tuition at member institutions varies — ENS is free; PSL Master programmes charge standard public rates (EUR 243 for EU, EUR 3,770 for non-EU).

2. Sorbonne University

Ranking: QS 2026 — 40th globally.
Location: Paris (Latin Quarter).
Strengths: Humanities, medicine, science, engineering.
What it is: The modern successor to the historic University of Paris, Sorbonne University is one of the most prestigious and recognised names in global education. It offers over 200 programmes across sciences, humanities, and medicine. English-taught Master's programmes include marine science, mathematics, and AI. Standard public university tuition applies.

3. École Polytechnique

Ranking: QS 2026 — 35th globally.

Location: Palaiseau (25 km south of Paris).
Strengths: Engineering, mathematics, physics, computer science, economics.
What it is: France's most prestigious engineering school, founded in 1794. It enrolls about 3,000 students, of whom roughly 30% are international. The flagship programme is the five-year Ingénieur Polytechnicien, which includes a broad scientific foundation followed by specialisation. English-taught Master's programmes are available in applied mathematics, artificial intelligence, and financial engineering. Fees for international students range from EUR 15,000–20,000/year for the engineering programme, with financial aid available.

4. University of Paris (Université de Paris)

Ranking: QS 2026 — 60th globally.
Location: Paris.
Strengths: Multidisciplinary — law, medicine, economics, political science, sciences.
What it is: Created in 2020 from the merger of several Parisian universities (Paris Descartes, Paris Diderot, and others), it is one of the largest universities in France with over 60,000 students. It offers programmes across all disciplines and a growing number of English-taught Master's. Standard public tuition rates apply.

5. Sciences Po

Ranking: QS 2026 — 242nd globally, but 2nd worldwide for Politics and International Studies.
Location: Paris (with regional campuses in Dijon, Le Havre, Menton, Nancy, Poitiers, and Reims).
Strengths: Political science, international relations, law, economics, journalism.
What it is: France's premier school for the social sciences. It trains a remarkable number of French politicians, diplomats, and journalists. Bachelor's programmes are taught across its regional campuses in a three-year liberal-arts format. Master's programmes are more specialised. Sciences Po charges EUR 13,000/year for Bachelor's and EUR 16,000/year for Master's, but offers need-based financial aid that can cover up to 100% of tuition. English-taught programmes are available at all levels.

6. University of Strasbourg

Ranking: QS 2026 — 150th globally.
Location: Strasbourg (border with Germany).
Strengths: Life sciences, chemistry, medicine, law.
What it is: A major research university with 4 Nobel laureates among its affiliates. Located in the beautiful Alsatian capital, it offers a unique Franco-German academic environment. Standard public university tuition. Several English-taught Master's programmes in molecular biology, neuroscience, and law.

7. University of Montpellier

Ranking: QS 2026 — 200th globally.
Location: Montpellier (south of France, Mediterranean coast).
Strengths: Medicine (one of the oldest medical schools in the world, founded 1220), law, economics, environmental science.
What it is: One of the oldest universities in continuous operation. Strong in health sciences and environmental studies. Affordable Mediterranean lifestyle. Standard public tuition. English-taught Master's available in epidemiology, global health, and earth sciences.

8. Aix-Marseille University

Ranking: QS 2026 — 250th globally.
Location: Aix-en-Provence and Marseille.
Strengths: Law, economics, political science, physics, marine science.
What it is: The largest university in the French-speaking world by student population (over 70,000). Offers a very broad range of programmes. Located in one of France's sunniest and most affordable regions. Standard public tuition. Several English-taught Master's in economics, marine biology, and data science.

9. HEC Paris

Ranking: Financial Times 2026 — 1st in Europe for business.

Location: Jouy-en-Josas (suburb of Paris).
Strengths: Business administration, finance, strategy, entrepreneurship, MBA.
What it is: The most selective and prestigious business school in Europe. The Grande École Programme admits about 400 students per year (mostly through Classes Préparatoires). The MBA is internationally recognised. Tuition is EUR 25,000–45,000/year for the Grande École Programme. HEC offers generous scholarships to international students. Admission for international students is possible through the HEC International Track.

10. ENS Paris (École Normale Supérieure)

Ranking: QS 2026 — 40th globally.
Location: Paris.
Strengths: Philosophy, mathematics, physics, computer science, economics, history.
What it is: France's most elite institution for research and teaching. ENS admits only about 40 students per year through an extraordinarily competitive examination. Tuition is free for all admitted students, and they receive a monthly stipend. ENS has produced 14 Fields Medallists and 13 Nobel laureates. International students can apply through the ENS International Selection process.

5. Campus France — Complete Step-by-Step Guide

Campus France is the mandatory application gateway for students from most non-EU countries. Understanding how it works is essential. Here is the complete process, step by step.

What is Campus France?

Campus France is the official agency of the French government for the promotion of French higher education. It operates in over 130 countries and manages the Études en France online platform. For students from countries where Campus France is mandatory, you cannot apply directly to French universities — you must go through this system.

The Études en France Platform: Step by Step

Step 1: Create your account. Go to the Campus France website for your country and register on the Études en France platform. You will need a valid email address. Select your nationality, country of residence, and the level of study you are applying for (Licence, Master, or Doctorat).

Step 2: Choose your programmes. You can apply to up to 7 programmes across multiple universities and institutions. You can mix programme types — for example, apply to both public university Master's and Grandes École programmes. Choose carefully because you cannot change your selections once submitted. Use the Campus France search tool to find English-taught programmes and filter by language, level, and field of study.

Step 3: Upload your documents. The platform requires the following documents (all in PDF or image format):

  • Passport (scan of the biographical page)
  • Academic transcripts (all years of study, translated into French or English if necessary)
  • Diploma or degree certificate
  • CV / résumé (preferably in French format with a photo, but English is acceptable for English-taught programmes)
  • Motivation letter (lettre de motivation) — one for each programme or a general one, depending on the platform's requirements
  • Language certificate — IELTS, TOEFL, DELF, TCF, or Duolingo score
  • Letters of recommendation (if required by specific programmes)
  • Portfolio (for art, architecture, and design programmes)
  • Research proposal (for doctoral applications)

Step 4: Pay the Campus France fee. The fee is EUR 50 for most countries (some countries charge up to EUR 220 including the interview). Payment is made online through the platform. This fee is non-refundable regardless of the outcome.

Step 5: Attend the Campus France interview. After submitting your application and payment, you will be invited to an interview at the Campus France centre in your country (or the French embassy). This is not a language test — it is a motivation interview. An evaluator will review your application, ask about your study plan, career goals, and why you chose France and your specific programmes. The interview lasts 20 to 30 minutes. It is conducted in the language of your chosen programme (English or French).

Step 6: Application forwarded to universities. After the interview, your complete application dossier is forwarded electronically to the universities you selected. You cannot contact universities directly during this process — all communication goes through Campus France.

Step 7: Receive responses. Universities review your application and respond through the platform. This can take 4 to 12 weeks depending on the institution and programme. You may receive acceptances, rejections, or requests for additional information.

Step 8: Accept an offer. Once you receive an acceptance, you must confirm your choice through the platform. You can accept only one programme. After accepting, you receive an official admission letter (lettre d'admission préalable or certificat de pré-inscription).

Step 9: Proceed to visa application. With your admission confirmed, Campus France will issue a recommendation for your visa. You then proceed to the French embassy to apply for your VLS-TS student visa.

Campus France Deadlines

Deadlines vary by country and are updated each year. As a general guide:

  • Applications open: November to January (depending on the country)
  • Application deadline: February to April (for September entry)
  • Interview period: March to June
  • University responses: April to July
  • Visa application: June to August

Pro tip: Start your Campus France application at least 12 months before your intended start date. The process takes longer than most students expect.

Interview Tips

  • Be clear about why you chose France specifically (not just "it is cheap")
  • Know your chosen programmes well — explain why each one fits your career plan
  • Have a clear career plan for after graduation
  • If your French is weak but you are applying for a French-taught programme, explain your plan to improve it
  • Dress professionally and arrive on time
  • Be honest about your academic record — do not exaggerate

6. Admission Requirements

Requirements vary by level of study and institution type. Here is what you need at each level.

Bachelor's (Licence)

  • Academic qualification: Baccalauréat or equivalent (12 years of schooling). For non-French systems, your diploma must be validated by ENIC-NARIC France (the French national information centre for academic recognition).
  • Campus France application: Mandatory for most non-EU nationalities.
  • Language certificate: For English-taught programmes — IELTS 6.0, TOEFL iBT 80, or Duolingo 100+. For French-taught programmes — DELF B2 or TCF DAP (Test de Connaissance du Français — Demande d'Admission Préalable).
  • Transcripts: All secondary school transcripts, translated into French or English.
  • Passport: Valid for at least 6 months beyond your planned stay.
  • Motivation letter: For some programmes, especially at selective universities.

Note: For French public universities, Licence admission is generally open to anyone holding a valid baccalauréat or equivalent. Selective programmes (e.g., at PSL, Sciences Po) have additional requirements and their own application processes.

Master's

  • Academic qualification: Bachelor's degree (minimum 3 years / 180 ECTS) in a relevant field.
  • Transcripts: All years of undergraduate study.
  • CV: Up-to-date, ideally in French format (with a photo) but English is acceptable.
  • Motivation letter (lettre de motivation): This is crucial for Master's admission. It should explain your academic background, why you chose this specific programme, your career goals, and how this degree fits your plans. Keep it to one to two pages.
  • Letters of recommendation: Typically two academic references from professors who know your work.
  • Language certificate: IELTS 6.0–6.5 / TOEFL iBT 80–90 / Duolingo 105+ for English-taught; DELF B2 / TCF for French-taught.
  • Portfolio: Required for art, architecture, design, and some creative programmes.
  • Research proposal: Required for research-oriented Master's (Master Recherche).
  • Work experience: Not required for academic Master's but may be valued for professional Master's (Master Professionnel), especially in business and management.

Grandes Écoles

The Grandes Écoles have their own admission routes, distinct from the public university system:

  • Classes Préparatoires (CPGE): Two years of intensive study after the baccalauréat, followed by competitive entrance exams (concours). This is the traditional route for French students but is also open to international students who have completed the baccalauréat.
  • DUT/BTS route: Students with a DUT (two-year vocational degree) or BTS (Brevet de Technicien Supérieur) can apply to certain Grandes École programmes via the e-candidat platform.
  • International admissions: Many Grandes Écoles (especially HEC, ESSEC, Polytechnique) have dedicated international admission tracks. These typically require the same documents as Master's applications plus a strong CV and motivation letter.
  • Application platforms: Some use Parcoursup (for Licence-level admissions), others use their own portals or the e-candidat system.

7. The Eiffel Excellence Scholarship

The Eiffel Excellence Scholarship (Programme Eiffel) is the French government's flagship scholarship for international students. It is highly competitive and extremely prestigious.

What It Is

Established by the French Ministry of Europe and Foreign Affairs, the Eiffel Scholarship is designed to attract the best international students to French higher education. It is managed by Campus France and awarded to students nominated by French universities and Grandes Écoles.

Benefits

  • Master's level: Monthly stipend of EUR 1,181 (approximately USD 1,280)
  • Doctoral level: Monthly stipend of EUR 1,700 (approximately USD 1,840)
  • Return travel allowance: Economy-class return ticket to France
  • Health insurance: Complementary health insurance (mutuelle)
  • Housing assistance: In some cases, CROUS accommodation or a housing allowance
  • Cultural activities: Access to Eiffel scholarship events and networking

The scholarship does not cover tuition fees — but since tuition at public universities is so low (EUR 243/year for Master's), this is rarely an issue. The stipend is more than sufficient to cover living costs outside Paris.

How to Apply

You cannot apply directly for the Eiffel Scholarship. The process works as follows:

  1. You apply to a French university or Grande École for a Master's or Doctoral programme.
  2. The institution nominates you for the Eiffel Scholarship based on your application.
  3. The institution submits your nomination to Campus France before the deadline.
  4. Campus France and the Ministry evaluate all nominations and make the final selection.

This means the best strategy is to apply to institutions that are known Eiffel nominators and to contact the admissions office of your chosen programme to express your interest in the scholarship early.

Eligibility

  • Non-French nationality (dual nationals with French nationality are not eligible)
  • Under 30 years old for Master's applications
  • Under 35 years old for Doctoral applications
  • Not already studying in France at the time of application
  • Applying to a French institution for the first time at the intended level

Deadline and Tips

The deadline is typically in January each year (for September entry). To maximise your chances:

  • Maintain a strong academic record — the Eiffel Scholarship is highly competitive
  • Have a clear and specific career plan in your motivation letter
  • Contact the admissions office of your chosen programme early to express interest
  • Choose programmes where the institution has a track record of Eiffel nominations
  • Prepare a research proposal if applying at doctoral level — it should be specific, feasible, and aligned with the institution's research strengths

8. The French Student Visa Process

For programmes lasting more than 90 days, you need a VLS-TS (Visa Long Séjour valant Titre de Séjour) — a long-stay visa that serves as your residence permit for the duration of your studies.

Step-by-Step Visa Process

Step 1: Complete Campus France application. If your country requires Campus France, this is mandatory before you can apply for a visa. If your country does not require Campus France (e.g., some EU nationals), you can apply directly to the university and proceed straight to the embassy.

Step 2: Attend Campus France interview. After the interview, Campus France will endorse your application and send a recommendation to the embassy. This recommendation is a key part of your visa file.

Step 3: Apply for the visa at the French embassy or consulate. Book an appointment at the French embassy or the visa application centre (VFS Global, TLS Contact) in your country. You will need to submit your complete file either online or in person.

Step 4: Prepare your visa documents. The required documents are:

  • Completed visa application form
  • Passport valid for at least 3 months beyond your planned departure from France, with at least 2 blank pages
  • Two recent passport-sized photos (35×45mm, white background)
  • University admission letter (lettre d'admission définitive or certificat de pré-inscription)
  • Campus France endorsement (if applicable)
  • Financial proof: Bank statements showing at least EUR 7,380 (EUR 615 × 12 months), or a financial guarantee letter (attestation de prise en charge) from a sponsor, or a scholarship award letter
  • Health insurance: Proof of health insurance covering your stay (private insurance or French social security registration)
  • Proof of accommodation: CROUS housing confirmation, a rental contract, or a letter from a host (attestation d'hébergement)
  • Visa fee: EUR 99 (non-refundable)

Step 5: Pay the visa fee and submit. Pay EUR 99 at the embassy or through the online portal. Keep the receipt.

Step 6: Wait for processing. Visa processing typically takes 2 to 4 weeks, but it can take longer during peak season (July–September). Apply at least 3 months before your programme start date.

Step 7: Receive your visa. The VLS-TS student visa is placed in your passport. Check all details — name, dates, type of visa.

Step 8: Travel to France. You can enter France up to 90 days before the start of your programme.

Step 9: Validate your visa at the local prefecture. Within 3 months of arriving in France, you must validate your VLS-TS at the local prefecture (préfecture). This converts your visa into a full residence permit. You will need to provide biometric data (fingerprints and photo). Do not skip this step — failure to validate means your visa is not legally valid.

Important Visa Notes

  • Students from EU/EEA countries do not need a visa.
  • Students from Switzerland, Monaco, Andorra, and some French overseas territories have special arrangements.
  • Some nationalities (e.g., Algerian students) follow a separate bilateral agreement process.
  • Keep copies of all your documents — the prefecture may ask for them during validation.

9. Living in France as an International Student

Living in France is one of the great rewards of studying there. Here is what to expect in terms of daily life and costs.

Accommodation

Housing is the biggest expense. Options include:

  • CROUS halls (résidences universitaires): Government-subsidised student housing managed by the Centre Régional des Œuvres Universitaires et Scolaires. Prices range from EUR 200–400/month depending on the city and room type. CROUS rooms are basic but clean and well-located. Apply through the CROUS portal as early as possible — demand far exceeds supply, especially in Paris.
  • Private rental: A studio or one-bedroom apartment in a private building. In Paris, expect EUR 700–1,200/month. In Lyon, Bordeaux, or Toulouse: EUR 400–800/month. In smaller cities: EUR 300–500/month.
  • Shared flat (colocation): Very popular among students. Share an apartment with one to three other people. Costs range from EUR 300–600/month depending on location. Use sites like Leboncoin, SeLoger, or LaCarteColoc to find flatmates.
  • University residences (Résidences des Grandes Écoles): If you attend a Grande École, on-campus housing may be available at subsidised rates.

Food

France is a food paradise, and eating well does not have to be expensive. Budget EUR 200–350/month for food. The cheapest option is the university restaurant (Resto U), where a complete meal costs just EUR 3.30 for students with the CROUS meal card. Groceries from supermarkets like Carrefour, Leclerc, or Lidl cost about EUR 150–200/month. A meal at an inexpensive restaurant costs EUR 12–18.

Transport

Public transport in France is excellent and affordable:

  • Paris: The Navigo Easy pass costs EUR 75/month for unlimited travel on metro, bus, and RER within Paris and the Île-de-France region.
  • Other cities: Monthly student passes cost EUR 20–50/month. Many cities have bike-sharing systems (Vélib' in Paris, Vélo'v in Lyon, V3 in Marseille).
  • SNCF trains: The Carte Avantage Jeune (EUR 49/year) gives you a 30% discount on all TGV and Intercités trains. The Abonnement Réduc gives 50% off for students under 26.

Healthcare

As a student in France, you are covered by the French social security system (Sécurité sociale). Registration is free and automatic through your university or the CPAM (Caisse Primaire d'Assurance Maladie). Social security reimburses about 70% of medical costs. For full coverage, purchase a mutuelle (complementary health insurance) for EUR 15–40/month. French healthcare is excellent — doctor visits cost EUR 25–50, and hospital stays are largely covered.

Banking

You will need a French bank account to receive housing aid, pay rent by virement, and manage your finances. The main banks are:

  • BNP Paribas — largest bank in France, student accounts available
  • Société Générale — popular with students
  • Crédit Agricole — widespread, especially outside Paris
  • Crédit Mutuel — good student offers
  • Boursorama — online bank, no fees, quick account opening

Opening a bank account requires your passport, visa, proof of address, and proof of enrolment. Online banks like Boursorama can open an account in a few days.

Phone and Internet

Mobile phone plans in France are very affordable. You can get unlimited calls, texts, and data for EUR 10–20/month from providers like Free Mobile, SFR, Orange, or Bouygues Telecom. The Free Mobile plan at EUR 9.99/month is a student favourite.

CROUS Housing Aid (APL)

International students can apply for APL (Aide Personnalisée au Logement) housing aid through the Caisse d'Allocations Familiales (CAF). This provides EUR 100–300/month to offset rent costs. To qualify:

  • You must have a French bank account
  • You must have a social security number (provided by your university or CPAM)
  • Your accommodation must meet CAF standards (CROUS rooms and most private rentals qualify)
  • Apply online at caf.fr — processing takes 4 to 8 weeks

10. Working While Studying in France

International students in France have good work rights, though they come with conditions.

Work Hours During Studies

On a VLS-TS student visa, you can work up to 964 hours per year (approximately 20 hours per week during the academic year). During official university holidays (typically August and January), you can work full-time. No separate work permit is required during the first year of study — your student visa automatically includes work authorization.

After the First Year

After the first year, you need to obtain an APT (Autorisation Précédente de Travail) — a temporary work authorization — from the prefecture. This is a simple administrative process and is usually granted without difficulty. The APT is valid for the duration of your studies.

Minimum Wage

The French minimum wage (SMIC) is EUR 11.65/hour gross (2026). After deductions (social contributions, income tax), you take home approximately EUR 9.50–10/hour. At 20 hours per week, this translates to roughly EUR 760–800/month gross, or EUR 620–650 net.

Popular Student Jobs

  • Hospitality: Restaurants, cafés, hotels (especially in tourist areas)
  • Retail: Shops, supermarkets, and boutiques
  • Tutoring: Teaching English, maths, or your native language (EUR 15–30/hour)
  • Research assistant (Attaché Temporaire d'Enseignement et de Recherche — ATER): Teaching and research at your university (reserved for doctoral students)
  • Campus jobs: Library, student services, administrative roles
  • Internships: Many Master's programmes include mandatory internships, some of which are paid (EUR 500–1,500/month)

After Graduation: Post-Study Work Rights

  • Autorisation provisoire de séjour (APS): A one-year residence permit that allows you to look for work or start a business. You cannot work more than 964 hours per year on this permit.
  • Work permit: Once you find employment, you can switch to a standard work permit or passeport talent (talent passport) for highly skilled workers and recent graduates of French institutions.
  • Permanent residence: After 5 years of continuous legal residence in France, you can apply for a carte de résident (permanent residence permit).

  • Upload all documents to the platform

1 Month Before (August 2026)

  • Travel to France

  • Register with social security (Sécurité sociale) through your university

  • Attend orientation events and start exploring your new city

12. Final Thoughts

France is one of the best decisions you can make for your education and career. The combination of very low tuition, world-class universities, a vibrant culture, and clear post-study work pathways makes France hard to beat — especially when you factor in the Eiffel Scholarship, the Grandes Écoles system, and access to the entire Schengen Area.

The Campus France process may seem complex, but it is actually well-structured. Start early, follow this guide step by step, and you will find the process manageable. The key steps are: choose your programmes, prepare your documents, apply on time through Études en France, attend your interview, accept your offer, and get your visa.

Start at least 12 months before your intended start date. Use this guide as your checklist. France is waiting for you.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to study in France?+

Public university tuition in France is very low: EUR 170 to 380 per year for bachelor's, EUR 243 to 380 for master's (set by government). Some universities charge higher fees for international students (EUR 2,770-3,770/year). Private business schools charge EUR 5,000 to 30,000 per year. Living costs are EUR 800-1,500/month depending on city.

What is Campus France?+

Campus France is the French government agency that promotes French higher education abroad. Students from many countries must apply through Campus France's Études en France platform as part of their visa process. The platform handles pre-enrolment applications, fee payments and interview scheduling at the French embassy.

Do I need IELTS to study in France?+

For English-taught programmes, most universities accept IELTS 6.0-6.5, TOEFL iBT 80-90, or Cambridge B2/C1. For French-taught programmes, you need DELF B2 or TCF DAP. Many universities accept Duolingo. Some universities waive language requirements if your previous degree was taught in English or French.

What is the Eiffel Excellence Scholarship?+

The Eiffel Excellence Scholarship is the French government scholarship for international students. It provides a monthly stipend of EUR 1,181 (master's) or EUR 1,700 (doctorate), return travel, health insurance, and sometimes housing. Applications are submitted by French universities, not students directly. Deadline is typically January.

What are the best universities in France?+

Top universities include Université PSL (Paris Sciences et Lettres), Sorbonne University, École Polytechnique, University of Paris, Sciences Po, University of Strasbourg, University of Montpellier, and Aix-Marseille University. France also has elite Grandes Écoles: HEC Paris, ESSEC, ESCP, Mines ParisTech, and ENS Paris.

How do I apply to French universities?+

Non-EU students apply through Campus France Études en France platform (mandatory for many countries). You create an account, choose up to 7 programmes, upload documents, pay fees, and attend an interview at the French embassy. Some Grandes Écoles have their own application processes (Parcoursup for bachelor's).

Can I work while studying in France?+

Yes. International students can work 964 hours per year (approximately 20 hours/week) on a student residence permit. No separate work permit is needed during the first year. After the first year, you need a temporary work authorization (APT). Many students find work in Paris and other major cities.

What is the French student visa process?+

For programmes over 90 days, you need a long-stay student visa (VLS-TS). Apply through Campus France Études en France, then at the French embassy. Required: Campus France interview, admission letter, financial proof (EUR 615/month minimum), health insurance, accommodation proof. Visa fee: EUR 50 for Campus France + EUR 99 visa fee.

What is the CROUS housing aid?+

CROUS (Centre Régional des Œuvres Universitaires et Scolaires) provides subsidised student housing and social support. International students can apply for APL (Aide Personnalisée au Logement) housing aid of EUR 100-300/month through CAF. You need a French bank account and social security number to apply.

Can I stay in France after graduation?+

Yes. After graduation, you can apply for a one-year residence permit (autorisation provisoire de séjour) to look for work or start a business. Once employed, you can switch to a work permit. France also offers a talent passport for highly skilled workers and entrepreneurs. After 5 years, you can apply for permanent residence.
