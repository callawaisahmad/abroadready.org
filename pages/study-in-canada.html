Study in Canada: The Complete 2026 Guide

Canada is the world's most popular destination for international students who want a degree and a realistic path to permanent residence. The study permit is inexpensive, the PGWP gives up to 3 years of open work rights, and Express Entry turns Canadian study into CRS points. Here's exactly how it works in 2026.

Why Study in Canada?

Canada hosts over 800,000 international students — the second-highest total in the world — and it's easy to see why. Tuition is lower than the US and UK at every level, the country is famously safe and welcoming, and no other major destination makes permanent residence as attainable for graduates. Work while studying (24 hrs/week), then a PGWP of up to 3 years, then Express Entry or a provincial nomination — the "study-to-PR" pathway is genuinely designed to work.

Fair warning for 2026: Canada has tightened intake — a national cap (408,000 total permits), a required Provincial Attestation Letter for undergraduates, and new language/field requirements for the PGWP. The era of "any course gets you PR" is over. Choose a master's/PhD or a field-of-study-eligible programme carefully.

The Study Permit (Student Visa)

Most international students need a study permit to study in Canada for longer than 6 months. In 2026, undergraduates from outside Canada also need a Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL) — your university's international office requests it from the province on your behalf.

Proof of funds (2026 thresholds)

  • Living expenses: CAD 20,635/year (outside Quebec) — ~CAD 24,617 in Quebec (from Jan 2026)
  • First year's tuition (varies by university — see below)
  • Return travel costs
  • Funds can be bank statements, a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC), scholarship letters or a sponsor's documented account (with proof of relationship). Funds should be held for a consistent period — IRCC scrutinises sudden deposits.
Approval rates have dropped: 2026 saw study permit approval rates fall for some nationalities (e.g., as low as ~36% in some countries, down ~9 points from 2025) because of the quota system. Master's/PhD applicants face the least competition — one more reason to aim for graduate programmes.

Work Rights: While Studying & After (PGWP)

During study

  • Work up to 24 hrs/week off-campus during academic terms; full-time during scheduled breaks (no separate permit needed)
  • Since April 1, 2026, co-op work placements no longer need a separate co-op work permit — authorisation is automatic for eligible students
  • Spousal open work permits are now limited to spouses of students in specific programmes (medicine, nursing, engineering, law, education, pharmacy, vet, optometry, rehab therapy)

After graduation — the PGWP

The Post-Graduation Work Permit is the world's most generous standard post-study offer: an open work permit (any employer, any province) lasting the same length as your programme, up to 3 years for degrees of 2+ years. New 2025–26 requirements: you must graduate from a PGWP-eligible programme, meet a minimum Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) score (CLB 7 for university graduates, CLB 5 for college), and your field of study must align with labour-market needs (for college programmes).

  • PGWP fee: CAD 255 (work permit $155 + open permit $100)
  • Processing: ~90–120 days; you can work full-time while it processes
  • Your PGWP can't outlast your passport — renew your passport before applying
Why everyone mentions the PGWP: it's the single most reliable bridge to permanent residence. Canadian work experience is the strongest factor in the Express Entry CRS score, and PGWP holders routinely get invited within 1–2 years. See Immigrate to Canada.

Admission: Intakes & Documents

📅 Intakes

Fall (Sept — main intake), Winter (Jan) and Summer (May). Fall applications generally open 12 months ahead; competitive programmes (medicine, business, engineering) close 8–10 months ahead. Applying to a master's also exempts you from the PAL/quota system.

📋 Documents

High school or university transcripts (WES evaluation for foreign degrees often required), English test (IELTS 6.5–7.0 / TOEFL 90–100 typical), letters of recommendation for grad school, SOP, proof of funds, passport, and the PAL (undergrads).

Standard timeline for Fall 2027

  1. Now – Dec 2026: Research DLIs; take language tests; prepare documents
  2. Oct 2026 – Feb 2027: Apply to universities (start early for competitive programmes)
  3. Mar–May 2027: Offers arrive; pay deposit; university requests your PAL
  4. May–Jun 2027: Apply for the study permit (before peak season)
  5. Jul–Aug 2027: Biometrics + decision; arrive before classes start

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the PAL mandatory for everyone?+

Almost — but master's and doctoral students are exempt from the Provincial Attestation Letter since January 2026, as are some exchange students and in-Canada extensions. If your category needs a PAL and you apply without one, your application will be returned and you could miss your intake. Confirm your status on canada.ca before applying.

How much money do I need for a Canada student visa?+

First-year tuition + CAD 20,635 in living costs (outside Quebec; ~CAD 24,617 in Quebec) + return travel. Realistic total for an undergraduate: CAD 40,000–55,000. Show the funds clearly — bank statements, GIC or scholarship letter — and have them in the account well before applying.

Can I work full-time during summer breaks?+

Yes — during scheduled academic breaks you can work full-time. During term you're limited to 24 hrs/week off-campus. On-campus work has no hourly limit.

What's the difference between a study permit and a visa?+

The study permit lets you study and stay in Canada. Many nationalities also need a Temporary Resident Visa (TRV) or eTA to enter the country. You'll often receive both together — the permit allows you to stay; the TRV/eTA allows you to enter.

Can studying in Canada lead to permanent residence?+

More reliably than anywhere else in the world. Study → PGWP (up to 3 yrs work) → Express Entry or Provincial Nominee Program → PR in typically 2–4 years after arrival. Canadian study and work experience are the two biggest CRS advantages. Full details in the immigration guide.
