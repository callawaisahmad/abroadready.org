Why Do an Internship Abroad?

An international internship is one of the strongest additions you can make to your CV. Beyond the experience itself, a funded internship abroad proves you can work in a new culture, earns you a professional reference from a foreign employer, and — crucially — many programmes pay you rather than charging you. For students who can't afford an unpaid placement, stipend-funded programmes like DAAD RISE or IAESTE make going abroad genuinely possible.

Start Here: How to Win a Funded Placement

🎯 1. Match your profile to a programme

Internship programmes are built for different profiles: research programmes for STEM undergrads, international-organisation internships for social-science and policy students, and tech internships for CS and design students. Read our step-by-step strategy guide to find your best fit.

📅 2. Respect the timeline

Most summer research programmes (like DAAD RISE) open applications in autumn and close in winter — roughly 6–9 months before the internship starts. Tech internships often open 6–12 months ahead with rolling deadlines. Start early.

✍️ 3. Prepare documents once, reuse everywhere

A strong CV, transcript, and 2–3 references cover 90% of applications. Add a tailored motivation statement per programme. See our profile-building guide for the full checklist.

🗣️ 4. Ask your university

Erasmus+ traineeships, IAESTE and many exchange-linked placements are arranged through your own university's international office. A 30-minute meeting there can unlock funded opportunities you'd never find online.

💼 5. Combine with scholarships

If a placement is unpaid, don't rule it out — pair it with funded scholarships that cover living costs, or a conference grant that funds the trip.

🎓 6. Think about what comes next

A funded internship abroad strengthens every future application — graduate school, more scholarships, or a full-time job. Use it as a stepping stone to study abroad or a work visa route.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which internships abroad are fully funded or paid?+

DAAD RISE, IAESTE, OECD and Google internships are paid or stipend-funded, and several UN agencies (UNICEF, UNDP, UNHCR) pay interns. Erasmus+ traineeships come with an EU grant. Start with our DAAD RISE guide and the IAESTE guide.

Can international students apply for NASA internships?+

Direct NASA internships are generally limited to US citizens. International students can instead join the L'SPACE Academy (a free NASA-funded program) or apply to their own country's space agencies and research institutes. See the NASA guide for alternatives.

Do UN internships pay?+

It depends on the agency. UNICEF, UNDP and UNHCR now offer paid internships in many locations, while other UN bodies may not. Always check the specific vacancy on the UN careers portal before applying.

When should I apply for summer internships abroad?+

Generally 6–12 months ahead. DAAD RISE applications open in autumn and close around November–December for the following summer. Tech internships like Google open up to a year in advance with rolling deadlines.

Do I need a visa for an internship abroad?+

Usually yes. EU short placements may fall under visa-free rules for some nationalities, while longer paid placements require a work or trainee visa sponsored by the host. Check our visa guidance and each programme's advice.

Can internships lead to a job or further study abroad?+

Very often. Internships build the professional references and experience that graduate schools and employers look for, and several programmes (like OECD's Junior Professional Programme) are a direct path to a paid job.
