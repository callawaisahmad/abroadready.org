apply for masters in germany step 6 portals: what this step actually is

Apply for masters in germany step 6 portals is the submission stage. Everything before it was preparation: you shortlisted programmes, checked whether your degree is recognised, sat a language test, applied for APS, assembled certified documents and wrote a motivation letter. Nothing has happened yet at this point. A polished file sitting in a folder gets you nothing. Step 6 is where the file is actually transmitted to the admissions offices that will decide your future, and it has two doors: the central service called uni-assist, and the online portal of each individual university. Most applicants need both within the same season, because different universities on the same shortlist use different doors.

This is where good applicants lose. The files are complete, the grades are fine, the letters are strong, and the applications are late, paid for incorrectly, uploaded into the wrong slots, or submitted as sideways phone scans that no one in the admissions office can read. Those are not judgement failures. They are technical failures, and they are entirely avoidable. Treat step 6 as an operations task with a checklist, a calendar and a second pair of eyes, exactly as you would treat a visa appointment or a bank transfer.

The good news is that the whole system is well documented, mostly in German and English, and entirely doable from Pakistan with a card, a scanner or a phone camera used properly, and an email account. The bad news is that the German system is distributed rather than central, so there is no single national application form. Knowing which door each programme uses, and in what order, is the entire content of this step. If you want the wider context of where this fits, read the complete roadmap and timeline first; this post assumes steps 1 to 5 are done.

Do I need uni-assist for every university?

No, and this is the single most misunderstood fact about the German application system. uni-assist is not Germany's national application portal that everyone must use. It is one route, offered by many but not all universities, and a surprising number of programmes have no connection to it at all. If you apply only to English-taught master's at research universities, a good share of them run their own online portals and never involve uni-assist. If you apply to German-taught master's, to public universities with restricted admission, or to anything coordinated through a state-level process, uni-assist is much more likely to be the required route. Getting this decision wrong is what turns apply for masters in germany step 6 portals into a six-week delay instead of a two-day task.

Some universities operate in a third way that confuses applicants. They do not run a general uni-assist application, but they will accept a VPD document that you have obtained through uni-assist, either because uni-assist is one of their document-checking partners or because they ask you to submit the VPD separately from your own portal application. So you may find yourself needing a VPD without applying through uni-assist at all. The distinction matters, because a VPD requested alone and an application submitted through uni-assist are billed and processed differently.

How do I find out which route my programme uses?

Read the application or Bewerbung section of the programme page, and look for the specific sentence that tells you the route. German pages usually say either that you apply through uni-assist, naming it explicitly, or that you apply via the online application portal of the university, sometimes with a named system. If the page is ambiguous, the Hochschulkompass at https://www.hochschulkompass.de is the official database of German study programmes and usually records the application route, the start of the programme and the application deadline. If both sources are unclear, email the programme's admissions office with one precise question and wait for a written answer; keep the reply, because it is the authority if anything is later disputed.

Can I mix routes in the same season?

Yes, and most strong applicants do. Plenty of candidates submit some applications through uni-assist and others directly to university portals in the same semester, because the shortlist contains a mix of German-taught and English-taught programmes. Keep the two systems clearly labelled in your tracking sheet, because the deadlines, the fee structures and the status vocabularies are all different and a mismatch is exactly how an application gets lost. The only real constraint is money and attention: uni-assist charges per additional university, direct portals charge little or nothing, but every application still needs its own programme-specific motivation letter paragraph from step 5.

What happens if I choose the wrong route?

Usually nothing catastrophic. If a university that requires uni-assist receives a direct portal application, the file is redirected or the admissions office asks you to also register with uni-assist, which costs you a queue of four to six weeks. If a university with its own portal receives an unsolicited uni-assist application, uni-assist may return the fee or forward your data only if the university accepts it, so you may end up applying twice. The avoidable version of this mistake is assuming. Check the route per programme, write it in your tracking sheet, and never rely on what a friend did for a different programme at a different university.

What is the VPD, and why does it matter?

The VPD is the Vorpruefungsdokumentation, the pre-examination documentation. It is uni-assist's official assessment of your foreign academic qualification, issued electronically to the universities you choose. In plain terms it answers one question: does this bachelor's degree, as issued in Pakistan, qualify as an academic basis for a German master's programme? The answer comes with a documented result, and the universities use that result instead of each one re-deriving it internally. In apply for masters in germany step 6 portals terms, the VPD is the product you are buying, and the application is what you are buying it for.

The VPD matters for three practical reasons. First, it is often the difference between an application being assessed and being closed as incomplete. Second, it produces a structured result you can reuse across many universities, so the effort scales: one VPD can support applications to a group of related master's. Third, if your qualification is unusual, borderline or not listed in the German database anabin, the VPD process is the formal channel through which an equivalence decision is made, and the result is something you can point to in later applications and in the visa file.

What exactly does uni-assist check?

It checks the authenticity and academic level of your school and university certificates, the number of credits and the grading, and whether the qualification is comparable to a German bachelor's degree. In practice it reviews the degree certificate, the full transcript, the secondary school certificate, and sometimes a subject-specific credit breakdown or a statement from your university. It does not assess your motivation, your CV, your language level or your grades as an admission decision. The VPD is about whether you are eligible, not whether you are competitive. If the result comes back as not equivalent, that is a formal finding about the qualification rather than about your ability, and the appeal routes exist but are slow.

What is the difference between the different VPD types?

uni-assist can issue documentation scoped to a specific subject and course, and documentation covering a broader group of related courses, and the university tells you which one it will accept. Universities doing direct assessment often prefer a document tied to the precise programme, because it removes ambiguity about whether your subject matches. Ask for the most specific type the target programme accepts, because a broader document may be rejected as not programme-specific. If you are unsure, uni-assist's own guidance explains the types, and the programme's admissions office can confirm in one line of email. Never guess, because a wrong type costs the same four to six weeks as a wrong document.

Does a VPD expire?

It is generally treated as valid for the qualification it certifies, but universities set their own rules about how recent the underlying documents must be, and a document older than a few years may trigger a request for updated proof. If your bachelor's is more than five years old, expect a question about your professional record, which is a different issue and sometimes a strength. Treat the VPD as a live document: keep the PDF, the uni-assist reference number and the confirmation email, because you will need them again at enrolment, for the visa file, and whenever you add a university to the same application.

How much does uni-assist cost, and how do I pay?

About EUR 75 for the first application in a semester and about EUR 30 for each additional university in the same semester. That structure is the key to the economics, and it is the line item most Pakistani applicants underestimate when they first encounter apply for masters in germany step 6 portals. The marginal cost of the second, third and fourth application is small, so if you are already using it, using it for everything you can is usually the cheapest strategy. A Pakistani applicant sending ten applications in one semester pays roughly 75 plus 9 times 30, so about EUR 345, before any bank or card charges. That is a figure worth writing into the study abroad cost and budget guide alongside tests, attestation and courier costs.

What will the total step 6 spend be?

For a realistic six-application shortlist, budget the uni-assist block of roughly EUR 225, plus a EUR 20 to EUR 100 fee if one or two direct portals charge one, plus a few euros of card or transfer charges. Add nothing for the application itself, because German public universities charge no tuition, only a semester contribution payable after you are admitted. This is the assumption behind how to study in Germany for free, and it is the single largest reason German master's remain financially attractive to Pakistani applicants even after the VPD and portal costs.

How do I pay from Pakistan?

uni-assist accepts the common international card payments and bank transfer, and the exact options shown at checkout are the authority. In practice most applicants pay by card, which converts at the day's rate plus a foreign transaction fee, so use a card that does not add a percentage markup if you have one. If you pay by transfer, remember that the fee is considered due on the day uni-assist receives it, not the day you send it, and international transfers can take several business days. This matters at a deadline. Plan the payment three to five days before your last possible submission date, keep the transfer receipt, and never assume a screenshot of a bank app is proof of anything.

Can the fee be waived?

uni-assist operates a fee waiver and reduction scheme for applicants in financial need and for certain recognised categories of hardship, and the criteria and application route are published on their site. Pakistani students are not excluded by nationality, but the scheme is means-tested and requires evidence, so treat it as a possible partial saving rather than a plan. If cost is the binding constraint on your shortlist, it is far more efficient to reduce the number of applications than to rely on a waiver, and the study abroad loan guide for Pakistan covers the financing side for the whole project.

What if I pay but my status does not change?

Give it one business day, then check three things: that the payment method matches the name on the account, that the card or transfer actually completed rather than being held, and that you clicked the final confirmation step rather than stopping at the payment page. uni-assist will not begin processing an application that is recorded as unpaid, and an unpaid application is indistinguishable from no application once the deadline passes. Keep the uni-assist reference number, printed on every confirmation, because it is the only reliable link between your email inbox and a file inside a German administrative system.

How do I create a uni-assist account and upload documents?

Registration is a short form: an email address, a password, your personal details exactly as they appear in your passport, and confirmation. The detail that causes the most trouble is name fidelity, and it is the one that most often forces a second order in apply for masters in germany step 6 portals. Your name in the account must match your degree certificate, your transcript and your passport bio-data page, character for character, including middle names and name order. A mismatch here does not fail loudly; it fails when the VPD is generated with a name that does not match the university's records, and then the university asks for a clarification that costs a week. Fix it at registration and it never becomes an issue.

What is the correct order of operations inside the account?

Register, verify your email, complete your personal data, then either order a VPD or start an application, then upload documents, then select universities, then pay. Uploading before you have chosen the university is a common waste of effort, because the destination determines whether you need a VPD at all. If you register before your certified translations are ready, you can still create the application and save it as a draft, but do not pay until the file is complete, because payment is what starts the clock and the queue.

Which documents does uni-assist ask for, in what form?

Degree certificate, complete transcript with credit values, secondary school certificate, and the identity page of your passport, each as a legible scan, plus a certified translation for anything not in German or English. uni-assist also asks which universities and which programmes you are targeting, which is how your VPD gets scoped to the right subject. If a document is issued in Urdu, the certified English translation prepared in step 5 is the version you upload. Upload the translation and the original scan as one combined PDF per document, in the correct order, with the translation immediately after the original, unless the portal asks for separate slots.

What are the technical rules for the uploads?

Unprotected PDF only, no password, no encryption, text readable at 100 percent zoom, seals and signatures visible, usually 200 to 300 dpi, one document per file, and file names that identify the document. Scan pages the right way up; a sideways or rotated page is unreadable to a human reviewer and useless to any automated check. Stay inside the stated size limits, which are commonly a few megabytes per file and a total package limit per application. Never upload a photograph of a screen, never upload a chat screenshot of a certificate, and never compress a stamp into illegibility to fit under a limit. If a file exceeds the limit, reduce the resolution or split the document rather than shrinking the scan into a grey blur.

How many universities can I add in one semester?

uni-assist sets a cap on the number of programmes you can apply to within a semester, and the current figure is published on their site. Assume a moderate cap and design your shortlist around it, because exceeding it is not a technical workaround problem, it is a rejected application. Within the cap, select only programmes where you have done the programme-specific work from step 5, because a uni-assist application sends the same core file to every destination and the only thing that distinguishes your applications is the motivation letter. If your shortlist is larger than the cap, split it across routes rather than cutting quality.

What do I do after I click submit?

Save the confirmation email, save the PDF of the submitted application summary, note the date and the reference number in your tracking sheet, and then watch the status rather than the inbox alone. uni-assist reports its processing states in German, with terms broadly meaning documents received, documents being checked, VPD issued, applications forwarded, and any request for something additional. Save every message, because when a programme asks for a supplementary document in April, the difference between answering in two days and two weeks is often the difference between a place and a rejection.

How long does uni-assist take, and can I track the status?

Plan for four to six weeks from payment to a completed VPD, and considerably longer at the two worst moments of the year: the weeks immediately before 15 April, when the whole season's winter applications arrive, and the period around Christmas and the New Year, when processing genuinely slows. This queue is the main reason apply for masters in germany step 6 portals must start months before the deadline rather than weeks before it. Add the university's own assessment time after that, commonly two to eight weeks for an English-taught programme and longer where a committee meets monthly. From your first click to a final decision, a realistic total for a uni-assist application is eight to fourteen weeks, and any plan shorter than that is a plan that produces anxiety rather than applications.

What status words will I see, and what do they mean?

Expect a small set of states, in German with English support on the site: received or registered, in processing, under review, VPD issued and forwarded, application received by the university, decision pending, and the outcomes offer, waitlist or rejection. Two of these require action. A request for something additional means you must upload within a stated window, often short, and a waitlist position means you may have to accept or decline within days. Neither is communicated by email alone in every case, so check the portal at least twice a week during the relevant months rather than assuming silence means nothing happened.

What if I need to add a university after submitting?

Within the same semester you can usually add further universities to an existing uni-assist application, paying the additional fee per university, and this is the normal way to expand a shortlist after a first round of results. Adding a university usually reopens the VPD question for the new subject, which can add time, so do it early rather than after a rejection wave. If the new university's deadline has already passed, adding it will not help you, because the destination deadline governs the application, not the uni-assist one.

What happens if my VPD comes back as not equivalent?

First, read exactly what the assessment says, because findings differ: a document rejected as unreadable is a fixable technical problem, while a formal finding of not equivalent is a substantive one. Fixable problems are the common case for applicants whose scans are poor or whose translations are not certified, and re-submitting the corrected document is far more often the answer than applicants expect. For a substantive finding, ask uni-assist about the review process, and simultaneously ask the target university whether it will assess the qualification itself, because some universities do. Keep copies of everything, and if the result blocks several programmes at once, re-read the eligibility and degree recognition step before spending more money.

How do I apply directly through a university portal?

A direct application is done on the university's own online system, in English or German, and it is a genuinely different process from uni-assist rather than a thinner version of it. Roughly half of a typical Pakistani applicant's shortlist will be handled this way, which is why apply for masters in germany step 6 portals has to be planned as two systems rather than one. There is usually no central qualification check, so the portal may ask you to answer questions about your degree that a VPD would otherwise have covered: the awarding body, the mode of study, the credits, the grading scale, whether the degree is a joint or dual degree. Answer those questions precisely and copy your documents exactly, because the portal's validation is often stricter about internal consistency than uni-assist's. Many direct portals also have their own account system, their own status vocabulary and their own PDF requirements.

What do university portals ask that uni-assist does not?

Three things dominate. First, programme-specific free-text fields: a statement of motivation, an outline of your study plan, a description of your intended thesis topic, or short answers on why this subject and this university. These are graded content, not form-filling. Second, structured data: exact start and end dates, the name of the awarding university in the university's own records, the credit total, and the grade expressed in the national scale. Third, uploads into labelled slots, where each document type has its own field, and mixing them up causes silent rejections. Expect also an explicit confirmation screen, a declaration that your documents are genuine, and sometimes a request for a fee.

How long do direct portals take to decide?

Four to ten weeks for a complete file, with a wide spread. Programmes that run rolling admissions and pre-screen applications as they arrive may answer in three weeks, while programmes that convene a selection committee once or twice a semester may take three months. Crucially, direct portals often publish a status page or send an automatic acknowledgement that distinguishes submitted, under review, and decision made, which makes them easier to track than a postal file. If your plan to arrive in Germany depends on a specific intake, apply early enough that even the slow end of the range leaves room for the visa stage.

Can I apply to several programmes at the same university?

Usually yes, often within one portal account, but the rules on how many are permitted and whether you can hold several offers vary. Some institutions limit the number of simultaneous applications; some will consider you for a second choice only if you are unsuccessful with the first; some treat a second application as a withdrawal of the first. Read the terms before clicking, and if the situation is unclear, write to admissions. This matters for Pakistani applicants because accepting an offer usually triggers enrolment, the blocked account and the visa timeline, and the consequences of a mishandled second offer are covered in the visa refusal reasons guide.

What about programmes with restricted admission?

Where a programme is capacity-limited, German universities apply fixed national or state-level deadlines that are later than the open programmes' deadlines, commonly 15 July for the winter semester, and applications after that date are not accepted at all regardless of quality. Local restrictions, known as NC procedures, use the same date and involve additional steps such as a ranking, an assessment of professional experience, or a language test check within the formal process. Check whether your programme is restricted before you choose a date, because the open-programme deadline and the restricted deadline are separate and missing the second is fatal.

Can I apply through the DAAD portal instead?

Is the DAAD portal a fourth application route?

Yes, and it is essential to understand what the DAAD portal actually is, because many applicants assume it is an alternative application portal for admission. It is primarily a funding and information organisation. Its website at https://www.daad.de is one of the best free search tools for German master's programmes, and it lists programmes together with their funding schemes, and it runs the application process for a number of its own scholarship programmes. But the DAAD is not the admissions authority for a German public university. Getting a place at a university is always a decision by that university, and the DAAD's role is to fund it. The same warning applies to a search portal inside apply for masters in germany step 6 portals planning: a listing tells you a programme exists, not that you can apply to it through that site.

So do I submit one application or two?

Two, for a DAAD-funded programme, and they are genuinely different documents. The admission application goes to the university, usually through uni-assist or through the university's own portal, exactly as described above. The scholarship application goes to the funder, on the funder's form, in the funder's format, usually with a dedicated scholarship CV, a scholarship motivation letter, a detailed budget and sometimes a research proposal. Both must succeed. The scholarship and funding mechanics are set out in DAAD scholarships explained for studying in Germany, and the wider Pakistani funding landscape is in scholarships for Pakistani students.

Can the two applications be submitted in the same order?

Usually the funding body wants a confirmation of admission or a conditional offer before it pays, so the sequence is usually: apply for admission, receive a conditional offer, apply for funding with that offer attached, then confirm the place once funding is approved. Some programmes invert this and want the scholarship form first because they only forward shortlisted candidates to the university, which is common in the development-related postgraduate schemes. The programme page will say which sequence applies, and following the wrong one is how a candidate ends up with a scholarship application that had to be thrown away and rewritten in a hurry.

Is a DAAD listing the same as a place you can apply to?

A DAAD listing tells you the programme exists, what it requires and whether funding is attached. It is a search result, not an application route, and a programme listed there may still require uni-assist, may still have its own portal, and may still have a deadline that has passed by the time you find it. Use the listing to identify targets, then go to the university's own page, which is the authority for the application route, the deadline and the document list. If a programme is listed on the DAAD site but the university page is unclear, contact the university, and keep the DAAD page open for the funding requirements, because the two sets of requirements often differ.

What are the real deadlines for German master's applications?

The deadline reality is that there is no single German deadline, there are dozens of dates, and the dates are set by programmes rather than by the country. Getting this right is the least glamorous and most decisive part of apply for masters in germany step 6 portals. The commonly cited 15 April date for the winter semester is a useful anchor rather than a rule. It is often the deadline for programmes that begin in October and have no earlier restriction, but a large number of English-taught programmes close much earlier, in December, January, February or March, and some keep places open until places run out. Programmes that fill early close early, and the popular ones, especially in business, computer science and data-related fields, can genuinely be full before the advertised date. Treat every date as a real date.

Two further rules catch applicants. First, deadlines are usually in German time, and a portal may lock at 23:59 or at midday on the stated day, not at your local midnight, so an application submitted at 11pm in Lahore on the deadline day can be locked out. Second, the visa timeline is what should drive your planning, not the application deadline. After an offer you still need APS processing, a blocked account, appointment availability at the German mission and visa issuance, and that chain takes longer than most applicants assume. Work backwards from your target arrival date rather than forwards from the deadline, and use the fall intake timeline as a sanity check on the arithmetic.

What is the safest submission strategy?

Submit everything at least one week before the stated deadline, treat uni-assist payment as a deadline in its own right, and keep one buffer week before the last date in your season for the one application that needs a fix. The buffer matters because a portal that rejects an incomplete file at the deadline will not reopen, whereas an office that receives a request for one missing document two days before closing will often accept it. If your shortlist has one dream programme, submit it first, while your attention is fresh and while a mistake still has time to be corrected.

What if I miss a deadline by a day?

Then it is almost certainly over, and chasing is a waste of the time you need for the next round. Email the admissions office once, politely, with the date and time of your submission attempt and your reference number, because a small number of systems record a late upload that the applicant believes failed, and occasionally a faculty will look at it. Do not build a strategy on that exception. Instead, plan the missed intake properly: apply for the next available start, use the extra months for the language test, the APS process, the blocked account and a stronger file, and consider that a summer semester start, which is smaller but real, can be a reasonable bridge. Studying in Germany for free covers the tuition side of that bridge.

What are the most common portal mistakes?

Every one of the failures below is invisible in the sense that the portal accepts the file and the admissions office never sees it properly. There is no rejection letter explaining that your transcript was upside down. The application simply produces nothing. Note that none of these are failures of content: the CV, the grades and the motivation letter assembled in step 5 may all be excellent, and the file still dies in transfer. That is the whole argument for treating apply for masters in germany step 6 portals as a technical discipline rather than an administrative afterthought. Ranked by how often they destroy otherwise good files, here they are.

Why do sideways and rotated scans cause silent failures?

Because phone scanning apps frequently save a page in the orientation the phone was held, so a portrait page becomes a landscape page with the text running vertically. A human reviewer can sometimes tilt their head and read it, but a reviewer processing two hundred files will not, and any automated extraction or check fails outright. The fix is trivial: open every PDF after scanning, confirm every page is upright, and check that the text reads horizontally at 100 percent zoom. Do this for every file, not the ones you scanned most carefully, because the mistake clusters in long documents scanned at speed.

What happens when I exceed a PDF size limit?

The upload fails, usually visibly, and applicants often respond by compressing the scan until it fits. That trades one failure for a worse one, because a heavily compressed stamp becomes a grey smudge and a grey smudge is treated as an unofficial copy. Reduce the resolution instead, from 300 dpi to 200 dpi, which is still perfectly legible for a document with standard type; or split a long transcript into a single combined PDF made from lighter pages; or ask the portal whether it accepts multiple files for one document. Know the limits before you scan, not after, and check the stated per-file and total package limits because they differ.

Why are password-protected PDFs rejected?

Many applicant tracking systems cannot open encrypted files, and several fail silently and record an empty attachment, which means your degree certificate simply was not submitted. Export unprotected PDFs, always, and never send your only copy of a protected file. Confirm the encryption was really removed: a PDF saved with a permissions password can look normal when you open it and still be locked for a script. Open each file in a private window, or ask someone else to open it, before uploading.

What does it mean when my document is not certified?

It means the file does not meet the university's authenticity standard and the admissions office will ask for a better one, or close the file. At the application stage most programmes want a clear scan of a sealed, stamped original, with a certified translation attached for anything not in German or English. What they will not accept is a photocopy of a photocopy, a photograph of a photocopy, a WhatsApp image, a portal printout of a result, or a self-made translation. This is the same chain covered in step 5, and the failures here are uploading the wrong artefact rather than producing the wrong one, so a five-minute comparison against the checklist prevents the whole category.

What other upload errors cause lost applications?

  • Wrong slot. Putting the transcript in the degree certificate field, or the language certificate in the CV field. Labelled slots exist because the admissions office uses them to build a checklist, and a misplaced file reads as a missing one.
  • Right file, wrong version. Uploading a draft CV, an old transcript without the final semester, or a motivation letter written for another university. Name your files with the programme and the date so the wrong version is obvious to you.
  • Name mismatch. A middle name added on the certificate but missing in the portal, or a portal that reorders names. Every document must match, and the passport is the reference.
  • Never pressing final submit. Several portals keep a saved draft indefinitely and the deadline passes with your file in draft. Save the confirmation email; if you do not have one, assume it was not submitted.
  • Unpaid application. uni-assist and a few university portals treat an unpaid file as not applied for. Pay early and verify the status changes.
  • Images instead of PDFs. Portals that ask for a PDF will reject a JPEG even if the image is perfect. Convert, do not photograph twice.
  • Cropped seals and signatures. Scanners that auto-detect edges cut off the university stamp that proves the document is official. Leave a margin of white space around every page.
  • Choosing the wrong intake. Selecting summer instead of winter, or a programme that has already closed, submits you to a queue for a term you cannot take.
  • Typos in the email. The confirmation goes to an address that does not exist, so you never see the status change and assume you were rejected.
  • One account, two people. Sharing a login with a cousin or a friend, so one of you overwrites the other's draft. Keep one account per applicant.

How do I check a submission before the deadline passes?

Re-open every uploaded file from the portal's own confirmation screen or your own downloads folder, not from the folder you scanned into. Confirm each page is upright, legible and complete. Check the programme, the intake, the degree and the name on the summary. Confirm the status says submitted or paid rather than draft or pending payment. Then save the summary as a PDF, screenshot the status, and put both in a dated folder. Ten minutes of verification after submission prevents the entire category of silent failure described above.

Step 6 is short in duration and heavy in consequence, so the plan is not about how long it takes but about not being late. Assuming your documents from step 5 are ready and your shortlist is final, here is a workable eleven-week version that ends with everything submitted two weeks before the main cut-off.

What should happen in each phase?

  • Weeks 1 and 2. Build a tracking sheet with one row per programme: university, programme name, route, portal or uni-assist, exact deadline with the time zone, fee, document requirements, and status.   • Weeks 9 and 10. Add universities to existing uni-assist applications where the programme allows and where the motivation letter is already written, and keep an eye on programmes that keep accepting late applications while places remain.
  • Week 11. Confirm that everything on your tracking sheet is submitted, paid and acknowledged, then stop refreshing. Key takeaways from apply for masters in germany step 6 portals

Step 6 is where a good application becomes a real one. Everything you built in steps 1 to 5 was preparation; this is the transmission, and transmission fails in boring, technical, entirely avoidable ways. A file that arrives intact, on time, paid for, with readable scans in the right slots, reaches a committee. A file that arrives sideways, unlocked from a draft, or eleven hours late does not, no matter what is inside it.

What are the five things that matter most?

One: know the route for every programme before you pay anything, because uni-assist, direct portal and DAAD are three different systems with three different rule sets. Two: pay uni-assist early, because the four to six week VPD queue, not the university's deadline, is what determines whether you are eligible in time. Three: treat your own submission as a technical operation, checking orientation, size limits, encryption, slots and the final submit button every single time. Four: save every confirmation and reference number, because that paper trail is how you prove what you sent. Five: treat the visa timeline, not the application deadline, as your real finish line, and start the next stage the moment your first offer arrives.

Frequently asked questions

What is uni-assist and do I need it?+

uni-assist is Germany’s central evaluation service that checks foreign school and university certificates for many universities. If your target university uses uni-assist, you submit your application there and it forwards a VPD result to the university.

How much does a uni-assist application cost?+

uni-assist charges about EUR 75 for the first application and EUR 30 for each additional one in the same semester. Direct university portals may be free or charge a small fee.

How do I submit my application to German universities?+

Create an account on uni-assist or the university portal, fill the online form, upload scanned documents in the requested order and pay the fee. Keep the confirmation and track your status in the portal.

What is the VPD document from uni-assist?+

VPD (Vorprüfungsdokumentation) is uni-assist’s document evaluation that confirms your bachelor’s degree qualifies for German masters study. Many universities require it before they assess your application.
