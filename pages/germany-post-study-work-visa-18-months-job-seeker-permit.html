Post Study Work Visa Germany: 18 Months

🗓️ Sep 28, 2026⏱️ 27 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Finishing a master's degree in Germany gives you something most countries do not: an 18-month legal window to look for work without leaving. This post study work visa germany route is officially a residence permit for job seeking under Section 20(3) of the Residence Act (AufenthG), and it applies to graduates of German universities.

The single most important rule is timing. You must apply at your local Ausländerbehörde before your student residence permit under Section 16b expires, because the day your student permit ends, your legal right to stay in Germany ends with it.

There is no second chance after expiry, no grace period, and no appeal that reliably buys time. If your student visa is still valid but your student residence permit has lapsed, you are already in an illegal stay.

Most students discover this only weeks before their thesis corrections, the exam date, or a job offer deadline. By then, appointments at the immigration office are booked three to four weeks out.

Book the Ausländerbehörde appointment the week you register for your final exam. This single habit removes almost all of the risk that the rest of this guide is designed to explain.

What is a Post Study Work Visa in Germany?

Germany does not formally name this a "post study work visa." The official document is a residence permit for job seeking, issued under Section 20(3) of the Residence Act, and it carries the stated purpose of job seeking on the electronic residence permit card.

Everyday English speakers call it the graduate visa, the post-study permit, or simply the 18-month permit. If you search for a germany visa for graduates, this is the permit that appears in every official answer.

The permit gives you 18 months from the date of issue to find employment. It is not renewable, extendable, or convertible in place; once you find a qualifying job, you switch to a different permit.

Because the electronic card records the purpose, border agents and employers can see the intent clearly. That is why a job offer letter must be consistent with what your permit says you are doing.

Who qualifies? Anyone who actually completed a qualifying degree at a German university, including public and private institutions, and whose degree is recognised or at least comparable in standard.

People who only studied in Germany but never sat their final exams are not graduates. They remain students under Section 16b and do not qualify until the degree is formally awarded or recorded.

People who studied entirely outside Germany do not qualify for this permit. They need a germany post study work visa in a different sense, usually the Opportunity Card, and the distinction matters when you are comparing routes.

Because the permit is for job seeking rather than employment, the law deliberately allows you to work while you search. It is a working search period, not a passive one.

The phrase job seeker visa germany is widely used, but it maps to two different legal instruments, and confusing them causes most of the mistakes students make when reading advice online.

When people say it about a German university graduate, they almost always mean the Section 20(3) permit described here. When they mean someone with a foreign degree and no job offer, they usually mean the six-month Opportunity Card.

Sorting out which one you are actually eligible for takes five minutes and saves months of wasted applications, so establish this before you book any appointment.

Compare this with the UK Graduate route, which gives two years but freezes your work rights during that time. Many applicants use the UK Graduate route comparison to decide which country's post-study system actually suits their situation.

Germany also counts this time toward permanent residency, which the UK route does not. That single difference changes the long-term maths for anyone who might settle here rather than use Germany as a launchpad.

Post Study Work Visa Germany: Eligibility Requirements

Eligibility is short and mostly documentary. There is no points test, no employer sponsor, no language exam at this stage, and no minimum grade requirement attached to this particular permit.

The first requirement is simple: you must have completed a qualifying degree at a German university. A completed master's, bachelor's, or state examination all count, and the award must already be recorded.

If your final certificate has not yet been issued, most offices will still accept a confirmation of successful examination from your university, provided the results are final and the date is certain.

The second requirement is timing, and it is the one that disqualifies the most applicants. You must file while your Section 16b student residence permit is still valid, or while you still hold a valid national student visa.

A national student visa typically remains valid for three to six months after enrolment ends, but that grace period applies to your entry status, not to a residence permit that has already ended.

Plan backwards from that expiry date rather than forwards from your graduation ceremony. Applications submitted two weeks late are routinely refused, and refusal ends your lawful stay.

The third requirement is proof of funds. For 2026, the standard is a blocked account of EUR 11,904 per year, which works out to EUR 992 for each month of intended stay.

The sum is a subsistence benchmark rather than a salary calculation. It is meant to show that you can live in Germany without becoming a burden on the state while you look for work.

Because the permit lasts 18 months, some offices ask for the entire amount rather than a single month. Confirming which calculation your city uses prevents a second appointment and a lost month.

Many Ausländerbehörden will also accept a job-seeker bank statement or an employment contract that evidences the same amount, and a few accept ordinary account statements instead of a formally blocked account.

Requirements differ by city and by case, so confirm the acceptable form of proof with your local office in writing before you transfer a large sum. Details are covered in our proof of funds guide for Pakistan.

The fourth requirement is valid health insurance. Your coverage must be continuous and appropriate, and student-specific rates usually stop applying the moment your student status ends.

Many students hold EXMAT or student supplementary cover that expires with enrolment. You will usually need to switch to a proper statutory or private policy before the immigration office will process the application.

The fifth requirement is suitable accommodation. This can be a signed tenancy agreement, a dormitory allocation, or a sublet confirmation, and it must cover the full intended period of the permit.

Universities and student services organisations often help with contracts in German, which removes a genuine barrier for graduates who have only ever read tenancy terms in English.

Some offices accept a simple accommodation confirmation without a full lease. Ask early, because this is a common reason for a document request that adds four more weeks to an already tight timeline.

A quick reference for the whole process is on the German government portal, which explains the job-seeker visa for academics in official terms.

Post Study Work Visa Germany: Documents and Costs

The document list for a germany post study work visa application is shorter than most students expect, because there is no employer letter and no contract to attach.

Bring your valid passport, your current student residence permit or national student visa, and a passport photograph or digital photo file for the electronic card.

Bring proof of your qualification: your master's certificate, or a university confirmation of successful completion with the exact award date.

Bring your financial evidence, which is either the blocked account confirmation, a job-seeker bank statement, or an employment contract showing the required monthly amount.

Bring your health insurance certificate. If your student policy ends before the permit does, bring the new policy certificate with you instead.

Bring your accommodation evidence, plus any registration certificate you already hold, since the Anmeldung often supports the address in your application.

Bring completed forms if your local office requires paper copies, and a bank transfer or card ready for the fee. Photocopies are usually fine, but originals are requested at the counter.

Get any documents that are not in German certified by a sworn translator, and check whether your country benefits from a visa agreement that removes the need for certification entirely.

The cost structure is deliberately low. Germany's public universities charge little or nothing in tuition, and the post study visa germany permit itself is a flat administrative fee rather than a large visa charge.

The electronic residence permit, or eAT card, is usually posted to you after the appointment rather than handed over on the day. Delivery commonly takes two to six weeks, and the card states the purpose of job seeking.

Keep your old student permit until the new card physically arrives, because the expiry date printed on the old document is what officials check if you are still within the application window.

How to Apply for the Post Study Work Visa in Germany

Application is a face-to-face appointment at the Ausländerbehörde for the city where you live, not at the consulate and not online. The process itself is straightforward once you have a slot.

Step one is booking. Many offices operate an online booking system, and in larger cities such as Berlin, Munich, Hamburg, Cologne, and Frankfurt, slots can disappear weeks ahead for the summer and autumn seasons.

Book the earliest available appointment, even if it is only a consultation slot, because a consultation gives you an official answer on funds and documents that no forum can match. Keep the confirmation email.

Step two is collecting your documents. Use the checklist the office publishes on its own website, because that list is the legally binding one for your city, and it is updated more often than national guidance.

Step three is the appointment itself. Arrive early, queue in person if required, and expect biometric capture for fingerprints and a photograph for the eAT card.

You will usually complete a short interview covering your degree, your current status, and your employment intentions. Answers should be consistent: you are looking for work, and your financial evidence supports living in Germany while you do.

Step four is paying the fee, which is roughly EUR 100 and payable by bank transfer or the accepted payment methods. Ask for a receipt, because you may need it later for tax or employer records.

Step five is waiting for the decision. Straightforward complete applications are often approved on the day, and in the typical case you receive a confirmation and keep your old documents until the physical card is posted.

Step six is receiving the electronic residence permit by post, usually within two to six weeks. The card states the purpose of job seeking, and it replaces the sticker in your passport if you still have one.

Shipping to a German address normally takes about a week within the country, but a move abroad during that window can delay or misdirect the card. Consider collecting it instead if your local office offers that option.

Also note that your old student permit is normally cancelled on approval rather than on collection. From the approval date, your legal basis is the new permit, not the expiring one.

If your application is refused, the written reasons will tell you whether it was timing, funds, insurance, or qualification. You can usually remedy the first three and reapply, which is why keeping evidence of your student permit validity is so important.

Individual universities also maintain their own international office checklists and often coordinate closely with the local immigration authority. A good example is the guidance published by Handbook Germany for international students and graduates.

What Can You Do During the 18 Months?

The 18 months job seeker permit germany route is unusually open compared with most post-study schemes, because your work is unrestricted while the job search is still in progress.

You may take any job at all, including one completely unrelated to your degree. Waiting tables, warehouse shifts, retail roles, and cleaning work are all permitted without a separate work permit.

The logic is straightforward: the permit exists so you can support yourself while you search, so income from any employer is valid evidence that you are not overstaying on savings alone.

You may also work as a freelancer, provided the activity is genuinely freelance and not disguised employment. A freelance contract or a freelance tax registration will make the distinction clear to an employer or tax office.

You may start a business, register a company, and operate commercially. The permit does not restrict entrepreneurship, and many graduates use the window to launch a venture that later becomes their main status in Germany.

Taking unrelated jobs while you search

Most students underestimate how useful unrelated work is. A warehouse job pays the rent, builds a German employment history, and gives you a work schedule you control while you attend interviews.

Employers also react well to the fact that you can legally start within weeks, which removes the notice period problem that delays many graduates in countries with stricter switching rules.

Make sure you are genuinely employed rather than working on cash without a contract. Undeclared work creates problems with the tax office and with any future residence permit application.

Freelancing and self-employment

Freelancing is a strong option if your skills are portable. Translation, programming, design, tutoring, and content work are common starting points for international graduates in Germany.

You will need a freelance tax registration, and in some professions a chamber or guild membership. A student visa usually prohibits these, but the job-seeker permit allows them from day one.

Starting a business in Germany

Starting a company during the 18 months is permitted. Many graduates use the period to register a holding company, consult for international clients, or build a product before the residence clock runs out.

Running a business and staying as a job seeker are compatible, but you must still be able to support yourself and explain your plan. Keep invoices and bank records from the first day.

Learning German and upskilling

Language ability is the biggest single factor in the salary you can reach. A B1 certificate widens your options, and a B2 certificate is often expected in professional roles even when the permit does not require it.

Technical certifications, cloud qualifications, and industry-specific courses are also well spent during the search, because they appear on your CV at the exact moment you need differentiation.

Travel and administration during the 18 months

You can travel freely within the Schengen area and re-enter Germany while the card is valid, provided it has not been cancelled and your residence registration is current.

Do not let the permit lapse while you are abroad. The 18 months runs continuously from the date of issue, and the card must be returned if you leave permanently.

Keep your Anmeldung current throughout. An address mismatch, an expired registration, or a landlord who does not report you to the Bürgeramt can create problems when you later apply for the EU Blue Card.

Health insurance must be maintained for the full 18 months without interruption, and gaps are a common reason offices refuse a follow-on permit. Set up a standing order rather than a manual payment.

Finally, treat the 18 months as a deadline rather than a cushion. Work in germany after masters graduates who wait until month twelve to apply for their Blue Card routinely lose weeks they could have avoided in month one.

After You Find a Job: Blue Card and Work Permits

When you receive an offer, the job-seeker permit ends and a new application begins. There is no in-place conversion, so you submit a fresh application and the new card reflects the new purpose.

The usual destination is the EU Blue Card, issued under Section 18g of the Residence Act. It is the standard route for graduates with a recognised degree and a qualifying salary.

The EU Blue Card germany 2026 salary thresholds are published annually and are the fastest way to test whether an offer qualifies. Compare your gross annual salary against the figures below before you sign.

New entrants and recent graduates often qualify under the reduced threshold, which is one reason graduating in Germany is strategically valuable even if your first job does not pay a premium salary.

The Blue Card also carries a genuine settlement permit after only 21 months if you hold German at B1 level, or 33 months without it. That is the fastest route to permanent residency available to most foreign professionals.

The alternative is the skilled worker residence permit under Section 18b, which is often called the standard work permit. It does not have the Blue Card salary thresholds, and it is a better fit for roles below EUR 41,042.

Section 18b also has a long-standing German language requirement for most applicants, so graduates who are still beginners may find the Blue Card easier to enter initially and switch later.

There is also a temporary provision allowing certain pending skilled worker applications to be processed faster, and graduates who can prove a qualifying offer can point their employer to it when time matters.

Under Section 18b, graduates also have a faster path to settlement. With German at B1 level and 24 months of pension contributions, many can qualify in roughly two years.

There is also the EU Blue Card's official portal, which explains the thresholds and process in plain language at the Make it in Germany visa overview.

Whatever route you take, the employer normally needs a work permit or approval before you start, which is why your employer will often prepare the documents for you. Confirm the salary against the current threshold in writing before you accept.

Remember that pension and health insurance contributions begin as soon as you start work, and those contributions are exactly what counts toward your later settlement permit. Every month of employment is a month closer to permanence.

Ask your employer at offer stage which permit they will apply for, and whether the salary figure on the contract is gross annual. Most salary thresholds are expressed gross, and a net figure on a contract is an easy trap.

From Post-Study Visa to Permanent Residency

Germany's post-study route is unusually good for long-term settlement, because time spent on the 18-month permit counts toward your permanent residence requirements.

That is the key difference from the UK Graduate route, where two years on the graduate visa do not reduce your eventual settlement waiting period at all.

The standard settlement permit, or Niederlassungserlaubnis, normally requires five years of lawful residence plus two years of B1 German, or seven years of residence without the language requirement.

However, the Blue Card has its own accelerated rules. Hold it for 21 months with B1 German and you can apply, or 33 months without German, and both are considerably faster than the standard track.

On the Section 18b skilled worker route, graduates can often qualify after around 24 months of contributions with B1 German, which works out to roughly two years of employment rather than seven.

Every route requires documented pension or retirement insurance contributions. This is not optional: the German social insurance system funds your retirement from your employment, and the settlement permit verifies that you have paid in.

Keep every payslip, every contribution statement, and every annual pension report. When you apply, the office will ask for a contribution history, and gaps in your records can delay an otherwise complete application by months.

Other requirements for a settlement permit usually include a secure livelihood, adequate housing, health insurance, and a clean record. A criminal conviction, even an old one, can complicate the assessment.

Language evidence must be certified, usually by a Goethe-Institut or telc certificate at B1. A university language course certificate is generally not accepted for this purpose, so plan the exam early.

One detail catches people out: the settlement permit clock does not pause while you travel, and time spent on a separate student visa in a different country does not merge with your German total.

Nor can you shortcut the B1 requirement with a placement test or an internal exam. Only a recognised certificate such as Goethe-Zertifikat B1 or telc Deutsch B1 is accepted, and you should sit it early enough to receive the result before you apply.

Post Study Work Visa Germany vs the UK Graduate Route

Both schemes let international graduates stay and work after a master's degree, but the legal designs are so different that choosing between them changes your life trajectory.

On length, Germany offers 18 months and the United Kingdom offers two years under the Graduate route, so on paper the UK looks longer.

On work rights, the UK Graduate route is restricted. Most graduate jobs are permitted but full-time work is capped and certain roles are excluded, which is a genuine constraint if your field is unusual.

Germany's job-seeker permit places no such restriction. You can take any job at all, freelance freely, or start a business, which makes it far easier to earn something while your career search develops.

On settlement, the two systems diverge sharply. UK time on the Graduate route does not count toward the five-year Skilled Worker settlement requirement, so many graduates eventually have to switch categories.

German time on the Section 20(3) permit does count toward your lawful residence total, and a Blue Card holder can reach a settlement permit in 21 months with B1 German. That is a decisive long-term advantage.

On cost, the UK route is more expensive. The Immigration Health Surcharge, visa fees, and the Certificate of Sponsorship add up, while the German post-study permit costs about EUR 100.

On salary access, a German master's carries strong recognition with employers, particularly in engineering, IT, and manufacturing, and there is no sponsor licence to obtain or renew. Our UK post-study work visa guide covers the British rules in detail if you are weighing the two directly.

For most graduates who studied in Germany, the decision is already made by the clock. The question is not whether Germany is better, but how early you can start the paperwork.

When to Start Preparing: A Practical Timeline

Graduates who handle this well do not treat it as an event. They treat it as a sequence that starts long before graduation and runs in parallel with the job search.

Six to nine months before graduation, confirm with your university the date your degree will be formally awarded, and ask what confirmation they can issue if the certificate is delayed.

Four to six months before, ask your insurer how long your student or EXMAT cover runs and what you need to switch to a standard policy. Book the Ausländerbehörde appointment as soon as the date is fixed.

Two to three months before, prepare your funds. If you are opening a blocked account, allow for bank transfer time, and keep the confirmation letter, since that is the document the office will check.

One month before graduation, gather your accommodation evidence and confirm that your Anmeldung can be updated. Address proof is asked for more often than candidates expect.

After graduation but before your student permit expires, attend the appointment, submit the file, pay the fee, and keep every receipt and confirmation letter in one folder.

Two to six weeks after approval, expect the electronic residence permit by post. When it arrives, check that the purpose states job seeking and that the expiry date is correct, and report any error immediately.

Then treat the remaining months seriously. The post study visa germany 2026 rules reward structured effort, and graduates who use the full window well rarely need an extension they cannot get.

Track applications in a simple spreadsheet with the employer, the salary, the permit category, and the date you submitted follow-up documents, and set a two-week reminder for every offer you have not heard back on.

Ask for feedback early and often. A single rejected application in month three is a lesson you can still act on, while the same rejection in month fifteen is a lost quarter you cannot recover.

Post Study Work Visa Germany vs Opportunity Card

The Opportunity Card, or Chancenkarte, is a separate six-month points-based permit for people who want to work in Germany without a degree from Germany.

It is not a replacement for the 18-month permit. The graduate permit is automatic on qualification, needs no points, and carries no job offer requirement, while the Chancenkarte is selective and points-based.

The Chancenkarte also requires its own financial proof and either German at B1 or a recognised level A1, plus qualifications that are comparable to a German degree. Those are real thresholds, not formalities.

A June 2026 court ruling, delivered as an obiter dictum rather than a formal holding, indicated that the Opportunity Card may be available after the graduate permit has been used.

The practical implication is significant: in principle, a graduate who has already spent 18 months in Germany could obtain a Chancenkarte and extend the job search by another 12 months.

That would take the total job-search window to roughly 30 months, which would be the longest such period anywhere in Europe. It is also a residence permit for job seeking germany question that thousands of graduates will now be asking.

Treat this as an emerging development rather than settled law. A ruling in obiter form does not bind every Ausländerbehörde, and individual offices may still refuse or treat the situation differently.

If you plan to rely on it, ask your local immigration office in writing and keep the written answer. Do not build a relocation or family plan around a ruling that has not yet been tested in practice.

Post study visa germany 2026 planning should therefore assume the 18-month permit as your baseline, and treat the Chancenkarte combination as a possible bonus that might add time if the practice develops.

FAQs

How long can I stay in Germany after my masters?

You can stay for 18 months on a residence permit for job seeking under Section 20(3) of the Residence Act, provided you apply before your student residence permit expires.

The clock starts on the date the permit is issued, not the date you graduated, and the permit cannot be extended beyond 18 months. You must use it to find qualifying employment.

Can I work any job on the German post-study work visa?

Yes. During the 18 months you may take any employment, including work unrelated to your degree, such as retail, hospitality, or warehouse roles.

You may also work as a freelancer or run a business. The only restriction is that you must not use the time purely to study a second degree while avoiding the job search.

How much does the German post-study work visa cost?

The permit itself costs about EUR 100, though the exact fee varies slightly by city and immigration office. The larger commitment is the proof of funds: EUR 11,904 per year, or EUR 992 per month.

Additional costs include health insurance, certified translations of non-German documents, and any fees charged by your university. The separate national student visa cost EUR 75.

Can I get a Germany job seeker visa without a degree from a German university?

No. The 18-month post-study permit is reserved for people who completed a qualifying degree at a German university. If you studied entirely outside Germany, the Opportunity Card is the relevant route instead.

That route still has points, funds, and language requirements you must satisfy. If you studied in Germany without completing your degree, you remain a student under Section 16b.

How do I switch from a job-seeker permit to an EU Blue Card?

You submit a new application at your Ausländerbehörde, usually with help from your employer. The package includes the employment contract, your degree certificate, and health insurance.

It also includes proof of the salary meeting the 2026 threshold of EUR 45,300, or EUR 41,042 for shortage occupations. The Blue Card is issued under Section 18g of the Residence Act.

Do the 18 months count toward permanent residency in Germany?

Yes, and this is one of the strongest arguments for using the German route. Time spent on the Section 20(3) job-seeker permit counts toward your lawful residence total.

If you then hold a Blue Card, you can apply for a settlement permit after 21 months with B1 German, or 33 months without it, provided your pension contributions are documented.

What happens if I miss the deadline?

If your student residence permit expires before you apply, your lawful stay ends and you must leave Germany. There is no automatic grace period and no retrospective fix.

In practice, an overstay can lead to a removal order, a multi-year visa ban, and difficulty ever returning to the EU. This is why booking your appointment early is critical.
