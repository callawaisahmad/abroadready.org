Paid Internships Abroad for US Students

🗓️ Aug 5, 2026⏱️ 10 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Paid internships abroad for US students exist, but they look different from the paid summer internships you might find at home. In most countries you cannot simply land in a city and start working: earning money requires legal work authorization, which is why the best-paid international internships come through structured programs that handle visas and stipends for you.

This guide covers the paid routes that genuinely work for American students, from research fellowships and government-run programs to reputable placement providers, plus the visa reality you need to understand before anything else.

Key takeaways

  • Working on a tourist visa is illegal nearly everywhere; choose programs that provide work authorization.
  • DAAD RISE pays US, Canadian, UK and Irish undergraduates for research internships in Germany.
  • IAESTE arranges STEM internships worldwide, usually with a stipend that covers living costs.
  • The JET Programme and Spain's auxiliar program are paid teaching routes that sponsor your visa.
  • Placement providers like CRCC Asia and The Intern Group often charge fees, and many placements are unpaid.
  • Working holiday visas in Ireland and New Zealand let US citizens earn money legally while they work.

The visa reality: you cannot just work abroad

The single biggest mistake students make is assuming a study abroad or tourist visa allows paid work. In the European Union, a short-stay Schengen visa explicitly forbids employment, and most countries only authorize paid internships through a formal sponsor. Instead of hunting for a random employer, target programs that come with work authorization built in, which is exactly what every paid program below provides.

Paid research internships

DAAD RISE in Germany

The DAAD RISE program (daad.de) places undergraduates from the US, Canada, the UK and Ireland in paid research internships at German universities and research institutes for 10 to 12 weeks each summer. You receive a stipend that covers living costs plus help finding accommodation, and applications open in the fall. Because German public universities charge no tuition, the entire experience is essentially funded. It is one of the most reliable paid international internships for STEM and science students, and our guide to studying in Germany for free explains the wider picture.

IAESTE

The IAESTE network (iaeste.org) organizes STEM internships in more than 80 countries through university-based committees. Placements usually pay enough to cover your living costs, and the organization handles much of the paperwork. Applications open early in the academic year, and many US schools have an IAESTE coordinator on campus, so ask at your career center.

Paid teaching and government programs

Several government-run programs pay salaries large enough to save money, sponsor visas, and still look outstanding on a resume. The JET Programme (jetprogramme.org) hires graduates, including Americans, to work in Japanese schools with a monthly salary around ¥280,000 and subsidized housing. Spain's North American Language and Culture Assistants program places US and Canadian citizens in schools for 12 to 16 hours a week in exchange for a modest monthly stipend. Both are technically teaching rather than internships, but they are the most realistic way for a new graduate to work abroad legally and get paid while doing it.

Placement providers: what they cost and whether to use them

Companies like CRCC Asia, The Intern Group, and the internship arms of CIEE and API Abroad sell structured placements in cities like London, Barcelona, Tokyo and Sydney. Be realistic about them: you pay a program fee, many placements are unpaid, and housing and visa support are added costs. Their real value is the network, the credit options, and the guaranteed experience if your home university awards credit. Ask three questions before paying: is the placement paid, is housing included, and does the fee cover visa help?

Working holidays and other legal ways to earn

For flexibility, look at working holiday visas. Ireland and New Zealand both run schemes open to US citizens aged roughly 18 to 30 that allow full-time work, while Canadian, British and Australian students have even broader working holiday agreements with several countries. These are not internships, but they let you fund an extended stay legally and build the same professional skills. US students still enrolled in a degree can also use the J-1 Summer Work Travel program through a designated sponsor.

How to apply and when to start

Start a full academic year ahead. DAAD RISE closes its application in December or January for the following summer; IAESTE and JET deadlines cluster in the fall and winter; and working holiday places are often released in waves. Treat the process like a job search: a tailored CV, a short cover letter, and one or two references. Frequently asked questions

Can I legally do an internship abroad without a visa?+

Almost never. Most countries prohibit working on a tourist or short-stay Schengen visa. Choose programs like DAAD RISE or IAESTE that provide work authorization, or use a working holiday visa where available.

Are international internships usually paid?+

No. Many placement providers place students in unpaid positions and charge a program fee. The paid routes are more likely to be government and research programs such as DAAD RISE, IAESTE and the JET Programme.

Who can apply for DAAD RISE?+

Undergraduates from the US, Canada, the UK and Ireland in STEM or related fields, typically in their second, third or fourth year. Applications open in the fall for the following summer.

Is teaching English abroad considered an internship?+

Not exactly, but programs like JET and Spain's auxiliar scheme offer paid, visa-sponsored work experience that many students use as their international internship.

Which working holiday visas are open to US citizens?+

Ireland and New Zealand operate schemes for US citizens aged roughly 18 to 30 that allow full-time work. Canadian, British and Australian citizens have broader working holiday agreements with several countries.
