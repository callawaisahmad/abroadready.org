What arrival covers in the masters in germany timeline

Arrival is where a masters in germany application stops being a file and becomes a life. The offer letter, the fee receipt, the interview and the visa stamp belong to the previous stage.

The stage begins when you open the front door of a flat with roughly ninety days of entry visa left and a list of German offices that all need to meet you.

Three statutory clocks now run at once. Register your address within 14 days of moving in. Enrol before the semester starts. Apply for a residence permit before your entry visa expires.

Miss one and everything downstream stalls. An unregistered address means no bank account, no insurance and no permit application. No enrolment means no exam registration.

The mechanics are identical for applicants running masters in germany from usa, masters in germany from asia and masters in germany from pakistan files. Only the portals, the currency and the travel days differ.

For wider context, read how to study in Germany from Pakistan in 2026, then step back to the complete roadmap and timeline if the earlier stages feel hazy.

Readers usually arrive from a study masters in germany guide, a requirements for masters in germany checklist, a ms in germany comparison, or the middle of a germany masters degree they already filed.

The dependency chain that runs the whole month

Everything hangs off one fact: a registered German address. Address registration in germany produces the confirmation that banks, insurers, universities and the immigration office ask for by name.

So the real order is address, then account, then insurance, then enrolment, then permit. Work that order and the month arranges itself. Work any other order and you create problems that take weeks to undo.

Who owns which task

The university international office owns enrolment, the semester contribution, the student ID and exam deadlines. The Bürgeramt owns your address. The Ausländerbehörde owns the residence permit.

The insurer owns the certificate. The bank owns the account. You own the bookings, the document folder, the follow-up emails and the calendar.

Realistic duration and the appointment bottleneck

Allow four to eight weeks for the whole stage, and read that spread as appointment availability rather than your own effort. The Anmeldung itself takes one morning once you hold a slot.

Enrolment can be a single online evening or an in-person hour with certified papers. A bank account takes one to three weeks waiting for a mailed card. The residence permit is the long pole.

In Berlin, Leipzig, Munich and Frankfurt, Ausländerbehörde slots run four to ten weeks ahead. The booking itself becomes the task, so book in week one and supply documents later.

Housing, documents and money to carry before you fly

Secure a permanent room before departure. It is the most important decision you can make from abroad, and it means a Studentenwohnheim place or a signed flat contract with a start date inside your visa window.

The Anmeldung requires a landlord's Wohnungsgeberbestätigung confirming that you live there. Large-city landlords will not list a room without your name and nationality.

On documents, your passport, the visa or entry sticker, the admission letter, four passport photographs and roughly EUR 800 to EUR 1,500 in immediately available funds are the baseline.

Add the full certified set for enrolment and immigration, in paper, in hand luggage. A photograph of a document on a phone is not a document.

If you must arrive with nothing, book a hostel for two nights and treat flat-hunting as a full-time job. Registering from a temporary address triggers a second registration later.

The first 72 hours after landing

Land, get connected, buy a German SIM in the arrivals hall, then resist the temptation to explore. Cities are full of students who treated week one as a holiday.

The appointment to book first

The Anmeldung at the Bürgeramt, Bürgerbüro, Einwohnermeldeamt or Serviceamt for your district, booked the day you land and ahead of every other task.

Every other job depends on a registered address, so this is the root of the dependency tree. Berlin's portal at https://service.berlin.de is the best documented example of how these portals work.

If the earliest slot is weeks out, add neighbouring districts and ask the Meldeamt directly whether student walk-ins are accepted in quiet hours.

Shortlisting a bank before you register

Shortlist two or three banks on day one and start the online application in the same week, unless a branch accepts a passport plus an entry visa as address proof.

Sparkasse, Volksbank, Commerzbank and Deutsche Bank normally want a registered address, so they are effectively stage two. N26, DKB and ING open from a German address with a permit or electronic identity document.

That is why anmeldung for international students comes first and opening a bank account in germany for students follows immediately after. The first is a legal duty, the second a practical dependency.

How to sequence a saturated week

Anmeldung, residence permit booking, enrolment, health insurance, bank account, semester contribution, then orientation week. The reason is dependency, not importance.

Orientation week is the most valuable item for daily life and the least time-critical, because it runs on fixed dates. The Anmeldung is the least enjoyable and the most urgent.

Students who rank these jobs by enjoyment end up in trouble, because nothing legal proceeds without a registered address and nothing financial without a bank account.

When must I complete the anmeldung in germany?

Within 14 days of moving into the flat. This is a statutory obligation under German registration law for anyone living in a dwelling, with no student exemption.

Registration makes you visible to the state. The confirmation unlocks a bank account, a health insurance policy, a permit application, a library card, official mail and eventually a tax number.

Treat 14 days as a ceiling, never a target. Book the appointment before you fly and attend it in your first week.

What documents does the registration office need?

A passport or national identity card, plus any previous residence permit. And a signed Wohnungsgeberbestätigung from your landlord or dorm office, naming the address, the household count and the landlord's details.

That landlord confirmation is the document most often forgotten. Add a signed registration form from the city portal, and a Meldeschein, though most cities generate it automatically.

For a shared flat, bring one confirmation covering all residents, plus one photocopy of each original, because some offices photocopy on site and charge for it.

Civil status documents count where relevant: a marriage certificate, birth certificates, a divorce decree or a death certificate for a changed surname. Fees run up to roughly EUR 25 to EUR 37.

When you have no landlord confirmation

A landlord is legally obliged to issue it. If you genuinely cannot obtain one, ask the registration office whether a Wohnraumverweis from the housing office is accepted.

In the last resort a self-declaration is accepted, usually because a student arrived with no contract or is subletting informally. Both routes are risky, because unregistered subletting in Munich or Berlin can become a permit issue.

Family members and dependants

Everyone living in the flat registers, each with their own appointment, though a family arriving together is often handled in one visit with a marriage certificate.

Bringing a spouse on a study visa is a separate immigration matter with its own funding proof and, in some cases, a language requirement. Children need birth certificates and a Kita place where applicable.

Inside the registration counter appointment

Thirty to sixty minutes in the office, after a wait of three to twelve weeks for a slot in high-demand cities. The clerk checks identity, records the address and prints a confirmation.

That confirmation is what banks, insurers, the university and the immigration office will ask for. Take two or three photocopies on the spot and photograph it the same day.

Bank account timing and address registration in germany

You need an account for daily life within days of registering. Rent is paid from a German current account by almost every landlord, and the semester contribution follows the same route.

Wages and scholarships are paid into German accounts, and the blocked account that funded your visa also pays out monthly, usually to a nominated German account.

Look for a student tariff: a low or zero monthly fee, a free German IBAN, English or German service, and no minimum balance requirement.

The public-sector Sparkasse banks and the Volksbanken Raiffeisenbanken cooperatives are strong on cash machines and in-person student services, which makes them a week-three task.

N26, ING, DKB, Commerzbank and Deutsche Bank open from a German address with an electronic identity document or permit, sometimes entirely by video.

Documents required to open a student account

A passport, a German address, and a residence permit or electronic identity document, or an entry visa plus a registration confirmation. Many banks also ask for proof of student status.

Online onboarding takes about fifteen minutes plus a video identity check. The card is posted to your registered address and arrives in five to eight business days.

Blocked account instalments and funding after the permit

The blocked account was a visa condition and it stays one. If you opened it through a Pakistani bank before travel, the monthly instalment arrives in Pakistan or at a nominated account.

Your visa officer wants evidence that you have drawn the correct sum each month, and continued instalments are a condition of the later residence permit as well.

The accepted proof-of-finance options, including the blocked account, a scholarship letter, a secured loan and a host declaration, are set out at https://www.making-it-in-germany.com. For the wider money picture, see the study abroad cost and budget guide.

Funding obligations once the permit is issued

Yes, for the duration of the permit in most cases. Sufficient funding is a statutory condition of the study residence permit, and the authority re-checks it at every renewal.

A scholarship replacing the blocked account should be documented and presented at renewal, because a switch of funding mid-degree is a reportable change.

One genuine exception is moving from a master's to a doctorate, where funding is assessed on its own terms. If funding fails, contact the Ausländerbehörde before it fails.

How do I complete university enrollment in germany?

Enrolment, called Immatrikulation or Einschreibung, converts your admission into student status. Until it is done you are not a student in the legal sense.

Most universities set a deadline a few weeks before lectures begin, and earlier for programmes with laboratory or clinical placements. Without enrolment you cannot register for examinations or borrow from the library.

This is also where the requirements for masters in germany stop being a checklist and become a counter queue. A conditional enrolment with one outstanding document is normal.

The document set for enrolment

The admission letter or Zulassungsbescheid, your passport, the beglaubigter Hochschulabschluss as a certified copy of your bachelor's certificate, and a certified transcript of records.

Add certified German translations where the originals are not in German or English, plus the APS certificate or individualised APS confirmation number and the required language certificate.

Also a student health insurance certificate, evidence of financing such as a blocked account statement, the registration form, a recent photograph, and a lease with landlord confirmation if asked.

Semester fee, semester ticket and student ID

The Semesterbeitrag is a legally required contribution that varies by university and state, commonly between EUR 150 and EUR 390 for a full-time student.

It bundles the student union fee, an administration fee, social services and the transport ticket, which covers a defined area for the whole semester.

The ticket, called Semesterticket or Semesterscheibe, usually runs longer than the official semester and ranges from around EUR 28 in Hamburg to over EUR 80 in Munich.

It frequently replaces a monthly transit subscription. Non-payment is one of the commonest reasons a university marks a student as not enrolled, so pay in week one and keep the confirmation.

Both the semester ticket and the student ID come with enrolment rather than separately. The Studierendenausweis is a chip card with a photo, normally free or a few euros.

It opens library borrowing, laboratory access, canteen payment and transport discounts. The paper Immatrikulationsbescheinigung appears on almost every administrative form, so keep three or four printed copies.

When the university asks for something you do not have

Write to the enrolment office the same day, in German if you can or in English with a request to reply in English. State exactly what is missing, why, and when you will have it.

The common gaps are a certificate still with HEC attestation, a language certificate older than the two-year rule, a missing APS confirmation, or a translation nobody commissioned.

Where a Pakistani attestation is in process, universities routinely grant conditional enrolment with the document due before the first assessment. What they do not forgive is silence.

A student who never chases a request is deregistered. A student who chases is nearly always accommodated. For the language requirements behind that paperwork, see IELTS requirements by country and TOEFL vs IELTS.

Health insurance before enrolment

Almost every student needs health insurance, and in Germany it is not optional for the same reason it is not optional for employees. The obligation is enforced as a legal duty.

Being uninsured can attract a blocking order from your local office. Student tariffs are cheap, and one certificate is accepted by the university, the immigration office and every doctor.

Apply in your first week, because the insurer needs several facts about you before it issues anything. An mphil in germany applicant follows the identical route.

Statutory or private cover

If you are under 30 you are almost always required to take gesetzliche Krankenversicherung, the statutory system, including funds such as Techniker Krankenkasse, AOK, BARMER and the IKK.

Student contributions in the public system typically run at EUR 40 to EUR 90 per month depending on age, income and fund, billed monthly or in semester instalments.

Private student tariffs exist at EUR 80 to EUR 200 per month and are often a poor deal, because age-banded pricing penalises exactly the group with no income.

The certificate and the chicken-and-egg problem

A German address is required, so the Anmeldung comes first. Then your full name, date of birth, nationality, passport number, university and programme, start of study, and any Versichertennummer from a previous fund.

The insurer then issues a Versicherungsbescheinigung, a stamped letter naming the university and confirming coverage. That letter is what enrolment wants and what a doctor will ask for.

There is a loop: the insurer asks for your matriculation number and the university asks for the insurance certificate. Break it with a printout of your enrolment application, which most universities issue on request.

Dental, optical and mental health

Statutory insurance covers medical, dental, hospital, psychiatric, preventive and rehabilitation treatment, with co-payments. Optical care is only partly covered, while mental health care is fully covered.

University psychosoziale Beratungsstellen give free short-term counselling to enrolled students regardless of insurance. For exam stress or homesickness, that service is the fastest route.

How do I get a residence permit for students in germany?

You apply at the Ausländerbehörde, the local foreigners authority, which is sometimes part of the Bürgeramt and sometimes a separate office.

A German student visa for study is normally valid for about 90 days, and that validity is your entire window to obtain the Aufenthaltserlaubnis.

The critical rule is simple. Apply before the visa expires, and never plan to arrive on the expiry date. If you apply in time, your existing residence is protected while the authority decides.

If the visa expires first, your legal position changes immediately, and repairing it takes months, money and often a lawyer. This is the highest-stakes item in the sequence.

Combined with enrolment, the permit completes the picture that the university and the authority check against each other at renewal. That is where masters in germany requirements for international students stop being a list and become a live status.

Booking the auslanderbehorde appointment

Online, through your city or Land portal, and as early as possible. Berlin, Munich, Frankfurt, Cologne and Leipzig typically show multi-week waiting times on any given morning.

Popular hours disappear within a day of new slots opening. Check whether your university runs a service that books immigration appointments for you, because several do.

The file for the permit appointment

Add a completed application form and proof of fee payment if the portal asked for an advance transfer, plus two photocopies of everything, because copying on site is slow.

Depending on the Land and your circumstances, the authority may also ask for a language certificate, a degree document, dormitory evidence, a photograph for the electronic residence card and a reachable phone number.

How much does the permit cost, and how long does it take?

The study permit is issued under section 16b of the Residence Act and is normally valid for one year, which means a renewal conversation every twelve months.

A second or longer period can be granted where course duration, funding and progress justify it, and doctoral students often receive longer periods.

The fee is approximately EUR 100, typically EUR 90 to EUR 120, depending on the Land, the length applied for, your age and any express processing charge.

Some cities add a supplement for a faster decision, which is legitimate but only worth paying if your visa is about to expire. The electronic residence card then arrives by post, often two to four weeks later.

While the permit is being processed

Live as a validly present applicant and keep every requirement alive. Stay enrolled, pay the semester contribution, keep insurance active, keep the blocked account drawing down, attend lectures and register for examinations.

Report any change of address, university, degree subject, side job above the permitted hours, or new scholarship. Reporting is cheap and keeps you compliant.

Working and travelling during the application

Yes, within the usual student rules. Enrolled students from non-EU countries may work up to 20 hours per week during the lecture period, as set out in part-time jobs for international students.

Short trips inside the Schengen area are normally fine, but your original passport is the visa document for some nationalities, and losing legal status by being abroad too long during a pending application is a real and avoidable failure.

If you intend to leave permanently, look at the post-study options first in the post-study work visa comparison.

How does the first week in germany for international students usually work?

Orientation is called Ersti-Woche, Einführungswoche, O-Woche or, in English-language programmes, International Student Orientation.

It normally runs in the week before term starts or in the first days of term, and it is the fastest way to settle into a German university you have never seen.

On a campus of 30,000 students where nothing is signed and everything is online, orientation is where you learn which system issues your exam registration number.

A student who attends typically saves three to four weeks in the first term. A student who skips it spends those weeks discovering what everybody else was told in a plenary hall.

The master in germany for international students route has no alternative sequence, so treat orientation as a task with a deadline rather than a social option.

What happens at orientation

Usually a mix: a session in English or a second common language, sessions in German for specific faculties, department introductions, and a programme of city tours and formalities desks.

Bigger universities run parallel tracks by faculty, so a law student does not sit through an engineering safety briefing. Expect to leave with a campus map, a timetable, a named contact and a social group.

The international office and welcome centres

The Internationale Hochschülerschaft or Akademisches Auslandsamt is the authority for enrolment, university-issued visas, scholarships and permit advice.

Several cities run dedicated welcome centres, and Berlin, Leipzig and Freiburg are the best known. They exist because municipal and university systems are separate, and they bridge the two.

Finding a mentor or buddy

Ask for one. Almost every university runs a Buddy or Mentor programme pairing a first-semester international student with an upper-year student, and some faculties arrange it automatically.

For a student from Pakistan, a mentor who already has an Anmeldung, a Girokonto, a permit and a working relationship with the Ausländerbehörde is worth more than most official handbooks.

Month-one mistakes that break masters in germany plans

Almost all of them are the same mistakes, made by students who arrived on a Friday and treated week one as a holiday.

Each is cheap to avoid and expensive to repair, and collectively they are why some students finish a term with no registered address, no bank account and an unfilable permit application.

Avoiding the pattern needs no cleverness, only a checklist and a willingness to sit in a waiting room. Every item below has ended somebody's plan to study masters in germany smoothly.

Do I lose legal status if my visa expires while I wait?

That is exactly what happens, and it is the most serious error on this list. Your entry visa and the pending permit are different legal objects.

The printed expiry date is not changed by a pending application. Applying in time is what protects you. Being in a queue is not a defence.

Check the expiry date in your passport the week you land, and tell the university before you fly if you are landing close to it.

Staying in a hostel while you flat-hunt

Not legally, but practically yes. Some cities will not register a commercial hostel address for long, and the standard Wohnungsgeberbestätigung does not exist for that address.

Meanwhile the 14-day clock started when you moved in, not when you found somewhere permanent. Book one or two nights before you fly and start looking on arrival.

Enrolling without a finished insurance certificate

You can often submit enrolment paperwork with a document pending, but you are not enrolled until the university says so and the semester contribution is paid.

Lectures may be open; exam registration, credit, transcripts, lab access and proof of status are not. Discover a documentation requirement in week six and you produce a term you cannot complete. Cover documented language requirements in advance.

Changes that put a permit at risk

Not if you report them. The permit's conditions are your course, your workload, your funding and your housing. A side job inside the permitted hours usually needs only standard notification.

A new scholarship replacing the blocked account must be evidenced, because the authority is confirming that you are funded for the permit period.

A realistic first-month schedule

Here is how the tasks distribute across the first month at a large city university when you arrive a week before term. The shape is identical in smaller towns, only faster.

Week one, day by day

  • Day one. Land, buy a German SIM, get a data connection, and confirm your contract and landlord confirmation are in hand luggage. Check your visa expiry date and photograph the visa page.
  • Day two. Book the Anmeldung appointment for days eight to fourteen. Download the registration form and document list, and book the residence permit appointment for anywhere between day 30 and day 75.
  • Day two to three. Apply for health insurance online with your university and programme details. Ask the university for an enrolment confirmation letter for the insurer.
  • Day three to four. Complete enrolment online or at the international office. Upload the certified documents, pay the semester contribution, and request the student ID and a stamped Immatrikulationsbescheinigung, with two extra copies.
  • Day four to five. Register for orientation week if it has not happened, join the faculty mailing list, activate your university account, and block the exam registration deadlines in your calendar.
  • Day five to seven. Order your bank card, assemble the permit file, and begin the permit application form if the portal has one. Buy the local transport pass the semester ticket covers.

Weeks two to four

Attend the Anmeldung appointment and collect two photocopies of the confirmation. Chase the health insurance certificate if it has not arrived, because enrolment and immigration both stall without it.

Attend the residence permit appointment with the complete file. Receive the student ID and the electronic residence card, and photograph both before they are used.

Claim what you are entitled to: the student transit ticket, a gym pass, a reduced-price Mensa card, and local museum discounts that accept the student ID.

Book your first supervisor meeting, then start building the social and administrative life that is the actual point of arriving.

Key takeaways for masters in germany

Arrival is the least glamorous stage of a masters in germany application and the only one with statutory deadlines attached. For masters in germany for international students, everything here is obtainable, mostly within a week, and mostly free.

The sequencing is what separates a settled student from a stressed one. Address registration unlocks the bank account, and the bank account plus insurance unlock the permit.

Enrolment plus the permit together define the legal basis for the whole degree. Follow the chain in that order and the month looks after itself.

The seven things that matter most

Book the Anmeldung appointment before anything else, and have the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung in hand. Book the residence permit appointment in week one even though you attend it in month two.

Complete enrolment before the semester starts and pay the semester contribution, which is where your transit pass comes from. Buy health insurance in week one, because two authorities will not move without the certificate.

Open the German bank account the week after registration, because the card takes a week to arrive. Carry your certified originals in hand luggage, because losing them costs weeks.

Attend orientation week, because it is the highest-yield item on the list in terms of time spent against results gained.

What to do first, today

Before you fly, secure the room and the landlord's Wohnungsgeberbestätigung, gather certified documents into one A4 folder with four spare passport photographs, and confirm your visa expiry date.

Then on day one of step 11, book the two appointments that are hardest to get, and write the dates down so you chase them rather than assume them.

Frequently asked questions

What is the first thing to do after landing in Germany?+

Register your address (Anmeldung) at the local citizens’ office within 14 days of moving in — it is the prerequisite for opening a bank account, health insurance and finally the residence permit.

How do I enroll at my German university?+

After arrival, complete enrollment (Immatrikulation) before the semester starts by uploading or submitting certified documents, showing proof of insurance and payments, and paying the semester contribution that includes the transport semester ticket.

When and how do I get my residence permit?+

Book an appointment at the Ausländerbehörde and bring your passport, Anmeldung, enrollment, insurance and blocked account proof. While valid, the visa covers your stay, so the permit appointment is a priority but not urgent.

Do I need a German bank account for daily life?+

Yes — a Girokonto (current account) is expected for rent, salary, semester fees and the blocked account payouts. Banks such as N26, DKB or Sparkasse allow online opening once you have a German address.
