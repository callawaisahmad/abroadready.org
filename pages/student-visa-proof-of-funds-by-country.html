Student Visa Proof of Funds by Country (2026): How Much Money You Need

📅 Aug 18, 2026 ⏱ 14 min read ✏️ AbroadReady Team

One of the most stressful parts of applying for a student visa is proving you have enough money. Every country has different proof of funds requirements, and getting the bank balance requirements wrong is one of the top reasons visas get refused. This guide breaks down the exact financial requirements for student visas in 2026, country by country, so you know exactly how much to show and what documents to prepare.

Key takeaways

  • Every country sets its own student visa bank balance requirements — there is no universal amount.
  • The UK uses a monthly formula (£1,529 London / £1,171 outside London × months of study). Canada requires CAD 20,635 plus tuition. The US varies by school.
  • Most countries accept parent sponsorship, education loans, and scholarship letters as valid proof of funds for student visa applications.
  • The UK's 28-day rule, Canada's 4–6 month holding period, and Australia's 6-month requirement mean you need to plan months in advance.
  • Cash, cryptocurrency, and property are almost never accepted as liquid proof of funds.

What is proof of funds for a student visa?

Proof of funds is official documentation showing you have enough money to cover tuition fees and living expenses for the duration of your study program. Immigration authorities require this to ensure international students do not become a financial burden. The specific financial requirements for student visas depend on the country, your program length, and whether you study in a major city or smaller town.

Most embassies look for liquid funds — money that is immediately accessible. This means bank balance for student visa applications should be in savings accounts, fixed deposits with maturity dates, or education loan sanction letters. Property deeds, car ownership, and stock portfolios generally do not count as proof of funds because they cannot be quickly converted to cash.

How much money do you need for a student visa? (Country-by-country breakdown)

United States (F-1 visa)

The US does not set a single national minimum for student visa bank balance requirements. Instead, your I-20 form from the university states the exact amount you need — typically covering one year of tuition plus estimated living expenses. For most programs, this falls between $10,000 and $30,000. You must show the full first-year amount on your most recent bank statement. The US values a student visa bank statement that reflects a current, stable balance rather than a long holding period.

United Kingdom (Student visa)

The UK uses a formula-based system for proof of funds. You need to show:

  • Tuition fees: Full amount for the first year (or the amount listed on your CAS).
  • Living costs: £1,529 per month if studying in London, or £1,171 per month outside London, for up to 9 months.

For a 12-month Master's in London, that's roughly £18,348 (tuition) + £13,761 (living) = £32,109. The UK enforces the strict 28-day rule — your bank balance must not fall below the required amount for 28 consecutive days before the application date. This makes the UK's student visa financial requirements among the most specific in the world.

Canada (Study permit)

Canada updated its proof of funds for student visa requirements in 2024. As of 2026, you need:

  • CAD 20,635 per year for living expenses (updated annually based on the Low Income Cut-Off).
  • Plus full first-year tuition fees.
  • Plus CAD 4,000 for a family member (if applicable).
  • Plus CAD 3,000 for a school placement (if applicable).

Canada wants to see the student visa bank balance held for at least 4–6 months before the application. This means sudden large deposits right before applying can trigger a refusal. The financial requirements for student visa to Canada are among the most scrutinized globally.

Australia (Student visa subclass 500)

Australia requires you to show:

  • AUD 29,710 per year for living costs (updated annually).
  • Plus full tuition fees for your course.
  • Plus AUD 2,000 for travel costs.
  • AUD 6,000 per year for each dependent child.

The Australian government wants evidence of funds held for at least 6 months before the application, though recent changes have allowed shorter periods with additional documentation. Scholarship letters from the Australian Government (like Australia Awards) can satisfy the proof of funds requirement entirely.

Germany (Student visa / blocked account)

Germany requires international students to deposit money into a blocked account (Sperrkonto). As of 2026, the required amount is €11,208 per year (approximately €934 per month). This is a real bank account where the funds are locked — you can only withdraw a limited amount per month once in Germany. This is one of the most straightforward student visa bank balance requirements because the amount is fixed nationally and does not depend on your specific university. The DAAD scholarship and other German government scholarships can waive this requirement.

Ireland

Ireland requires €12,000 per year for living expenses, plus proof of payment of tuition fees (or a scholarship covering them). Bank statements should show the funds held for a reasonable period. Ireland's student visa financial requirements are among the more affordable in Europe.

New Zealand

New Zealand requires proof of NZD 20,000 per year for living costs, plus tuition fees and NZD 2,000 for travel. A scholarship letter or confirmed accommodation booking can support your proof of funds for student visa application.

France

France requires proof of at least €615 per month (€7,380 per year) for living expenses. This is one of the lowest student visa bank balance requirements in Europe. Tuition at public universities is also very affordable (around €2,770–€3,770 per year for non-EU students), making France a budget-friendly option.

Netherlands

The Netherlands requires proof of approximately €12,000–€14,000 per year (known as the Pro Forma calculation). This covers tuition plus living costs. Your student visa bank statement should show this amount or proof of a scholarship/loan covering it.

What counts as proof of funds for student visa applications?

Each country has slightly different rules, but here are the generally accepted forms of proof of funds for student visa applications worldwide:

  • Personal bank statements: Most common. Usually 3–6 months of statements showing a stable or growing balance.
  • Sponsor bank statements: Parent or guardian bank statements, usually accompanied by a sponsorship letter and proof of relationship.
  • Education loan sanction letter: Accepted by the UK, Canada, Australia, and most European countries. Must state the loan amount and disbursement terms.
  • Scholarship award letter: If your scholarship covers tuition and living costs, this can fully satisfy proof of funds. See our scholarship database for funded opportunities.
  • Fixed deposit certificates: Accepted in some countries, especially for demonstrating a held balance over time.
  • Government sponsorship: If your home government is funding your studies, an official sponsorship letter is strong proof.

What is NOT accepted: Cash holdings, cryptocurrency balances, property deeds, vehicle ownership, gold/jewelry, and informal income. These are either too volatile or too illiquid for immigration authorities.

Can I use my parents' bank account for student visa proof of funds?

Yes, using parents' bank account for student visa applications is one of the most common approaches worldwide. However, you need additional documents to prove the financial relationship:

  • Sponsorship letter: A signed letter from your parent stating they will fund your studies.
  • Birth certificate: To prove the parent-child relationship.
  • Parent's bank statements: Usually 3–6 months, showing the required balance.
  • Parent's income proof: Employment letter, tax returns, or business registration.

The UK, Canada, and Australia all explicitly accept parents' bank account for student visa proof of funds. The US accepts it through the I-20 sponsorship process. Germany's blocked account can be funded by parents before you arrive.

Common mistakes that cause proof of funds refusal

Even with enough money, applications get refused for technical reasons. Here are the most common mistakes:

  • Sudden large deposits: In Canada and the UK, a large deposit added right before the application can look suspicious. Show a consistent balance history.
  • Wrong currency conversion: Calculate the exact amount in the destination country's currency. Exchange rate fluctuations can put you below the threshold.
  • Expired statements: UK requires statements dated within 31 days of application. Canada wants 4–6 months of history.
  • Missing documents: Not including the sponsorship letter, birth certificate, or loan sanction letter alongside bank statements.
  • Showing only tuition: The student visa financial requirements always include living costs on top of tuition.

Do you need proof of funds if you have a scholarship?

It depends on what the scholarship covers. If your scholarship is a fully funded scholarship that explicitly covers tuition plus living expenses (like Chevening, Fulbright, or Erasmus Mundus), you typically do not need to show additional personal proof of funds. However, you must provide the official scholarship award letter, and some countries may still ask for a small amount of personal funds for initial settling costs (airfare, first few weeks of accommodation).

If your scholarship only covers tuition, you will still need to show living-cost proof of funds for student visa purposes. Check our fully funded scholarships guide for programs that cover all costs.

Planning your finances: a step-by-step approach

  1. Check the official embassy website for the latest student visa financial requirements. These figures update annually.
  2. Calculate total costs: Tuition + living costs + travel + dependents (if applicable).
  3. Start building your bank balance 6+ months before the application to meet holding period requirements.
  4. Prepare supporting documents: Sponsorship letter, birth certificate, loan sanction letter, or scholarship award letter.
  5. Get documents translated into English (or the destination country's language) by a certified translator.
  6. Keep digital copies of all documents for your records.

Frequently asked questions

How much bank balance is required for a student visa?+

The required bank balance varies by country. The US requires roughly $10,000–$30,000 depending on the school, the UK requires £1,529 per month (London) or £1,171 per month (outside London) for up to 9 months, Canada requires CAD 20,635 plus tuition, and Australia requires AUD 29,710 plus tuition and travel. These figures update annually, so always check the latest government guidance.

Can I use my parents' bank account for student visa proof of funds?+

Yes, most countries accept sponsor funds from parents or legal guardians. You will typically need a sponsorship letter, the sponsor's bank statements for the last 3–6 months, proof of relationship (birth certificate), and sometimes proof of the sponsor's income. Some countries like the UK and Canada also accept education loan sanction letters.

What counts as proof of funds for a student visa?+

Accepted proof varies by country but generally includes: personal or sponsor bank statements (last 3–6 months), education loan sanction letters, scholarship award letters, fixed deposit certificates, and government sponsorship letters. Cash, crypto, and property are usually NOT accepted as liquid proof of funds.

How long do bank statements need to show the balance?+

Most countries require the balance to be maintained for a specific period. The UK uses a 28-day rule (balance must not fall below the required amount for 28 consecutive days). Canada and Australia typically want to see the funds held for 4–6 months. The US generally wants to see the most recent bank statement. Requirements change, so verify with the embassy.

Do I need proof of funds if I have a full scholarship?+

If your scholarship covers tuition plus living expenses, you typically do not need to show additional proof of funds. However, you still need the official scholarship award letter stating exactly what it covers. Some countries may still ask for a small amount of personal funds for initial settling costs even with a full scholarship.
