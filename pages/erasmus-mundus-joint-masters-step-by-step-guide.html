Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters: How to Apply (2026)

🗓️ Aug 12, 2026⏱️ 12 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters are among the most attractive fully funded study opportunities in Europe, and applying for the 2026–27 intake is one of the smartest moves a prospective master's student can make. Funded by the European Union, an Erasmus Mundus program lets you complete a single master's degree while studying in at least two different countries, taught by a consortium of top universities, and with a scholarship of up to €1,400 per month for the full program. For students who want an international, multi-country experience with strong funding, few options compare.

This 2026 step-by-step guide explains how the Erasmus Mundus scholarship works, who can apply, when to apply, and how to build an application that gets shortlisted. The program structure can look confusing at first, so we will break it down clearly.

Key takeaways

  • An Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters (EMJM) is an integrated master's delivered by a consortium of universities in at least two countries, with mandatory mobility between them.
  • The EU scholarship is currently €1,400 per month, up to a maximum of 24 months (€33,600 total), and scholarship holders cannot be charged tuition or mandatory participation fees.
  • For the 2026–27 cycle, most consortia open applications between October 2026 and January 2027 for a September 2027 start.
  • You apply directly to each program's consortium website, not a central portal, and each has its own deadline and limit on how many programs you can apply to.
  • Applicants from anywhere in the world are eligible, and there are a limited number of scholarships per program.

What are Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters?

An Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters is a prestigious, integrated study program jointly designed and delivered by a group of universities, called a consortium, based in different countries. When you graduate, you receive either a joint degree or multiple degrees recognised by all participating institutions. The defining feature is mobility: you spend time studying at two or more universities in different countries, following one common curriculum.

The 2026 Erasmus Mundus scholarship

Under current EU funding rules, the scholarship is a monthly allowance of €1,400 for the official duration of the program, normally up to 24 months, giving a maximum of €33,600. That is a contribution to living, travel, visa and installation costs, and it comes on top of a full waiver of tuition and mandatory participation costs, which programs are forbidden to charge scholarship holders. Some consortia offer an optional separate travel-and-installation payment that reduces the monthly stipend instead, so read each program's exact funding terms.

Eligibility

Anyone from anywhere in the world can apply. You normally need a recognised bachelor's degree, a strong academic record, and the English level each program sets (usually IELTS 6.5–7.0 or equivalent). There are usually limits on how many scholars come from the same country to keep the allocation fair, so applicants from countries with many applicants face stiffer competition for a fixed number of places.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Browse the official European Commission EMJM catalogue to find programs that match your background and goals.
  2. Check each consortium's website for the exact deadline, required documents and application limit, since windows typically run October to January.
  3. Prepare your transcripts, CV, two or three recommendation letters and your English test score early.
  4. Write a tailored motivation letter or statement of purpose that explains why this specific consortium, not a generic master's.
  5. Submit through the consortium portal before its deadline, and keep your confirmation email.

Because you can typically apply to only a limited number of Erasmus Mundus programs per intake, shortlist programs that genuinely fit you rather than applying widely. Our statement of purpose guide and our recommendation letters guide cover the two documents that decide most files.

What happens after you apply

Consortia review files, shortlist, interview some candidates and announce results in the spring. Selected students receive a scholarship agreement with the funding terms, then handle visas and travel, with the first monthly allowance covering many of the setup costs. For the bigger picture on studying in Europe, see our study in Europe for free guide and the full scholarship list.

Erasmus Mundus Tuition and Stipend: What You Actually Receive

The financial package of an Erasmus Mundus Joint Master is one of the strongest in international education, but the exact amount depends on several factors. The EU typically funds scholarship holders with a contribution to participation costs that covers tuition, a monthly living allowance, a travel contribution and an installation cost. A

t the time of writing, the standard monthly stipend is around 1,400 euros for non-EU students and around 700 euros for programme country students, though these figures can shift with each funding cycle. Tuition fees vary by consortium: some charge nothing to scholarship holders, while others deduct the participation cost contribution directly from the stipend. You must read the individual programme description carefully to understand the net amount you will actually receive each month.

What does erasmus mundus cover?

Beyond the basic stipend, the travel allowance is calculated based on the distance between your home country and the first programme country, using an EU distance band table. A student flying from Pakistan to Portugal, for example, would receive a higher travel grant than someone travelling from Germany to Spain. The installation allowance is a one-time payment intended to cover initial costs such as housing deposits, residence permits and basic supplies. H

ealth insurance is usually arranged by the consortium and included in the participation cost, but you should confirm this, as some programmes require you to arrange your own cover. When you add up tuition, stipend, travel and installation, a full Erasmus Mundus scholarship for a non-EU student can be worth more than 50,000 euros over the full duration of the programme.

What funding and benefits does erasmus mundus provide?

It is important to understand what the scholarship does not cover. The stipend must pay for rent, food, transport, books and personal expenses, and in cities like Paris, Amsterdam or Stockholm, 1,400 euros a month goes further in some fields than others. Students who choose programmes with expensive host cities should budget carefully and look for ways to reduce accommodation costs, such as university residences or shared flats. S

ome consortia offer additional top-up grants for students from specific regions or for particular subject areas, so always check the programme page for supplementary funding. Finally, the scholarship is paid in instalments, usually monthly or quarterly, and delays at the start of the programme are common, so arriving with a small emergency fund of at least one month expenses is wise.

Which erasmus mundus tips actually work?

The prestige of the Erasmus Mundus label carries value beyond the money. Employers and PhD admissions panels across Europe and internationally recognise the programme as evidence that you were selected through a rigorous, multi-university process. The joint degree or multiple degrees you earn are fully accredited in the countries where the consortium operates, and in most cases they are also recognised by relevant professional bodies. When you complete the programme, you should obtain official documentation of the joint degree from the coordinating university and confirm how it is classified in your home country, particularly if you plan to pursue further study or professional registration in Pakistan.

How to Apply to Multiple Erasmus Mundus Programmes Strategically

One of the most practical advantages of the Erasmus Mundus system is that you can apply to up to three different Joint Master programmes in a single application cycle, all through the same online portal. This policy exists because consortium deadlines and requirements are coordinated by the European Education and Culture Executive Agency, and it gives you a realistic chance of securing a place even if your first-choice programme is extremely competitive. H

owever, applying to three programmes requires a disciplined strategy. You cannot simply copy and paste the same motivation letter: each programme has a distinct curriculum, faculty expertise and set of partner universities, and committees can immediately tell when a letter has not been tailored to their specific offering.

What are the erasmus mundus requirements?

Start by researching programmes on the official Erasmus Mundus catalogue, which lists every funded programme with full descriptions, partner universities, entry requirements and contact details. Pay close attention to the programme structure: how many countries will you study in, what is the core curriculum, what elective tracks are available and what are the thesis and internship arrangements. Look at the academic profiles of the teaching staff and identify whose research aligns with your interests. C

heck the career outcomes of recent graduates, which some consortia publish on their websites or social media. Once you have shortlisted your three programmes, create a comparison table that maps your background against the stated requirements for each one, and identify the two or three strengths you will emphasise differently in each application.

How do you apply for erasmus mundus?

The motivation letter is where most of your differentiation happens. For each programme, open with a clear statement of why that specific programme attracts you, referencing particular modules, faculty members or the multi-country structure. Then explain how your academic and professional background prepares you for the programme, using specific examples rather than general claims. The third paragraph should address your career goals after the programme, showing that you have thought about how the degree fits into a longer trajectory. C

lose by explaining what you will contribute to the cohort, drawing on your experience working in diverse teams or your perspective from a specific professional or cultural background. Each letter should feel like it was written for that programme alone, even though the underlying narrative about your goals may be similar across all three.

What are the erasmus mundus deadlines and timeline?

Letters of recommendation are another area where advance planning saves you from last-minute problems. Most programmes require two academic or professional references, and your referees will need to submit their letters directly through the portal before the deadline. C

ontact your referees at least six weeks before the deadline, provide them with your CV, your motivation letter draft and a summary of each programme you are applying to, and ask them to write separate letters that address the specific qualities each programme values. Some referees prefer to write one strong generic letter that you can use across applications, but the strongest applications feature referees who have tailored their support to the programme stated selection criteria. F

ollow up politely two weeks before the deadline to confirm that the letters have been submitted, and again forty-eight hours before closing, as incomplete applications without references are automatically disqualified.

Financial planning also benefits from applying to multiple programmes. While all three may offer similar scholarship amounts, the cost of living varies significantly between host cities, and some programmes include tuition waivers while others deduct participation costs from the stipend. If you receive offers from more than one programme, you will need to compare the net financial package rather than just the headline scholarship figure. A

programme in a lower-cost city with a slightly smaller stipend may leave you with more disposable income than a programme in a capital city with a higher award. Additionally, some home-country governments, including Pakistani state schemes, offer supplementary grants for Erasmus Mundus scholars, so check whether your national funding bodies provide top-up support and what the application requirements are.

Erasmus Mundus Language Requirements and Preparation

Language proficiency is a critical eligibility requirement for Erasmus Mundus, and the specific demands vary significantly between programmes. Most English-taught programmes require IELTS scores of at least 6.5 overall with no component below 6.0, or equivalent TOEFL or Cambridge results. Some competitive programmes, particularly those in the social sciences, humanities or public policy, set higher thresholds of 7.0 or above. E

uropean-taught programmes may require proof of proficiency in the language of instruction, whether that is French, German, Spanish or another European language, and these requirements can be as demanding as the English requirements for English-taught tracks. Always check the specific requirements on the programme website rather than relying on general Erasmus Mundus guidelines, as each consortium sets its own thresholds.

Am I eligible for Erasmus mundus?

If your English proficiency is close to but not quite at the required level, you have several options. The most straightforward is to retake the IELTS or TOEFL with focused preparation. Some programmes accept a Duolingo English Test, which can be taken at home and results delivered within forty-eight hours, making it a useful fallback if test centre availability is limited. A

small number of programmes will accept a medium-of-instruction letter from your previous university if your degree was taught entirely in English, but this waiver is not guaranteed and should be confirmed directly with the programme coordinator before you rely on it. For Pakistani students who completed their bachelor degree in English-medium institutions, obtaining this letter early is a practical step that can save you from sitting an English test you may not need.

For programmes taught partly or wholly in a European language, preparation should begin well in advance. If you are applying to a programme taught in German, for example, reaching B2 level within a year is realistic with consistent study, but starting from zero requires at least eighteen months. M

any Erasmus Mundus programmes include a language preparation module at the start, particularly for programmes where you will study in a country whose language you do not yet speak, but these modules are introductory and will not bring you from zero to academic proficiency. S

cholarship committees also view proactive language learning favourably: even if the programme is taught in English, demonstrating that you have begun learning the language of one of the host countries shows commitment to integration and cultural engagement. This is particularly relevant for programmes that include a semester in a non-English-speaking country.

When preparing your application, list all language skills accurately, including any Urdu, regional languages or additional languages you speak. Multilingual ability is an asset in the Erasmus Mundus context, where cohorts are intentionally international and collaborative. In your motivation letter, you can briefly mention your language learning goals as part of your broader preparation for living and studying across multiple European countries. D

o not, however, claim proficiency you do not have: interview panels may test your language ability informally, and misrepresenting your level will damage your credibility. If you have taken any language exams beyond IELTS or TOEFL, such as DELF for French or TestDaF for German, include the results in your application, as they provide additional evidence of your readiness for a multilingual programme.

Choosing the Right Erasmus Mundus Consortium for Your Goals

With more than 150 Erasmus Mundus Joint Master programmes available in any given cycle, choosing the right consortium is one of the most important decisions you will make. The programme title alone is not enough: you need to examine the curriculum structure, the partner universities involved, the faculty specialisms, the thesis arrangements and the career outcomes of past graduates. A

programme that looks ideal on paper may involve study in cities where the cost of living is prohibitive on the scholarship stipend, or it may be led by a university whose research focus does not align with your interests. Thorough research before you apply saves you from accepting a place that is not the right fit.

What is Erasmus mundus?

Start by reading the programme description on the Erasmus Mundus catalogue in full, not just the headline summary. Pay attention to the study pathway: which university will you attend in which semester, what modules are offered at each location and where will you complete your thesis or final project. Some programmes have a fixed pathway where every student follows the same route, while others offer elective tracks that allow you to specialise. C

onsider the language of instruction at each location, even if the programme is nominally taught in English, as some modules may be offered in the local language. Look at the partner universities strengths: a programme co-led by a top technical university and a leading social science institution will offer a very different experience from one led by two generalist universities.

Faculty expertise is another critical factor. Browse the programme website for staff profiles and identify whose research interests match yours. If you plan to write a thesis in a specific area, the availability of a supervisor with relevant expertise at one of the partner universities can make or break your experience. Some consortia list recent thesis topics, which gives you a concrete sense of what students actually work on. Y

ou can also search for faculty members on academic platforms to read their recent publications and assess whether their work genuinely aligns with your goals. A programme where two or three faculty members work in your area of interest is far more valuable than one with a higher overall ranking but no relevant supervision.

Graduate outcomes provide the most honest assessment of a programme value. Some consortia publish employment statistics, average starting salaries or lists of employers that have hired their graduates. If this information is not available on the website, look for the programme on LinkedIn and see where recent graduates are working. C

ontact current students or alumni directly: most are happy to answer questions about the academic experience, the quality of supervision, the social life and the practical realities of moving between countries. Ask specifically about the administrative support the consortium provides for visa applications, housing and health insurance at each location, as the quality of this support varies considerably between programmes. A

consortium with strong administrative coordination can make the transition between countries seamless, while a poorly organised one can leave you dealing with bureaucracy alone.

Common Erasmus Mundus Application Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

The most frequent Erasmus Mundus application mistake is treating the motivation letter as a personal autobiography rather than a focused argument for why that specific programme is the right next step for you. Committees read thousands of letters that begin with a childhood story or a general statement about wanting to change the world. T

he strongest letters begin with a clear, specific reason for choosing that programme: a research question you want to pursue, a faculty member whose work inspires you, or a particular combination of study locations that no other programme offers. Keep the opening tight and specific, then expand into your qualifications and goals. Every sentence in the letter should serve the argument that you are a strong candidate for this programme and that this programme is the right match for your trajectory.

A second common error is applying to programmes that do not match your profile. The Erasmus Mundus catalogue includes programmes at every level of specialisation, from highly technical engineering tracks to broad interdisciplinary social science degrees. Applying to a programme whose prerequisites you do not meet wastes one of your three application slots and signals to the committee that you have not read the entry requirements carefully. B

efore you commit to an application, verify that your bachelor degree is in a compatible field, that you meet the stated language requirements and that any required work experience or prerequisite courses are documented in your application. If you are uncertain whether your background fits, email the programme coordinator before applying: most are responsive and will give you an honest assessment.

What documents do you need for erasmus mundus?

Recommendation letters that are too generic or too short are another avoidable problem. A letter that says a student is hardworking and intelligent, without specific examples of academic achievement, research capability or collaborative skills, does not help the committee differentiate you from other applicants. W

hen you ask your referees for letters, provide them with a brief document that includes your motivation letter draft, your CV, the specific programme you are applying to and two or three concrete examples of work or projects you want them to highlight. Ask them to address the programme stated selection criteria directly. A

letter that explicitly discusses your ability to work in multicultural teams, for instance, is far more useful for an Erasmus Mundus application than one that focuses solely on your exam results.

Missing deadlines is surprisingly common, partly because the single portal handles applications for all three choices and the deadline applies to all of them simultaneously. Some applicants focus on perfecting one application and run out of time for the others. Create a backwards timeline from the deadline, assigning specific dates for completing each component: motivation letters, language test results, referee submissions and document uploads. T

he portal can be slow in the final forty-eight hours as thousands of applicants submit at once, so aim to complete your submission at least three days before the official deadline. Check that all documents are uploaded in the correct format and that your referees have submitted their letters before you finalise your application. Once you submit, you will receive a confirmation email: if you do not receive one, contact the programme coordination immediately.

A final mistake that particularly affects Pakistani and South Asian applicants is underestimating the importance of the post-programme plan. Erasmus Mundus is funded as a development and capacity-building tool, and committees want to know how you will use the degree after graduation. Vague statements about wanting to work internationally or pursue further study are not enough. Y

ou need to explain specifically what you intend to do in your home country or region, which sector or organisation you aim to contribute to and how the specific skills and networks you gain through the programme will enable you to do that work. The strongest applications present a clear before-and-after narrative: where you are now, what you will gain from the programme and what you will do with it when you return. This is not just a formality; it is one of the selection criteria that committees weigh most heavily.

Erasmus Mundus Funding From Home Country and Supplementary Grants

While the Erasmus Mundus scholarship is the primary source of funding, many students supplement it with grants from their home countries, international organisations and the host universities themselves. Pakistani students should investigate whether the Higher Education Commission of Pakistan offers any top-up grants or travel support for Erasmus Mundus scholars, as bilateral education agreements sometimes include supplementary provisions. Some European university development cooperation offices also offer small grants for students from developing countries, and these are often under-advertised. Checking the programme website, contacting the programme coordinator directly and asking current students about supplementary funding opportunities are all worth the effort before you arrive in Europe.

Host universities sometimes offer additional support that is not included in the Erasmus Mundus scholarship package. This can include access to university residences at subsidised rates, free or discounted language courses, health insurance top-ups and student employment opportunities. Some universities have hardship funds that students can apply for if unexpected expenses arise during the programme, and others offer travel grants for conference attendance or thesis fieldwork. T

hese additional resources are not guaranteed, but they exist at many institutions, and asking about them before you arrive means you can plan your budget more accurately. University international offices are generally responsive to enquiries from incoming Erasmus Mundus students, and a brief email asking about available support can reveal resources that are not listed on the programme website.

Private foundations and international organisations also fund Erasmus Mundus students through supplementary grants. The Open Society Foundations, the Aga Khan Foundation and various European development agencies offer scholarships and grants that can be combined with the Erasmus Mundus award. These grants may cover travel costs, provide additional monthly support or fund specific activities such as language courses or professional development workshops. T

he key is to research these opportunities early, as most have application deadlines that precede the Erasmus Mundus application deadline. Applying for supplementary grants in parallel with your Erasmus Mundus application is a practical strategy that maximises your funding options without creating additional workload during the most intensive period of your application.

When budgeting for the Erasmus Mundus programme, it is worth creating a detailed spreadsheet that maps your expected income against your expected costs for each semester. Include the scholarship stipend, any supplementary grants, personal savings and expected part-time income on the income side, and tuition, accommodation, food, transport, health insurance, books, travel between study locations and personal expenses on the cost side. T

his exercise reveals whether your budget is realistic and whether you need to take steps to increase your income or reduce your costs. For students from Pakistan, the cost of living in Western European cities can be a shock, and arriving with a clear budget prevents financial stress that can affect academic performance. T

he Erasmus Mundus student community is also a practical resource: current and former students share budgeting tips, accommodation recommendations and cost-saving strategies that are specific to each programme and each study location.

Erasmus Mundus Degree Recognition and Professional Registration in Pakistan

The recognition of your Erasmus Mundus degree in Pakistan is an important consideration for your long-term career, particularly if you plan to pursue professional registration or return to the Pakistani academic or professional system. Erasmus Mundus Joint Master degrees are awarded by the consortium and are accredited in the countries where the partner universities are located. I

n most cases, this means the degree is recognised across the European Union and in countries that have mutual recognition agreements with the EU. For Pakistani graduates, the practical question is whether the degree will be recognised by the relevant Pakistani authorities, including the Higher Education Commission (HEC), professional regulatory bodies and potential employers.

The HEC has a process for recognising foreign degrees, and Erasmus Mundus degrees, which are issued by established European universities, are generally accepted through this process. However, the recognition process requires documentation that you should prepare before you leave for Europe. This typically includes the original degree certificate, a transcript of courses and grades, proof that the university is accredited in its home country and a detailed course description. H

aving these documents translated into Urdu or English (as required) and notarised before you leave Europe saves you from dealing with European bureaucracy after you have departed. Some Pakistani employers or professional bodies may additionally require verification of your degree through the relevant European education authority, so understanding these requirements early in your programme is practical.

For Pakistani graduates in regulated professions, such as engineering, medicine, architecture and law, the recognition process is more complex. Professional registration in Pakistan is managed by sector-specific bodies, such as the Pakistan Engineering Council, the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council and the Pakistan Council of Architects and Town Planners. T

hese bodies have their own criteria for recognising foreign qualifications, and in some cases, additional assessments or examinations may be required before you can practise in Pakistan with your Erasmus Mundus degree. Contacting the relevant regulatory body before you begin your programme, explaining your qualification pathway and asking about the recognition process ensures that you are not surprised by additional requirements when you return.

The Erasmus Mundus degree itself carries significant weight with Pakistani employers, particularly in the private sector, international organisations and the development sector. The competitive selection process, the multi-country study experience and the European accreditation of the degree are all markers that Pakistani employers recognise and value. In the academic sector, an Erasmus Mundus degree from a well-known consortium can strengthen your candidacy for faculty positions at Pakistani universities, particularly if your research area aligns with the university priorities. F

or government positions, the recognition process is typically straightforward, but understanding the specific documentation requirements of the hiring authority before you apply prevents delays in your employment. The Erasmus Mundus Alumni Association can also provide guidance on degree recognition and career planning, as many alumni have navigated the same process before you.

Preparing Your Documents for the Erasmus Mundus Application

The documentation requirements for Erasmus Mundus applications are specific, and preparing them early prevents last-minute stress. Every consortium requires certified copies of your academic transcripts, your degree certificate, a valid passport, language proficiency test results and a CV in the Europass format or a format specified by the programme. Some programmes also require a research proposal, writing samples or evidence of work experience. A

ll documents that are not in English or the language of instruction must be translated by a certified translator, and some programmes require notarisation or apostille certification. Begin gathering these documents at least three months before the application deadline, as obtaining certified copies and translations from your university or relevant authorities can take several weeks.

The Europass CV format is widely accepted across European institutions and provides a standardised way to present your academic and professional background. If the programme specifies a different format, follow their instructions precisely. Your CV should highlight your education, work experience, research activities, publications, language skills and any awards or recognitions you have received. Keep the CV concise, ideally no more than two to three pages, and focus on the experiences that are most relevant to the programme you are applying to. For Pakistani applicants, including any experience with international organisations, cross-cultural projects or multilingual environments strengthens the CV by demonstrating the qualities that Erasmus Mundus values.

Language test results must be sent directly from the testing agency to the programme or uploaded as certified copies through the application portal. Plan your test date at least two months before the application deadline to allow time for results to be processed and delivered. I

f your test scores are close to but not quite at the required level, consider retaking the test with targeted preparation rather than relying on borderline scores, as programmes may not make exceptions for applicants who fall slightly below the threshold. Some programmes accept the Duolingo English Test as an alternative to IELTS or TOEFL, which can be taken at home and provides results within forty-eight hours, making it a useful backup option.

When uploading documents to the application portal, verify that each file is in the correct format (usually PDF), is clearly legible and does not exceed the file size limit. Name your files clearly, using a convention such as your surname and the document type, so that the admissions committee can easily identify each file. B

efore you finalise your application, review every uploaded document to confirm that it is the correct version, that your name appears consistently across all documents and that any translations are complete and accurate. A well-organised, error-free application signals professionalism and attention to detail, and it ensures that the committee evaluates your candidacy on its merits rather than being distracted by administrative shortcomings.
