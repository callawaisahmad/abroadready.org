1. What Is the Duolingo English Test?

The Duolingo English Test (DET) is an online, adaptive English proficiency exam created by Duolingo — the same company behind the popular language learning app. Unlike traditional English tests that require you to travel to a test centre, the Duolingo English Test can be taken entirely from home using your own computer.

The test was first launched in 2016, but it gained massive traction during the COVID-19 pandemic when test centres around the world were closed. Since then, acceptance has grown rapidly. In 2026, over 5,000 institutions worldwide accept Duolingo scores, including top universities in the United States, Canada, Australia, and Europe.

The Duolingo English Test measures your ability to read, write, listen, and speak English. It uses adaptive technology, which means the difficulty of questions adjusts based on your performance. If you answer correctly, the questions get harder. If you answer incorrectly, they get easier. This makes the test efficient and accurate.

Here are the key facts about the Duolingo English Test:

  • Duration: Approximately 45 minutes for the scored section
  • Cost: USD 65 (same price worldwide)
  • Results: Available within 48 hours
  • Score range: 10 to 160
  • Validity: 2 years from the test date
  • Proctoring: AI-powered with human review
  • Equipment: Computer with camera, microphone, speakers, and stable internet
  • Score sending: Free to send to unlimited universities

The test is designed for non-native English speakers who need to prove their English proficiency for academic purposes. Whether you are applying to undergraduate programmes, postgraduate degrees, or professional certifications, the Duolingo English Test can serve as proof of your English ability.

One of the biggest advantages of Duolingo is its accessibility. You do not need to book a slot weeks in advance or travel to a test centre. You can take the test any time you want, from anywhere in the world, as long as you have a quiet room and a reliable internet connection.

However, it is important to understand that Duolingo is not universally accepted. Some countries, particularly the United Kingdom, do not accept Duolingo for student visa purposes. Some universities accept it for admission but not for visa applications. Always verify acceptance with your specific institution and visa authority before choosing Duolingo over other tests.

The test is also not accepted for immigration purposes in most countries. If you are applying for permanent residency or citizenship, you will likely need IELTS, PTE, or TOEFL instead.

2. Duolingo Test Format — Complete Breakdown

The Duolingo English Test has three parts. The first part is scored and adaptive. The second and third parts are unscored but are sent to institutions as supplementary material.

Part 1: Adaptive Test (Scored — 45 Minutes)

This is the main scored section of the test. It contains various question types that test all four language skills: reading, writing, listening, and speaking. The test is adaptive, meaning the difficulty adjusts to your performance in real time.

Here is a detailed breakdown of each question type:

Read and Complete — You are shown words with some letters missing. Your job is to fill in the missing letters to complete the word. This tests your vocabulary knowledge and ability to recognise English words quickly. The words become harder as you progress.

Read Aloud — A sentence appears on screen and you must read it aloud into your microphone. Duolingo evaluates your pronunciation, fluency, and rhythm. Speak clearly and at a natural pace. Do not rush or whisper.

Write About the Photo — An image is displayed and you must write a short description of what you see. You have a limited time to write. Focus on describing the main elements of the image clearly and concisely.

Interactive Reading — You are given a passage with blanks. You must select the correct word or phrase to fill each blank from a list of options. This tests your reading comprehension and understanding of grammar in context.

Listen and Type — You hear a sentence played through your speakers and must type exactly what you hear. This tests your listening ability. Make sure your speakers are clear and your volume is at a comfortable level.

Read and Respond — You read a short passage and then answer a question about it. This tests your ability to understand written English and extract key information quickly.

Interactive Listening — You listen to a conversation or monologue and then answer questions about it. This is similar to the reading comprehension sections but focuses on your listening skills.

Writing Sample — You are given a prompt and must write a response. This is unscored but is sent to institutions as part of your results. It gives admissions officers a sample of your writing ability.

Speaking Sample — You are asked to speak about a topic for a set amount of time. This is also unscored but is shared with institutions. It provides a recorded sample of your spoken English.

The adaptive nature of the test means that no two tests are exactly the same. If you are a strong English speaker, you will encounter harder questions. If you struggle, the test will adjust to easier questions. This makes the scoring fair and personalised.

Part 2: Video Interview (Unscored)

The video interview consists of two open-ended questions. You record your responses on camera. These responses are not scored but are sent to the universities you apply to. They give admissions officers a chance to hear you speak naturally and assess your communication style.

Treat this section seriously. Even though it is unscored, universities may use it to evaluate your personality, confidence, and communication skills. Speak clearly, stay on topic, and be yourself.

Part 3: Writing Sample (Unscored)

The writing sample asks you to write a response to a prompt. Like the video interview, this is unscored but is shared with institutions. It gives them a raw sample of your writing without any AI scoring involved.

Write in clear, organised paragraphs. Avoid grammatical errors where possible. This section is your chance to demonstrate your writing competence in a natural way.

What to Expect on Test Day

Before the test begins, you will go through an identity verification process. You need a valid government-issued ID (passport, national ID, or driver's licence). You will also need to pass an equipment check — your camera, microphone, speakers, and internet connection will be tested.

During the test, your camera and screen are monitored by AI proctoring software. A human reviewer may also review the recording after the test. If suspicious activity is detected, your test may be invalidated.

You are not allowed to leave the test window, use external materials, take notes, or have anyone else in the room. Breaking these rules can result in your test being cancelled.

The test must be taken on a desktop or laptop computer. Tablets and phones are not supported. Make sure your operating system and browser are up to date before starting.

3. Duolingo Scoring System — How It Works

The Duolingo English Test uses a scoring scale from 10 to 160. The score is calculated using an adaptive algorithm that measures your English proficiency across multiple skills.

Subscores

In addition to your overall score, you receive four subscores. These subscores give you (and universities) a detailed breakdown of your strengths and weaknesses:

  • Literacy — Your ability to read and write in English
  • Comprehension — Your ability to read and listen in English
  • Conversation — Your ability to listen and speak in English
  • Production — Your ability to write and speak in English

Each subscore is scored on the same 10–160 scale. Universities may look at your subscores in addition to your overall score, especially if they want to assess specific language skills.

How Adaptive Scoring Works

Duolingo uses Computer Adaptive Testing (CAT). This is the same technology used in GRE and GMAT. The test starts with questions of medium difficulty. If you answer correctly, the next question becomes harder. If you answer incorrectly, the next question becomes easier.

This approach means the test can accurately measure your ability in a shorter time compared to a fixed-format test. It also means that two students who take different tests may see completely different questions but receive comparable scores.

The algorithm considers not just whether you got an answer right or wrong, but also how quickly you responded and the difficulty level of the question. This gives a more nuanced picture of your English ability.

5. Where Is Duolingo Accepted? — Country-by-Country Guide

Duolingo acceptance varies significantly by country. Some countries embrace it widely, while others have limited or no acceptance. Here is a detailed breakdown.

United States — Widely Accepted

The USA is the largest market for Duolingo. Over 1,000 American universities and colleges accept Duolingo scores for admission. This includes many top-ranked institutions such as:

  • Harvard University
  • Yale University
  • Columbia University
  • New York University (NYU)
  • University of Southern California (USC)
  • Georgia Institute of Technology
  • University of California system campuses

Most US universities require a Duolingo score between 105 and 120 for undergraduate programmes and 110 to 125 for postgraduate programmes. Competitive programmes at top universities may require 125 or higher.

For the F-1 student visa, the US embassy does not specify which English test you must take. They accept proof of English proficiency from any recognised test. However, the embassy may ask you about your English ability during the visa interview, so having a strong score helps.

Canada — Accepted with Caveats

Many Canadian universities and colleges accept Duolingo for admission purposes. Institutions like the University of Toronto, McGill University, and the University of British Columbia have accepted Duolingo scores.

Typical Duolingo requirements for Canadian universities range from 105 to 115. Some programmes may require higher scores.

However, there is an important limitation. The Student Direct Stream (SDS) programme, which provides faster processing for study permits, does not accept Duolingo. SDS requires IELTS (with a minimum of 6.0 in each band) or TEF/TCF for French.

If you are applying through the regular study permit stream (not SDS), Duolingo scores may be accepted as proof of English proficiency, but this is at the discretion of the visa officer.

Australia — Limited Acceptance

Australia has limited acceptance for the Duolingo English Test. Some Australian universities accept Duolingo for admission, but the major institutions prefer IELTS, PTE, or TOEFL.

For student visa purposes, the Australian Department of Home Affairs has a list of accepted English tests. Duolingo is not on this list. You must take IELTS, PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, Cambridge C1 Advanced, or OET to meet the visa English requirement.

If you are applying to an Australian university, check whether they accept Duolingo for admission. But for the visa, you will almost certainly need IELTS or PTE.

Some UK universities accept Duolingo for admission purposes only. This means you could receive an offer letter based on your Duolingo score, but you would still need to take IELTS for UKVI to get your visa approved.

In practice, this makes Duolingo of limited use for UK-bound students. If you are applying to the UK, your best option is IELTS for UKVI.

6. Limitations of the Duolingo English Test

While the Duolingo English Test has many advantages, it also has important limitations that you must understand before choosing it. These limitations can significantly impact your study abroad plans.

Not Accepted for UK Student Visas

The most significant limitation is that Duolingo is not accepted for UK student visas. The UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) authority requires a Secure English Language Test (SELT) from an approved provider. Duolingo is not on this list. If you plan to study in the UK, you must take IELTS for UKVI, PTE Academic UKVI, or another approved test.

Not Accepted for Canada SDS

Canada's Student Direct Stream (SDS) programme requires IELTS with a minimum of 6.0 in each band. Duolingo is not accepted for SDS applications. If you want the faster processing times that SDS offers, you need IELTS.

Fewer Institutions Accept It

While over 5,000 institutions accept Duolingo, this is still fewer than IELTS, which is accepted by over 11,000 institutions worldwide. If your target university does not accept Duolingo, you have no choice but to take another test.

Not Accepted for Immigration

Duolingo is designed for academic purposes only. It is not accepted for immigration, permanent residency, or citizenship applications in any major English-speaking country. If you plan to immigrate after your studies, you will need IELTS or another immigration-approved test.

Not Accepted for Professional Registration

If you plan to work in a regulated profession (medicine, engineering, law, nursing) in an English-speaking country, Duolingo is not accepted for professional registration. These fields require IELTS, PTE, or TOEFL.

AI Scoring Concerns

Duolingo uses AI-based scoring with human review. Some students and institutions have raised concerns about the accuracy of AI scoring, particularly for speaking tasks. The technology is improving, but it may not capture nuances in accent, tone, or complex language use as effectively as a human examiner.

Home Environment Risks

Since the test is taken at home, there are environmental risks. Internet disconnections, background noise, or technical issues can affect your performance. While Duolingo offers retakes, this adds to your cost and stress.

Score Acceptance May Change

University acceptance policies can change. A university that accepts Duolingo today may not accept it next year. Always verify current acceptance with your target institution before relying solely on Duolingo.

7. How to Prepare for the Duolingo English Test

Preparing for the Duolingo English Test does not require expensive prep courses or months of study. However, you should understand the format and practise regularly to achieve your best score.

Step 1: Take the Free Practice Test

Duolingo offers a free practice test on their official website. This test gives you a taste of the actual test format, question types, and timing. Take it first to understand where you stand and which areas need improvement.

The practice test is not scored the same way as the real test, but it gives you a good indication of your current level. Use it as a diagnostic tool to identify your weak areas.

Step 2: Focus on Read Aloud

The Read Aloud section is one of the most important parts of the test. It affects your speaking subscore significantly. To prepare:

  • Read English texts aloud every day for at least 15 minutes
  • Focus on clear pronunciation rather than speed
  • Record yourself and listen back to identify areas for improvement
  • Pay attention to word stress, sentence rhythm, and intonation
  • Practise with a variety of texts — news articles, academic passages, and stories

Step 3: Practise Listening with Headphones or Speakers

The Listen and Type section requires you to type what you hear. To prepare:

  • Listen to English podcasts, audiobooks, and news broadcasts daily
  • Practise typing what you hear — use dictation exercises online
  • Work on your typing speed and accuracy
  • Familiarise yourself with different English accents (American, British, Australian)
  • Use the speakers you plan to use during the actual test

Step 4: Build Vocabulary for Fill-in-the-Blank Questions

The Read and Complete section tests your vocabulary. To prepare:

  • Read widely in English — newspapers, academic journals, novels
  • Keep a vocabulary notebook and learn 5–10 new words daily
  • Practise cloze tests (fill-in-the-blank exercises) online
  • Pay attention to word families and common prefixes/suffixes
  • Use flashcard apps like Anki for spaced repetition learning

Step 5: Write Concise Descriptions

The Write About the Photo section requires you to describe images clearly. To prepare:

  • Practise describing photos in writing — use news photos or stock images
  • Focus on clear, structured descriptions
  • Describe the main subjects, actions, setting, and any notable details
  • Keep your descriptions between 2–5 sentences
  • Practise writing within a time limit (3–5 minutes)

Step 6: Take Timed Practice Tests

The Duolingo test is timed. You need to manage your time effectively. Take full-length practice tests under timed conditions to build your stamina and pacing.

  • Simulate test conditions — quiet room, no distractions, same equipment
  • Time each section and track your progress over weeks
  • Review your mistakes after each practice test
  • Focus on improving your weakest areas

Step 7: Use the Duolingo App for Daily Practice

The Duolingo language learning app can help you build your English skills daily. While the app is not a direct preparation tool for the test, it helps with vocabulary, grammar, and listening skills. Spend 15–20 minutes on the app daily as a supplement to your test preparation.

Step 8: Record Yourself Speaking

For the speaking sections, record yourself speaking on various topics. Listen to the recordings and evaluate your:

  • Pronunciation clarity
  • Grammar accuracy
  • Vocabulary range
  • Fluency and naturalness
  • Organisation of ideas

You can also use AI tools to get feedback on your speaking. Some apps analyse your pronunciation and suggest improvements.

Preparation Timeline

For most students, 2–4 weeks of focused preparation is sufficient. If your English is already strong, you may only need a few days to familiarise yourself with the format. If your English needs more work, consider 4–6 weeks of preparation.

8. When to Choose Duolingo vs IELTS — Decision Guide

Choosing between Duolingo and IELTS depends on your specific circumstances. Here is a clear decision guide to help you decide.

Choose Duolingo If:

  • Budget is tight — Duolingo costs USD 65 while IELTS costs USD 235–270. If money is a concern, Duolingo is the clear winner.
  • You need results fast — Duolingo results come within 48 hours. IELTS results take 3–5 days (computer) or 13 days (paper).
  • You prefer testing from home — No need to travel to a test centre. Take the test from your bedroom.
  • Your target universities accept it — If all the universities on your list accept Duolingo, there is no reason to pay more for IELTS.
  • You are applying to USA — The USA has the widest Duolingo acceptance. Most American universities accept it.
  • You are applying to Canada (non-SDS) — If you are not using the SDS stream, many Canadian institutions accept Duolingo.
  • You want free score sending — Duolingo lets you send scores to unlimited universities for free.
  • You are a strong digital test-taker — If you are comfortable with computers and online testing, Duolingo's format may suit you well.

Choose IELTS If:

  • You are applying to the UK — UKVI requires IELTS for UKVI for student visa applications. There is no workaround.
  • You are applying to Canada SDS — SDS requires IELTS with 6.0 in each band. Duolingo does not qualify.
  • You are applying for immigration — Duolingo is not accepted for immigration in any country.
  • You need professional registration — Medicine, engineering, law, and nursing require IELTS or equivalent.
  • More institutions require it — IELTS is accepted by 11,000+ institutions. If you are applying to many universities, IELTS covers more ground.
  • You are applying for scholarships — Some scholarships require IELTS specifically. Check the scholarship requirements.
  • You prefer face-to-face speaking assessment — IELTS includes a face-to-face speaking interview with a human examiner. Some students perform better in this format.
  • You want wider recognition — IELTS is recognised by more employers, immigration authorities, and professional bodies worldwide.

Can You Take Both?

Yes. Some students choose to take both Duolingo and IELTS to maximise their options. Duolingo can serve as a quick, cheap backup while IELTS serves as the primary test. This strategy works if your budget allows it.

9. Common Duolingo Test Mistakes to Avoid

Many students lose marks not because of their English ability but because of avoidable mistakes. Here are the most common mistakes and how to avoid them.

Mistake 1: Unstable Internet Connection

A stable internet connection is critical for the Duolingo test. If your connection drops during the test, you may lose your progress or have your test invalidated. Use a wired Ethernet connection if possible. If you must use Wi-Fi, make sure you are close to the router and no one else is using the bandwidth.

Mistake 2: Noisy or Disrupted Environment

You need a completely quiet room for the test. Background noise can trigger the AI proctor and flag your test for review. Choose a room where you will not be interrupted. Inform family members or roommates that you are taking an important test.

Mistake 3: Looking Away from the Screen

The AI proctoring system monitors your eye movements. If you frequently look away from the screen, it may be flagged as suspicious behaviour. Keep your eyes on the screen throughout the test. Do not look at notes, your phone, or anything else.

Mistake 4: Speaking Too Quietly

For the speaking sections, your microphone must pick up your voice clearly. If you speak too quietly, the recording may not capture your words properly. Speak at a normal conversational volume. Test your microphone before the test begins.

Mistake 5: Ignoring the Writing Sample

Even though the writing sample is unscored, it is sent to universities. Many students write minimal responses or do not complete this section. Take it seriously. Write a well-organised response of at least 3–5 sentences.

Mistake 6: Not Practising the Adaptive Format

If you have only practised with fixed-format tests like IELTS, the adaptive format of Duolingo may feel unfamiliar. The changing difficulty can be disorienting. Practise with the official Duolingo practice test to get comfortable with how the questions adjust.

Mistake 7: Rushing Through Questions

The test is timed, but that does not mean you should rush. Some students answer questions too quickly, leading to careless errors. Read each question carefully before answering. Quality matters more than speed.

Mistake 8: Not Checking Equipment Before the Test

Technical issues on test day can ruin your performance. Test your camera, microphone, speakers, and internet connection at least a day before the test. Make sure your browser is up to date and your operating system is compatible.

Mistake 9: Using a Tablet or Phone

Duolingo only works on desktop and laptop computers. Tablets and phones are not supported. If you attempt to take the test on an unsupported device, it will not work properly.

Mistake 10: Not Reviewing Your Score Before Sending

Duolingo allows you to review your score before sending it to universities. Take advantage of this feature. If your score is lower than expected, you can retake the test before sending results to your target institutions.

10. Duolingo English Test Tips for High Scores

Here are additional tips to help you score well on the Duolingo English Test:

  • Practise daily — Even 20 minutes of English practice every day adds up over weeks
  • Use English in daily life — Change your phone language to English, watch English content, think in English
  • Focus on your weakest skill — If speaking is your weakness, spend extra time practising it
  • Stay calm during the test — The adaptive format can be stressful when questions get harder. Remember, harder questions mean you are doing well
  • Manage your time wisely — Do not spend too long on any single question. Move on if you are stuck
  • Use proper grammar — Even in short responses, try to use correct grammar and sentence structure
  • Expand your vocabulary — Read academic texts, newspapers, and journals to build your vocabulary
  • Practise with a timer — Simulate test conditions during practice to build speed and accuracy
  • Get feedback — Ask a teacher or fluent English speaker to review your practice writing and speaking
  • Rest before test day — A good night's sleep helps you focus and perform better

12. Key Takeaways and Final Advice

The Duolingo English Test is a powerful, affordable, and accessible alternative to traditional English proficiency exams. It is the cheapest major English test at USD 65, offers results within 48 hours, and can be taken from home. Over 5,000 institutions worldwide accept it, including top universities in the USA and Canada.

However, it is not a universal solution. If you are applying to the UK, you need IELTS for UKVI. If you are applying to Canada via SDS, you need IELTS. For immigration or professional registration, IELTS, PTE, or TOEFL are required.

The best approach is to check your target universities and visa requirements first. If Duolingo is accepted for both admission and visa purposes in your destination country, it is an excellent choice. If not, consider IELTS or another accepted test.

For students on a budget who are applying to USA or non-SDS Canadian programmes, Duolingo can save you hundreds of dollars compared to IELTS, while providing faster results and greater convenience.

Whatever test you choose, start preparing early. Use the free practice tests available online, focus on your weak areas, and practise under timed conditions. A well-prepared student can score well on any English proficiency test.

The Duolingo English Test costs USD 65 globally. This is significantly cheaper than IELTS (USD 235-270) and PTE (USD 180-220). There are no additional fees for sending scores to universities — you can send results to as many institutions as you want at no extra cost.

Which countries accept Duolingo for student visa?+

Duolingo is accepted for university admission in USA, Canada, Australia, and many European countries. However, it is NOT accepted for UK student visas (UKVI does not list Duolingo). Canada accepts Duolingo for some institutions but not for SDS. Always check the specific visa authority.

Can I take Duolingo at home?+

Yes. The Duolingo English Test is designed to be taken from home on a computer with a camera, microphone, speakers, and stable internet connection. You need a quiet room, a valid ID, and a computer (not a tablet or phone). The test is proctored by AI and human review.

What is the Duolingo test format?+

The test has 3 parts: Adaptive test (45 minutes) — various question types testing reading, writing, listening and speaking; Video interview (unscored) — 2 recorded speaking responses; Writing sample (unscored) — 1 written response. The adaptive test adjusts difficulty based on your performance.

How long are Duolingo scores valid?+

Duolingo scores are valid for 2 years from the test date. You can send your results to universities at any time during the validity period. There is no limit on how many institutions can receive your scores.

How do I prepare for Duolingo?+

Use Duolingo's free practice test to familiarise yourself with the format. Focus on reading aloud clearly, practising listening with headphones, and writing concisely. Take practice tests under timed conditions. The Duolingo English Test website offers free preparation materials and sample questions.

Can I retake Duolingo if I fail?+

Yes. You can retake Duolingo as many times as you need. There is no waiting period between tests. However, you must wait for results (48 hours) before booking another test. Each test costs USD 65.
