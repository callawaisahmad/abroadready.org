TOEFL vs IELTS: Which English Test Should You Take?

🗓️ Mar 27, 2026⏱️ 11 min read✍️ AbroadReady Team

The TOEFL vs IELTS question is one of the first decisions international students face, and choosing well can save you time, money, and stress. Both tests measure academic English and both are accepted by thousands of universities worldwide, but they differ in format, style, and the type of candidate they suit. This guide breaks down the TOEFL vs IELTS comparison across everything that actually matters so you can pick the test that plays to your strengths rather than fighting against them.

There is no universally easier test. The right answer depends on your English background, how you handle a computer versus a face-to-face examiner, and what your target institutions accept. Let us work through it methodically.

Key takeaways

  • Both TOEFL and IELTS are widely accepted; always confirm what your specific programme requires first.
  • The core TOEFL vs IELTS difference is style: TOEFL is fully computer-based and American-leaning, while IELTS Academic offers a human Speaking examiner.
  • TOEFL Speaking is recorded into a microphone; IELTS Speaking is a live conversation, which suits different personalities.
  • IELTS uses a 0 to 9 band scale; TOEFL iBT is scored out of 120.
  • Choose based on your strengths, comfort with technology, and destination, not on rumours about difficulty.

The basics of each test

The TOEFL iBT is produced by ETS and is delivered almost entirely online, either at a test centre or at home. It leans towards North American English and academic contexts, with lecture-style listening passages and integrated tasks that combine skills.

The IELTS is jointly run by the British Council, IDP, and Cambridge. The Academic version, which most applicants take, uses a mix of British and international English. Its defining feature is a face-to-face Speaking test with a real examiner, which no other major test offers in the same way.

Who accepts which test

Acceptance is rarely the deciding factor anymore because the vast majority of universities in the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and Europe accept both. That said, some specific programmes, professional bodies, or immigration routes prefer one. The golden rule in any TOEFL vs IELTS decision is to check the exact requirement for your target course before you book anything.

Format and structure compared

Both tests assess Reading, Listening, Writing, and Speaking, but the experience of each differs meaningfully.

Reading

TOEFL Reading uses long academic passages with multiple-choice questions, all on screen. IELTS Academic Reading offers a wider variety of question types, including matching headings, True/False/Not Given, and sentence completion, which some candidates find more varied and others find fiddly.

Listening

TOEFL Listening features university lectures and campus conversations, and you take notes then answer afterward. IELTS Listening includes everyday and academic scenarios, and you answer as you listen, which rewards candidates who process information in real time.

Writing

TOEFL Writing includes an integrated task, where you read, listen, then write a response combining both sources, plus an independent essay. IELTS Academic Writing has Task 1, describing a chart, graph, or process, and Task 2, an opinion or argument essay. If you dislike interpreting data visuals, that IELTS Task 1 is worth weighing carefully.

Speaking

This is the sharpest difference in the whole TOEFL vs IELTS comparison. TOEFL Speaking is delivered by speaking into a microphone, with your responses recorded and scored later, sometimes partly by automated systems. IELTS Speaking is a live, three-part conversation with a human examiner. Introverts and those who freeze when recorded often prefer the human interaction of IELTS, while people who dislike being watched sometimes prefer the privacy of the TOEFL microphone.

Scoring: understanding the two scales

IELTS reports each section and an overall score on a 0 to 9 band scale, in half-band increments. Most competitive universities ask for an overall 6.5 to 7.5, often with minimums in each section.

TOEFL iBT scores each section from 0 to 30, giving a total out of 120. Typical requirements sit around 80 to 100 for many programmes, with more selective courses asking for higher. Because the scales are so different, it helps to convert your target university requirement into both systems before deciding, so you know which score feels more attainable for you.

Which test is easier?

There is no honest single answer, but there are patterns. TOEFL tends to suit candidates who are comfortable with academic, lecture-heavy content, typing quickly, and working entirely on a computer for several hours. Its question types are more predictable and repetitive, which some learners find easier to drill.

IELTS tends to suit candidates who prefer variety, who write comfortably by hand or on screen, and who perform better talking to a real person. Its wider range of question types can feel less monotonous, though it means more formats to master.

Ask yourself one question: do I perform better speaking to a human being or to a microphone? Your honest answer settles the Speaking section, which is where many candidates gain or lose the most.

Practical factors: cost, availability, and results

Pricing for both tests is broadly comparable and varies by country, so check current local fees rather than relying on old figures. Availability also depends on where you live; in some regions IELTS has more test dates and centres, in others TOEFL is easier to book, including its at-home option.

Results turnaround is fast for both, typically within a couple of weeks, and both offer computer-delivered IELTS options in many locations that speed things up. If you are on a tight application deadline, availability and result speed in your specific city may matter more than any format preference.

How to make your decision

Work through these steps in order. First, confirm which tests your target universities and any funders accept, and note the exact score required. Second, take a free sample or diagnostic of each test to feel the difference directly rather than relying on rumours. Third, weigh your personal strengths: comfort with technology, handwriting versus typing, and human versus recorded Speaking. Fourth, check practical logistics of cost and test dates in your area.

How Your English Learning Background Affects the TOEFL vs IELTS Decision

One of the most overlooked factors in choosing between TOEFL and IELTS is the way you learned English. Students who studied in English-medium schools, consumed English media regularly, and practised academic writing in English tend to perform differently on each test compared to students who learned English primarily through formal classroom instruction.

If your education was entirely in English, you likely have strong listening and reading comprehension skills developed through exposure to lectures, textbooks and media in English. These students often perform well on the TOEFL listening section, which requires you to understand extended academic lectures and answer questions about their structure and content. The TOEFL listening format rewards sustained attention and the ability to follow a single speaker for several minutes.

Students who learned English more formally, perhaps through Urdu-medium schooling with English as a second language, may find the IELTS format more familiar and comfortable. IELTS uses a wider variety of English accents, including British, Australian and occasionally New Zealand English. The IELTS reading section also uses a wider variety of text types, including advertisements, newspaper articles and book extracts, rather than exclusively academic passages.

The speaking section is where the difference is most pronounced. The TOEFL speaking section is entirely recorded and timed, which suits students who are comfortable speaking into a microphone but who may feel nervous in a live interaction. The IELTS speaking section involves a face-to-face conversation with an examiner, which suits students who thrive on human interaction and who can adjust their responses based on the examiner follow-up questions.

The writing section differences are subtler. TOEFL writing requires you to integrate information from a reading passage and a lecture in one task. IELTS writing Task 1 requires you to describe and analyse visual information such as graphs, charts or diagrams. If you are strong at data interpretation, IELTS Task 1 plays to your strengths. If you are stronger at synthesising written and spoken information, the TOEFL integrated task may be more comfortable.

TOEFL vs IELTS Preparation Strategies: What to Focus on for Each Test

Preparing for the TOEFL and preparing for the IELTS require different strategies, even though both tests measure the same underlying English proficiency. The most efficient preparation plan understands the specific demands of each test and targets your preparation accordingly.

For TOEFL preparation, the single highest-impact activity is practising with full-length official practice tests under timed conditions. The TOEFL is a long test, approximately three hours, and stamina matters as much as language skill. Take at least three to four full-length practice tests in the weeks before your exam, simulating real test conditions. Pay particular attention to the integrated tasks, which combine reading, listening and writing, as these are unique to TOEFL.

For IELTS preparation, focus on understanding the specific format of each section. In the listening section, you hear each recording only once and have a short time to transfer answers, so practising quick note-taking is essential. In the reading section, the question types vary significantly, including matching headings, sentence completion, true or false statements and multiple choice. Familiarise yourself with every question type and develop a reliable strategy for each one.

Vocabulary preparation differs between the two tests. TOEFL tends to use more academic vocabulary, which means building familiarity with academic word lists is worthwhile. IELTS vocabulary requirements are broader and more varied, including everyday language, idiomatic expressions and collocations that appear in the speaking and writing sections. Reading widely in English is one of the most effective ways to build vocabulary for either test.

Time management during the test is a critical skill for both exams but manifests differently. TOEFL gives you a fixed time for each section and does not allow you to move between sections. IELTS allows some flexibility within sections, particularly in reading and writing. Practising under timed conditions for both tests helps you develop the pacing instincts that prevent you from running out of time on any section.

Daily preparation works because each test rewards consistent, low-stakes exposure more than heroic sprints. Twenty minutes a day of the right kind of practice, a TOEFL lecture transcript one day, an IELTS suthinking preparation note in your own words the next, compounds quickly, and it is gentler than weekend marathons that cut into your other application work. Keep a small error log of the three or four recurring slips, and check each new practice test against that list, because fixing a handful of habits lifts scores faster than buying another book of exercises ever will.

What Each Test Actually Looks Like: Section-by-Section Walkthrough

Understanding the concrete structure of each test helps you visualise what you will face on test day. The following walkthrough covers the key sections of both TOEFL and IELTS.

The TOEFL reading section presents three to four academic passages, each approximately seven hundred words long, followed by ten questions per passage. Passages are drawn from university-level textbooks across various disciplines. You have approximately fifty-four minutes for the entire reading section. The passages are dense and academic, and the questions require careful reading rather than skimming.

The IELTS reading section contains three passages with a total of forty questions, and you have sixty minutes. The question types are more varied than TOEFL, including matching information, matching headings, labelling diagrams, summary completion, sentence completion, short-answer questions and multiple choice.

Toefl vs ielts explained

The TOEFL listening section consists of three to four lectures, each three to five minutes long, followed by two to three conversations. You hear each recording once and then answer questions. The entire listening section takes approximately thirty-six minutes.

The IELTS listening section has four sections with ten questions each, totalling forty questions in thirty minutes. Section one is a conversation in an everyday context. Section two is a monologue on an everyday topic. Section three is a conversation in an academic context. Section four is an academic monologue. You hear each recording once, and you must transfer answers in the final ten minutes.

toefl vs ielts interview questions

TOEFL speaking has four tasks combining independent responses with integrated tasks. IELTS speaking is a three-part face-to-face interview. TOEFL writing has an integrated task and an independent essay. IELTS writing has a task based on visual data and a discursive essay. Understanding these structures before preparation ensures that you practise effectively.

Walking through the sections before you register does not cost practice time; it buys you confidence that saves far more. You learn, for example, that IELTS listening places all its questions in the order of the audio, while TOEFL allows you to take notes during lectures and answer afterwards, and those two mechanics reward opposite habits of attention. By the time the real test arrives, the format should feel familiar enough that the only variable left is the content, which is exactly where preparation belongs.

TOEFL vs IELTS Acceptance by Destination Country and University Type

Both TOEFL and IELTS are accepted by thousands of universities worldwide, but the degree of acceptance varies by destination country and institution type.

In the United States, TOEFL has historically been the dominant test, but IELTS acceptance has grown significantly, and most US universities now accept both tests equally. For undergraduate and most graduate programs, either test is equally accepted, and your choice should be based on which test suits you better.

In the United Kingdom, IELTS has traditionally been the preferred test. The UKVI requires IELTS for visa purposes in certain categories. For Pakistani students targeting the UK, IELTS is the more natural choice, both because of its widespread acceptance and because the British accent used in the test is closer to what you will encounter in the UK.

In Canada, both tests are accepted by virtually all universities and colleges. Canada immigration authorities accept both TOEFL and IELTS for study permit purposes. The choice should come down to personal preference and test format suitability.

Australia and New Zealand strongly favour IELTS. While TOEFL is accepted by Australian universities, IELTS is more widely understood and is the default choice for most Australian applications. For students targeting Australia, IELTS is generally the more practical choice.

In Europe, where Germany, the Netherlands and Scandinavian countries have a growing number of English-taught programs, both tests are widely accepted. For Pakistani students considering multiple destinations, choosing IELTS gives you the broadest acceptance across the UK, Australia and Europe, while choosing TOEFL is most advantageous for US-focused applications.

toefl vs ielts funding and benefits

Treat the acceptance question as a programme-by-program fact rather than a country-wide generalisation, because the two tests trade advantages in ways that brochures cannot capture. A department that promises full funding may quietly prefer the TOEFL because the score report is machine-read by many US systems, while a UK university that publishes a single IELTS requirement for its international office will expect that number and no equivalent. A Quick Decision Framework for Choosing Between TOEFL and IELTS

If you have read through the differences and are still uncertain which test to take, the following decision framework distils the key factors into a simple series of questions.

First, where are you applying? If your primary target is the United States, TOEFL gives you the widest acceptance. If your primary target is the United Kingdom, Australia or New Zealand, IELTS is the more natural choice. If you are applying to multiple countries, IELTS offers the broadest global acceptance.

Second, how do you handle speaking tests? If you are comfortable with a face-to-face conversation, IELTS speaking will likely feel natural. If you prefer speaking into a microphone with structured preparation time, TOEFL speaking may produce a higher score by removing that pressure.

Third, how strong is your academic vocabulary? TOEFL tends to use more academic vocabulary. If you have a strong academic vocabulary, TOEFL rewards that strength. If your English is strong but more general, IELTS may be more accessible.

Fourth, how is your stamina? TOEFL is a longer test, approximately three hours, with a single continuous session. IELTS is slightly shorter overall, and the speaking component is scheduled separately. If you find long tests exhausting, IELTS structure may help you perform better.

Fifth, what is your timeline? If you need results quickly, the computer-delivered IELTS delivers scores in three to five days. TOEFL scores are typically available within six to ten days.

Finally, consider taking a practice test for each. Free official practice tests are available on both the ETS and British Council websites. Compare not just your scores but how you felt during each test. Your comfort level and performance on these practice tests are often the most reliable indicator of which test will produce your best real score.

Once the mock results narrow the choice, resist the urge to keep comparing in circles and lock the decision in writing: test, target score, test date in three options, and the practice materials you will finish first. Set the decision rule now, for example, if the computerised TOEFL mock equals or beats the IELTS mock within target bands, the TOEFL gets the registration, and thereafter no more comparison shopping, only execution. A

decision made once, rather than re-litigated every evening, preserves the energy that actually raises your score, and it leaves you free to convert the identical knowledge, grammar and vocabulary into the format the chosen test wants to see.

How Pakistani Students Typically Perform on TOEFL vs IELTS and Where to Focus

Patterns in how Pakistani test-takers perform on the two tests are useful signals, not guarantees. While every student is different, the consistent feedback from preparation centres and language teachers points to the same few areas where Pakistani candidates gain or lose marks on each exam.

Toefl vs ielts tips explained

In the IELTS listening section, Pakistani students often lose marks on the gap-filling and note-completion tasks because answers must be spelled correctly and within a word limit. Practice groups words such as dates, times and place names carefully, because errors there are marked wrong even when the meaning is clear. In the TOEFL listening section, the challenge for many Pakistani students is stamina: sustaining concentration across lectures that run several minutes long. Building sustained listening sessions into your preparation, rather than only short clips, is the most effective fix.

In the reading sections, vocabulary is the recurring issue. IELTS passages include a wider variety of text types, which suits students who read general English well, while TOEFL passages are strictly academic. If you read academic papers and textbooks regularly, the TOEFL passage style will feel familiar. If you read news and general non-fiction, IELTS gives you a more natural path. Build a vocabulary log of words you meet while reading and test yourself on them weekly, because passive recognition under timed conditions is what the test actually measures.

The speaking sections reward different habits. Pakistani candidates who learned English through formal grammar classes sometimes sound careful but slow, and in the IELTS face-to-face format, an examiner can prompt you, which helps. In the TOEFL recorded format, that prompting does not happen, and hesitation stands out more. Practice recording yourself on a phone and listening back for pauses, fillers and unfinished sentences. The goal is not perfect fluency but consistent, structured responses that stay on topic.

toefl vs ielts essays: what to write

In the writing sections, the difference is between argumentative and descriptive tasks. IELTS Task 1 turns graphs and charts into text, a skill that many students never practise because it does not appear in school writing. TOEFL integrated writing asks you to compare a reading passage with a lecture, which is closer to academic note-taking. Whichever test you choose, write at least two full timed essays per week and get feedback from a teacher or an online marking service, because self-practice without feedback can cement the same errors.

The most reliable way to know where you stand is to take one full timed practice test of each exam before deciding. Compare your section scores, and look at which sections lagged and by how much. Many students find the decision becomes obvious once they see the breakdown, because the test that fits their reading and stamina habits almost always scores higher.

Frequently Asked Questions About TOEFL vs IELTS

Students tend to repeat the same set of questions when choosing between the two tests, so the practical answers below address the most common ones directly.

Can I take both tests and submit the better score? Yes, and some students do. If your applications are spread across countries, or if you simply want options, taking both is legitimate. The cost of preparation and the exam fees are the main drawbacks, so most students who try both take one first, see the result, and only take the second if the first is below the target for their chosen programs.

Toefl vs ielts tips explained

Which test is easier, TOEFL or IELTS? Neither is universally easier. The answer depends on your speaking style, your vocabulary range and your stamina. Students who speak comfortably with a person usually find IELTS speaking easier. Students who prefer structured typing and recorded responses often find TOEFL more predictable. Take a practice test of each rather than relying on general opinions.

toefl vs ielts tips that actually work

Are there differences in how universities view the two tests? For admissions purposes, most universities in Canada, the US, the UK and Australia accept both and scale them against the same English proficiency standard. Where a difference appears is in the context of immigration: the UK and Australia use IELTS more commonly in their visa materials, while the US historically lists TOEFL more often. Neither fact closes any realistic door for you, but it can tip the choice if you have a primary destination.

How to apply for toefl vs ielts

How long do scores stay valid? Both tests report that scores are valid for two years from the test date. Some universities interpret this as valid until the start of your program rather than the date of application, so a score taken more than a year before enrolment can be borderline. If your gap year is long and your first score is near your validity edge, retaking is usually easier than arguing for an exception.

What score do I actually need? The requirement depends on your university and program. A common starting point is an overall band of 6.5 or 7.0 on IELTS, or roughly 90 to 95 on the TOEFL, for taught master programs across most English-speaking countries. Competitive programs ask for higher scores, and some set separate minimums for each section. Confirm the exact figures for each of your target programs and build a preparation target that clears the highest one by a small margin.

If I perform badly on one section, can I retake it? Yes. Both ETS and the IELTS organisations allow you to retake the whole test as often as you like, and there is no limit on attempts. You report whichever sitting gives your best overall profile. On TOEFL, your best scores across multiple sittings can even be combined. Treat the first sitting as a realistic baseline and plan your retake date in advance, rather than deciding whether to retake only after seeing a disappointing score.

Frequently asked questions

Do universities prefer TOEFL or IELTS?+

Most universities accept both equally, so preference rarely decides anything. A minority of specific programmes, professional registrations, or immigration routes favour one, so always check the exact requirement for your target course before booking.

Is TOEFL or IELTS easier to get a high score in?+

Neither is universally easier. TOEFL suits candidates comfortable with computers, typing, and American academic content, while IELTS suits those who prefer varied question types and a live Speaking examiner. The easier test is the one that matches your strengths.

How are the TOEFL and IELTS scores different?+

IELTS is scored on a 0 to 9 band scale in half-band steps, while TOEFL iBT is scored out of 120, with 30 points per section. Convert your target university requirement into both scales before deciding which target feels more achievable.

Can I switch between TOEFL and IELTS if I do poorly?+

Yes. Scores from both are independent, so you can retake either test or switch entirely. However, switching means learning a new format, so it is usually smarter to identify the better-fitting test from the start.

Which test has a better Speaking format?+

That depends on you. IELTS uses a live conversation with a human examiner, which many people find more natural. TOEFL records your spoken answers into a microphone, which suits candidates who prefer privacy over face-to-face interaction.
